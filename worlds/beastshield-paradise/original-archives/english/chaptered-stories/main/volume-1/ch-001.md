# Chapter 1: The Bear and the Door

## Scene-1: Awakening in the Corner

In the corner of the warehouse, piles of old wooden crates emitted a musty odor, mixed with an indescribable, sweet-and-sour stench like metal rust intertwined with biological decay. The concrete floor was rough and cold, with dark stains accumulated in its cracks. He woke from this unpleasant corner, his back pressed against the edge of a cardboard box, a stiff soreness at the back of his neck.

A system prompt box hovered semi-transparently in his field of vision, its cold text stating the cruel rules of this world. He closed the prompt box and slowly swept his gaze across the surroundings. A few steps away, several beastkin bodies lay sprawled—they had wolf muzzles and ears, or the thick, curved horns of cattle, their fur colors indistinguishable in the dim light. They all wore the same tight military-green uniform, short-sleeved shirt and shorts style, the fabric stretched taut over either burly or lean body contours. There were no obvious external wounds, but the crotch area of each corpse was a mess, thick milky-white fluid seeping from the fabric of the tight pants, drying into crusted chunks and winding trails on the dark green surface. That sweet-and-sour stench mainly originated from here.

He propped himself up; the sound of fabric rubbing against concrete was jarringly loud in the overly quiet warehouse. He checked himself: simple gray coarse cloth shirt and pants, rough to the touch but sturdy. He flexed his fingers—ordinary strength, no special equipment. He was Memetic Singularity, a player who had loaded a few small "wish modules." He didn't like this game's default playstyle.

The warehouse was spacious, its high roof lost in shadow, rusty metal beams crisscrossing overhead. Rows of shelves stood like silent giants in the distance, stacked with dust-covered crates and cages, their outlines blurred. The only light came from a few high, grime-covered strip windows, pale daylight slanting in and cutting through the air, defining clearly visible columns of suspended dust. The air was stagnant and cold.

Then he heard a sound—heavy, slow, rhythmic breathing, carrying the low throat-rumble of a large animal. The sound came from the direction of the warehouse door.

He pressed against the edge of the shelves, letting his body melt into the shadows they cast, moving slowly. With each step, the outer edge of his sole touched the ground first, confirming no small debris would make a sound, then slowly pressing down fully. Dust motes floating in the air swirled gently in the faint breeze of his movement. After rounding two shelf corners, he saw it.

The warehouse's heavy metal sliding door was shut tight, and before it stood a guard. A brown bear beastkin.

The guard had his back to him, his figure extremely burly. Even from a distance, he could clearly see the outline of the other's back muscles swelling beneath the tight uniform. The military-green short sleeves were fully stretched, the scapular muscle groups like two heavy shields, rising and falling slightly with slow breaths. The sleeves tightly hugged the upper arms, tracing the full arcs of the biceps and triceps. The waist and hips were broad, the green shorts stretched taut by firm glutes and thick thighs, the hems ending mid-thigh, revealing a section of lower leg wrapped in white knee-high socks. The socks were heavy cotton, with military-green vertical stripes on the sides, the cuffs digging into the full calf muscles and leaving a shallow indentation. On his feet were a pair of matching military-green sneakers.

The guard just stood there, like a brown-green hill, blocking the only exit. His tail was short, fluffy, pressed against his rear, motionless. He wore a brimless cap of the same color, from beneath which the outline of round, thick bear ears peeked.

He observed quietly for several minutes. The guard would occasionally turn his neck left and right with extreme slowness, emitting a slight clicking sound of bone rubbing, or lift a thick leg clad in white socks to tap the ground lightly with his sole, but mostly he just stood in silence. The guard's very presence radiated a thick, impossible-to-ignore oppressive aura, mixed with the faint animal scent unique to large bear beastkin—something like fur and heavy body heat—and the faint smell of uniform fabric mixed with sweat.

The system mission prompt was concise and cruel: Kill him. Complete the tutorial.

He did not choose to attack. He retreated backward, back into the deeper shadows of the shelves, leaning against a cold metal container. He closed his eyes—not in surrender, but to focus his spirit.

Then, he moved forward again. This time, he was even more cautious, using the shelves, stacked wooden crates, and the interplay of light and shadow as cover. His movements were slow and steady, his breathing held to the minimum. The distance shortened.

Ten meters. Eight meters. Five meters.

He could now see the texture of the guard's uniform fabric more clearly, see the short, stiff brown-black hairs at the nape of his neck poking out from under the cap brim, see the fine down on the skin surface of the calf muscles slightly bulging beneath the top of the white socks, just below the elastic cuff. That mixed scent of clean sweat, leather conditioner, and faint bear body odor drifted over faintly with the weak air currents.

Three meters.

He stopped. At this distance, any tiny abnormal sound might be detected. The guard still had his back to him, the tip of his thick tail twitching very slightly.

He took a deep breath—not from nervousness, but to bring his body into optimal coordination. Then, he moved.

His steps were swift yet light and nimble. Two strides crossed the final distance. His left hand shot out from the side-rear, fingers spread, targeting the short-haired, thickly muscled area below the back of the guard's neck—trying to cover the mouth and nose would face powerful neck strength, while controlling the back of the neck could better limit violent head turning. At the same time, his right hand was faster, following some "rule" set by this world, precisely threading through the gap between the guard's side and arm, and through the fabric of those tight military-green shorts, grabbing the heavy, warm bulge between the guard's legs.

## Scene-2: The Bear's Terror

**Legs are a bit numb from standing...**

Xiong Zai—his colleagues all called him that, and he was used to it—muttered inwardly. His round, thick ears twitched unconsciously beneath his cap brim, catching any unusual sounds in the warehouse. Only the low hum of distant ventilation ducts, and his own heavy, slow heartbeat. He gently flexed his left ankle; the cuff of the white knee-high sock was a bit tight, the bulging calf muscle compressed, blood circulation not quite smooth. He didn't dare make big movements; the captain said, when guarding a door, one must look like a guard.

**This damn place is so cold... when is the shift change...** He sighed inwardly, his round eyes staring at the mottled rust traces on the metal door ahead, his gaze somewhat vacant.

**Seems like there's stew in the cafeteria at noon... hope that kid A-Lie isn't late again...**

Thinking of steaming hot stew, his empty stomach rumbled softly. He swallowed saliva, his Adam's apple bobbing, the short, stiff brown hairs on his thick neck rippling with the movement.

His thoughts drifted far away. **Mom mentioned back pain in her last letter... wonder if this month's salary sent back is enough to buy some medicated patches...**

He remembered that small town in the north, always drifting with snow and the scent of pine needles. He remembered his mother's stooped figure before the stove, remembered the empty feeling in his heart when he left home. This made him a bit distracted.

In that very instant—

The hairs on the back of his neck suddenly stood on end!

Some instinct honed over years of survival in the wilderness, like an icy needle, pierced through his drowsiness!

**Wrong—**

This thought had just risen, not yet transformed into a muscle action command, when a palm shot out from the side-rear, fingers spread, with irresistible force, pressing firmly over his mouth and nose!

The palm was warm, with some rough calluses on the center, pressing tightly against his thick lips and black nose, completely stifling the instinctive roar of terror in his throat! The force was astonishing, pressing his jawbone to the point of slight deformation.

At the same time—

The other hand was faster.

Fast as a striking viper, threading through the gap between his side and arm, through that layer of uniform shorts fabric already warmed by his body heat and stretched taut, grabbing the heavy, soft, warm bulge between his legs!

**Mmph—!?!!**

Indescribable agony!

It wasn't just pain; it was more like an extreme vulnerability of being grasped at a fatal weakness, originating from the deepest part of physiological structure, like high-voltage electricity instantly racing through his entire body! That soft organ in his crotch suddenly contracted in his own pants, then stiffened, followed by uncontrollable, intense, bone-shattering pain spreading through every nerve!

Xiong Zai's massive body went rigid!

All muscle tension—those thick back muscles, broad shoulder muscles, thick arms, pillar-like legs—collapsed and disintegrated completely within a thousandth of a second. His over-two-hundred-kilogram heavy body completely lost support, going limp backward and falling heavily against the attacker's body.

The impact made him collide solidly with a relatively slender but exceptionally steady body. He could feel the rise and fall of the other's chest, could smell an extremely faint, dry, clean scent on the other like sunlight on wood, mixing with his own adrenaline-tinged, faintly sour sweat brought on by pain and fear, filling his nasal cavity.

**Down there... going to break... hurts so much... can't move... no strength at all...**

His mind went blank, leaving only pure physiological pain and animalistic fear. The edges of his vision darkened, his ears buzzing. He tried to struggle, but his thick arms only lifted halfway before falling limply, the once bulging muscles now soft as overcooked noodles. Legs clad in white socks and sneakers kicked futilely against the ground, soles scraping the rough concrete with "shh-shh" sounds, unable to provide any force to push him away. That hand was still gripping his vital point, stable and sustained pressure making every attempt to gather strength dissolve into deeper helplessness. The pressure wasn't trying to crush that organ, but maintaining a sustained, crushing sense of control with perfect precision.

He was half-dragged, half-carried to the side. His heavy body was like a bag of waterlogged sand, all its weight pressing on the attacker behind him, whose figure was clearly much smaller. He could hear the other's slightly hurried but still steady breathing right by his ear, warm breath spraying on the short hairs at the back of his neck, bringing a minute itch, but soon overwhelmed by stronger fear. His back rubbed against the coarse cloth of the attacker's chest; he could feel the other was also straining, waist and back muscles taut, bearing his weight.

**Going to die... like those corpses... played to death and shot to death...**

This thought pierced through his chaotic fear like an ice spike. He remembered the colleagues' corpses in the corner with their messy crotches, remembered the pain and humiliation they might have experienced before death. That white fluid, those limp limbs, those hollow eyes...

**I'll become like that too...** Fear flooded him like icy tide.

**I don't want to die... Mom is still waiting for me to send money...** He remembered his mother's aged face, remembered her nagging instructions in her letters. Sourness rushed to his nasal cavity, his eyes instantly wet.

Finally, he was dragged into a deeper corner formed by tall shelves and a wall. Here the light was even dimmer, the air seemed colder too. The attacker hooked a heel against an empty wooden crate nearby; the bottom of the crate scraped against the concrete with a slight sound, moving a bit to partially block the view from the corridor direction.

Then, he was pressed to sit against the cold wall. The rough concrete surface, through the thin uniform fabric, transmitted chill to his thick back muscles. The hand covering his mouth relaxed slightly, allowing him to breathe more smoothly through his nose, but fingers still pressed against his lips and the sides of his nose, maintaining readiness to clamp down again at any moment. Fresh air rushed into his lungs, carrying the warehouse's dust smell and the dust kicked up during the dragging, making his heaving chest calm slightly.

"Don't move." A voice sounded by his ear, steady, low, even carrying a deliberately maintained calmness. The voice was very close, ensuring only the two of them could hear clearly. "Breathe slowly. I said, I don't plan to kill you immediately."

Xiong Zai's eyes were wide open, pupils shrunk to pinpoints in the dim light, filled with tears—partly physiological, partly from extreme terror. Tears overflowed from the edges of his eyes, flowing down the fur of his cheeks, glinting faintly in the dim light. His body was still trembling uncontrollably, his thick chest muscles heaving greatly beneath the tight green uniform, each expansion making the metal Beastshield insignia on his chest reflect slightly. He could feel his heart pounding madly in his chest like a trapped beast, slamming against his ribs.

He could feel that hand controlling his fatal weakness still hadn't let go. That clear threat and sustained sense of helplessness bound every muscle that tried to resist like cold chains. The fingers of that hand even adjusted position slightly, making him more acutely aware of the fact that he was being controlled.

"Can you hear me?" That voice sounded again, still steady, no emotion detectable.

Xiong Zai nodded with extreme difficulty, the movement minute. This made the short hairs at the back of his head rub against the rough wall. His cap brim was askew, exposing most of his round, thick bear face, which at this moment was written full of terror and pain, his black nose constantly flaring, exhaling hot, hurried breaths.

"Good." The attacker—he could now determine it was a monkey beastkin, the voice similar to those agile, cunning jungle neighbors in his impression, but more steady and forceful, lacking those neighbors' habitual frivolity and mockery. "Now, I ask, you answer. Keep your voice low. First question: What's your name? What's your Beastshield designation?"

Name? Designation?

Xiong Zai's sluggish brain worked with difficulty. **Why is he asking this? To record an execution list? Or...** Fear made his thoughts chaotic. He didn't want to say it; saying it might be the final stroke on a death warrant.

"Speak." That hand gripping his crotch applied a little pressure, neither light nor heavy.

"Urrgh...!" Xiong Zai's body contracted from the pain, a suppressed groan squeezed from his throat, tears welling up again. The pressure wasn't fatal, but the humiliation and fear it brought far exceeded the pain itself. He didn't dare hesitate anymore.

"...Xiong... Xiong Zai..." His voice was hoarse, carrying a heavy nasal tone and crying quality, the low voice characteristic of bear beastkin, but now shattered by fear, almost squeezed out from between his teeth. "They all call me Xiong Zai... designation... G-1..."

"Xiong Zai. G-1." The other repeated, tone flat, like confirming the label of an object, no joy or anger detectable. "How many people came this time? What's the specific mission?"

Xiong Zai pressed his thick lips together, his Adam's apple bobbing violently. His lips were dry, with tiny tooth marks from when he had bitten them nervously earlier.

**Giving up intel is betrayal... might be punished by the company...**

He remembered the captain's cold face, remembered those colleagues who disappeared due to "incompetence." But the sustained pressure from that hand, and the unignorable, crushing pain and vulnerability from below, overwhelmed all considerations of loyalty. Survival instinct took the upper hand.

He licked his lips, trying to moisten his parched throat, but his tongue was also full of the astringent taste brought by fear.

"...Eleven." He finally spoke in a low voice, very slowly, as if each word was being fished out with difficulty from a swamp of fear, his voice light as a whisper. "Ten in green clothes, like me. And one in blue clothes, the captain."

"Specifically. Ten green uniforms, who are they? Who's the captain?"

He breathed a few times, trying hard to recall. **Outside in the truck... inside the warehouse...** His memory wasn't good; normally he didn't pay attention to these things, but now at the moment of life and death, his brain was forced to operate at high speed.

"...Outside in the truck, there's a driver, from the Huang cattle family, even bigger than me but a bit slow, likes sleeping..." he said断断续续地, "Inside the warehouse, besides me, there are seven. There are two wolf brothers, gray fur, one fast runner, one with a good nose, responsible for east and south patrol. There's a black panther, doesn't talk much, likes staying in shadows, moves on top of the shelves. There's a wild boar, bad temper, pretty strong too, guarding the west side door. And two dog clan, one is a German shepherd, pretty alert, one is a hyena, a bit... sinister. They patrol the north and middle areas together."

He paused, carefully recalling the captain's appearance. That blue, upright figure, the authoritative gaze, that scar on his face... "The blue-uniformed captain is from the tiger clan, he's in charge of command, checking the cargo list, seems to be looking for something... specifically I'm not clear, us small soldiers only responsible for cleaning the scene and guarding." He confessed honestly, not daring to hide anything.

"Clean the scene?"

"...It's... dealing with all those still moving." Xiong Zai's voice dropped lower, carrying a trace of barely detectable hardship. He remembered the scene when they first entered, those still twitching bodies, the captain's cold orders, colleagues' numb actions... He didn't want to recall those. "When we came in, there were already several lying here, like those over there..." His gaze involuntarily glanced toward the nearby corpses, a complex emotion flashing in his eyes—fear, perhaps a trace of sympathetic grief, but quickly forcibly suppressed by him, leaving only deeper fear. He lowered his head, staring at his white-socked feet that were trembling slightly from helplessness.

"Did you know them? Those corpses."

He shook his head, the cap brim swaying with it, rubbing against the wall with a rustling sound. "Didn't know... not our people. Maybe... original warehouse people, or something else..." His voice was almost inaudible.

The monkey beastkin fell into brief silence. This silence was like torture to Xiong Zai. The warehouse's stillness seemed to have weight, pressing on his heart, making him unable to breathe. He could only hear his own heavy breathing, the other's steady breathing, and the distant, rhythmic footsteps—probably other green uniforms on patrol. Each time those footsteps sounded, his heart tightened, both afraid of being discovered by companions and faintly generating a twisted hope, but soon overwhelmed by deeper fear: if discovered, his current appearance... he didn't dare think.

His thick fingers unconsciously dug at a small pit in the concrete floor, the hard calluses on his fingertips rubbing against the rough ground, making an extremely faint "shh-shh" sound. Sweat continuously seeped from his forehead and temples, flowing down the fur of his cheeks, some flowing into his eyes, bringing stinging pain, but he didn't dare raise his hand to wipe.

Time passed second by second, each second as long as a century.

## Scene-3: The Leaf and the Door

The silence lasted about ten heartbeats.

Xiong Zai's breathing calmed slightly, but his body's trembling didn't stop. That hand was still on his leg, neither applying more pressure nor letting go. This state of suspense was more torturous than direct pain. He didn't dare look at the other, only staring at a thin crack on the ground, black dirt accumulated in it.

"Xiong Zai."

The voice sounded again, still steady, but seeming to have something more than before. Xiong Zai wasn't sure what it was. He carefully lifted his eyelids, looking toward the direction of the voice.

The monkey beastkin was looking at him. At such close distance, Xiong Zai could see the other's eyes clearly—not the predatory sharp gaze he imagined, but rather... a calm, focused, even slightly inquisitive look. Like the forest ranger back home checking a sick tree.

"I asked you many questions just now." The monkey beastkin spoke, very slowly, as if each word was carefully considered. "Now, I want to ask you one last question. This question, you can choose not to answer. But if you answer, please tell the truth."

Xiong Zai's throat moved. **The last question?** Was it going to ask the captain's weakness? Or what was hidden in the warehouse? He nodded, the movement so small it was almost invisible.

The monkey beastkin's free left hand moved. He laboriously reached into his bosom—from inside that coarse gray cloth shirt, and took out something.

A wooden badge.

It wasn't large, slightly smaller than Xiong Zai's palm, appearing warm light brown in the dim light. The wood grain was clear and natural, like the rings of an old tree. The shape was somewhat peculiar, like a slightly closed leaf, or like a pair of protective hands cupped together, with a small circular indentation in the center. The badge surface was smooth and warm, carrying a soft luster, as if it had been long and gently rubbed by someone.

Xiong Zai stared blankly at that badge. It was different from everything he had ever seen—not a metal weapon, not a plastic product, not synthetic uniform fiber. It was wood, real, warm wood. It made him remember the trees in the forest back home, remember the old wooden stick his mother used to poke the firewood by the winter stove, remember some distant, almost forgotten sense of security.

The monkey beastkin held the badge in his palm, steadily raising it before his eyes. That hand was very steady, fingers slender, knuckles distinct, the movement of holding the badge carrying a strange solemnity.

"Xiong Zai," the voice was very light, but each word was terrifyingly clear, "just now, when I asked you those questions, when you told me that information... in your heart, was there ever a moment when you thought of something else?"

Xiong Zai blinked, tears making his vision somewhat blurry. **Something else?** He didn't understand.

"For example," the monkey beastkin paused, seeming to search for suitable words, "did you ever think... if only things weren't like this? If only you didn't have to stand here? If you could... leave here, go somewhere else, do something else?"

Xiong Zai's body went rigid again.

**How does he know?**

That deeply hidden thought, which even he himself didn't dare clearly acknowledge, that in countless cold nights of standing guard, when witnessing companions become corpses, when receiving his mother's nagging yet warm letters, would quietly emerge before being quickly suppressed by him...

**I thought about it.** He said in his heart.

I thought about how good it would be if I could leave this damn place. I thought about how good it would be if I could go home. I thought about how good it would be if I didn't have to listen to the captain's orders anymore, didn't have to worry about whether tomorrow I would become a corpse. I thought about how good it would be if... if I could follow someone not so scary, someone who wouldn't casually throw me away like garbage.

But he didn't dare say it. Saying it was too shameful. A Beastshield grunt, a silly bear who only knew how to stand guard and exert dumb strength, actually dared to have such thoughts? Saying it would only invite mockery, punishment, or... as now, being used as a ridiculous handle.

He lowered his head, digging at that small pit on the ground even harder, the fingertips transmitting a hard sensation. His face grew hot again, this time not from fear, but from shame. Tears welled up again, dripping onto the back of his hand, warm, then quickly turning cold.

He didn't answer. But his violently trembling shoulders, his suppressed sobs, his posture of almost trying to curl into himself, had already said everything.

Some time passed. The distant patrol footsteps seemed closer, then gradually faded.

The monkey beastkin withdrew the hand holding the badge. Xiong Zai thought he was going to take it back, and something inexplicably emptied in his heart. But that badge didn't return to the other's bosom.

Instead, the monkey beastkin reached out and gently grasped Xiong Zai's large, furry hand that was digging at the ground.

Xiong Zai jumped in fright, his fingers suddenly retracting, but not breaking free. That hand was warm, dry, steady. The other slowly unfolded his hand, palm upward, then gently placed that warm wooden badge into his palm.

The warm touch of the wood instantly transmitted through the skin of his palm. The warmth wasn't hot, wasn't burning, like a stone sunbathed at noon in spring, mild and lasting.

Xiong Zai stared blankly at the badge in his palm, then looked up at the monkey beastkin, completely not understanding what this meant.

"Hold it." The monkey beastkin said, voice softer than before, but still clear, "This is for you."

**For me?** Xiong Zai's brain was even more chaotic. Why give me this? What kind of trap is this? A new torture method?

The monkey beastkin looked at his confused and fearful eyes, and continued: "Xiong Zai, I said just now, I ask one last question. That question actually was: Are you willing to accept this? Are you willing... to believe what I'm about to say?"

Xiong Zai's breathing stopped. He stared at the badge in his palm, the wood grain appearing deep in the dim light. **Believe?**
Believe what? A person who just attacked me, made me suffer unbearable pain, and is still controlling me?

But he didn't throw the badge away. That warmth of the wood, like magic, stuck to his palm.

The monkey beastkin let go of the hand holding his wrist, and also let go of that hand which had been near his vital point all along, bringing him endless terror and helplessness. Completely let go.

The sudden freedom made Xiong Zai's body sway. He instinctively wanted to curl up, wanted to cover his painful crotch, wanted to retreat. But he didn't move. He just sat there, sitting against the wall, one hand spread open, palm holding that unfamiliar wooden badge.

"I won't kill you, Xiong Zai." The monkey beastkin said, voice calm as if stating a fact, "Never planned to kill you from the start."

Xiong Zai's round eyes grew even wider. Tears were still flowing, but he had forgotten to wipe them.

"I asked your name, asked how many of you there were, not for an execution list." The monkey beastkin continued, gaze always on his face, "I did it to know you. To know who stands before me."

**Know me?** Xiong Zai had never heard such words. In Beastshield, no one needed to know "Xiong Zai," they only needed to know "G-1."

"I saw you standing here." The monkey beastkin's gaze swept over his robust body, over his tight uniform, over the red marks on his white knee-high socks, "I saw you were cold, your legs were numb, you were thinking of home, of your mother, looking forward to cafeteria stew, worried about colleagues being late, afraid of becoming like those corpses."

Each sentence was like a key, precisely opening one after another tightly closed door in Xiong Zai's heart. Those trivial thoughts he considered weakness and shame, now spoken by the other in such a calm tone, without mockery, without judgment, just... spoken.

"I also saw," the monkey beastkin's voice grew even lighter, like sharing a secret, "there was a very, very small voice in your heart, saying 'I don't want this.' Saying 'If only I could leave.' Saying 'If only someone could take me away.'"

Xiong Zai's lips trembled. He could no longer control himself, suppressed whimpering sounds leaking from his throat. He lowered his head, large teardrops hitting the ground, splashing fine dust. He couldn't speak, could only nod forcefully, again and again.

**Yes, yes, yes... that's what I thought... that's what I always thought...**

"That voice," the monkey beastkin said, "isn't stupid at all, Xiong Zai. That's the part of your heart that's still alive. That's the thing that can't be erased even by wearing a uniform, holding a gun, and standing here."

Xiong Zai cried even harder. He raised his other large hand, clumsily trying to wipe away tears, but the more he wiped, the more came. He was like a lost child finally found, all his grievances, fears, and loneliness bursting their banks at this moment.

The monkey beastkin waited patiently, without urging, without comforting, just quietly sitting across from him, sitting on the same cold concrete floor, waiting for him to cry.

Gradually, the crying subsided, becoming intermittent sobs, finally becoming tired, satisfied sighs. Xiong Zai still used his sleeve to roughly wipe his face; the sleeve was wet with tears and snot. He sniffled, raised his head, eyes and nose red.

The monkey beastkin looked at him, a very faint but very real smile appearing on his face.

"Feeling a bit better?" he asked.

Xiong Zai nodded, still hiccuping slightly.

"Then, Xiong Zai," the monkey beastkin leaned forward, gaze looking directly into his wet eyes, voice becoming especially solemn, "now, I have a very important question to ask you. This might be the most important question in your life. So, please listen carefully, think carefully, then answer with your heart."

Xiong Zai straightened his body, using all his strength to concentrate. He gripped the badge in his palm tighter, the wood's edges pressing into his palm flesh, bringing real sensation.

The monkey beastkin said word by word:

"**Are you willing to become my family?**"

...

The warehouse was completely silent.

Xiong Zai's mouth hung open, staring blankly at the other. His brain crashed again. **Family?** This word echoed repeatedly in his mind, like a bell, one strike, then another.

**Family...** Not superior and subordinate, not master and servant, not using and being used. It was family. People who take care of each other, support each other, can share happiness, and can also share pain... Like mother, no matter if you're stupid or useless, would always leave you a bowl of hot soup...

He had never imagined this possibility. Never dared to imagine.

"Fa... family..." he murmured in repetition, voice hoarse, "I... can I? I can't do anything... I only know how to..."

"You can stand guard," the monkey beastkin gently interrupted him, "which shows you have patience and a sense of responsibility. You're strong, which means you can protect important people. You think of home, of your mother, which shows your heart is warm, hasn't grown cold. These are all precious qualities of a family member."

Xiong Zai's tears welled up again. But this time, the tears were hot, scalding.

"Becoming family," the monkey beastkin continued, voice steady and forceful, "means you're no longer alone. It means there's a place—not just one place—whose door is always open to you. It means on your birthday, someone will remember, someone will prepare a real cake for you, instead of only being able to imagine it in your heart."

He paused, looking at Xiong Zai's shocked and chaotic eyes: "Of course, this also means responsibility. It means when other family members need you, you will also extend a helping hand. It means we must protect this small, warm world together, not letting the outside cold swallow it. Your past won't disappear; your experiences in Beastshield, your longing for your mother... all of these, family members will face with you, not solve for you. We share strength, but don't erase scars."

After speaking, he said no more, just quietly waited. The light of a fireplace fire jumped on his face—no, there was no fireplace, that was the cold daylight filtering through the high strip windows of the warehouse. But Xiong Zai looked at his face and felt that light was unbelievably warm.

Xiong Zai lowered his head, looking at his palm. That wooden badge lay quietly there, warm, simple, yet seemingly as heavy as a thousand jun.

He remembered Beastshield's cold corridors, remembered colleagues' numb or even mocking expressions when someone died, remembered himself being sent to one post after another like a tool, life's meaning reduced to just the number on his uniform and the ever-approaching death.

He also remembered the crooked characters in his mother's letters, remembered the crackling fire in winter back home, remembered that never-extinguished desire deep in his heart to "go back."

Then, he looked at the monkey beastkin before him. This person who appeared inexplicably, subdued him, asked him a bunch of questions, saw through all his weaknesses and desires, and now was handing him a possibility of "home." No, not a weird person.

Xiong Zai took a deep breath. This breath was drawn very deep, making his chest bulge high, then slowly exhaling. He wiped away the last tears, and with those still-red but no longer confused round eyes, looked at the monkey beastkin.

"I... I have many questions." His voice was still hoarse, but much steadier, "About... where home is, about what I'll do in the future, about... Mom."

"I know." The monkey beastkin smiled, "We have plenty of time. Can talk slowly, a day, a month, a year."

"I might... still be stupid, slow to react."

"Doesn't matter. Home doesn't lack smart people; what it lacks is someone as steady and reliable as you."

"I... I'm willing." Xiong Zai said, voice not loud, but exceptionally clear. He gripped the badge in his palm tighter, the warm edges of the wood deeply imprinting into his skin. "I'm willing to become your family. I'm willing... to learn."

Some things had already changed forever.

### Story Reflections

When the heavy body can finally relax against the wall, instead of tensing into a mountain with only outward show; when hot tears can flow freely, instead of being frozen in the eye sockets by fear; when the palm can grip a simple piece of wood that belongs to oneself, instead of forever holding empty or clenching weapons—some changes happen in these most minute tactile sensations.

There are no heroes in Xiong Zai's story. Only an ordinary soul, crushed by a vast, indifferent system until he grew accustomed to bending over, who at the most unexpected moment was truly "seen" by another pair of eyes. Seen in his exhaustion, seen in his trivial concerns, seen in his hidden desires, and seen in his pure, reliable essence masked by inferiority.

That question "Are you willing to become my family?" wasn't asking about ability, or value, or loyalty. It was asking something more fundamental: after stripping away all numbers, uniforms, missions, and fears, does that remaining, naked "you" still dare to believe, to connect, to choose a different possibility?

This choice is so difficult because it requires Xiong Zai to first admit that "unqualified" desire in his heart; this choice is also so simple because it only needs the most instinctive "willing" expressed through tears and nodding. When the wooden badge falls into the palm, what warms is not just the skin, but that nearly frozen "self," that tiny nerve under the cold rules.

From now on, a full semen gauge may no longer mean a terminal ejaculation, but a fatigue signal that needs to be cared for; strength will no longer be used only for "cleaning" missions or meaningless standing guard, but perhaps one day will be used to move a brick for a real home, to hand a cup of water to a crying family member. These tiny possibilities, like seeds, have been buried into Xiong Zai's sleeping dreams.

The warehouse is still vast and cold; other green-uniformed figures still move regularly in the shadows. But in this unremarkable corner, a brand-new door has quietly opened for a brown bear named Xiong Zai. What's behind the door, he still doesn't know. But he grips the wooden badge in his hand tightly, and in sleep, reveals a smile.

This is perhaps the gentlest, yet most determined way to resist a cold world: planting one after another wooden, sprouting promise in the chests of those designated as "enemies."
