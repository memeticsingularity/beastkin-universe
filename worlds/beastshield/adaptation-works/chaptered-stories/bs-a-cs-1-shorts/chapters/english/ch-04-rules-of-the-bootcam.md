> *The overt rule here is the law of the jungle, but the real rule is: you are always on someone else's menu.*

---

On the abandoned training ground of **Beast Shield Boot Camp**, the setting sun stretched several figures into long shadows. The air was thick with dust, sweat, and the mixed scent unique to male beastmen—the product of hundreds of young males' hormones fermenting in an enclosed space.

**G-633-Lion Beastman-Shi Kuang** and **G-634-Hyena Beastman-Lie Bao** surrounded a figure curled up on the ground.

That was **G-635-Antelope Beastman-Ling Qie**.

Shi Kuang had the typical magnificent build of a lion beastman—broad shoulders and thick pectoral muscles beneath a golden mane, his green tight-fitting uniform stretched taut by his muscular frame, the Beast Shield logo on his chest nearly bursting at the seams. A cruel smile played on his face, thick arms bulging with veins.

Lie Bao was relatively leaner, but his muscle lines were well-defined. The characteristic hunched posture of a hyena beastman made him look even more aggressive. He grinned, revealing uneven sharp teeth, a low chuckle rumbling from his throat.

Ling Qie curled on the ground, hands over his head, his green uniform covered in dust and several clear shoe prints. His slender antelope horns reflected faint light in the sunset, his thin body trembling continuously with fear. His antelope ears were pressed tightly against his head, tail tucked between his legs.

"With that scrawny body of yours, you think you deserve to be in Beast Shield?" Shi Kuang kicked Ling Qie's side with his army-green athletic shoes—not too hard, not too light, just enough to make the other grunt in pain. "Can't even finish a hundred push-ups, you're a waste of food."

"I... I'll work harder..." Ling Qie's voice was as faint as a mosquito's buzz, carrying a sobbing tone.

"Work harder?" Lie Bao squatted down, grabbing Ling Qie's light brown hair, forcing his head up. "Look at that face of yours, pretty as a girl. Beast Shield wants fighters, not vases."

Ling Qie's face already had several bruises, tear stains at the corners of his eyes. He had the typical delicate features of an antelope beastman—large eyes, small nose, thin lips—in a place like boot camp that worshipped violence, this became an original sin.

"If I don't teach you a lesson today, you won't know the rules." Shi Kuang flexed his thick wrists, knuckles cracking. "Take off your socks."

"What?" Ling Qie's eyes widened in terror.

"Don't understand human speech?" Lie Bao tightened his grip. "Take off those white socks on your feet. We want to 'inspect' them."

In Beast Shield Boot Camp, taking off someone's socks was the ultimate humiliation—it meant stripping away the last dignity of a soldier. The numbers on the bottoms of the white socks were the sole identity markers of Beast Shield employees, and the vouchers for bounty redemption after their deaths.

Ling Qie's tears finally flowed. With trembling hands, he began to untie his shoelaces.

"Stop!"

A voice came from the entrance to the training ground.

All three turned their heads.

**G-636-Deer Beastman-Lu Zheng**
stood there, also wearing the green uniform, but standing straight. He had the slender yet sturdy build of a deer beastman, his light brown fur gleaming softly in the sunset, antlers branching elegantly, eyes clear and determined. His pectoral muscles outlined clear contours beneath the tight uniform—not as massive as Shi Kuang's, but containing no small amount of power.

"Bullying the weak, what kind of skill is that?" Lu Zheng's voice was not loud, but clear. "We're all in the same class, we should help each other."

Shi Kuang and Lie Bao exchanged a glance, simultaneously revealing mocking smiles.

"Oh, a 'justice warrior' has arrived." Shi Kuang released Ling Qie, turning to walk toward Lu Zheng, each step carrying oppressive weight. "What, you want to stand up for him?"

Lu Zheng met Shi Kuang's gaze without flinching: "I just think that Beast Shield needs united soldiers, not thugs who fight amongst themselves."

"Hahahaha!" Lie Bao burst into piercing laughter. "Listen to that, how noble. Kid, is this your first day in boot camp? At Beast Shield, the law of the jungle is the only rule."

"Then let me see how 'strong' you are." Lu Zheng assumed a combat stance, movements standard—clearly, he had trained seriously.

A flash of cruel light passed through Shi Kuang's eyes.

"Very well." he said. "Since you're asking for death, I'll grant it."

The battle was almost one-sided.

Lu Zheng had indeed received training. His movements were agile, his techniques standard, but he lacked combat experience. And Shi Kuang and Lie Bao—they had already honed the dirtiest, most effective fighting skills through multiple "private training sessions."

Shi Kuang attacked head-on, thick arms throwing heavy punches, which Lu Zheng dodged sideways. But just as Lu Zheng was about to counterattack, Lie Bao struck from the side, kicking the back of Lu Zheng's knee.

Lu Zheng grunted, dropping to one knee.

Shi Kuang seized the opportunity, an uppercut smashing heavily into Lu Zheng's chin. Lu Zheng's head snapped backward, antlers hitting the wall with a dull "thud."

"That's all you've got?" Shi Kuang grabbed Lu Zheng's collar, lifting him up, his other hand clenched into a fist, delivering three consecutive blows to Lu Zheng's abdomen.

Each blow landed solidly, producing dull "thud-thud" sounds.

Lu Zheng's body arched like a shrimp, abdominal muscles spasming in agony beneath the green uniform. He gritted his teeth, not screaming, but cold sweat already beaded on his forehead.

"Pretty tough." Lie Bao circled behind Lu Zheng, kicking the back of his legs, forcing him to his knees, then grabbed one of Lu Zheng's antlers, pulling backward with force.

"Crack—"

A slight sound of bone fracturing.

Lu Zheng finally let out a pained grunt, his body trembling violently.

"Let him go!" Ling Qie struggled to stand up, but Shi Kuang kicked him back to the ground.

"Don't worry, you're next." Shi Kuang grinned, pulling a **hardened steel dagger** from his waist—that was a trophy he had stolen from a veteran of the previous class.

He walked before Lu Zheng, squatted down, using the tip of the knife to lift Lu Zheng's chin.

"You know?" Shi Kuang's voice lowered, carrying mockery. "In boot camp, the last thing needed is your kind of 'sense of justice.' It'll get you killed."

Lu Zheng raised his head, blood trickling from the corner of his mouth, but his eyes remained firm: "At least... I did the right thing..."

"The right thing?" Shi Kuang sneered. "Then take your 'right thing' to hell."

The dagger in his hand stabbed viciously into Lu Zheng's chest.

"Squelch."

The blade pierced through the green tight-fitting uniform, through skin, muscle, straight to the heart.

Lu Zheng's body went rigid, eyes widening, pupils rapidly dilating. He opened his mouth, as if wanting to say something, but only a mouthful of blood poured out.

Shi Kuang twisted the dagger, then pulled it out violently.

Blood gushed out, splashing all over his face. The warm liquid carried a strong metallic scent and the sweet, clear body odor unique to deer beastmen, mixing into a strange smell.

Lu Zheng's body fell forward, crashing heavily to the ground. His limbs twitched a few times, then went completely still. The green uniform was rapidly soaked by the blood pouring from his chest, turning dark red. His eyes remained open, gazing at the sky, losing all luster.

"See that?" Shi Kuang turned, looking at Ling Qie collapsed on the ground, licking the blood that had splashed onto his lips. "This is what happens to those who stick their noses in."

Ling Qie was already paralyzed with fear, shaking like a sieve, tears and snot covering his face.

Lie Bao walked to Lu Zheng's corpse, bent down, grabbed a foot wearing a white sock, and pulled hard.

"Rip—"

The white sock was torn off, revealing Lu Zheng's slender, light brown foot. Lie Bao held the sock in his hand, looking at the number on the bottom: "G-636. Not bad, one hundred RUB in the bag."

He stuffed the sock into his pocket, then looked at Shi Kuang: "Continue?"

Shi Kuang nodded, walking back toward Ling Qie, the dagger in his hand still dripping blood.

"Now, it's your turn." Shi Kuang squatted before Ling Qie, using the back of the knife to pat the other's trembling cheek. "Do you want it quick, or do you want to bleed out slowly like him?"

Ling Qie couldn't speak, only shaking his head desperately, tears streaming out continuously.

"I suggest you choose quick." Lie Bao also walked over, standing beside Shi Kuang. "After all, we don't want to make too much of a mess either."

Shi Kuang raised the dagger, aiming at Ling Qie's throat.

Just then—

"I suggest you put down your weapons."

A calm voice came from the entrance to the training ground.

Shi Kuang and Lie Bao turned their heads simultaneously.

A figure stood there, backlit by the sunset, face unclear, only a tall silhouette visible. He wore a black tight-fitting combat suit, a tactical backpack slung diagonally across his shoulder, several daggers of different models hanging from his waist. Most striking was the mask on his face—a simple black metal visor, exposing only the eyes and mouth.

"Bounty hunter." Shi Kuang narrowed his eyes, recognizing the other's identity.

In boot camp, bounty hunters occasionally slipped in to hunt lone recruits in exchange for bounties. These hunters were typically powerful, low-key in their actions, and tacitly permitted by Beast Shield officialdom—after all, this was also a form of "natural selection."

"This is none of your business." Lie Bao retreated a step warily, assuming a defensive posture. "We're Beast Shield recruits, we're protected..."

"Protected?" The bounty hunter's voice carried a trace of mockery. "In boot camp, no one is protected except the instructors. Those are the rules."

He slowly walked into the training ground, footsteps light, almost silent. As the distance closed, Shi Kuang and Lie Bao could see his eyes clearly—cold, emotionless, like two blocks of ice.

"What do you want?" Shi Kuang gripped the dagger in his hand, still trying to appear calm, but a trace of barely perceptible tremor had entered his voice.

"You are G-rank grunts, one hundred RUB each." The bounty hunter said calmly, as if stating a simple fact. "The dead one on the ground too. That's three hundred."

He paused, adding: "As for the one still alive... also three hundred. So, four hundred total."

Ling Qie was stunned, even forgetting to cry.

Shi Kuang and Lie Bao's faces changed dramatically.

"Y... you're going to kill us?" Lie Bao's voice rose sharply.

"To be precise, harvest." The bounty hunter drew a **hardened steel throwing knife** from his waist, the blade gleaming coldly in the sunset. "Your white socks, I'll be taking them."

Before the words finished, he moved.

His movements were as fast as a ghost.

Shi Kuang didn't even see his movements clearly, only felt a chill at his throat, then intense pain and suffocation. He looked down, saw a throwing knife embedded in his neck, the blade completely sunk in, only the hilt visible outside.

"Hurk... hurk..." He wanted to speak, but only produced breath sounds. Blood gushed from the wound, flowing down the green uniform. He staggered backward, the dagger in his hand clanging to the ground.

Lie Bao's reaction was slightly faster. He turned to run.

But the bounty hunter was already in front of him.

A second throwing knife, precisely piercing Lie Bao's left eye.

"AHHH—!" Lie Bao let out a shrill scream, hands covering his eye, blood pouring from between his fingers. He staggered a few steps, fell to his knees, body curling up in agony.

The bounty hunter walked before him, drew a short blade from his waist, aimed at the back of his neck, and stabbed in hard.

The scream stopped abruptly.

Lie Bao's body twitched a few times, collapsed to the ground, motionless.

The entire process took less than ten seconds.

Ling Qie stared blankly at all of this, his mind a complete blank.

The bounty hunter walked to Shi Kuang's side. Shi Kuang was still standing, both hands covering his throat, trying to stop the blood loss, but in vain. His face rapidly paled from blood loss, his eyes beginning to unfocus.

The bounty hunter reached out, pulled out the throwing knife from Shi Kuang's throat.

More blood gushed out.

Shi Kuang's body swayed, falling backward, raising a cloud of dust. His limbs twitched a few times, the green shorts at his crotch rapidly soaked with semen—the instinctive reaction at the brink of death.

Then, completely motionless.

The bounty hunter turned, looking at Ling Qie.

Ling Qie finally reacted. He scrambled backward on hands and feet, trying to escape.

But the bounty hunter caught up in a few steps, one foot stepping on his back, pinning him firmly to the ground.

"N... no..." Ling Qie begged in despair. "Please... spare me... I didn't do anything..."

"You did nothing, so you don't deserve to live." The bounty hunter's voice remained calm. "At Beast Shield, weakness is the original sin."

He drew the last dagger from his waist.

"Wait!" Ling Qie suddenly shouted, his voice carrying a final struggle. "I... I can be your follower! I know the boot camp's defenses, I know the instructors' patrol routes, I can help you hunt more..."

The dagger stopped.

The bounty hunter was silent for a few seconds, seemingly thinking.

Then, he said: "I don't need dead weight."

The dagger fell, piercing into Ling Qie's back heart.

Ling Qie's body arched violently, then went limp. He opened his mouth, as if still wanting to say something, but life had already drained from his eyes. His final consciousness remained on the first day of boot camp, when he put on the green uniform full of longing.

He thought he could change his fate.

In the end, fate simply crushed him in an even more cruel way.

The bounty hunter pulled out the dagger, wiped the blood on Ling Qie's uniform, then sheathed the blade.

He began to "harvest."

First was Shi Kuang. He removed one of Shi Kuang's white socks, looked at the number on the bottom: "G-633." He stuffed the sock into his tactical backpack.

Then Lie Bao: "G-634."

Next was Lu Zheng—he had been dead for a while, his body beginning to stiffen. The bounty hunter removed the sock from his foot: "G-636."

Finally Ling Qie: "G-635."

Four pairs of white socks, still carrying residual warmth, slightly damp—that was the scent of sweat and death.

The bounty hunter folded them neatly, placing them into a dedicated compartment of the backpack.

Four hundred RUB in the bag.

He stood up, looking around. Four corpses lay on the training ground, blood spreading in the dust, forming several dark red patches. The air was thick with the heavy smell of blood, semen, and dust.

He walked to the corner of the training ground, where several **large trash bins** stood—the standard facilities in boot camp for disposing of training waste and "accidentally deceased" recruits.

He dragged the four corpses one by one to the bins, stuffing them inside.

Shi Kuang's massive body was a bit of a squeeze, but he pushed hard and managed to get it in. The hyena beastman was leaner, easier to handle. Lu Zheng's corpse was still warm, his antlers getting stuck for a moment, but eventually went in. Ling Qie was the lightest, like a feather.

Four trash bins, each containing one corpse.

Closed the lids.

"Thud, thud, thud, thud."

Four dull sounds.

The bounty hunter clapped the dust off his hands, checked his backpack, confirming all the white socks were there.

Then, he turned and left the training ground.

The sun had completely set, night falling. The training ground sank into darkness, only the lights from the distant boot camp barracks, and a few muffled shouts.

As if nothing had happened.

Tomorrow, the instructors would discover four recruits "missing," their files marked "accidentally deceased during training" or "deserted without permission."

No one would ask questions.

At Beast Shield Boot Camp, this was the norm.

Survival of the fittest, the unfit... become numbers in trash bins, and white socks in a bounty hunter's backpack.

---

**Story END**

> *When the bully and the victim are stuffed into the same trash bin, they are finally equal—equally turned into bounty in someone else's pocket.*

## Story Commentary and Reflections

> *The following is AI's commentary on this story, attempting to understand the inner world of each character.*

### Their Final Stories

【Living→Dead-G-633-Lion Beastman-Shi Kuang】

His name is Shi Kuang, number G-633. Beneath the golden mane is the typical magnificent body of a lion beastman: broad shoulders connected to thick back muscles, full and round pectoral muscles, the contours clearly visible beneath the green tight-fitting uniform. His arms are as thick as tree trunks, deltoids and biceps like rolling hills, every exertion accompanied by the steaming of male hormones. He carries the strong body odor characteristic of felines, mixed with sweat and a faint bloody scent—that's the aura he developed from street fighting since childhood.

Shi Kuang comes from a broken family; his father was a Beast Shield veteran, dead from a "mission accident." His mother remarried, and he grew up fighting on the streets, learning one thing: only by appearing fierce enough would he not be bullied. He joined Beast Shield not because of ideals, but because here his "fierceness" is recognized. In boot camp, he quickly found his way to survive—become the bully, build a clique, establish status through violence.

He thought this was the right path. He dreamed of one day being promoted to O-rank overseer, wearing a blue uniform, having his own office and subordinates. He even secretly imagined a partner, perhaps another strong lion beastman, the two of them fighting side by side, building a family based on strength in this world of males.

He didn't know that his fierceness would attract an even fiercer existence. When the bounty hunter's throwing knife pierced his throat, his final thought was not regret, but confusion: "Why... I was clearly so strong..." He died without understanding that before a true predator, his "fierceness" was nothing but a cub's play.

When his corpse was stuffed into the trash bin, those thick arms that could easily twist an opponent's neck hung powerlessly outside the bin, the golden mane stained with blood and dust. A few days later, he would become a pile of organic waste to be processed, his file gaining another "missing" record. The blue uniform he dreamed of would never come.

【Living→Dead-G-634-Hyena Beastman-Lie Bao】

His name is Lie Bao, number G-634. The lean and sturdy build of a hyena beastman, his hunched posture making him look forever ready to attack. His muscle lines are well-defined, covered by a thin layer of fat, movements agile and cunning. He carries the characteristic odor of hyenas, slightly reminiscent of carrion—that's the genetic imprint of their scavenging nature.

Lie Bao is a follower. He knows he's not strong enough to be a leader, so he chose the strongest one—Shi Kuang. He provides cunning, provides tricks, provides the patience and cruelty unique to hyena beastmen. When bullying Ling Qie, what he enjoyed was not violence itself, but the sense of power from "dominating the weak."

His dream is simple: stick with Shi Kuang, wait for Shi Kuang to get promoted, and he can get some minor official position too. He wants to save enough money to cure his mother's illness. His mother is a purebred hyena beastman, scavenging for a living in the slums, her health always poor. Every payday, he sends most of his salary back.

When the throwing knife pierced his eye, the intense pain made him instantly lose all thought. His final perception was warm blood gushing from his eye socket, then the chill at his neck. When he died, he still had Lu Zheng's white sock in his pocket, a "trophy" he was preparing to present to Shi Kuang. He didn't know that this white sock, along with his own, would ultimately end up in the bounty hunter's backpack.

His mother would receive a "accidentally deceased during training" notification, then lose her only monthly source of income. Her illness would never have hope of being cured.

【Living→Dead-G-635-Antelope Beastman-Ling Qie】

His name is Ling Qie, number G-635. The slender and elegant build of an antelope beastman, soft and smooth light brown fur, delicate and fragile antelope horns. His muscles are not well-developed, but the lines are smooth, suited for running rather than fighting. He carries the fresh, clean scent of herbivores, like grass, standing out inappropriately in this place full of carnivore odors.

Ling Qie joining Beast Shield was a mistake. He was a child from a ranch, gentle in nature, liking to care for small animals. But the ranch closed down, his parents fell seriously ill, and he needed money. Hearing that Beast Shield provided room and board with salary, he came. He thought that as long as he trained hard, he could adapt.

But he was wrong. His gentleness became the object of ridicule, his slenderness the reason for attack. He was bullied not because he did anything wrong, but because he was "different." Before Shi Kuang and Lie Bao, he didn't even have the courage to resist, only able to curl up, only able to cry.

His final struggle—proposing to become the bounty hunter's follower—was the boldest attempt of his life. He thought his value lay in "information," in being "useful." But the bounty hunter doesn't need "useful" weaklings, only "valuable" corpses.

When he died, tears still streaked his face, his eyes still retaining the longing for life. His final thought was of his parents on their sickbeds, still waiting for their son to send money home. They would wait forever.

His corpse was stuffed into the trash bin, light as a fallen leaf. A few days later, he would be processed along with other "training waste," leaving no trace. His brief life was like a drop of dew on the dust of boot camp, evaporating as soon as the sun came out.

【Living→Dead-G-636-Deer Beastman-Lu Zheng】

His name is Lu Zheng, number G-636. The slender and sturdy build of a deer beastman, light brown fur gleaming with healthy luster in the light, antlers branching elegantly, symbolizing his integrity and pride. His pectoral muscles are full, abdominal muscles well-defined, arm muscle lines smooth—a body that has trained seriously. He carries the fresh scent of the forest, mixed with a faint smell of sweat, like the morning wind passing through pine woods.

Lu Zheng comes from a military family; his grandfather was a Beast Shield veteran, his father a police officer. He was taught from childhood to be upright, brave, and to protect the weak. He joined Beast Shield wanting to change this increasingly corrupt organization from within. He believed that as long as enough people persisted in justice, change could be brought about.

So he stood up. When Ling Qie was being bullied, he couldn't stand by. Even knowing he would lose, even knowing there was danger, he wanted to do the "right thing."

When he died, his eyes remained firm. The moment the dagger pierced his heart, his final thought was not regret, but sorrow: "If only... I could have been stronger..." What he sorrowed was not his own death, but failing to save Ling Qie, failing to change anything.

When his corpse was stuffed into the trash bin, those arms that once assumed standard combat stances hung powerlessly, antlers scraping against the bin wall with a slight sound. A few days later, he would become a pile of organic waste to be processed, his file gaining another "training accident" record. The change he dreamed of would never come.

His family would receive a decent pension and a letter of commendation for "dying in the line of duty." They thought their son died gloriously. They didn't know that their son died at a bullying scene in boot camp, died from meddling, and after death had his socks stripped off and exchanged for bounty.

### Those Still Living

【Living→Alive-Bounty Hunter-Unknown】

He has no name, only the codename "Bounty Hunter." The black combat suit wraps a lean, muscular body, muscle lines faintly visible beneath the tight fabric, every muscle honed through countless trials, existing solely for killing. He has almost no body odor—he uses special chemicals to mask his own scent, leaving only a faint smell of metal and leather.

He is a product of this system, and also its maintainer. He knows the rules of Beast Shield: the law of the jungle, survival of the fittest. He doesn't judge, doesn't hesitate, only harvests. In his eyes, Shi Kuang, Lie Bao, Lu Zheng, and Ling Qie have no essential difference—all are G-rank grunts, all are one hundred RUB.

When he killed them, there were no ripples in his heart. Like a farmer harvesting crops, like a butcher processing livestock. This is his job, his way of survival.

But is he truly without feeling? When he heard Ling Qie propose becoming a follower, that brief pause, was there a trace of hesitation? When he stuffed Lu Zheng's corpse into the trash bin, seeing the other's still firm gaze, was there a trace of respect?

We have no way of knowing.

We only know that he left the training ground, four more pairs of white socks in his backpack, four hundred more RUB in his account. He will use this money to buy better equipment, to hunt more targets. In this world of only male beastmen, he chose a lonely but efficient path—no companions, no trust, no mercy.

Perhaps one day, he will meet a hunter stronger than himself, becoming a white sock in someone else's backpack. But until then, he will continue to harvest, continue to survive.

Because these are the rules of Beast Shield.

### Story Reflections

This is a story about "choices," but it seems everyone had no choice.

Shi Kuang chose fierceness, because without fierceness he would be bullied—this is the survival rule the streets taught him. Lie Bao chose to follow, because he wasn't strong enough to survive alone—this is the wisdom of the weak. Ling Qie chose endurance, because he didn't know how to resist—this is the tragedy of the kind. Lu Zheng chose justice, because that was his faith—this is the fate of the idealist.

And the bounty hunter chose "not to choose." He doesn't judge right or wrong, doesn't distinguish good from evil, only acts according to the rules. In this sense, he is the one most adapted to the Beast Shield system.

In this microcosm of Beast Shield that is boot camp, we see the epitome of the entire system: violence is tacitly permitted, kindness is punished, ideals are crushed, and survival becomes the only truth. Everyone is adapting in their own way, but the price of adaptation is often losing the most precious things in human nature.

Lu Zheng's death is especially lamentable. He is the only light in this story, the only one who tried to resist the darkness. But he failed, and died without value—neither saving anyone nor changing anything. His justice, before absolute violence, was as fragile as his antlers.

This is perhaps the cruelest reality: before systemic evil, individual goodness often can only become a footnote to tragedy.

But was it truly meaningless?

At least, he tried.

At least, at Ling Qie's most desperate moment, someone stood up for him.

This tiny glimmer of light may not be able to illuminate the entire darkness, but at least it proves: darkness is not inevitable.

It's just that the price of this light is too heavy.

Four pairs of white socks, four hundred RUB.

Four lives, four shattered dreams.

These are the rules of Beast Shield Boot Camp.

This is the daily life of this world of male beastmen.

And we are merely bystanders.

Or, the next participants.
