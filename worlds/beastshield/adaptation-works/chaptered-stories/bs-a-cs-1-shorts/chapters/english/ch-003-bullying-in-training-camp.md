# Story The Price of Standing Up

> *In the Beast Shield boot camp, the first to stand up is often the first to fall.*

---

**Beast Shield Company Boot Camp**
Afternoon. Sunlight filtered through dirty glass windows, casting mottled shadows on the concrete floor. The air was thick with the mingled scents of sweat, dust, male hormones, and the faint metallic tang of blood—the inevitable atmosphere produced by hundreds of newly recruited male beastmen training, rubbing against one another, and competing.

Behind the barracks, in an abandoned equipment storage area, a small "ritual" was underway.

**G-589-Dog Beastman-Gou Zhong** was pressed to the ground, his face against the cold concrete. He wore the standard **olive-green tight-fitting uniform**, the Beast Shield LOGO on the left side of his chest already somewhat worn, his white long socks covered in dust. His build was average for a dog beastman—not particularly strong, but with well-defined muscles covered by a thin layer of fat. At this moment, he was struggling to suppress his body's trembling.

A foot wearing olive-green athletic shoes pressed against his back, grinding down with force.

"Hey, mongrel." A coarse voice rang out. "The cigarettes I told you to steal?"

The speaker was **G-501-Boar Beastman-Zhu Mang**. He stood half a head taller than Gou Zhong, with broad shoulders and a thick waist. The characteristic tusks of a boar beastman protruded slightly from the corners of his mouth, and his face wore a cruel grin. His uniform was stretched tight across his massive frame, nearly bursting at the seams, the contours of his pectoral and abdominal muscles clearly visible beneath the fabric.

"I... I couldn't steal them..." Gou Zhong's voice came muffled from the ground. "The tiger clerk at the convenience store was watching too closely..."

"Worthless!" Another foot kicked Gou Zhong in the side.

This was **G-523-Rhino Beastman-Xi Jian**. He was even taller and more massive than Zhu Mang, his skin rough and thick, the horn on his forehead gleaming dangerously in the sunlight. His uniform sleeves were rolled up to the elbows, revealing forearms as thick as tree trunks, muscles bulging, veins writhing like earthworms.

Gou Zhong grunted, his body curling up. He could feel the pain in his ribs, but more than that, he felt humiliation. He had joined **Beast Shield Company** three weeks ago, and from the very first day, he had become the target of these two "classmates." The reason? There was no reason. At Beast Shield, the weak existed to be bullied—that was the unwritten rule.

"Looks like you need a lesson." Zhu Mang squatted down, thick fingers grabbing Gou Zhong's hair, forcing his head up. "To help you remember what happens when you don't deliver on your promises."

Gou Zhong could see the cruel light in Zhu Mang's eyes, could smell the strong boar odor on him—a mixture of sour sweat, earth, and a violent, brutish scent. His heart pounded like a drum, his throat dry.

Xi Jian picked up a rusty iron pipe from nearby, weighing it in his hand.

"I heard dog beastmen have brittle leg bones." Xi Jian grinned, revealing uneven teeth. "Breaking one shouldn't affect your ability to move things around, right? After all, you grunts are only good for manual labor."

Gou Zhong's pupils contracted sharply. He wanted to struggle, but Zhu Mang's strength was overwhelming, thick arms clamping down on him like iron vises. He could feel cold sweat sliding down his forehead, dripping onto the concrete floor.

"Wait."

A voice came from the entrance to the storage area.

All three turned their heads.

A **wolf beastman** in the same olive-green uniform stood there, sunlight streaming from behind him, gilding his outline in gold. He had the typical lean, wiry build of a wolf beastman, his muscle lines smooth but not exaggerated. His eyes gleamed with calm resolve from within the shadows.

**G-477-Wolf Beastman-Lang Yi**.

"That's enough." Lang Yi's voice was calm but carried an unshakable firmness. "Training starts soon. The instructor is taking roll call."

Zhu Mang released Gou Zhong and slowly stood up, squinting at this unexpected visitor.

"Oh, if it isn't our 'justice warrior'?" Zhu Mang's tone was dripping with mockery. "What, sticking your nose in other people's business again?"

Xi Jian also turned, thick arm muscles tensing, the iron pipe tapping lightly against the ground.

Lang Yi did not back down. He walked forward a few steps, entering the shadows of the storage area. Now his face was visible—angular features, a serious expression, wolf ears alert and erect. He carried the characteristic cool, clean scent of a wolf, mixed with the faint fragrance of soap.

"This isn't 'other people's business." Lang Yi said. "Company regulations prohibit private fighting within the boot camp. Do you want to face a military tribunal?"

"Ha!" Zhu Mang burst into loud, grating laughter. "Regulations? Here, fists ARE the regulations!"

He pointed at Gou Zhong on the ground. "This mongrel didn't finish the task we gave him, so he deserves punishment. What, you want to take his punishment for him?"

Lang Yi's gaze swept over Gou Zhong, seeing the mixture of fear and a glimmer of hope in the other's eyes. Then he turned back to Zhu Mang and Xi Jian.

"I'm telling you one last time," Lang Yi's voice grew cold. "Let him go. Return to the training ground. Or I will report you to the instructor."

The air froze.

Zhu Mang and Xi Jian exchanged a look. There was no hesitation in that look—only deeper, darker malice.

"Report to the instructor?" Xi Jian stepped forward, his massive shadow engulfing Lang Yi. "You'll need to be alive to do that."

Before the words finished, the iron pipe was already swinging.

Lang Yi's reaction was extremely fast—the natural agility of a wolf beastman allowing him to dodge sideways. The pipe grazed his uniform, bringing a gust of wind.

But Zhu Mang had already lunged from the other side. A boar beastman's charge carried astonishing force and speed, like an out-of-control war chariot.

Lang Yi barely avoided the frontal impact, but his shoulder was still struck. Pain shot through him, and he grunted, staggering backward.

"Lang Yi! Run!" Gou Zhong scrambled up from the ground, shouting anxiously.

But it was too late.

Xi Jian's second blow followed immediately. This time, Lang Yi couldn't dodge completely. The iron pipe smashed heavily into his left arm.

"Crack!"

The clear sound of bone fracturing rang out in the silent storage area, exceptionally sharp.

Lang Yi's face instantly went pale, cold sweat beading on his forehead. His left arm hung at an unnatural angle, waves of pain flooding over him like a tide. But he didn't fall. Instead, he gritted his teeth, braced himself against the wall with his right hand, and steadied his body.

"Oh? Pretty tough." Zhu Mang tilted his head, revealing a cruel smile. "I appreciate tough guys, because..."

He slowly walked toward Lang Yi.

"...breaking a tough guy is especially satisfying."

Lang Yi's breathing grew heavy. He looked around—the storage area was remote, rarely visited. The training ground was on the other side; even if he shouted, the sound might not carry.

He looked at Gou Zhong, signaling with his eyes for the other to run.

But Gou Zhong didn't run. He stood frozen in place, legs like lead, unable to move. Fear and guilt intertwined in his heart, nearly suffocating him.

"You know?" Zhu Mang was now standing before Lang Yi, less than a meter apart. "In boot camp, there's an unwritten rule—don't stick your neck out."

He extended a thick finger and poked Lang Yi's chest.

"Because the one who sticks his neck out..."

Suddenly, Zhu Mang's right hand clenched into a fist and slammed into Lang Yi's abdomen.

"...dies the fastest!"

Lang Yi's body arched violently, the contents of his stomach nearly spilling out. The pain made his vision go dark, but he remained standing, did not fall.

Xi Jian grabbed him from behind by the shoulders, wrenching him around, then drove his knee into Lang Yi's lower back.

This time, Lang Yi finally couldn't support himself. He dropped to one knee.

"Enough!" Gou Zhong suddenly screamed. "Enough! Stop hitting him! I... I'll go steal the cigarettes! I'll go right now! Please, let him go!"

Zhu Mang turned to look at Gou Zhong, a flash of impatience in his eyes.

"Shut up, mongrel." he said coldly. "This isn't about cigarettes anymore."

He squatted down, looking at the kneeling Lang Yi. The corner of Lang Yi's mouth was already bleeding, his left arm hanging uselessly, but his eyes remained firm, neither begging nor yielding.

"That look of yours, it really pisses me off." Zhu Mang said. "As if you're saying you're nobler than us, more righteous."

He reached out, grabbed Lang Yi's hair, forcing his head up.

"Let me tell you what reality is." Zhu Mang's voice was low and dangerous. "Here, there's no right or wrong, only strong and weak. If you're strong, you make the rules. If you're weak, you follow the rules."

"And our rules are..." Xi Jian chimed in from the side, raising the iron pipe. "...meddlers die."

Lang Yi's pupils contracted sharply.

He wanted to resist, but his injured left arm and the pain in his abdomen left him almost powerless. He wanted to shout, but his throat was clamped by Zhu Mang's other hand, only able to produce a "gurgle" sound.

"Lang Yi!" Gou Zhong finally moved. He charged at Zhu Mang, trying to pull him away.

But Xi Jian kicked him in the chest, sending him flying. Gou Zhong's body crashed into a pile of old tires, then slid to the ground, curling up and coughing, tearing pain radiating from his chest.

Lang Yi saw this, a flash of despair in his eyes, then deeper anger.

He used his last strength, his right hand clenched into a fist, smashing toward Zhu Mang's face.

But the blow was weak, powerless.

Zhu Mang didn't even dodge. The fist struck his cheek, neither painful nor itchy.

"Admirable courage." Zhu Mang grinned. Then his expression suddenly turned ferocious. "But useless."

He released the hand gripping Lang Yi's throat, switching to grab the collar of his uniform, then slammed Lang Yi's head into the ground.

"Bang!"

Lang Yi's forehead struck the concrete heavily, producing a dull thud.

Once.

"Bang!"

Twice.

"Bang!"

Three times.

Each impact was accompanied by the dull sound of bone colliding with concrete. With each impact, Lang Yi's body convulsed violently. Blood gushed from his forehead, nose, and the corner of his mouth, spreading on the concrete floor into a glaring patch of red.

Gou Zhong watched all of this, tears streaming down uncontrollably. He wanted to shout, but no sound came out. He wanted to move, but his body was nailed to the ground.

After the fourth slam, Zhu Mang let go.

Lang Yi's body went limp, collapsing to the ground, his face buried in the pool of blood, motionless. His left arm still twisted at that unnatural angle, the fingers of his right hand twitching slightly, then going completely still.

Zhu Mang stood up, breathing heavily. His uniform was splattered with dots of blood, his face wearing a satisfied, cruel expression.

"Dead?" Xi Jian asked, prodding Lang Yi's corpse with the iron pipe.

Lang Yi did not respond.

"Should be." Zhu Mang wiped the blood from his face. "A wolf beastman's skull isn't as hard as a boar's."

He turned to Gou Zhong, walking toward him step by step.

"Now," Zhu Mang squatted before Gou Zhong, the back of the knife patting the other's trembling cheek, "it's your turn."

"W... why?" Gou Zhong's voice trembled. "I... I didn't do anything wrong..."

"You didn't do anything wrong?" Zhu Mang laughed. "Your existence is the mistake. The weak shouldn't live in this world, especially not at Beast Shield."

He reached out, gripping Gou Zhong's neck.

"And, you saw things you shouldn't have." Zhu Mang's hand slowly tightened. "So, you have to die."

Gou Zhong felt it becoming hard to breathe, his vision beginning to darken. He clawed futilely at Zhu Mang's arm, but the other's arm was like cast iron, unmoving.

He could see Lang Yi not far away, that corpse lying in a pool of blood. Lang Yi's eyes were still open, gazing vacantly at the sky, as if questioning this world.

Sorry, Lang Yi. Gou Zhong said in his heart. Sorry, I got you killed.

Then, he heard the sound of his own neck breaking.

"Crack."

Crisp. Clean.

Zhu Mang released his hand. Gou Zhong's body went limp, falling beside Lang Yi, the two corpses almost touching.

Xi Jian walked over, looking at the two bodies on the ground.

"How do we deal with them?"

Zhu Mang looked around, pointing at a **large trash bin** in the corner of the storage area—the kind used in boot camp for training waste and garbage, just the right size to stuff a beastman inside.

"Stuff them in." he said. "The cleaning machines will come collect them tonight."

The two began to work.

They lifted Lang Yi's corpse first. Lang Yi's body temperature had not completely faded; he was still warm. Blood dripped continuously from his forehead, forming an intermittent trail of red on the ground. His uniform was already in tatters, the fracture in his left arm visibly protruding.

They stuffed Lang Yi headfirst into the trash bin. Lang Yi's legs were rather long, requiring forceful pushing to get them completely inside. Finally, a pair of legs wearing white long socks stuck out of the bin, the number "G-477" clearly visible on the bottom of the socks.

Then came Gou Zhong.

Gou Zhong's corpse was lighter, but equally troublesome to handle. They stuffed him into the same trash bin, squeezed together with Lang Yi's body. The two corpses overlapped in the narrow trash bin, Lang Yi's face pressed against Gou Zhong's back, Gou Zhong's head buried between Lang Yi's legs.

The trash bin was nearly full.

Zhu Mang closed the lid, producing a dull "thud."

He clapped his hands, wiping away the blood.

"Let's go, we'll be late for training." His tone was light, as if he had just disposed of two bags of garbage.

Xi Jian nodded, tossing the bloody iron pipe aside.

The two left the storage area one after another, heading toward the training ground.

Sunlight still streamed through the dirty glass windows, casting mottled shadows on the floor. The smell of blood was gradually covered by the smell of dust, as if nothing had happened.

Only that trash bin standing quietly in the corner, and a trace of dark red liquid seeping from the gap in the lid, proved that a "lesson" had just ended here.

That night, the cleaning machines would arrive on schedule, empty the trash bin, and transport it to the processing plant.

Two corpses, two lives, two male beastmen who once had dreams, fears, and hopes, would be crushed, decomposed, and disappear along with other garbage.

In the boot camp records, Lang Yi and Gou Zhong would be marked as "missing during training (cause unknown)."

No one would ask questions.

At Beast Shield, this was normal.

---

**Story END**

> *Some justice ends before it begins; some lives wither before they bloom.*

## Story Commentary and Reflections

> *The following is DeepSeek's commentary on this story, attempting to understand the inner world of each character.*

### Their Final Stories

【Living→Dead-G-477-Wolf Beastman-Lang Yi】

His name is Lang Yi, number G-477. A typical lean wolf beastman build, muscle lines smooth, covered by a thin, even layer of fat, moving like a gray shadow. He carries the characteristic cool, clean scent of a wolf, mixed with the fragrance of soap—even in a place like Beast Shield, he strives to maintain his dignity.

Lang Yi comes from an ordinary wolf beastman family; both parents are honest laborers. His reason for joining **Beast Shield Company** is contradictory: on one hand, he detests the company's violence and corruption; on the other, he knows it's one of the few places where bottom-rung beastmen can obtain stable income and promotion opportunities. He tells himself that perhaps from within, he can change something, even if just a little.

In boot camp, he quickly gained a small reputation for his integrity and courage. He would help bullied recruits, remind teammates about safety during training, and privately criticize the company's unreasonable regulations. He wasn't unaware of the risks in doing so, but he believed that if everyone remained silent, evil would run unimpeded.

His name "Yi" (义, righteousness) was given by his father, hoping he would become a beastman with a sense of justice. He has always tried to live up to that name.

Today, when he heard the commotion in the equipment storage area, he immediately knew what was happening. He could have walked away, could have reported to the instructor, but he chose the most direct method—intervention. Because he had witnessed bullying too many times; he knew that every silence was an endorsement of violence.

When he walked in, there was no fear in his heart, only calm determination. He thought he could intimidate them with regulations, thought the threat of the instructor would make them back down. But he underestimated the depth of Beast Shield's culture—here, violence is not the last resort, but the first language.

When the iron pipe broke his arm, what he felt was not only pain, but deep powerlessness. When his head was slammed into the ground again and again, in his final consciousness, he had no regrets, only sorrow—sorrow that he wasn't strong enough, sorrow that he couldn't save that dog beastman, sorrow that he wouldn't see the day change came.

When he was stuffed into the trash bin, squeezed together with Gou Zhong's corpse. Perhaps this was his final comfort—at least the beastman he tried to protect wouldn't die alone.

In another world, perhaps he would meet a partner who understood him, in this world of male beastmen, find another "him" who also believed in justice, and build a warm home. But now, all of these can only be dreams that will never come true.

In his file, it will be written "missing during training." His parents will receive a meager pension and a cold notification. No one will tell them that their son was killed for trying to protect someone.

Lang Yi, number G-477, a wolf beastman who believed in justice, died in a place that didn't believe in justice.

【Living→Dead-G-589-Dog Beastman-Gou Zhong】

His name is Gou Zhong, number G-589. An average build for a dog beastman, not particularly strong, but with good endurance, muscles covered by a thin layer of fat, giving a gentle, reliable impression. He carries the characteristic slightly moist scent of a dog, mixed with the dusty smell of boot camp.

Gou Zhong's personality is just like his name—loyal, honest, obedient. He joined Beast Shield because work was hard to find back home, and he heard this place provided room and board with stable income. He wanted to save some money, open a small shop in the future, or go back home and do some small business.

In boot camp, from the very first day, he became a target for bullying. Not because he did anything wrong, but simply because he looked easy to bully. He tried to resist, but quickly discovered that resistance only invited more severe retaliation. So he learned to endure, learned to obey, learned to walk with his head down.

When Zhu Mang and Xi Jian told him to steal cigarettes, he knew it was wrong, but he agreed anyway. He thought obedience could buy peace, thought meeting their demands would make his life a little easier. But he was wrong.

Today, when Lang Yi appeared, a spark of hope ignited in Gou Zhong's heart. He thought someone had finally come to save him. But when Lang Yi was beaten down, that hope instantly extinguished, transforming into deeper despair and guilt. He felt that he had gotten Lang Yi killed—if only he had been stronger, if only he had resisted earlier, perhaps Lang Yi wouldn't have been dragged into this.

When his neck was being gripped, his final thought was not fear, but relief. Maybe this was for the best—no more enduring bullying, no more living in fear. He only regretted that he never got to say thank you to Lang Yi, never got to tell him what his courage meant.

He and Lang Yi were stuffed into the same trash bin, two corpses pressed close together. At the final moment of life, at least he wasn't alone.

Gou Zhong, number G-589, a dog beastman who only wanted a peaceful life, died in a place that didn't let the weak live in peace.

### Those Still Living

【Living→Alive-G-501-Boar Beastman-Zhu Mang】

His name is Zhu Mang, number G-501. A typical boar beastman build—broad-shouldered and thick-waisted, heavy muscles, astonishing power beneath the layer of fat. He carries a strong boar odor—a mixture of sour sweat, earth, and a primal, violent aura; standing beside him, you can feel the oppression.

Zhu Mang comes from a chaotic neighborhood, grew up in violence from childhood. To him, the law of the jungle is not philosophy, but daily life. He joined Beast Shield not for any ideal, but because here he can legally use violence, climb to higher positions, and have more power.

In boot camp, he quickly found his place—the bully. He enjoys the feeling of dominating others, enjoys seeing the fear in the eyes of the weak, enjoys the thrill of solving problems with violence. To him, weaklings like Gou Zhong exist to be stepped on, and "justice warriors" like Lang Yi exist to be broken—this is a necessary step in establishing his authority.

When he killed Lang Yi and Gou Zhong, he had no hesitation, no guilt. In his view, this was just removing obstacles, just maintaining his authority. He even derived a sense of satisfaction from it—see, those self-righteous guys, in the end they're just disposed of like garbage.

He will continue to throw his weight around in boot camp, continue to bully the weak, continue to climb upward by stepping on others. At Beast Shield, such behavior is not punished, but may even be appreciated—ruthlessness is precisely the quality the company needs.

Perhaps one day, he will be promoted to overseer, become an executive, have greater power. He will forget the two beastmen who died by his hands today, just as he would forget two specks of dust.

But can he really forget? In some deep night, when he is alone, will he occasionally recall Lang Yi's firm eyes, recall Gou Zhong's final gaze? Will there be a moment of doubt—if one day he meets a beastman stronger than himself, what will his fate be?

【Living→Alive-G-523-Rhino Beastman-Xi Jian】

His name is Xi Jian, number G-523. A rhino beastman's massive body, skin rough as armor, muscles hard as rock. He carries a heavy, earthy, metallic scent, moving like a mobile mountain.

Xi Jian is Zhu Mang's "partner." He is not the leader, but the enforcer. He enjoys the process of using violence, enjoys the feeling of power crushing others, but he is too lazy to think about why—Zhu Mang points the way, and he just follows.

Today, when he broke Lang Yi's arm with the iron pipe, what he felt was a pure, primal satisfaction. When he stuffed the two corpses into the trash bin, all he thought was "mission accomplished."

He will continue to follow Zhu Mang, continue to solve problems with violence, continue to build his "prestige" in boot camp. He doesn't think about right or wrong, doesn't think about consequences; he only believes in power.

But deep in his heart, perhaps there is a trace of unease that he himself is unwilling to admit—if rules truly don't exist, if violence is truly the only criterion, then one day, he too will become a victim of violence. But before that day comes, he will try to make himself stronger, strong enough that no one dares challenge him.

### Story Reflections

This is a story about "choices," but the irony is that in this story, the right to choose seems to lie only in the hands of the strong.

Lang Yi chose to intervene, chose justice, and paid with his life. Gou Zhong chose to obey, chose endurance, and also paid with his life. And Zhu Mang and Xi Jian chose violence, chose oppression, and they survived, possibly even thriving.

In the twisted ecosystem of **Beast Shield Company**, kindness and integrity become fatal weaknesses, while violence and cruelty become tools for survival. This is a world without right or wrong, only strong and weak—a world that worships "the law of the jungle" as truth.

Lang Yi's death is especially heartbreaking. He wasn't naive, wasn't stupid; he simply believed that even in the darkest places, someone should stand up and say "no." His tragedy lies in overestimating the power of rules and underestimating the darkness of human nature. In a place that doesn't respect rules, invoking rules itself is a gamble.

Gou Zhong's tragedy is more universal. He is the epitome of countless bottom-rung beastmen—no power, no background, only wanting a stable life, yet unable to realize even this humble wish. His death tells us: at Beast Shield, the weak have no room for survival, unless they are willing to completely abandon their dignity and become vassals of the strong.

And Zhu Mang and Xi Jian, they are not born demons, but products of their environment. In a system that rewards violence and punishes kindness, their behavior is a rational choice. If they didn't do this, they might be done unto by others. This is the horror of Beast Shield—it not only tolerates violence, but encourages violence, ultimately shaping every member into either an executor or a victim of violence.

When commenting on this story, I kept thinking: if one day, Zhu Mang meets a bully stronger than himself, what will he do? If one day, Xi Jian loses Zhu Mang as his "brain," can he still survive? If one day, Beast Shield Company needs to clean house, will those beastmen who once "eliminated the weak" for the company also be treated as garbage to be disposed of?

But these questions have no answers. Because in boot camp, in Beast Shield, today, violence remains the only language, and silence remains the majority's choice.

Two male beastmen died, died together, died in a trash bin. Their corpses will be processed, their names forgotten, their stories covered up.

But some things cannot be covered up.

Like the grunt Lang Yi made when his arm was broken.

Like the tear Gou Zhong shed when his neck was being gripped.

Like the dull thud when the trash bin lid closed.

These sounds, these images, will always linger in the air of boot camp, reminding every living beastman: here, kindness is a luxury, justice is contraband, and life... is just a consumable.
