## Story Reflections

As Chapter Two unfolds, the names on the list are erased one by one. The deaths of Hui Ren and Dun Shan, together with Lei Zhao, A Huang, and Hou Po from Chapter One, constitute the prelude to tonight's silent massacre. Each character is so different: Lei Zhao's懵懂 longing, A Huang's timid earnestness, Hou Po's mediocre stability, Hui Ren's proud diligence, Dun Shan's simple-minded simplicity... But they all ultimately walked toward the same ending — dying from essence exhaustion in the humiliation and pain of forcibly ignited desire, corpses casually stacked, designations coldly recorded.

Yan's actions are like a precise harvesting machine, efficient, cold, without any superfluous emotion. His "Scorching Flow Hidden Force" is the core of this death ritual; it not only takes life but plays with desire, destroys will, stretching the death process into a cruel performance. And each "second count" he reports is more like a ruthless scoring of these lives' final resistance, quantifying life's value into laughable data. Liang's pity and sighs are like small stones thrown into a deep pool, able to stir a trace of ripples but unable to change the pool water's coldness and depth. His cleanup work — recording designations, handling corpses — seems to be handling the final "procedures" for this death performance, making everything appear more "standardized" and "orderly." This standardization itself is a profound irony.

The slow-paced narrative, like a movie's slow-motion shot, magnifies every detail to a suffocating degree: muscle trembling, liquid spraying, breath changing, eyes unfocusing... Sensory descriptions are maximized, making readers feel as if personally on the scene, able to smell the mixed odors, feel the sticky touch, see how those strong bodies step by step lose their vitality. This writing style certainly enhances immersion and impact, but also makes the reading process full of a heavy sense of discomfort. It forces us to directly confront the details of violence and death, to think about the complex propositions about life, desire, power, and indifference contained behind these details.

In this world where all characters are hermaphroditic male beastkin, the robustness of the body (fat-wrapped-muscle), strength, desire (forcibly aroused), and ultimate death (dying from essence exhaustion) are tightly bound together. The body is both a tool for combat and survival, and a stage for desire and death. Uniforms (different colors and styles) identify rank and division of labor, but also become props that bind and highlight the body, ultimately stained by various bodily fluids, becoming part of death. This setting strengthens the direct connection between flesh and destiny, also giving the manner of death a unique imprint tightly integrated with the world view.

The list continues, footsteps outside the door may sound again. These new recruits, they joined Beastshield with their respective purposes, on this night drawn toward death's trap by a baffling "summons." They never understood why until death. And Yan, who executes the massacre, his motive seems purely for "bounty" and "practicing skill." Liang's participation is more out of practical considerations (lack of money) and some kind of compromise with his partner. There is no simple opposition of justice and evil, only cold calculations of survival and interest, and within this calculation, easily sacrificed, one by one vibrant lives with their own stories.

As bystanders (readers), we witness all this, feeling not only the impact of blood and eroticism, but even more a deep sense of powerlessness and sorrow. These characters, regardless of good or evil, their dreams, fears, love, and attachments are all brutally ended on this night. Their stories, just beginning, have already concluded. And we can only follow the author's pen, in this long and dark night, continue to watch, waiting for the next name to be called, the next body to fall, the next dream to shatter. Perhaps this is the cruelest law of this world: between the massive shadow and individual desire, ordinary lives are always the first to be crushed.
