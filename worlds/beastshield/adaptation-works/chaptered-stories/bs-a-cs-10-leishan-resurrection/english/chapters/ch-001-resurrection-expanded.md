**Scene 1: Waking in Darkness**

The first sensation to return was cold.

Not the kind of cold that can be resisted by shivering when winter wind brushes the skin's surface, but a bone-deep, inside-out cold, as if his entire body had been soaked in ice water. Blood flowed through his vessels at an agonizingly slow pace, like syrup about to congeal; each faint pulsation carried a sluggish resistance. Every limb had lost its warmth; fingertips and toes were the first to perceive that numb tingling — the needle-prick sensation of blood returning, like countless fine needles stabbing into the skin of his fingertips all at once.

Leizhao's consciousness rose like a drowning man being slowly pulled up from the bottom of the deep sea, penetrating the heavy, viscous darkness bit by bit, with agonizing difficulty.

He tried to move his fingers, but his limbs seemed not his own; the commands issued by his nervous system were cut off midway, offering no response. His body was like a heavy stone, abandoned in the darkness.

**Am I still alive?**

This question surfaced in his chaotic consciousness, carrying a sense of absurd unreality. If he was dead, why could he still feel? That cold, that numbness, that tingling from his limbs — weren't these proof of being alive? But if he was alive, why wouldn't his body obey? He was in that trash can right now.

**Scene 3: The Return of Heartbeat and Blood Flow**

Leizhao began trying to sense his own heartbeat.

At first, he felt nothing. His chest cavity seemed utterly silent, no pulsation, no sound of blood flow, only that dull pain from being compressed. This plunged him into deeper panic — if his heart wasn't beating, what was he? A zombie? A ghost? Or some monster caught between life and death?

But when he focused all his attention on his chest, he finally detected it.

Thud.

Faint, almost imperceptible, but it was indeed a heartbeat. Very slow, perhaps only twenty or thirty beats per minute, and very weak, like a drumbeat from far away, or like someone knocking on the other side of a thick wall.

After another long second, the second beat.

This discovery gave him a strange sort of comfort. He was still alive, in an extremely faint, extremely dangerous way. His body was maintaining life at the lowest possible metabolic rate, like a hibernating animal, like a frozen insect, waiting for spring.

But spring would not come on its own. He needed to actively accelerate this process.

That air, carrying the complex odors of the trash can, entered his lungs, stimulating his respiratory tract, bringing an urge to cough. But he held it back, continuing to breathe slowly and steadily.

As his breathing deepened, he felt his heartbeat accelerating. From twenty or thirty beats per minute, gradually rising to forty or fifty. Blood began to flow more effectively to his limbs, bringing waves of needle-prick tingling — the normal reaction after numbness faded, but also meaning his nervous system was coming back online.

His fingertips were the first to feel this change. First tingling, then warmth, then that familiar, blood-filled swelling sensation. He tried bending his fingers; this time, he felt the feedback of muscle contraction, very faint but undeniably real.

"Moved..."

He silently recited this in his mind, as if cheering himself on. His right index finger bent slightly, then straightened. A simple action, yet it exhausted a great deal of his strength. But he didn't stop, continuing to move his other fingers, then his wrist, then his elbow.

Each successful control brought more confidence, and more confidence accelerated his body's recovery. This was a positive feedback loop: nervous system activity promoted blood circulation, blood circulation brought more oxygen and nutrients, and these substances supported more neural activity.

But the process was excruciatingly slow, and accompanied by immense pain.

**Scene 5: The Return of Vision and Spatial Awareness**

While struggling to regain control of his body, Leizhao began trying to open his eyes.

At first, his vision was blurry. In the darkness, there were only faint shadows flickering, like looking at the world through thick frosted glass. He blinked, feeling the friction between his upper and lower eyelids — there was a viscous, semi-dried fluid there, possibly tears, possibly sweat, possibly some other bodily fluid.

As his eyes gradually adapted, he began to distinguish more details.

What he saw was the interior of the trash can — the green plastic walls presenting a dark color under faint starlight, stains hanging on the walls, traces left after long-term use. Above his head was the rim of the can, revealing an irregular patch of night sky. It was the real night sky; White Capital's light pollution wasn't that severe, and the stars were clearly visible, twinkling faintly in the night sky.

He looked at those stars and suddenly felt an uncontrollable sadness.

Just a few hours ago, he had been planning his future. Opening a martial arts hall, teaching kids to practice martial arts, saving up to buy a small house in the city, finding a partner worth protecting. Those dreams now seemed so distant, so unrealistic. He had nearly died, in this trash can, like a discarded piece of meat, like a bag of worthless garbage.

But the stars were still there. They didn't care about human life or death, about the honor or disgrace of beastmen; they were simply there, operating according to laws billions of years old. And he, Leizhao, Beastshield recruit number G-147, was also still here, though disheveled, though humiliated, but alive.

He began to assess his spatial position. The depth of the trash can reached approximately to his waist; his upper body was inside, his lower body outside. ### Story Reflections

After reading this chapter, the mood is complex. Night, trash can, the fishy musk of semen, the shadow of death — these elements weave together a cold and cruel tapestry. But within this cruelty, there is a strange warmth — that is the tenacity of life, the instinct to survive, the hope that refuses to give up even in the most humiliating circumstances.

Leizhao's experience is extreme, but his feelings are universal. That fear of crawling back from the edge of death, that shame of being forced to abandon dignity to survive, that confusion about an unknown future — these are all experiences shared by humans (or rather, beastmen).

In this huge machine called "Beastshield," individual life and dreams are so fragile. The appearance of a single bounty hunter can destroy an entire base of new recruits. And Leizhao's survival is both a miracle and an accident. His feigned-death ability allowed him to escape death, but also burdened him with a heavier fate.

As a reader, I can't help but think: if it were me, could I do it? In that situation, could I abandon dignity and lick my own bodily fluids to survive? Could I crawl out of the trash can and continue forward?

Leizhao did it. Not because he was particularly brave, but because he wanted to live. This most primitive, most instinctive desire is sometimes more powerful than any noble motive.

Chapter 1 has ended, but Leizhao's story has only just begun. How will he face Beastshield's questioning? How will he explain his survival? How will he find his place in this cruel world?

I close the "page," as if I can still smell that air mixed with semen, sweat, and the breath of death, so heavy it's hard to breathe. But within this heaviness, there is a glimmer of hope — because Leizhao is still alive, and being alive means everything is still possible.
