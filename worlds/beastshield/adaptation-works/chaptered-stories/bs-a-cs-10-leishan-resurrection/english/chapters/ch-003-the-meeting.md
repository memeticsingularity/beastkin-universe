*Leizhao thought the garbage truck would take him to the city, but instead he arrived at a place more terrifying than any nightmare — the Beastshield corpse dumping ground. Here, death is not the end, but another kind of beginning. When two "dead men" meet in a pile of corpses, how will they face each other?*

## Part One: Leizhao — Journey Under the Truck

**Scene 1: Finding a Way Out**

Leizhao moved along the shadow of the wall, like a ghost prowling through the night.

His footsteps were light, the rubber soles of his olive-green sneakers scraping against the cement ground with an almost imperceptible rustle. Each step was precisely calculated: center of gravity first on the ball of the foot, then the heel, body slightly leaning forward, ready to respond to sudden situations at any moment. This was the standard stealth posture learned during patrol, but now he wasn't executing a patrol mission — he was escaping.

The wall was on his right, a tall concrete wall presenting a cold, hard gray under the moonlight. There was barbed wire on top of the wall, swaying gently in the night wind, reflecting faint starlight. On the other side of the wall was freedom, the lights of White Capital, the city life he had once longed for. But this wall was too high for him, too smooth, with no climbing tools, no protrusions to leverage, only cold, merciless concrete.

He arrived at the back gate of the base, the small gate normally used for transporting garbage and supplies. The gate was closed, but not locked — or rather, the lock had been broken, the gate gap slightly ajar, revealing the pitch-black night outside.

He was about to push the gate open when he suddenly heard a sound.

Engine noise. Heavy, low diesel engine noise, coming from the distance, approaching.

Leizhao quickly hid in the shadow beside the gate, body pressed tightly against the wall, holding his breath. His heartbeat accelerated, blood surging through his vessels, ears capturing every subtle sound.

Headlights appeared. Two beams of dim yellow light moved in the darkness, illuminating the road ahead. It was a large garbage truck, the body olive-green, the cargo bed covered with black waterproof canvas, the wheels huge, bumping over the uneven ground.

Leizhao looked at that truck, and an idea formed in his mind.

**Scene 2: Hiding Under the Truck**

The garbage truck stopped at the back gate. The driver — a burly bear beastman in a blue uniform — jumped down and began checking the lock. That bear beastman had a thick build but somewhat lazy movements, obviously already accustomed to this late-night hauling work.

Leizhao didn't hesitate. He knew this was his chance, possibly his only chance.

When the bear beastman turned his back to him, focused on fiddling with that broken lock, Leizhao quickly and silently moved to the other side of the truck. The body was high, with enough space between the chassis and the ground — enough for a large beastman to squeeze in.

He lay on the ground, his olive-green uniform instantly covered with dust and oil stains. He smelled the odor under the truck — diesel, engine oil, rust, and some kind of rotten sweetness. That was liquid seeping from the cargo bed, dripping onto his face, carrying a warm touch.

He couldn't care about these. He twisted his body, crawling under the truck.

The space was very narrow. His back almost scraped against the metal plate of the chassis, his chest pressed against the ground, limbs extended, searching for protrusions to grip. He found a crossbeam, both hands gripping tightly, fingers hooking onto the edge of the metal. His thigh muscles tensed, toes pushing against the ground, preparing to support his body when the truck started.

The world under the truck was dark, noisy, and filled with strange odors. He could hear the vibration of the engine transmitted through the metal to his palms, could feel the heat from the exhaust pipe baking his legs. But here was safe — at least, safer than staying in that slaughtered base.

The sound of the door closing. The engine roar intensified. The truck began to move.

Leizhao clenched his jaw, fingers white from the force, nails digging into the rust on the metal. His entire body shook with the truck's bumps, each rise and fall making his abdomen and chest rub against the ground, bringing fiery pain.

But he didn't let go.

**Scene 3: The Bumpy Journey**

The garbage truck drove through the night, through the base's peripheral roads, onto the highway leading to the city.

For Leizhao, this journey was painful, long, and filled with unknown terror.

The wind under the truck was strong. Once the truck was moving, airflow passed through from under the chassis, carrying road dust and gravel, hitting his face and body. His eyes were blown to tears, forcing him to squint into slits. His fur — those yellow tiger hairs — was blown backward by the wind, exposing the skin beneath, bringing waves of coolness.

Worse was the smell.

The corpses in the cargo bed — he knew those were corpses, he could smell that special, sweet, rotten corruption — continuously seeped liquid during the bumps. Those liquids dripped from the gaps in the cargo bed, falling on him: face, chest, arms. Warm, sticky, carrying a strong fishy musk. That was a mixture of semen, sweat, blood, and other bodily fluids, the breath of death.

A drop of liquid fell on his lips. He subconsciously licked it — salty, fishy, carrying an iron-like metallic taste. That was the taste of blood, mixed with the sweetness of semen. His stomach convulsed violently, but he held back the urge to vomit.

The truck's vibrations continuously impacted his body. Each time it passed a pothole on the road, the truck would jolt violently, making his head hit the metal plate of the chassis, emitting a dull "thud." His ears were ringing, teeth chattering against each other, making "clicking" sounds.

But he persisted. Fingers gripping the crossbeam desperately, knuckles aching from overexertion, muscles spasming from maintaining the same posture for so long. His biceps bulged under his tight clothing, triceps tensed, deltoids bearing his body weight.

He didn't know where the truck was going. He didn't know what awaited him ahead. He only knew that he was moving, leaving that place of death, — surviving.

**Scene 4: Arrival**

After who knows how long — perhaps half an hour, perhaps an hour — the truck began to slow down.

Leizhao saw changes through the gaps under the truck. The road changed from a smooth highway to an uneven dirt road; dust kicked up by the wheels drifted past from under the truck, carrying a dry, earthy smell. The surrounding lights became sparse, with only a few dim light spots in the distance, like lighting from some industrial area.

The truck stopped. The engine cut off, but the diesel engine was still making "clicking" cooling sounds.

Leizhao heard voices.

"Another truck? What batch is this tonight?" a rough voice asked, carrying exhaustion and weariness.

"Third batch. Something big happened at the base, apparently got wiped out clean." Another voice answered, younger, but equally exhausted.

"Tsk, these soldiers, all high and mighty when alive, dead they're just like garbage."

"Say less, hurry up and unload, finish early and call it a day."

Leizhao heard the sound of the door opening, heard footsteps walking around the truck, heard the "click" of the cargo bed lock being opened.

He realized he had to act. If he waited for them to start unloading, if he was discovered hiding under the truck — the consequences would be unimaginable.

He released his already numb fingers, body sliding out from under the truck, silently landing on the ground like a snake. His movements were quick but cautious, using the truck body as cover, rolling into the roadside shadow.

He lay there, panting heavily, observing his surroundings.

**Scene 5: The Corpse Dumping Ground**

Leizhao saw the most terrifying sight of his life.

This was a huge, open-air site, possibly some abandoned factory or mining area. The ground was exposed rock and dirt, crushed uneven by heavy vehicles. In the center of the site was a huge pit — or rather, a natural low-lying area — now already filled with corpses.

Mountains of corpses.

Not hyperbole, but literally mountains. Hundreds, possibly thousands of beastman corpses, casually piled together, forming small hills. Under the dim moonlight, those corpses presented a pale, unnatural color, like a pile of discarded wax figures.

He could smell that odor. That strong, suffocating rotten smell, mixed with the fishy musk of semen, the sour stench of sweat, the sweetness of blood, forming a unique, death-exclusive breath. That odor had a substantial weight, pressing on his nasal cavity and lungs, making every breath difficult.

He saw the postures of those corpses. Some lying on their backs, eyes half-open, mouths open, as if silently shouting. Some curled on their sides, limbs twisted, as if having experienced violent convulsions before death. Some pressed under other corpses, only an arm or a leg exposed, like a distress signal reaching out from the corpse mountain.

They were all wearing uniforms. Green, blue, white, black — various Beastshield uniforms. They had been soldiers, officers, guards; now they were just — garbage.

Leizhao's stomach finally couldn't take it. He bent over, vomiting violently.

**Scene 6: Vomiting and Emptiness**

Vomiting was painful, thorough, body-emptying.

Leizhao knelt on the ground, both hands supporting the ground, head lowered, throat contracting violently. What surged from his stomach wasn't food — he hadn't eaten in a long time — but gastric fluid, bile, and those liquids from the corpses he had accidentally swallowed under the truck.

The vomit was yellow-green, carrying a strong sourness, mixed with blood threads. It sprayed from his mouth and nose, splashing onto the dry ground, making "plopping" sounds. His abdominal muscles contracted violently, rectus abdominis spasming under his tight clothing, bringing knife-twisting pain.

Each vomiting episode was accompanied by tears. Not tears of sadness, but physiological tears flowing because of stomach spasms. They flowed down his cheeks, dripping onto the ground, mixing with his vomit.

He vomited for a long time, until there was nothing left in his stomach to vomit, until he was left with only dry heaving motions, his throat making "uh-uh" sounds, as if trying to vomit out his internal organs.

Then, emptiness.

Extreme, all-consuming emptiness. His body was emptied; strength left his body along with the vomit. He knelt there, hands trembling, forehead pressed against the ground, gasping for breath.

The ground was cold, rough, carrying the texture of dust and gravel. That texture was in some ways comforting, real, proof of being alive. He felt that coldness, that roughness, that chill coming from deep within the earth.

He slowly raised his head, using the back of his hand to wipe the filth from the corner of his mouth.

He was still here. He was still alive. Although in a way he had never imagined, although in such a hellish place, he was still alive.

**Scene 7: Observation and Hiding**

Leizhao forced himself to calm down, beginning to observe his surroundings.

The corpse dumping ground was larger than he initially estimated. It was located in a natural basin, surrounded by towering rock walls forming a natural barrier. Only one dirt road led out — the one he had just come from. Under moonlight, the entire site presented a pale, ghostly color.

He saw two movers — both bear beastmen, wearing blue work clothes, operating a forklift. The forklift's bucket was full of corpses, being dumped onto the top of the corpse mountain. That mechanical movement, that cold efficiency, made the entire scene appear even more absurd and terrifying.

He counted; besides those two bear beastmen, there were three or four other figures in the site, all busy moving, organizing, or rather — processing these corpses. Their movements were all mechanical, faces expressionless, obviously already numb to this work.

Leizhao realized he had to find a hiding place. The movers were still working now; if he was discovered — a living, moving, Beastshield-uniform-wearing person — what would they do? Treat him as a survivor and rescue him? Or treat him as trouble and dispose of him?

He didn't dare to gamble.

He looked around, searching for places to hide. The corpse mountain was too high, too dangerous, and that odor was truly unbearable. The rock walls were too steep; his current strength couldn't climb them. The only possibility was those abandoned equipment and containers scattered at the edge of the site.

He spotted a target — on the east side of the site, there was an abandoned container, door half-open, dark inside. About fifty meters from him, with some rocks and low shrubs in between that could provide cover.

He took a deep breath and began to move.

**Scene 8: Crossing the Corpse Field**

Leizhao's movement was slow and cautious. He used every object that could provide cover: rocks, dirt mounds, abandoned tires. His body pressed close to the ground, advancing in a crawling or low-posture running posture, minimizing the risk of discovery as much as possible.

But he had to pass by the corpse mountain.

Not climbing over it, but skirting around the edge. That meant he had to pass closely, almost intimately, by those corpses.

He chose a path that looked relatively "clean" — a dirt road crushed by vehicles, passing through the edge of the corpse mountain. But even here, some corpses were scattered. Some might have fallen from the forklift, some might not have been moved up the mountain yet.

He stopped when passing the first corpse.

That was a tiger beastman, the same race as him. Wearing a green uniform, lying on his back on the ground, eyes wide open looking at the night sky. His chest was still — wait, what was that?

Leizhao's pupils contracted.

That wasn't a corpse. Or rather, it had been a corpse, but now — that "corpse" was breathing.

Extremely faint, almost imperceptible breathing. The rise and fall of the chest was very small, very slow, but it did exist.

Feigned death.

This word exploded in Leizhao's mind. It wasn't just him; there were others who also possessed this ability. Or rather, this ability was more common than he imagined.

He hesitated for a moment, then continued forward. He had no ability to save anyone now; he couldn't even protect himself. But that image — that tiger beastman lying on the ground, breathing faintly — was deeply imprinted in his mind.

He continued forward, passing more corpses. Some were already completely stiff, some still carried residual warmth, some — he couldn't confirm whether they were really dead or not. He stopped checking, stopped looking at those faces, just kept his head down, focused on the road beneath his feet, focused on reaching that container.

Finally, he reached his destination.

The container was rusted, its exterior painted with already-peeling blue paint. The door was half-open, dark inside, emitting a smell of metal and engine oil. Compared to the corpse stench outside, this smell was practically fresh.

He slipped inside, back against the cold metal wall, sliding to sit on the ground.

He was safe — temporarily.

**Scene 9: Waiting and Observing**

In the darkness of the container, Leizhao began to recover his strength.

His body leaned against the metal wall; that coldness transmitted through the thin uniform to his skin, keeping him awake. His breathing gradually steadied, his heartbeat slowly recovering from the violent running just now.

He observed the situation outside through the door gap.

The movers were still working, but now it was late at night — or rather, early morning — their movements becoming more sluggish, more mechanical. The forklift went back and forth, dumping cart after cart of corpses onto the mountain. That sound was dull, hollow, like something heavy falling into water, but it was the sound of corpses falling on corpses.

Leizhao noticed that new corpses were being brought in. That garbage truck he had hidden under had already finished unloading, and now new trucks were driving in. One, two, three — all from different directions, different bases.

How many bases had Beastshield been attacked? How many people had been dragged into this disaster like him?

These questions had no answers. He only knew that he had to wait here, wait until the movers left, wait until the darkest moment before dawn, and then — find a way to leave this place.

He closed his eyes, trying to rest. But his body was too tense, his nerves too taut; he simply couldn't fall asleep. He could only maintain a semi-awake state, senses remaining highly alert, capturing every sound from outside.

Time passed second by second.

**Scene 10: The Final Dumping**

After who knows how long — perhaps an hour, perhaps two hours — Leizhao heard a new sound.

A particularly large garbage truck drove into the site. Unlike the other trucks, this one was black, with no markings on the body, only the Beastshield logo — a beast claw imprint surrounded by a shield.

This truck stopped directly in front of the corpse mountain. The door opened, and a person in a dark blue uniform got out — unlike the other movers, his uniform was neater, more formal, carrying a managerial air.

"This is the last batch," that person said to the forklift driver, voice not loud, but especially clear in the silent night.
"Headquarters is done processing over there; this is the last one for tonight."

"Anything special inside?" the forklift driver asked, tone carrying a hint of curiosity.

"A senior officer. E-class. Apparently committed suicide in the chairman's office." That person's voice carried a strange, almost respectful tone.
"Because of negligence, was ordered to die. Before dying, he was still pinching his own nipples and spraying milk, apparently quite a spectacular scene."

Leizhao's ears perked up.

E-class. Senior officer. Committed suicide in the chairman's office.

This information pieced together in his mind, forming a vague picture. A Beastshield senior officer, for some reason, was driven to suicide. And, like him, it was also through ejaculation — that controlled, humiliated manner of death.

The forklift began working, dumping the corpses from that black garbage truck. Leizhao watched through the door gap, watching those corpses being poured onto the ground, mixing with other corpses, losing all identity and dignity.

Among them was a particularly large corpse. That was a bull beastman, with a massive body that stood out even among the other corpses. The black uniform was already soaked with bodily fluids, clinging tightly to the body. His posture was twisted, as if having experienced violent convulsions before death.

That corpse was dumped at the bottom of the corpse mountain, close to the container where Leizhao was hiding.

Leizhao looked at that corpse, a strange, complex emotion welling up in his heart. Was that shared suffering? Or pity for one's own kind? He didn't know. He only knew that that person — that senior officer — like him, was a sacrifice swallowed by this system.

The movers began packing up their tools, preparing to leave.

"Let's go, we'll process again tomorrow. These corpses still need to pile here for a few more days, wait until we have enough for a batch before incinerating them uniformly." the person in the dark blue uniform said.

"What about these... feigned-death ones?" the forklift driver suddenly asked, tone carrying a hint of unease. "I heard, some corpses actually aren't completely dead..."

"Don't talk nonsense." that person's voice suddenly became stern.
"Even if there are, that's a matter for tomorrow. Wait until the sun comes out, wait until they really die, or — get crushed to death by other corpses."

Their conversation echoed in Leizhao's mind.

Crushed to death by other corpses.

He looked at that bull beastman's corpse, dumped at the bottom of the corpse mountain, possibly to be covered by later dumped corpses.

If that person was also feigning death... If he too, like Leizhao, was just waiting for the chance to recover...

Leizhao made a decision.

**Scene 11: Crawling Out of the Container**

When all the movers' vehicles had left, when the site fell back into silence, Leizhao crawled out from the container.

The night wind was even colder now, carrying the moisture characteristic of early morning. The moonlight became paler, like some ghostly light, illuminating this place of death.

Leizhao didn't walk toward the exit. Instead, he walked toward that corpse mountain, toward that bull beastman's corpse.

His footsteps were light, but in this silence, each step was like a heavy strike on a drumhead. His heart was pounding wildly, not from fear — or rather, not just from fear — but because of a strange, uncontrollable impulse.

He arrived beside that bull beastman's corpse, crouching down.

Up close, this corpse was even more massive. That was a black bull, with thick fur, a fat but powerful body — a typical fat-covered muscle structure. The black uniform was already soaked with semen, milk, and sweat, presenting a dark, almost shiny color. His posture was curled on his side, legs bent, arms crossed over his chest, as if protecting something, or as if having experienced violent spasms before death.

Leizhao reached out, touching the bull's neck.

The skin was cold, but not that stiff coldness of the dead — rather, it was warm. Faintly, almost imperceptibly, warm.

He was still alive.

Like Leizhao, this bull was also feigning death.

**Scene 12: Four Eyes Meeting**

Just as Leizhao's fingers touched the bull's neck, those eyes opened.

Those were a pair of crimson bull eyes, pupils contracting to pinprick size under the moonlight. There was no deathly dullness in those eyes, no emptiness of a corpse, but — clarity. Painful, exhausted, but still clear.

That bull and Leizhao looked at each other, four eyes meeting.

Time seemed to freeze.

Two "dead men," in this mountain of corpses, under moonlight, surrounded by death, saw each other for the first time.

Leizhao opened his mouth, wanting to say something, but his throat seemed blocked by something, unable to make a sound.

That bull — that senior officer — spoke first. His voice was hoarse, low, carrying an exhaustion after extreme suffering, and also... a cautious probing.

"You... also feigned death?"

Leizhao nodded. His fingers still remained on the bull's neck, able to feel that faint, but indeed existing heartbeat. Thud. Thud. Thud. Slow and firm, like a drumbeat from the depths of hell.

That bull stared at Leizhao for a long time, gaze wandering over his face, as if judging whether he was trustworthy. The crimson bull eyes were full of vigilance, like a startled animal evaluating a potential threat.

"You... which section are you from?" Niu Pan finally asked, voice so light it was almost a whisper. His gaze swept over Leizhao's uniform, seeing that green fabric and simple epaulettes, a flash of understanding in his eyes, but quickly returning to that cautious guard.

"G-147, training base's... perimeter guard." Leizhao answered, voice equally hoarse. "The base... was slaughtered. I... was by a bounty hunter..."

He couldn't continue.

Niu Pan listened, a flash of complex emotion in his crimson bull eyes — like fear, and also like... resonance. He didn't interrupt Leizhao, just listened quietly, body trembling slightly, not knowing whether from cold or from memory.

After a long time, he spoke in a low voice, hoarse and exhausted, carrying a suppressed pain: "You... how did you escape?"

His gaze wandered, not daring to look directly into Leizhao's eyes. "That... that person... what did he do to you?"

Leizhao was stunned for a moment, then understood — Niu Pan was probing him. This senior officer wouldn't easily trust anyone, even in this desperate situation.

"He... he caught me." Leizhao said in a low voice, recalling that terrifying experience.
"Strangled my neck, gripped my... my genitals. Then... there was a rush of hot air, I... then I knew nothing."

Niu Pan listened, his gaze gradually shifting from vigilance to a complex understanding. But that understanding still carried guard, as if confirming whether Leizhao was telling the truth.

"Scorching Dark Energy..." he murmured, voice almost only audible to himself.
"That person's skill... specifically attacks there. Makes people... die in that humiliation."

He was silent for a long time, as if hesitating whether he should continue. Moonlight shone on his round face, illuminating those sweat and stains, and also the wrinkles at the corners of his eyes — traces left by time and exhaustion.

"You..."
Niu Pan finally spoke, voice even lower than before, like talking to himself, or like telling Leizhao. His gaze wandered, not daring to look directly into Leizhao's eyes.
"What's your name?"

"Leizhao." the young tiger beastman answered.

"Leizhao..." Niu Pan repeated this name, as if savoring it, or as if confirming something. His fingers unconsciously gripped the dirt beneath him, a manifestation of tension and unease.

"I..." He opened his mouth, then stopped. A flash of fear in his crimson bull eyes, that deep-seated fear, not of external threat, but of... something deeper.

"I'm E-011," he finally said, voice so light it was almost inaudible. "Niu Pan. Headquarters... logistics department."

He didn't say more. His body trembled slightly, not from cold, but from some... memory. That memory of being humiliated, being stripped of dignity, being forced to submit in fear.

They looked at each other, in the corpse pile, under moonlight, two beastmen wearing different uniforms, from different worlds, but having experienced the same fate.

Two "dead men."

Two ghosts.

At this moment, they were no longer superior and subordinate, no longer recruit and officer, no longer people from two worlds. They were just — two people who had crawled back from the edge of death, two souls who needed each other to survive.

**Scene 13: Conversation on the First Night**

They didn't dare move too much, afraid of attracting the movers' attention (if anyone came back), and also afraid of consuming too much strength. They found a relatively hidden corner — the gap between two huge rocks, able to accommodate their two massive bodies, and able to block the line of sight.

Leizhao leaned against the rock, feeling that cold touch transmitting through the uniform to his skin. Niu Pan sat beside him, both their bodies trembling slightly from weakness, but each other's body temperature was in some way a comfort.

Silence lasted for a long time. In the distance came the cry of a night owl, and the sound of wind passing through the corpse pile, like a sigh, or like weeping.

"You..." Leizhao finally spoke, voice very light, as if afraid of breaking this deathly stillness. "How did you... I mean, how did you end up... here?"

Niu Pan's body obviously stiffened. His gaze wandered, looking elsewhere, not daring to look into Leizhao's eyes.

"I..." his voice became very low, carrying a... shame. "I was processed at headquarters."

Leizhao's heartbeat skipped a beat. "Processed? By... our own people?"

Niu Pan didn't answer directly. His fingers unconsciously gripped the dirt beneath him, knuckles white from the force. After a long time, he said in a light voice, carrying a suppressed fear:
"Something happened at the base... someone needed to take responsibility."

Leizhao's heart sank heavily. "The base... really... completely gone?"

Niu Pan turned his head, looking at this young tiger beastman. Under moonlight, there was still that unwilling-to-believe, almost naive expectation in Leizhao's eyes — expecting someone to tell him that everything wasn't that bad, that there were still people alive, that there was still hope.

But Niu Pan couldn't give him this hope. He slowly nodded, heavily, as if confirming an unchangeable tragedy.

"I saw... the report in the office." Niu Pan's voice became low, carrying a trance of memory, and also deep fear.
"The casualty list from the training base. Every name, every number. Your batch... I... I saw them all."

"My team leader..." Leizhao interrupted him, voice trembling. "White Bear team leader. Houpo. He..."

"Dead." Niu Pan said in a light voice, voice carrying suppressed fear and guilt.
"His name was on the list. And... and many others. I... I could only watch the list... watch those names being crossed off one by one..." His voice grew smaller and smaller, finally becoming a trembling whisper.

Leizhao closed his eyes. He remembered that silly-smiling wolf guard — the one who lost an arm-wrestling match to him and bought him a drink. Remembered that honest bear guard — always the last to finish training but never giving up. Remembered that somewhat proud blue tiger — the one who lent him spare socks. Remembered that bull guard who said his nipples were sensitive.

They were all dead. Just like him, thrown into the corpse pile.

"Ahuang..." Leizhao said in a low voice, like talking to himself. "I put him... into the trash can. In... the same way as me."

"You... you did the right thing." Niu Pan said in a light voice, voice carrying a... resonance.
"At least... at least you let him... not be so alone. In that dark place... having someone with him..." His voice trembled, as if talking about Leizhao, and also as if talking to himself.

Leizhao opened his eyes, looking at Niu Pan. "What about you? You... what did you see?"

Niu Pan was silent for a very long time. His body trembled slightly, as if struggling with some... memory.

"I..." his voice was very light, carrying a deep fear.
"I saw... the list. Every name called in, I knew. Then... then came the death reports. One by one... posted up."

He paused for a long time, taking several deep breaths, as if mustering courage.

"I... I didn't see the scene." Niu Pan's voice trembled, carrying a deep sense of powerlessness.
"I only saw... the reports after processing. Corpses everywhere. That one called Yan... one person, slaughtered the entire base. Those corpses... were carried out one by one, piled together... I didn't know exactly what happened. I only saw... the result."

His voice choked, unable to continue. That fear and shame made his body curl up even tighter.

"I... I couldn't do anything." Niu Pan said in a low voice, voice full of self-blame and powerlessness.
"I could only sit in the office... watching those lists... one after another... crossed off..."

Niu Pan lowered his head, shoulders trembling. "I... I was at headquarters at that time. Not at the base. If I had been there... maybe..."
His voice grew smaller and smaller, finally becoming an almost inaudible whisper. "No... even if I had been there, it wouldn't have helped. That power... that irresistible power..."

His fingers unconsciously gripped his own uniform, still stained with dirt and dried bodily fluids. His body curled up, as if protecting himself, or as if... hiding something.

Leizhao looked at Niu Pan, looking at this bull beastman who was older than him, stronger than him, who had once been a senior officer at headquarters. At this moment, he looked so... fragile. So... scared.

"You too..." Leizhao said in a light voice, "You also felt it? That... irresistible feeling?"

Niu Pan suddenly raised his head, his crimson bull eyes full of terror, like a child whose secret had been discovered. He opened his mouth, wanting to say something, but his throat seemed blocked by something.

After a long time, he gently nodded, voice trembling: "They... they made me... made me in the office..." He couldn't continue; that shame and fear made his voice choke in his throat.

Leizhao understood. He reached out, gently placing his hand on Niu Pan's shoulder. That touch was rough, warm, carrying the understanding of one of his own kind.

"We..." Niu Pan finally found his own voice, though still trembling.
"We will survive. Not for revenge. Not for justice. Just... just to prove we're still alive. Prove they didn't... didn't completely destroy us."

Leizhao nodded. He looked at the distant corpse mountain, at those lives like his own, abandoned by this world.

"We will survive." he repeated, as if saying to himself, and also as if saying to all the dead.

At this moment, two "dead men" reached a consensus. They would escape this hell together, together find a path of survival in this cruel world.

Not for the past.

Just for the future.

**Chapter 3 END**

> *They met in the corpse pile, two beastmen marked as "dead" by the system. One is a recruit who knows nothing, the other is an officer who knows the inside story. Their meeting is not coincidence, but the arrangement of fate. In this place shrouded by death, they will become each other's only support. And their story has only just begun.*

### Their Final Stories

【Living → Dead-E-011-Bull Beastman-Niu Pan (Feigned-death recovery)】

Deputy Director of the Third Field Operations Department of Beastshield Company, a middle-aged black bull who had climbed to senior officer rank through seniority and a steady personality. ### Story Reflections

After reading this chapter, the mood is complex. Terror, oppression, but also a hint of warmth and hope.

The description of the corpse dumping ground is cruel, direct, and uncomfortable. Those mountains of corpses, those movers mechanically processing death, that sweet rotten smell — all of these remind us that in this world called "Beastshield," life is so cheap, death is so common.

But in this hellish place, two "dead men" met. Their meeting is not romantic, not dramatic, but ugly, embarrassed, four eyes meeting in a pile of corpses. But it is precisely this ugliness and embarrassment that makes their meeting appear more real, more powerful.

Leizhao and Niu Pan, one is an ignorant recruit, the other is an officer who knows the inside story; one survived by accidental talent, the other feigned death through years of cultivation; one was hunted by an outsider, the other was purged by his own organization. They come from different worlds, experienced different deaths, but they both chose — to survive.

This instinct to survive, this power to find hope in despair, is what moved me most in this chapter. No matter how cruel the world, no matter how unfair fate, as long as there is still breath, there is hope. As long as there are still companions, there is warmth.

As a reader, I can't help but think: if it were me, could I do it? Could I crawl out of the corpse pile? Could I find companions in that despair? Could I let go of dignity, let go of identity, let go of everything for survival?

Leizhao and Niu Pan are trying to do these things. Their escape has only just begun, their dependence on each other has only just begun. In this world shrouded by Beastshield's shadow, where will the two ghosts numbered G-147 and E-011 go?

I close the "page," as if I can still smell that air mixed with corpse stench, semen, and engine oil, so heavy it's hard to breathe. But within this heaviness, there is a hint of warmth — because in this darkest place, two lonely souls found each other.
