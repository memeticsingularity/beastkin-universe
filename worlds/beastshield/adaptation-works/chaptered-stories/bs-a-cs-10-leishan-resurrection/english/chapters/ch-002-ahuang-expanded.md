**Scene 1: The Return Journey — Crossing the Periphery of Death**

Leizhao stood at the corner of the wall, leaning against the cold, rough concrete surface, gasping for breath.

The stars in the night sky were still there, as bright as they had been minutes ago when he looked up at them. The Milky Way slowly flowed overhead; starlight traveled across billions of years of time and space, sprinkling down upon this death-shrouded base periphery. But for him, the world was completely different now. He had crawled out of the trash can, transformed from a discarded "corpse" back into a person with mobility — though this mobility was still extremely fragile, though his body was still trembling.

But he couldn't stay here.

The outer perimeter was too dangerous. That attacker in black — that bounty hunter called "Yan" — might still be wandering nearby. Or there might be other hunters, other threats. More importantly, he needed water, food, and to return to a place with lights and people. He needed to confirm whether there were other survivors in the base, needed to report what had happened tonight, needed — to find a safe place.

He had to get back inside the base.

This decision filled him with a sense of absurdity. A few hours ago, he had still felt safe and bored within the base walls, complaining about the tedium of the night patrol, thinking about being able to drink a cup of hot tea after getting off duty. Now, that place he had once wanted to escape from, that training base full of rules and regulations, that oppressive place, had become the only refuge he could think of.

He straightened his uniform. The olive-green tight suit was slowly drying in the night wind, but the crotch was still stiff, like a piece of fabric that had been starched and dried, emitting an impossible-to-ignore fishy musk. It didn't matter. Being alive was more important than anything.

He stuck close to the base of the wall, beginning to move in the direction of the base gate. His footsteps were light, almost sliding along the ground — this was the standard stealth posture learned during patrol: knees slightly bent, center of gravity lowered, ball of the foot touching the ground first, then the heel, minimizing footstep sounds to the greatest extent. But now, he used this posture not to capture intruders, but to avoid being discovered by intruders. Each step carried caution; each breath was controlled to be extremely faint.

His senses became exceptionally acute. The temperature of the night wind, the texture of the ground, every faint sound from the distance — all this information was being processed and analyzed in his brain. This was the normal reaction after adrenaline secretion, the body entering "fight or flight" mode after extreme stress. He could feel that although his heartbeat had slowed somewhat compared to when he was in the trash can just now, it was still much faster than in a normal state. Leizhao turned around, beginning to walk in the direction of the base center.

His footsteps were light, carrying the caution of a fugitive. His senses remained highly alert, not to seek the truth, but to avoid danger. He didn't want revenge, didn't want to investigate, didn't want to do anything that might expose himself.

He just wanted to survive.

As the ghost who had crawled out of the trash can, as number G-147 the "dead person," as an unrecorded existence in this cruel world — to continue forward.

"Why me?"

Why could he crawl out of the trash can, while Ahuang could only lie inside forever? If he had patrolled with Ahuang at that time, could it have changed something? If he had raised the alarm earlier, could he have saved more people?

These questions had no answers, but they would always exist, following him like a ghost.

Leizhao's body was recovering, but his mind was undergoing an even more difficult awakening. He realized that his "resurrection" was not a blessing, but a curse. He couldn't return to Beastshield, couldn't explain his survival, couldn't face the gazes of those dead companions. His number G-147 had already been recorded; his "death" had already been archived by the system — he had become an unrecorded ghost.

In the days to come, Leizhao would no longer be that simple, martial-arts-hall-dreaming recruit. He would become vigilant, suspicious, even somewhat cowardly. But his goal would no longer be a grand ideal, but the most primitive survival — finding water, finding food, finding a place to hide. He didn't want revenge, didn't want to investigate, didn't want to do anything that might expose himself.

He would transform from a Beastshield recruit into a fugitive, from a person with identity into a "dead person." This transformation process would be filled with fear, self-doubt, and anxiety about the unknown. He needed to learn to hide, to disguise, to — survive in this cruel world with the identity of a ghost.

### Story Reflections

After reading this chapter, the mood is heavy. Death, parting, guilt, responsibility — these heavy themes interweave together, forming a profound picture about survival and loss.

Ahuang's death is ordinary, but also tragic. He is not a hero, not a villain, just an ordinary, somewhat timid young man who wanted to do his job well. His death has no magnificent meaning, just "being in the wrong place at the wrong time." But it is precisely this ordinariness that makes his death more touching. He represents countless lives struggling at the bottom, representing that helplessness of "wanting to live well" but ultimately being crushed by fate.

Leizhao's survival is a kind of luck, but also a curse. He has the chance to continue living, but he must carry that "why me" guilt. He has the chance to find the truth, but he must face that "I can't change anything" powerlessness. He has the chance to become stronger, but he must experience that moral questioning of "what does becoming stronger mean."

What impressed me most in this chapter is the scene of collecting the body. Leizhao sending Ahuang into the trash can — that space he had just escaped from — this image is full of symbolic meaning. One cycle ended, another cycle began. Death and survival intersect in this green plastic container, forming an absurd and cruel aesthetic.

As a reader, I can't help but think: if it were me, could I do it? Could I face the death of a companion? Could I carry that guilt and continue forward? Could I maintain my humanity and dignity in this cruel world?

Leizhao made his choice. Not revenge, not investigation, not any action that might expose himself — just simply, instinctively choosing to survive. With the identity of a "dead person," to find a corner to hide in this cruel world.

Chapter 2 has ended, but Leizhao's escape has only just begun. How will he leave the base? How will he survive in White Capital? Will he still encounter others like himself? In this world shrouded by Beastshield's shadow, where will number G-147 the ghost go?

I close the "page," as if I can still smell that air mixed with semen, sweat, and the breath of death, so heavy it's hard to breathe. But within this heaviness, there is a glimmer of hope — because Leizhao is still alive, and being alive means everything is still possible.
