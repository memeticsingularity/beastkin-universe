# Chapter 2: Ahuang's Twilight

---

**Scene 1: The Return Journey**

Leizhao stood at the corner of the wall, leaning against the cold concrete, gasping for breath.

The stars in the night sky were still there, as bright as they had been minutes ago when he looked up at them. But for him, the world was completely different now. He had crawled out of the trash can, transformed from a discarded "corpse" back into a person with mobility.

But he couldn't stay here.

The outer perimeter was too dangerous. The attacker in black who had assaulted him might still be nearby, or there might be other bounty hunters. More importantly, he needed water, food, and to return to a place with lights and people.

He had to get back inside the base.

This decision filled him with a sense of absurdity. A few hours ago, he had still felt safe and bored within the base walls, complaining about the tedium of the night patrol. Now, that place he had once found "boring" had become his only refuge.

He straightened his uniform. The olive-green tight suit had mostly dried, but the crotch was still stiff, emitting an impossible-to-ignore odor. He tried using the hem of his upper garment to cover it, but with limited effect.

It didn't matter. Being alive was more important than anything.

He stuck close to the base of the wall, beginning to move in the direction of the base gate. His footsteps were light, almost sliding along the ground — this was the standard stealth posture learned during patrol. But now, he used this posture not to capture intruders, but to avoid being discovered by them.

**Scene 2: Discovery at the Fork**

That fork in the road was just ahead.

A street lamp above the fork emitted a dim yellow light, illuminating a small area. Leizhao remembered this place — just a few hours ago, while patrolling here, he had hesitated about whether to check out a suspicious sound.

Now, beneath that lamp, a foot extended from the bushes.

A foot wearing white knee-high socks and olive-green sneakers, sole up, the black number faintly visible.

Leizhao's heartbeat skipped a beat.

He recognized that foot. Or rather, he recognized that posture — so similar to his own in the trash can just now. Upper body stuffed deep into the bushes, lower body exposed outside, legs stretched out limply.

He slowly approached, each step carrying an ominous premonition.

When he reached the edge of the bushes, crouched down, and pushed aside those waist-high holly branches, he saw that face.

Ahuang.

That yellow-furred dog beastman, his batchmate, number G-208, was lying on his back deep within the bushes. His eyes were half-open, pupils already dilated, losing focus, but the corners of his eyes still held dried tear stains. His mouth was slightly open, tongue half-protruding, saliva and some white liquid mixed together, flowing from the corner of his mouth to his chin, then dripping onto his chest.

His olive-green uniform was still on, but the crotch was completely soaked, the dark green fabric turned into an almost black dark brown, clinging tightly to his body, outlining the silhouette of that once-erect, now-flaccid organ. Some white stains seeped from the gaps at the waist and cuffs of his pants, forming mottled traces on his white socks.

He was dead. Just like what Leizhao had nearly experienced.

**Scene 3: Facing the Dead**

Leizhao crouched beside Ahuang's corpse, his fingers trembling as they reached toward that familiar face.

Ahuang's fur was yellowish-brown, mixed with some black spots, typical dog beastman features. During the day, this fur always looked glossy, because Ahuang paid great attention to personal hygiene, often grooming his fur during breaks. But now, this fur was soaked with sweat and bodily fluids, clinging to his skin in strands, having lost all vitality.

Leizhao's fingers touched Ahuang's cheek.

Ice-cold. Completely without body temperature.

That touch made Leizhao's hand jerk back, as if burned. But immediately after, he reached out again, this time more firmly, gently closing Ahuang's half-open eyes.

"Ahuang..."

He called this name in a low voice, hoarse, not sounding like himself.

He began examining Ahuang's corpse, as if executing some standard patrol procedure. But his hands were trembling, each touch carrying a sense of sinful desecration.

There was an obvious bruise on Ahuang's neck, purple-red, encircling the entire neck. That was the mark of being strangled, identical to the scar on Leizhao's throat. Ahuang's hands still maintained a strange curled posture, fingers bent, as if scratching at something, or as if trying to protect his own genitals.

Leizhao's gaze fell on Ahuang's crotch.

It had mostly dried there, but white hard lumps could still be seen on the fabric. Semen. A large amount of semen. Mixed with that strong, fishy male musk emanating from Ahuang's body, forming a nauseating odor.

Leizhao recognized this manner of death. Because less than an hour ago, he himself had nearly become such a corpse.

The same murderer. The same method.

**Scene 4: Memories**

Memories surged up like a tidal wave.

That was daytime, in the cafeteria, sunlight streaming in through the high windows, forming bright patches on the cement floor. Leizhao and Ahuang sat at the same long table, synthetic meat steaks and starch blocks before them.

"Leizhao, are you scared of the dark on tonight's patrol?" Ahuang asked while chewing his food, his ears drooping, his eyes carrying obvious unease.

Leizhao remembered laughing at that time, revealing sharp tiger fangs. "Scared of what? What could happen in the base? I'll just treat it as a walk to help digestion."

"I... it's my first solo night patrol." Ahuang lowered his head, ears drooping even lower. "I'm a bit nervous. I heard there have been bounty hunters active nearby recently..."

"Don't scare yourself." Leizhao patted Ahuang's shoulder, the feel thick and warm. "Those bounty hunters target senior officers; small fry like us, they don't care about."

Ahuang raised his head, forcing a smile. "Right. Then see you at the cafeteria tomorrow morning?"

"Sure, see you tomorrow."

That was their last conversation.

Now, Ahuang lay here, dead on his first solo night patrol. And Leizhao, who had said "what could happen in the base," who had said "they don't care about" — he himself had nearly died on the same night.

Leizhao felt a strong nausea, not from the corpse's odor, but from inner guilt and self-blame.

If he had seriously listened to Ahuang's concerns at that time, if he had said "I'll walk with you for a stretch," if...

There were no ifs. Ahuang was dead, and he was alive.

**Scene 5: Collecting the Body**

Leizhao stood up, looking around.

The fork in the road was quiet, the street lamp emitting a faint electric hum. The lights at the distant base center were still on, but no sounds came from there, no patrol footsteps, no walkie-talkie static.

The entire base seemed to have become an empty city, or a huge grave.

He couldn't let Ahuang just lie here, exposed in the bushes, like a piece of meat casually discarded.

He needed to take Ahuang away, needed to give him a decent resting place. But where could he go? The base was probably full of corpses; the murderer might still be active, and with his current strength, he couldn't carry Ahuang's over-two-hundred-pound body very far at all.

His gaze fell on that green trash can at the corner of the wall.

That trash can he had just crawled out of.

Ironic. Too ironic. He himself had just escaped from that dark space, and now he was going to send his companion into it. But that was the only method he could think of right now — placing Ahuang in a hidden spot, waiting until he found rescue, or until dawn to deal with it.

He bent down, grabbing Ahuang's wrist.

Ahuang's wrist was still thick, muscles still firm, but had lost the elasticity of life. Leizhao exerted force, beginning to drag Ahuang out from the bushes.

A corpse was heavier than a living person. This was common knowledge learned during patrol — a living person would cooperate, adjust their center of gravity, exert their own strength; a dead person was just a pile of meat, all weight needing to be borne by the carrier.

Ahuang's back slid against the rough ground, making a rustling friction sound. Leizhao clenched his jaw, retreating step by step, dragging Ahuang toward the direction of the trash can.

His waist hurt — that was the injury from when he was caught at the trash can rim. His legs trembled — that was the trace of his strength not having fully recovered. But he didn't stop.

"Sorry..." he said in a low voice while dragging, not knowing whether he was speaking to Ahuang or to himself.

**Scene 6: Ritual and Farewell**

When he reached the trash can, Leizhao was already panting, covered in sweat.

He lifted the trash can lid, revealing the dark space inside. That space was so familiar to him — less than an hour ago, he himself had been curled inside, upper body forced into the darkness, lower body exposed outside, like a captured fish.

Now, he was going to send Ahuang inside.

He adjusted his posture, bent down, both hands passing under Ahuang's armpits, trying to lift the corpse. But Ahuang's weight exceeded his capacity; he tried several times, only able to lift the upper body, the waist down still dragging on the ground.

He changed strategy.

He first stuffed Ahuang's upper body into the rim, like stuffing a heavy bag of cargo. Ahuang's head entered the darkness first, then shoulders, then chest. The plastic edge of the rim scraped against Ahuang's uniform, making a faint sound.

Just like when Leizhao was there, Ahuang's waist got caught at the rim.

Leizhao panted heavily, using his knee to press against Ahuang's buttocks, pushing inward with force. He could hear the sound of the corpse's muscles and bones rubbing against the plastic can wall, could hear his own heavy panting echoing in the night sky.

Finally, Ahuang's upper body completely entered the trash can. But his two legs — those thick dog legs in white socks and olive-green sneakers — were still sticking out, pointing upward at an awkward angle, knees bent, calves hanging down.

Exactly the same posture Leizhao had been in.

Leizhao stopped, looking at this scene. Under the moonlight, those two legs extended from the edge of the trash can, the white knee-high socks particularly eye-catching in the dim light.

He reached out, helping Ahuang adjust his posture. He pulled Ahuang's pant cuffs down, covering those white stains. He crossed Ahuang's hands over his chest, though those hands were already beginning to stiffen. He took one last look at Ahuang's face — only a blurry outline visible in the darkness — then gently closed the trash can lid.

*Thud.*

The lid didn't close completely, because it was caught on Ahuang's thighs, leaving a gap. But those two legs were at least no longer so conspicuous; from a distance, they might just look like a pile of discarded garbage.

Leizhao stepped back, standing silently in front of the trash can for a while.

He didn't have the habit of praying, nor did he know what to say. In the end, he only said in a low voice: "See you tomorrow, Ahuang."

That was what they had said when parting in the cafeteria. Now, it had become a promise that could never be fulfilled.

**Scene 7: Awakening**

Leizhao turned around, beginning to walk in the direction of the base center.

His footsteps were firmer than when he had arrived. Not because his strength had recovered, but because he had a clear goal in his heart.

He was the only survivor among his batchmates. He didn't know how many people were still alive in the base, didn't know if the murderer was still there, didn't know how to explain his "resurrection from the dead." But he knew he had to survive, had to tell Beastshield headquarters what had happened.

His uniform still carried the odor of semen and sweat, his throat still had strangle marks, his mind still carried trauma that could never be erased. But he was still alive.

And being alive meant responsibility.

Moonlight stretched his shadow very long, projecting onto the cold cement ground. That shadow looked like a tiger, and like a person, moving forward alone in the darkness.

From this moment on, he was no longer just a recruit who wanted to open a martial arts hall, no longer just Beastshield number G-147 cannon fodder.

He was the ghost who had crawled out of the trash can, the survivor who had witnessed his companion's death, an avenger who had to find the truth.

**End of Chapter 2**

---

*He sent Ahuang into that dark space he had once escaped from. One cycle ended, another cycle began. On this night filled with death, Leizhao found the reason to continue forward — for those who could no longer continue.*
