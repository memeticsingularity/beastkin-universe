**Scene-1 Waiting in the Dark**

In the darkness, G-378's breathing was heavy and rapid, like wind passing through a dilapidated barn, carrying the echo of something nearing death.

He was a pig beastman, wild boar race, with a sturdy and heavy build. Even in the dim light, one could see his broad physique—not fat, but the kind of solid build developed through long-term physical labor. Brown bristles covered his entire body, like a mass of shadow in the faint light, those bristles rough and hard, each one like a stubborn memory drilled out from deep within the skin.

His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraint strap biting deep into the skin of his wrists. For pig-kind, accustomed to using their forelimbs for digging and carrying, this binding was especially uncomfortable—the strength he prided himself on was completely stripped away at this moment. The bristles at his wrists were flattened by the restraint strap, skin showing red marks beneath the rough nylon, sweat seeping into those tiny wounds, bringing waves of stinging pain.

His mouth was sealed, a rubber ball stuffed inside. He had tried to pry it open with his tusks, but the rubber was too resilient, only making his gums hurt more. Those tusks—once his proudest feature, curved and sharp, protruding from the corners of his mouth like two white scimitars—could now only leave shallow scratches on the rubber surface.

He could taste the bitterness of rubber, mixed with the fishy-salty taste of his own saliva, and a trace of blood—that was from biting the inner wall of his mouth when he tried to struggle. That taste reminded him of his childhood, in the countryside hometown, when father used a rubber hose to whip disobedient piglets, the smell that permeated the air.

The air was filled with that complex smell. The smell of sweat, blood, fear, and that deathly odor that terrified him. But in this mixture of foul odors, G-378 could still distinguish his own unique scent—that belonging to pig-kind, the intertwined smell of earth and labor. Countless tiny dust particles were hidden in his brown bristles, each hair absorbing his sweat and toil over four years.

His uniform was already soaked through, the dark blue fabric clinging tightly to his back, outlining the contour of his broad spine. That uniform was once a symbol of honor, now it had become a prisoner's garb. He could feel the touch of fabric rubbing against skin, rough and heavy, like the palm of fate pressing on his shoulder.

The first six were already dead.

Wolf, bear, tiger, ox, dog, leopard—they had all walked that white corridor, stepped onto that circular stage, then walked toward their end amidst the audience's laughter.

Now, it was his turn.

His crime was greed.

For three months, he had been secretly reselling warehouse supplies. Each time just a little, thinking he wouldn't be discovered. He would replace high-grade equipment with inferior goods, pocketing the difference; he would manipulate records, exaggerating losses, then selling the excess supplies to the black market.

He thought he was clever.

He thought no one would find out.

But Beast Shield's eyes were everywhere.

Yesterday, he was arrested.

The interrogator laid all the evidence before him—account books, photos, witness testimonies, even recordings of his transactions with black market merchants. He couldn't deny it, could only confess.

"You embezzled supplies worth 20,000 RUB," the interrogator said, "this is betrayal."

He wanted to defend himself, wanted to say he was just terrified of poverty, that he had an old mother to support, that he just wanted to improve his life. But the interrogator didn't give him a chance.

"In this world," the interrogator said coldly, "greed is the most shameful crime."

**Scene-2 The Eyes of Others**

The metal door emitted a harsh sound.

G-378 raised his head, seeing G-612—that black panther, being dragged out.

His steps were still light, maintaining that cheetah-like elegance even in a desperate situation. The door closed behind him, cutting off all light and sound.

The waiting room fell into silence.

G-378 turned his head, looking at the other survivors.

G-891, that white deer, still sat quietly, like a white jade statue. His eyes were calm and distant, as if already transcending all this. That pure white fur gleamed with soft luster in the dim light, forming a sharp contrast with G-378's rough brown bristles. Deer-kind were always like this, elegant and detached, as if they came from another world.

G-567, that male lion, leaned against the wall, maintaining a kingly posture. Even while bound, his spine remained straight, eyes burning with unyielding flames. The golden mane, though disheveled, still emanated a majestic aura. Lion-kind never bowed their heads, even when facing death.

G-734, that red fox, restlessly shifted his body. His eyes wandered everywhere, as if looking for an opportunity to escape—or looking for someone to sell out. Foxes were always like this, cunning and alert, even in a desperate situation still calculating pros and cons. His reddish-brown fur was like a dancing flame in the darkness.

G-378 lowered his head, not daring to meet anyone's gaze.

He knew his crime was the most dishonorable.

The wolf was a deserter, but at least he survived; the bear was negligent, but at least he admitted his mistake; the tiger was arrogant, but at least he had dignity; the ox was wronged, but at least he was innocent; the dog was nervous, but at least he was a newcomer; the leopard was overconfident, but at least he had been brave.

And he, was greed.

Selfish, shameful, unforgivable greed.

"Heng heng—" He tried to make a sound, wanted to say something, but the gag made this impossible.

He just wanted to survive.

He just wanted a better life.

Was that wrong?

**Scene-3 Dialogue in the Waiting Area**

Time passed minute by minute, the waiting in darkness like a chronic torture.

G-378 could feel his sweat sliding down his spine, leaving wet traces on the uniform lining. That sweat carried his unique body odor—a mixture of earth, bristles, and fear. His brown bristles absorbed the sweat, becoming heavy and damp, like wearing a soaked blanket.

"Are you alright?"

A voice softly rang out. G-378 raised his head, surprised to find G-891—that white deer, looking at him. Deer eyes glowed softly in the darkness, like two warm pearls.

G-378 couldn't answer, could only emit a vague whimper. He felt a wave of shame—being seen in his most wretched state by the most elegant race.

"No need to answer," G-891 said softly, "I know you can't speak."

His voice was calm and gentle, as if coming from very far away.

"I know what you're thinking," G-891 continued, "you're thinking, why you. You're thinking, you just wanted a better life, what was wrong with that."

G-378's body trembled slightly. Did deer-kind have the ability to read minds? Or was he just too easy to see through?

"But in this world," G-891 said, "greed is the original sin. Not because greed itself is so terrible, but because it challenges order."

"Heng—" G-378 emitted a low sound, like protest, like pleading.

"I'm not judging you," G-891 said, "I'm just telling you the truth. Here, we are all sinners, just with different crimes."

"Sounds nice." A hoarse voice cut in. G-567—that male lion, opened his eyes. Even in the darkness, those golden eyes still flashed with majestic light.

"Deer-kind are always like this," the lion said, "using those ethereal words to comfort themselves. What transcendence, what relief, they're just excuses for cowardice."

G-891 didn't retort, just gently closed his eyes.

"What about you, pig?" The lion turned to G-378, "How much did you embezzle?"

G-378 lowered his head, not daring to answer, also unable to answer.

"Twenty thousand RUB," G-734—that fox suddenly spoke, his voice sharp and rapid, "I heard. Twenty thousand RUB, enough to buy a small house."

The fox's eyes flashed with calculating light in the darkness.

"You're well-informed," the lion snorted coldly, "foxes are always like this, wanting to find out everything, wanting to use everything."

"Information is power," the fox didn't mind, "in this place, the more you know, the greater your chance of survival."

"Survive?" The lion let out a low laugh, "you think you can still survive?"

The fox fell silent.

G-378 listened to their conversation, feeling a deep loneliness. They were all dying people, yet still attacking each other, still despising each other. Perhaps this was the nature of beasts—even in a desperate situation, they couldn't truly unite.

"I've heard of you," the fox suddenly turned to G-378, "you're the warehouse manager, right? I have a friend who once bought things from you."

G-378's body stiffened. His friend? A black market merchant?

"Don't be nervous," the fox smiled, "I'm not here to expose you. I just want to say, you have quite a business mind. Too bad, bad luck."

"Luck?" The lion scoffed, "greed is greed, don't make excuses."

"As if you're so noble," the fox retorted, "don't lion-kind also live by plundering? It's just that you plunder prey, he plundered supplies. What's the difference?"

"The difference is," the lion's voice was low and majestic, "I never betray my tribe."

"Tribe?" The fox sneered, "here, none of us have tribes. We only have numbers."

G-378 listened to their quarrel, feeling dizzy. His tusks ached faintly beneath the rubber gag, wounds from when he tried to speak. He wanted to tell them, he wasn't greedy, he just—just wanted dignity, wanted recognition, wanted to prove he wasn't just a laborer who moved things.

But he couldn't say it.

**Scene-4 Whirlpool of Memories**

The waiting was too long, long enough for memories to surge like a tide.

G-378 closed his eyes, those images beginning to emerge in the darkness.

He remembered the scene of his first day at Beast Shield. At that time he was twenty-nine, in his prime, muscles firm and powerful, brown bristles shining in the sunlight. He wore a brand new uniform, those tusks polished snow-white, like two proud scimitars.

"G-378," the instructor looked at his number, "pig-kind, skilled at carrying and guarding. You're assigned to the supplies warehouse."

He remembered his mood at that time—excited, proud, full of hope. He had finally found stable work, could send money home, could make his parents proud.

The first year of work was hard. Moving tons of supplies every day, registering countless accounts, guarding through long nights. But he never complained, never slacked off. His spine slightly bent from the heavy load, but his eyes remained firm.

His palms were covered with calluses, traces left from years of carrying. His nails were thick and hard, suitable for digging and gripping. His knees were somewhat worn, the price of years of crouching and standing.

But he was proud. He was pig-kind, a laborer, the pillar of his family.

The change began in the second year.

He saw the gap. Those advanced ranks, those management levels, they wore bright uniforms, frequented high-end restaurants, discussed topics he couldn't understand. And he, could only wear dust-covered work clothes, moving goods in the warehouse.

He saw the corruption. Those leaders, using their positions to fill their own pockets, yet never punished. They could take public supplies for themselves, could falsify accounts, could accept bribes.

Why can they, but I can't?

Once this thought was born, it grew like weeds.

The first embezzlement was on a late night. He replaced a batch of high-grade equipment with inferior goods, pocketing the difference. That amount wasn't much, only a few hundred RUB, but that thrill made him addicted.

He became bolder. Falsifying losses, reselling supplies, trading with black market merchants. After each success, he would feel a strange satisfaction—as if he had finally proven his cleverness, proven he wasn't just a laborer who moved things.

He used that money to improve his life. Bought new clothes, changed to a better place to live, even started investing in some small businesses. He thought he was on an upward path, thought he had finally grasped fate.

But he was wrong.

Beast Shield's eyes were everywhere.

He remembered the scene of his arrest. Early morning, he had just arrived at the warehouse, when surrounded by a group of security personnel. They pressed him to the ground, put handcuffs on him, dragged him away like a pig awaiting slaughter.

"G-378," the interrogator said, "you are suspected of embezzling supplies, worth 20,000 RUB. Do you have anything to say?"

He wanted to defend himself, wanted to say those leaders also embezzled, why only arrest him. But he knew, these words were meaningless.

"I confess," he said, voice hoarse and desperate.

**Scene-5 The Meaning of Tusks**

"Your tusks," a voice suddenly rang out, interrupting G-378's memories.

He raised his head, finding G-891 looking at him, those warm eyes carrying some complex emotion.

"They're beautiful," the deer said softly, "curved, sharp, like crescent moons."

G-378 was stunned. No one had ever praised his tusks. In most people's eyes, they were just symbols of pig-kind savagery, weapons for rooting and attacking.

"In our deer-kind tradition," G-891 continued, "antlers represent dignity and pride. Every deer cherishes their antlers, because that is the glory we are born with."

He paused, gaze falling on G-378's tusks.

"Your tusks, to you, should be the same, right?"

G-378 felt a wave of sourness. Yes, his tusks were once his pride. When he was young, father told him, tusks were pig-kind's most precious wealth, could be used to dig for food, could protect oneself, could win respect.

He remembered his excitement when his tusks first grew. That pair of small, sharp white scimitars, protruding from the corners of his mouth, like a gift from nature. He would polish them with stones every day, keeping them sharp and white.

But as he grew older, tusks became a burden. In the city, they made him look savage and vulgar. In Beast Shield, they labeled him as "suitable for physical labor."

He began to hide his tusks, deliberately pursing his lips when speaking, deliberately turning his face when taking photos. He envied those races without tusks, envied that they could smile elegantly, could converse confidently.

Until he began to embezzle.

That money let him regain his confidence. He began to no longer care about others' gazes, began to boldly display his tusks. He even thought about getting tusk cosmetic procedures, making them even whiter and shinier.

How ridiculous.

"No matter how beautiful the tusks," the lion's voice suddenly rang out, "they can't change the nature of greed."

G-378 lowered his head, feeling a wave of shame.

"Perhaps," G-891 said softly, "but the tusks themselves are not wrong. What's wrong is the way they are used."

**Scene-6 Fear Before Going On Stage**

The metal door made a sound again.

"G-378," that voice rang out, "your turn."

Two people walked in, one on each side holding up his arms. Their hands were strong, but for pig-kind's weight, still somewhat strained.

"So heavy," the one on the left muttered, "pig-kind is trouble."

G-378 was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along that white corridor. His legs went soft, almost being dragged along. His tears had already begun to flow—from arrest until now, he had been crying.

The white walls of the corridor reflected glaring light, making him feel dizzy. He could smell the disinfectant on the walls, that pungent smell reminding him of hospitals, reminding him of death.

His uniform rubbed against his skin, the rough fabric making him uncomfortable. Sweat had already soaked the lining, bringing waves of chill. His brown bristles trembled slightly in the air conditioning, each hair seeming to speak of fear.

He regretted it.

He really knew he was wrong.

If he could do it over, he would absolutely not embezzle those supplies. He would be a loyal employee, a law-abiding citizen, a—

A person who wouldn't die.

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes.

The staff members pushed open the door.

Light, sound, smell, everything rushed in at once.

G-378 was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining directly down.

He raised his head, facing hundreds upon hundreds of gazes.

Those gazes held contempt, disgust, hatred.

He was tonight's most despised auction item.

**Scene-7 Display and Humiliation**

"Ladies and gentlemen!"

The fox host's voice rang out, carrying a special coldness.

"Today's seventh auction item is a real 'financial expert'!"

He walked to G-378's front, his fiery red fur shining under the lights.

"G-378, wild boar beastman, employed for four years!" The fox's voice was full of sarcasm, "For three months, using his position, he embezzled supplies worth 20,000 RUB! What a spectacular 'side business'!"

A burst of booing and cursing came from the audience.

"Let's take a look at this 'financial expert's' physical condition," the fox walked to G-378's front, beginning to unbutton his uniform.

"First, the chest."

The fox's palm pressed against G-378's chest, forcefully pressing. That palm was ice-cold and dry, contrasting with G-378's burning hot skin.

"Look here," his fingers slid across a scar above the right chest, that scar about five centimeters long, like a white centipede lying between the brown bristles, "this was from moving goods last year, right? An injury suffered for the company, what a proof of loyalty!"

He pressed down hard.

G-378 emitted a muffled hum, filtered by the gag into vague whimpering. That scar was left when he worked for Beast Shield, that knife almost took his life at the time. The blade cut through skin and muscle, blood dyeing his uniform red. He lay in the hospital for half a month, the wound stitched with twelve sutures.

He thought that could win the company's trust, could win a promotion opportunity.

But he was wrong.

"Hurt a lot, right?" the fox said in a low voice, fingers drawing circles around the scar, "this is the price of loyalty. When you betray, these scars become jokes."

His fingers continued to move, coming to the left chest, where there was another shallower scar.

"What about this one?" the fox asked, "was it from trading with black market merchants?"

G-378 shook his head, tears sliding down his cheeks.

"Forget it, doesn't matter," the fox smiled, "let's look elsewhere."

His hand continued to move, coming to the abdomen.

"The fat here," the fox patted it, that thick touch making the corner of his mouth rise, "thick, warm. Pig-kind's abdomen is sensitive, especially sensitive to temperature."

He deliberately paused, a trace of malice flashing in his eyes.

"In a moment, you'll experience this sensitivity."

G-378's body fiercely trembled. He didn't understand the fox's words, but that tone made him feel fear.

"Turn around," the fox commanded.

G-378 was forcibly turned around, back facing the audience.

"Look at this back," the fox's palm slid across his spine, from neck all the way to waist, "broad and thick, like a city wall."

His fingers stopped at the waist, where there was a long scar, left from an accident many years ago.

"This scar," the fox pressed down hard, "looks like it's been some years."

G-378 emitted a painful whimper. That scar was from when he was twenty, working at a construction site, hit in the waist by a collapsed shelf. The doctor said he was almost paralyzed, but he pulled through.

"Very soft here, very vulnerable," the fox's fingers rubbed on the scar, "just like your morality."

His hand continued downward, coming to the buttocks, forcefully slapping. That thick flesh emitted a dull sound.

"Buttocks full, thighs thick. This physique in normal labor would be a hot commodity—too bad, used in the wrong place."

Then, he grabbed G-378's tusks—that was the wild boar's proudest feature.

"Tusks," the fox said, "the wild boar's weapon, symbol of dignity, and also—"

He forcefully pulled.

"The easiest thing to break."

G-378 emitted a sharp whimper, pain making him almost unable to stand steady. A sharp pain came from the base of his tusks, as if about to be broken at any moment.

"How pitiful," the fox let go, turning to the audience, "looks like our Mr. Pig has already regretted."

**Scene-8 Unsold and Fate**

"Ladies and gentlemen!" the fox announced loudly, "such a 'financial expert,' starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

Then booing.

No one was willing to bid for a betrayer.

"Alright—unsold!" the fox announced, "unsold auction items will have their fate decided by the wheel!"

The screen lit up, the wheel spun.

G-378 watched that spinning pointer, feeling his heart almost jumping out of his chest. His brown bristles stood up from fear, sweat flowing down his spine.

The wheel stopped.

The pointer pointed to a special area.

"Branding iron heating + stimulation!" the fox excitedly announced, "looks like our Mr. Pig is going to experience 'heat'!"

**Scene-9 Execution**

G-378 was fixed to a metal frame, limbs pulled apart.

His uniform was completely undone, revealing his sturdy body. The spotlight shone on his skin, making that layer of brown bristles flicker with dim luster. Every bristle was clearly visible under the light, like a brown ocean, covering his broad chest and thick abdomen.

"Pig-kind is sensitive to temperature," the fox said, "let's see what reaction this branding iron can trigger."

A staff member walked forward holding a branding iron. That branding iron had already been heated, emitting a dark red glow, radiating scorching heat. Heat twisted in the air, like an invisible snake.

G-378 saw that branding iron, eyes suddenly widening. He began to struggle, trying to break free from the restraints, but the frame didn't move at all. His wrists were勒出 red marks by the restraint straps, brown bristles soaked with sweat, sticking to his skin.

The staff member brought the branding iron close to G-378's abdomen.

G-378 felt a wave of scorching heat, that heat penetrating his skin, reaching straight to his internal organs. He emitted a shrill whimper, body violently twisting. His abdominal muscles instinctively contracted, but the branding iron came closer and closer, the heat more and more intense.

"Look, starting to react," the fox said, "heat is rising."

The branding iron wandered across his abdomen, leaving red marks. That heat was like a living thing, drilling into his skin, stimulating his nerve endings. G-378's breathing became rapid and heavy, his eyes wide open, tears uncontrollably flowing.

"Pig-kind's abdomen is the most sensitive," the fox explained, "the skin here is thin, nerves dense, especially sensitive to temperature changes."

The branding iron drew circles around his navel, that dark red glow casting strange shadows on his skin. G-378 felt an indescribable feeling—not pure pain, but a complex sensation mixed with burning, stinging, and some strange pleasure.

"Twice."

The third time, his body began to twitch, limbs uncontrollably trembling. Sweat and tears mixed together, sliding down his cheeks, dripping on his brown bristles.

"Three times."

The fourth time, he felt his heart beginning to beat violently, as if about to jump out of his chest. His breathing became difficult, every inhalation carrying scorching heat.

### Story Reflections

This chapter is a story about "greed."

Liaoya's tragedy lies not in his poverty, but in his greed.

He was originally a hardworking laborer, a reliable family pillar. But when he saw the gap between rich and poor, he chose the wrong path.

Greed made him betray trust, betray principles, ultimately betray himself.

Under this system, greed is an unforgivable sin. Not because greed itself is so terrible, but because it challenges the company's authority, challenges the system's control.

Liaoya's death is a warning to all greedy people—before absolute power, individual cleverness is insignificant.

But we should also ask: what kind of system drove a hardworking laborer to become an embezzler? What kind of injustice made greed the only way out?

Liaoya is guilty, but not guilty enough to die.

What truly should be judged is perhaps the system that created greed itself.
