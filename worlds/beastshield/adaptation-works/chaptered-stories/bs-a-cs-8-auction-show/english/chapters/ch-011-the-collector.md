**Scene-1 Suffocation in the Waiting Area**

Darkness. Thick as overcooked asphalt, wrapping this narrow waiting area airtight.

Ten figures, ten breaths, ten different fears interweaving in the air into an invisible net.

G-724 leaned against the cold metal wall, his gray-white fur already soaked with sweat, clinging to his skin in strands. He was the first, tonight's opening act, the "auction item" about to be displayed to hundreds upon thousands of spectators. His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraint strap biting deep into the skin of his wrists, leaving red marks across that layer of gray-white short fur, like some cruel decoration.

He could smell his own scent—the sour stench of fear, the fishy saltiness of sweat, and that disinfectant smell of the Beastshield training camp that could never be washed away no matter what. His mouth was sealed, a black rubber ball stuffed inside, a belt looped over the top of his head to secure it, leaving him able to produce only vague whimpering sounds.

Beside him, G-556 the brown bear leaned against the wall, his body heavy as a mountain about to collapse. His breathing was rough and rapid, every inhale carrying resonance from deep in his chest. G-724 could hear his heartbeat—heavy, slow, like a war drum being struck, announcing some inevitable doom.

Further away, G-891 the tiger was scanning the surroundings with orange-yellow eyes, a trace of the last pride still remaining in his gaze. His body was tense; even in this situation, tiger instincts still kept him in a posture ready to pounce at any moment. But G-724 knew, that was just an illusion—when the restraint strap bit into the wrists, when the gag blocked the mouth, even the most powerful beast was just a lamb awaiting slaughter.

The air was filled with that complex smell. The smell of cleaning agent was the most superficial, like a thin veil covering deeper foul odors—aged sweat, fear-secreted pheromones, and that smell that made G-724's stomach spasm, the smell of death. That was the fishy膻 of semen, the smell emitted when flesh collapsed under extreme stimulation, the indelible memory left by countless previous "performances."

G-724 closed his eyes, trying to calm himself down.

He remembered that night three days ago. The squad was ambushed, companions fell one by one, fresh blood staining the forest's fallen leaves. He wanted to fight, wanted to protect them, but when that stun grenade exploded at his feet, instinct took over everything—run, run fast, survive.

He ran.

Through the forest, across the river, finally returning to the据点. He thought the company would understand, would investigate, would give him a chance to explain. After all, he was the only survivor, the only one who could tell the truth.

But he was wrong.

"Deserter," the interrogator only said one word, and decided his fate.

Now, he stood here, waiting for that moment that would push him into the abyss.

The metal door emitted a harsh sound.

G-724 snapped open his eyes, gray-white pupils contracting into needle points in the dim light. His body instinctively tensed, heart beginning to wildly beat, like a war drum thundering in his chest.

The door opened.

Light flooded in, illuminating a corner of the waiting room. A staff member walked in, holding an electronic tablet, expressionlessly scanning these ten prisoners awaiting slaughter.

"G-724," that voice was calm and indifferent, "prepare to go on stage."

**Scene-2 The Wolf's Entrance**

G-724 was dragged out of the waiting room, proceeding along the white corridor.

His legs went soft, almost being carried along by two staff members. On both sides of the corridor walls, there were faint traces—scratch marks, bloodstains, and that kind unable to be washed away, traces of despair. G-724's gaze swept over those traces, a strange resonance welling up in his heart.

How many people had walked this path?

How many G-rank grunts like him, had once thought their strength and courage could change fate, ultimately could only become "auction items" in this underground auction house?

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes.

The staff members stopped, adjusting their posture. G-724 could hear the sounds from behind the door—low conversation sounds, occasionally ringing laughter, and that kind of hair-raising, expectant silence. Hundreds of people were there, waiting for him, waiting to see his body displayed, waiting to see his dignity trampled, waiting to see him collapse under that irresistible stimulation.

"Remember," the staff member on the left said in a low voice, "don't try any tricks. The more you cooperate, the faster it ends."

Cooperate?

G-724 smiled bitterly in his heart. Did he have a choice?

The door was pushed open.

Light.

Sound.

Smell.

Everything rushed in at once, like a heavy punch to G-724's senses. He was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining straight down, illuminating his body with nowhere to hide. That strong light made him almost unable to open his eyes, only able to squint, adapting to the sudden brightness.

Then, he saw the surroundings clearly.

The audience.

Hundreds upon thousands of gazes fell on him.

Those gazes held curiosity, contempt, anticipation, and that nauseating excitement of hunters. G-724 could see their faces—wealthy merchants in gorgeous clothes, officers wearing medals, heavily made-up ladies, and expressionless professional buyers. They sat in tiered seats, like spectators in a Roman colosseum, waiting for the upcoming "performance."

Before him, a fiery red figure stood under the spotlight.

That was the fox host, Beastshield Public Relations Department's gold medal emcee, and also the soul of this death auction. His white suit shone under the lights, face carrying a professional smile, those amber eyes sparkling with cunning light.

"Ladies and gentlemen!" The fox's voice was loud and cheerful, like an experienced variety show host, "Welcome to tonight's auction! I am your old friend, host Hong Yan!"

A burst of applause and whistling came from the audience.

"Tonight, we have ten carefully prepared 'auction items,' each with a unique story and 'value'!" The fox deliberately emphasized the word "value," drawing meaningful laughter from the audience, "And now, let's welcome tonight's first auction item—G-724!"

The spotlight hit G-724, leaving him with nowhere to hide.

"G-724, gray wolf beastman, employed for one year!" The fox's voice was infectious, "Three days ago, he encountered misfortune during a mission—the squad was ambushed, companions all wiped out, and he—chose to flee!"

A burst of hissing and snickering came from the audience.

"Deserter!" Someone shouted loudly, drawing more laughter.

G-724 lowered his head, shame flooding over him like a tide. His body trembled under the spotlight, gray-white fur looking especially glaring under the strong light. He wanted to loudly defend himself, wanted to tell them it wasn't cowardice but survival instinct, wanted to tell them he had tried his best—but he couldn't. The rubber ball blocked his mouth; he could only produce vague whimpers.

"Raise your head, let everyone see you," the fox lifted his chin with a finger, "look at this face. So young, only twenty-two!"

G-724 was forced to raise his head, facing hundreds upon thousands of spectators. He could see the contempt in those gazes, the cruelty in those smiles. He was just a G-rank grunt, a consumable with a monthly salary of one hundred RUB, a part arbitrarily disposed of by the system.

"Look at this build," the fox's hand began to move on his body, "wolf-kind's build is just well-proportioned."

His palm lightly patted G-724's chest, feeling the firm muscle beneath that layer of gray-white short fur.

"These pecs," the fox exclaimed, "tight, elastic, young bodies are just different. Ladies and gentlemen, this is 'fresh stock'!"

His fingers sank deep into those firm pecs, pressing from the collarbone all the way to the sternum, feeling the texture of every muscle. G-724 could clearly feel every detail of that touch—the fox's fingers first touched the fur, then pressed into the muscle, that from-surface-to-depth pressing making his skin break out in goosebumps.

"Look here," the fox's finger stopped above his right chest, where there was a shallow scar, "this was left by combat, right? Although you ran in the end, at least you fought, right?"

He pressed down hard.

G-724 emitted a muffled hum, filtered by the gag into a low whimper. That scar was from being scratched by a companion during training camp; at the time they were doing combat training, and he lost to a wolf older than him. The wound wasn't deep, but left this scar, reminding him that he was never strong enough.

"Hurts a lot, right?" the fox said in a low voice, "this is the price of fleeing."

His hand continued moving, coming to the abdomen.

G-724's abdomen was typical wolf-kind build—flat, tight, with faint abs lines. That layer of gray-white short fur covered the skin, feeling soft and smooth to the touch. The fox's palm covered it, feeling the hardness and temperature of that layer of muscle.

"This belly," the fox patted, "flat and firm, young metabolism is just good. Pity, there's no future."

Laughter came from the audience.

G-724 closed his eyes. He regretted it. He regretted joining Beastshield, regretted participating in that mission, regretted fleeing—but if he hadn't run, he would now be like his other companions, rotting in the forest. No matter the choice, the ending seemed already destined.

"Turn around," the fox commanded.

G-724 was forcibly turned around, back facing the audience.

"Look at this back," the fox's palm slid across his spine, from shoulders all the way to waist, "smooth lines, well-proportioned muscle. Wolf-kind's build is just coordinated, like a carefully designed work of art."

His fingers stopped at the waist, lightly pinching.

G-724 emitted a muffled hum. That touch brought not only pain, but also a strange sense of shame. He felt his skin trembling, gray-white fur standing on end under the strong light.

"Buttocks are even tighter," the fox patted those two firm mounds, "full, elastic, full of youthful vitality. This build, under normal circumstances should be very popular."

His hand continued downward, coming to the thighs.

Wolf-kind's thighs were slender and powerful, quadriceps and biceps femoris lines smooth. The fox's hands simultaneously gripped his thighs, kneading from the base all the way to the knees, feeling the hardness and elasticity of that layer of muscle.

"These legs," the fox exclaimed, "slender and powerful, run fast—pity, ran in the wrong direction."

More laughter.

G-724's body was trembling. He was too afraid, afraid to the point of being unable to breathe, unable to think. He was just a twenty-two-year-old young man; he shouldn't be here, shouldn't be treated like this.

"Finally, let's look at this fur," the fox's hand returned to his chest, grabbing a handful of gray-white chest fur, "gray wolf's fur, soft and smooth, feels like fine silk. This texture, simply the symbol of youth!"

He let go, turning to the audience.

"Ladies and gentlemen! Such a 'fresh stock,' starting price—1 RUB!"

**Scene-3 Countdown and Salvation**

Silence.

No one in the audience raised their hand.

G-724 knew no one would. Who would buy a deserter? His crime was too dishonorable, his past too shameful, his future—there was no future.

"1 RUB!" The fox repeated, voice echoing through the empty auction hall, "Doesn't anyone want this 'deserter'? Give him a chance?"

Still silence.

G-724 could feel his heart wildly beating in his chest, every beat bringing a dull pain. He looked at those spectators—their expressions indifferent, eyes carrying scrutiny and contempt. Some were talking in low voices, some were playing with their communicators, some just looked at him expressionlessly, like looking at a piece of waste about to be destroyed.

Time seemed to stretch.

Every second was like a century. G-724 could hear his own breathing—rough, rapid, carrying the breath of despair. He could smell the various odors permeating the air—perfume, tobacco, alcohol, and that sweet, deathly smell that made his stomach spasm.

"Looks like no one wants him," the fox's voice carried some regret, but more of a programmed indifference, "a deserter's life isn't worth much either."

G-724 felt a wave of despair.

It was over.

Everything was over.

He would be unsold, then have his fate decided by the wheel, then be fixed to a metal frame, then ejaculate his last life essence under that irresistible stimulation, and then—die.

Like countless people before.

Like countless people after.

"3!" The fox began the countdown, voice clear and loud.

G-724 closed his eyes. He didn't want to look anymore, didn't want to face those indifferent gazes, didn't want to feel this humiliation of being displayed.

"2!"

The final images flashed in his mind—mother's smiling face, hometown's snowy plains, and that person he would never see again. Those memories were like distant starlight, flickering with the last warmth in the darkness.

Goodbye, world.

"1!"

"I bid!"

A deep voice cut through the silence of the auction hall.

**Scene-4 The White Figure**

The hall erupted in uproar.

G-724 snapped open his eyes, gray-white pupils violently contracting under the spotlight. He turned his head, looking toward the direction of the voice—the back row of the auction hall, near the exit.

There stood a figure.

White.

White from head to toe.

That was a white wolf beastman, tall, with broad shoulders and a wide back, draped in a pure white fox fur coat, shimmering with soft luster under the lights. His fur was silver-white, like the purest snow on the northern snow plains, a pair of ice-blue eyes especially striking in the dim auction hall.

There was a scar on his face—from the left corner of the eye all the way to the jaw, like a centipede lying on his face, adding a bit of ferocity to his originally handsome features. But the light in those eyes was calm and deep, like a lake with no visible bottom.

In his hand he held a black plaque, with a number written in gold font: 100.

"100 RUB," the white wolf's voice was deep and powerful, carrying an unquestionable authority, "I buy him."

The auction hall fell into dead silence.

Everyone turned their heads, looking at that suddenly appearing white wolf. Some recognized him, gasping; some discussed in low voices, tones full of surprise and speculation; others just looked blankly, not understanding what had happened.

The fox host's expression froze for an instant. His eyes slightly narrowed, as if assessing this unexpected variable. But quickly, he restored his professional smile—only that smile carried a bit more caution.

"100 RUB?" The fox repeated, voice carrying a trace of uncertainty, "Sir, are you sure? The starting price is only 1 RUB, you could just bid 1—"

"100 RUB," the white wolf interrupted him, tone calm but firm, "now, restart the countdown."

His voice wasn't loud, but carried a kind of irresistible power. That was the confidence of someone long accustomed to high position, the authority of someone used to giving orders.

The fox was silent for a second, then nodded.

"Alright, this gentleman bids 100 RUB! Any higher bids?"

No one responded in the audience.

100 RUB. For a G-rank grunt, this was already an astronomical price. Normally, these "auction items" had a starting price of 1 RUB, and the transaction price usually wouldn't exceed 10 RUB. 100 RUB was equivalent to a G-rank soldier's yearly salary.

"100 RUB first time!"

The fox's voice echoed through the auction hall.

G-724 stared blankly at that white wolf, mind blank. He didn't understand what was happening, didn't understand why this person was bidding, didn't understand why he suddenly had value.

"100 RUB second time!"

The white wolf stood quietly there, the white fox fur coat behind him gently swaying in the air conditioning wind. His expression was calm, eyes focused on G-724 on the stage, as if examining a collection about to come into his possession.

"100 RUB third time! Sold!"

The sound of the gavel falling echoed through the auction hall, like a heavy hammer striking everyone's heart.

G-724 was bought.

He wasn't unsold, wasn't executed, wasn't killed in that humiliating way. He was bought, at an astronomical price, by that mysterious white wolf.

His body began to tremble, not from fear, but from an indescribable relief. Tears welled up in his eyes, flowing down his cheeks, dripping onto his chin, then falling to the floor.

He was saved.

He was really saved.

"Congratulations to this gentleman," the fox's voice carried a trace of barely detectable complex emotion, "you have obtained tonight's first auction item. Please come to the backstage later to handle the handover procedures."

The white wolf nodded, not speaking. He just quietly looked at G-724, those ice-blue eyes flashing with a hard-to-fathom light.

Then, he sat down.

G-724 was taken into a room backstage. There were already several people in the room—all "auction items" bought earlier, sitting on chairs with varied expressions, some blank, some relieved, some still fearful.

**Scene-6 Buying Out the House**

The atmosphere in the auction hall became strange.

Everyone's gaze was focused on that white wolf, eyes full of surprise, confusion, and speculation. Some began discussing in low voices, guessing the identity of this mysterious buyer; some frowned, obviously displeased with this intervention; others just watched with interest, like watching an unexpected good show.

The fox host's expression became more complex. He looked at that white wolf, eyes flashing with vigilance.

"Sir," the fox weighed his words, "you have already bought one auction item."

"So?" The white wolf's voice was still calm, "is there a rule limiting the quantity a buyer can purchase?"

The fox was silent.

Indeed, there was no such rule in the rules. Because no one had ever thought that someone would want to buy all the auction items.

Then, he made a move that shocked everyone—

He raised the plaque in his hand, nodding slightly at the fox.

"Continue," he said, "next item."

G-891, yellow tiger beastman, orange fur covered with black stripes, even in this situation, his body still emitted a kind of primitive majesty. But at this moment, those amber eyes were full of anger and unease—he had heard the commotion in front, didn't understand what was happening.

**Scene-9 Untying the Bonds**

The atmosphere in the auction hall solidified.

The fox host looked at the white wolf, then looked at G-734 on stage, as if weighing pros and cons. Ultimately, he nodded.

"...Alright."

The white wolf slowly walked onto the stage.

His steps were steady and powerful, every step carrying an indescribable majesty. The white fox fur coat gently swayed behind him, the black cane tapping the floor, emitting crisp sounds.

He walked to G-734's front, stopping.

Two foxes looked at each other—one was fiery red, one was silver-white; one was bowing and scraping, one was high above; one was a criminal about to be executed, one was a savior (or, some more terrifying existence) from who knows where.

G-734 shook his head, eyes full of fear and confusion.

"Because," the white wolf slowly reached out, grabbing the restraint strap on G-734's wrist, "I want you to know—you're worth 1000 RUB. Not because you're worth this price, but because I want you to live."

G-734's eyes suddenly widened.

"I want you to know," the white wolf untied the belt on his head, removing the rubber gag, "your betrayal didn't kill anyone. Your self-preservation was futile."

The gag was removed, G-734 opened his mouth, gasping for breath. His jaw ached, saliva still hanging from the corner of his mouth, but he couldn't care about these. He just stared blankly at the white wolf, not understanding his meaning.

"You... who are you?" G-734 asked trembling.

The white wolf looked at him, ice-blue eyes flashing with a complex emotion.

"My name is Shuang Ya," he said, "once a Beastshield R-rank executive. Now, I am a collector."

He turned, facing the audience.

"What I collect is not art, not antiques, not jewelry. What I collect—are people discarded by you."

His voice echoed through the auction hall, deep and powerful.

"These ten people, I buy them all tonight. They won't return to Beastshield, won't become your cannon fodder again, won't be crushed by your system again. They'll go with me, to a new place, start a new life."

He raised his cane, heavily tapping it on the floor.

He sneered.

"But remember tonight. Remember there was a person who used 5000 RUB to buy away ten lives you were going to kill. Remember, you are not God, you cannot decide who should die and who should live."

He turned, walking toward backstage.

"Let's go," he said to those ten newly bought prisoners, "follow me home."

**Scene-10 Shuang Ya's Collection**

Three months later.

Northern border, a valley surrounded by snow mountains.

Here there was an estate called "Qi Tu," covering thousands of acres, with an independent farm, factory, training ground, and residential area. The estate's owner was that white wolf who "bought out the house" at the auction—Shuang Ya.

G-724 stood at the edge of the training ground, looking at the distant snow mountains, gray-white fur gently swaying in the wind.

Three months ago, he thought he would die at that auction. But now, he stood here, breathing free air, feeling the warmth of sunlight on his skin.

He didn't die.

None of them died.

"What are you thinking about?"

A voice came from behind. G-724 turned his head, seeing G-556 the brown bear walking over, holding two cups of hot drinks in his hands.

"Nothing," G-724 took the cup, feeling that warmth transmitting through the ceramic to his palm, "just thinking... whether all this is real."

"It's real," G-556 sat beside him, his heavy body making the bench groan, "I've pinched myself three times. It hurts, so it's not a dream."

G-724 smiled.

Over the past three months, the ten of them had started new lives at the "Qi Tu" estate. Shuang Ya didn't treat them as slaves or tools, but gave them choices—they could choose to accept training to become his private security, could choose to work and learn a craft at the estate, or could choose to leave, go anywhere they wanted.

G-724 chose to stay, to accept training. He no longer wanted to be a deserter; he wanted to become a true warrior—not for Beastshield, not for money, but to protect those he cared about.

"Where are the others?" G-724 asked.

"Tiger and leopard are sparring over at the training ground," G-556 said, "ox is helping at the farm, dog is learning mechanical repair, deer is caring for the wounded, lion is teaching tactics to newcomers, boar... uh, he's still adapting."

"What about the fox?"

G-556's expression became complex.

"The fox... is still in solitary confinement. Shuang Ya said he needs to think some things through."

G-724 fell silent.

That fox who betrayed his companions, G-734, was the only one not given freedom. He was locked in a room deep in the estate, someone delivered food every day, but couldn't go out, couldn't contact others.

Shuang Ya said, this was his punishment—not physical punishment, but spiritual. He wanted the fox to watch, to see those people he sold out were all alive, all doing well, all having their own new lives. And he himself, could only face his own sins day after day in a small room.

"What do you think... why did Shuang Ya save us?" G-724 asked.

G-556 took a sip of his hot drink, silent for a long time.

"I don't know," he finally said, "maybe because he was also a Beastshield person and knew the horror of that system. Maybe because he truly believes everyone deserves a second chance. Or..."

He paused.

"Or, he's just collecting 'discarded soil,' proving that system's wrong."

G-724 looked at the distant snow mountains, pondering these words.

No matter what, he survived.

They survived.

And that auction house that once let them wait for death, was still continuing—every week there were new "auction items," every week there were new deaths, every week that host's voice echoed: "Starting price—1 RUB!"

But at least, they escaped.

They became fish that slipped through that system's net, became "collections" of the collector Shuang Ya, became living proof that that system could be challenged.

"Let's go," G-724 stood up, patting G-556's shoulder, "time for training."

"Yeah."

Two beastmen walked side by side toward the training ground, sunlight shining on them, casting long shadows.

At the highest point of the estate, a white figure stood by the window, ice-blue eyes watching all this.

Shuang Ya held that black cane in his hand, the scar on his face especially striking in the sunset.

"Ten people," he murmured to himself, "ten lives, ten stories."

He turned, walking toward the desk. On the desk was a list, densely packed with names—all Beastshield's "pending executions," all "auction items" about to be sent to the auction block.

"Next auction," Shuang Ya said, the corner of his mouth slightly rising, "I'll buy another ten."

He picked up a pen, crossing out ten names on the list.

"Until I buy out all of you," he said to the snow mountains outside the window, "until that auction house has no more auction items."

Outside the window, night fell.

And in some southern city, the underground auction house's lights came on again, the fox host's voice ringing out once more:

"Ladies and gentlemen! Welcome to tonight's auction!"

**Chapter 11 END**

## Story Commentary and Reflections

> *The following commentary is based on this chapter's story content, attempting to understand the meaning of this "what if" world.*

### The Fate of Being Bought

This chapter is a story about "salvation."

But not that cheap, fairy-tale salvation—the prince appears, the princess is saved, and they live happily ever after.

Shuang Ya's salvation is heavy, complex, carrying some indescribable motive.

Is he a savior? Not entirely. He has his purposes—collecting "discarded soil," proving the system's wrong, or rather, satisfying some collecting desire of his own.

Is he a savior? To some extent, yes. He spent 5000 RUB, bought ten lives, gave them a second chance.

But this salvation has a price—G-734 is imprisoned in a spiritual cage, the others carry the psychological burden of "being saved," and Shuang Ya himself seems to be fighting against something in this way.

### The System's Loophole

The most important meaning of this chapter lies in proving that **the system is not invincible**.

Beastshield's auction house considers itself absolute—unsold means death, no one can escape. But Shuang Ya used 5000 RUB to prove that as long as there's money, as long as there's will, anyone can break this rule.

This gives the previous ten chapters an added layer of meaning—those characters' deaths were not inevitable, but **chosen**. Someone chose not to save them, someone chose to ignore them, someone chose to treat them as entertainment.

Shuang Ya chose another path.

### The Collector's Paradox

Shuang Ya said he is a "collector," collecting "discarded people."

This statement itself is very contradictory—he turned people from commodities into collections, from those awaiting execution into collected objects. He gave them life, but also incorporated them into his own system, making them tools for his resistance against Beastshield.

Is this true freedom? Or another form of bondage?

G-724 and the others could leave; Shuang Ya gave them this choice. But most chose to stay—because they had nowhere to go, because they owed Shuang Ya a life, because "Qi Tu" gave them a sense of belonging.

Is this salvation, or another form of control?

Maybe both.

### The Unfinished Story

Has this chapter ended?

No.

The auction continues, every week there are new "auction items," every week there are new deaths. Shuang Ya says he'll continue buying, until he buys out everyone, until the auction house has no more auction items.

But that's impossible.

As long as Beastshield exists, as long as that system exists, there will be new failures, new scapegoats, new "G-724s" and "G-556s."

Shuang Ya can buy ten, a hundred, a thousand—but he can't buy them all.

Because the root of this system is not in the auction house, not in those spectators, but in the entire society, in the entire world, in that thought in each of our hearts of **"treating life as a commodity."**

As long as that thought remains, the auction will continue.

**But Shuang Ya's existence at least proves—someone can choose not to stand by.**

That's enough.

That's enough.
