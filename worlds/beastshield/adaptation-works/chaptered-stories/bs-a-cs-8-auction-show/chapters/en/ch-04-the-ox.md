**Scene-2 The Tiger's Afterimage**

G-445 was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along the white corridor.

His legs went soft, not from fear, but from that unspeakable grievance. He hadn't done anything wrong, he just did what he was asked to do, why was he the one bearing the responsibility in the end?

On both sides of the corridor walls, there were faint traces—scratch marks, bloodstains, and that kind unable to be washed away, traces of despair. G-445's gaze swept over those traces, a strange sorrow welling up in his heart.

How many people had walked this path?

How many honest people like him, had once thought that as long as they did their own work well they could live in peace, ultimately became the system's scapegoats?

"Another big guy," the staff member on the left muttered, "why are they all heavyweights today."

"Ox-kind is heavy," the one on the right said, "but honest, won't struggle. Much easier to deal with than that tiger just now."

G-445 listened to their conversation, bitter taste welling up in his heart.

Honest, turned out to be a flaw.

Honest, turned out to mean easy to bully.

Honest, turned out to be a crime in this world.

He thought of his father, thought of that equally honest and simple ox elder. Father worked diligently all his life, enduring hardship without complaint, finally lost both legs due to an accident. And that superior who caused the accident was unharmed, even got promoted.

"This is our fate," father said at the time, "honest people always suffer losses."

He didn't believe it at the time. He thought, as long as he worked hard, as long as he was honest and trustworthy, he could change fate.

But now, he finally understood father's words.

The audience. Hundreds upon hundreds of gazes fell on him.

He raised his head, facing those gazes.

In his heart, there was only an unspeakable sorrow.
