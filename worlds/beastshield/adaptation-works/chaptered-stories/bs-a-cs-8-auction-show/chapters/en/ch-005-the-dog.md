**Scene-1 Trembling in the Waiting Area**

Darkness wrapped this narrow waiting area like a thick quilt, sealing it airtight.

G-203 curled up in the corner, his body trembling violently, like a fallen leaf in the wind. He was a dog beastman, yellow dog race, with a well-proportioned build, young. He was only twenty-one years old, employed for just two weeks, the newest member of this batch, and also the youngest.

His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraint strap biting deep into the skin of his wrists, leaving red marks across that layer of yellow short fur. That touch made him feel fear—he was just a newcomer, he wasn't ready to face all this.

His mouth was sealed. A black rubber ball stuffed in his mouth, fixed with a belt looped over the top of his head. He tried to make a sound, wanted to plead, wanted to explain, but that rubber ball made this impossible. He could only emit vague whimpering, that sound like some kind of injured small animal, sorrowful and helpless.

Tears had already flowed all over his face, forming shiny trails on the yellow fur.

From capture until now, he had been crying. He couldn't control himself—too scared, too desperate, too regretful.

The air was filled with that complex smell—the smell of cleaning agent covering deeper foul odors: aged sweat, fear-secreted pheromones, and that smell which made his stomach spasm, the smell of death. That was the fishy膻味 of semen, the odor emitted when flesh collapsed under extreme stimulation.

The first four were already dead.

That wolf was dying in the corner, that bear had died peacefully, that tiger had collapsed and been dragged back, that ox had just been dragged out. He heard that ox's heavy footsteps, heard his hesitation in the corridor, heard the noise that poured out when that door opened.

Now, it was his turn.

His crime was a mistake.

Two days ago, he executed his first official mission—guarding the warehouse gate. He was too nervous, his palms were all sweaty, the gun he held was trembling. That was his first time truly facing work, first time bearing real responsibility, first time knowing that the adult world was this terrifying.

Then, a truck came. The driver showed his credentials, he checked them, everything was normal. That driver looked very friendly, even smiled at him, saying "hard work, young man."

But he forgot one step—checking the cargo list.

That truck was carrying not the ordinary supplies on the list, but contraband. The driver was a disguised bounty hunter, and he, G-203, let them go.

"Your mistake caused the company huge losses," the interrogator said. "Beast Shield does not tolerate incompetence."

He wanted to explain, wanted to say he was just a newcomer, that he was too nervous, that he would definitely pay attention next time—but the interrogator didn't give him a chance.

"There is no next time," the interrogator said. "Your life is at the company's disposal."

He had been crying.

From that moment until now, his tears hadn't stopped.

He was too young.

He hadn't truly started living yet, hadn't fallen in love, hadn't seen the world—yet he was going to die.

G-203 opened his eyes, gaze wandering in the darkness. His eyes had adapted to the dimness, able to see the outline of this waiting area. Not far to his left, that wild boar G-378 restlessly shifted his body. His build was sturdy, brown bristles like a mass of shadow in the dim light.

"Heng heng—" The wild boar tried to make a sound, turned into a comical nasal noise after being blocked by the mouth-sealing device.

G-203 looked at him, sorrow welling up in his heart. That wild boar was convicted for embezzlement, a truly guilty person. And he, just because of a mistake, a mistake any newcomer would make, had to pay the price with his life.

This wasn't fair.

But he knew, fairness didn't exist in this world.

He turned his head, looking across.

That black panther G-612 sat quietly, like a black statue. Even in this desperate situation, he still maintained that cheetah-like alertness. That white deer G-891 was also there, sitting quietly, like a white jade statue, even in this desperate situation still maintaining that surprising calm.

They were all waiting, waiting for their turn.

And in the farther corner, that male lion G-567 sat quietly, golden mane still flickering with majestic luster in the dim light. He was the king of this batch, a former leader, and now, just another prisoner waiting for death.

And there was the last one—that red fox G-734, restlessly shifting his body, eyes wandering everywhere. He was the last of this batch, and also the one with the most despicable crime—betrayal, selling out companions.

G-203 looked at that fox, complex emotions welling up in his heart. That fox was shameful, but at least he had lived more days, at least had more experiences. And himself, had just begun, and was about to end.

The metal door emitted a harsh sound.

G-203 jerked up his head, yellow eyes flashing with tearlight in the dimness. His body trembled violently, heart wildly beating, like a war drum thundering in his chest.

The door opened, a dazzling ray of light shot in.

Two staff members walked in, dragging a limp body.

It was G-445, that black ox. He had already "performed" and was now back alive—or rather, once alive. His body was limp, like a collapsed mountain, tear stains still hanging from the corners of his eyes, those black eyes open, as if asking "why."

G-203 looked at that ox, a strange resonance welling up in his heart.

That ox was wronged, just like him. They were both innocent, both sacrifices of this system. But that ox at least lived to twenty-nine, at least had three years, while he, only twenty-one, only two weeks.

"Your turn," the staff member said, "youngster."

**Scene-2 The Ox's Afterimage**

G-203 was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along the white corridor.

His legs went soft, almost being dragged along. His tears continued to flow, dripping on the olive-green uniform, forming dark traces. He didn't want to die, he really didn't want to die, but he had no choice.

On both sides of the corridor walls, there were faint traces—scratch marks, bloodstains, and that kind unable to be washed away, traces of despair. G-203's gaze swept over those traces, an unspeakable sorrow welling up in his heart.

How many young people had walked this path?

How many newcomers like him, had once thought that as long as they worked hard they could live in peace, ultimately paid the price of life because of a mistake?

"A newcomer," the staff member on the left muttered, "so young. What a pity."

"Pity what?" the one on the right said, "his own mistake, he bears it himself."

"But it was just his first mission—"

"Rules are rules."

G-203 listened to their conversation, bitter taste welling up in his heart.

Rules.

So rules were more important than human life.

He thought of his parents, thought of that warm home, thought of the meals mother cooked, thought of the days when father taught him to ride a bicycle. Those memories were like distant starlight, flickering with the last warmth in the darkness.

He could never go back.

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes.

The staff members stopped, adjusted their posture, then pushed open the door.

Light.

Sound.

Smell.

Everything rushed in at once, like a heavy punch hitting G-203's senses.

He was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining directly down, illuminating his body with nowhere to hide. That strong light made him squint, adapting to the sudden brightness.

Then, he saw the surrounding environment clearly.

The audience. Hundreds upon hundreds of gazes fell on him.

Those gazes held curiosity, contempt, anticipation, and that hunting excitement which made him want to vomit.

He raised his head, facing those gazes.

In his heart, there was only an unspeakable fear.

He was too small, too young, too unprepared.

**Scene-3 Display and Sorrow**

"Ladies and gentlemen!"

That voice rang out, resonant and cheerful, like an experienced variety show host.

"Today's fifth auction item is a real 'fresh item'!"

The fox host walked out from the side of the stage, his white suit sparkling under the spotlight. His steps were light, face carrying a professional smile, gaze sweeping over the audience, finally landing on G-203.

"G-203, yellow dog beastman, employed for two weeks!" The fox's voice was infectious, "Two days ago, on his first official mission, he forgot to check the list due to nervousness, letting a disguised bounty hunter go! What a spectacular 'debut'!"

A burst of snickering came from the audience.

G-203 lowered his head. His body was trembling violently, tears flowing down his cheeks, dripping on his chin, then falling on the floor.

He was too regretful.

If time could turn back, he would definitely be more careful, definitely double-check, definitely not make that mistake—

But time couldn't turn back.

"Raise your head, let everyone see you," the fox lifted his chin with a finger, "look at this face. So young! Only twenty-one!"

G-203 was forced to raise his head, facing hundreds upon hundreds of audience members. He could see the interest in those gazes, the cruelty in those smiles.

He was just a newcomer.

He just wanted to do his job well.

Why so cruel?

"Look at this build," the fox's hand began to move on his body, "dog-kind's physique is truly well-proportioned."

His palm lightly patted G-203's chest.

That firm pectoral muscle trembled slightly beneath the olive-green uniform. Dog-kind pectoral muscles weren't as tight and defined as tiger-kind, nor as thick as ox-kind, but were well-proportioned and coordinated, just right. The fox's fingers sank into that firm pectoral muscle, feeling the power and temperature beneath.

"This chest," the fox exclaimed, "firm, elastic, young bodies are just different. Ladies and gentlemen, this is a 'fresh item'!"

His fingers slid across that layer of muscle, from collarbone all the way to sternum, feeling the texture of every muscle. G-203 could clearly feel every detail of that touch—the fox's fingers first touching yellow short fur, then pressing into muscle, that transition from surface to depth made his skin break out in goosebumps.

"Look here," the fox's fingers stopped above his right chest, where there was a shallow scar, "what's this? From falling as a child?"

He lightly pressed down.

G-203 emitted a muffled hum, filtered by the mouth-sealing device into a low whimper. That scar was from when he fell off a bicycle at ten. At that time father helped him up, patting his shoulder saying "a man, what are you crying for."

"A mark of youth," the fox said in a low voice, "too bad, no more chances for more marks."

His hand continued to move, coming to the abdomen.

G-203's abdomen was a typical dog-kind physique—flat, firm, with faint abdominal muscle lines. That layer of yellow short fur covered his skin, feeling soft and smooth. The fox's palm covered it, feeling the hardness and temperature of that muscle layer.

"This belly," the fox patted it, "flat and firm, young metabolism is indeed good. Too bad, no future."

Laughter came from the audience.

G-203 closed his eyes. Tears continued to flow.

He regretted it.

He really regretted it too much.

"Turn around," the fox commanded.

G-203 was forcibly turned around, back facing the audience.

"Look at this back," the fox's palm slid across his spine, from shoulders all the way to waist, "smooth lines, well-proportioned muscle. Dog-kind's physique is just coordinated, like a carefully designed work of art."

His fingers stopped at the waist, lightly pinching.

G-203 emitted a muffled hum. That touch brought not only pain, but also a strange sense of shame.

"Buttocks are even tighter," the fox slapped those two firm mounds of flesh, "full, elastic, full of youthful vitality. This physique under normal circumstances should be very popular."

His hand continued downward, coming to the thighs.

Dog-kind thighs were long and powerful, quadriceps and biceps femoris lines smooth. The fox's hands simultaneously gripped his thighs, kneading from the base all the way to the knees, feeling the hardness and elasticity of that muscle layer.

"These legs," the fox exclaimed, "long and powerful, run fast, stand steady—too bad, stood in the wrong place."

More laughter.

G-203's body was trembling. He was too scared, scared to the point of being unable to breathe, unable to think.

He was just a twenty-one-year-old child.

He shouldn't be here.

"Finally, let's look at this fur," the fox's hand returned to his chest, grabbing a handful of yellow chest fur, "yellow dog fur, soft and smooth, feels like premium silk. This texture is simply the symbol of youth!"

He let go, turning to the audience.

"Ladies and gentlemen! Such a 'fresh item,' starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

"1 RUB!" the fox repeated, "anyone want to give this 'newcomer' a chance?"

No one raised their hand.

G-203 knew no one would. But even knowing, when that sliver of hope was shattered, he still felt a wave of heartbreak.

Really no one?

Really no one?

"Looks like no one wants it," the fox's voice carried a certain regret, "a newcomer's life is also not worth money."

G-203 felt a wave of despair.

"3! 2! 1!" the fox counted down, "anyone bidding?"

No one.

"Alright—unsold!"

That word fell, and G-203 felt the world collapse.

Over.

Everything was over.
