**Scene-1 Arrogance in the Waiting Area**

Darkness wrapped this narrow waiting area like a thick curtain, sealing it airtight.

G-891 leaned against the cold metal wall, his body as tense as a fully drawn bow. He was a tiger beastman, yellow tiger race, 1.85 meters tall, weighing 80 kilograms. Even in the dim light, one could see his distinctive physique—orange-yellow fur covered with black stripes, broad shoulders, firm muscles, that innate kingly aura still emanating even in this desperate situation.

But in his eyes, there was already a trace of wavering.

He heard the endings of the first two. That wolf, that bear—they were both dead. Amidst the audience's laughter, the auctioneer's banter, under that irresistible force, they shot out their final life essence, then became cold corpses.

He told himself, he wouldn't be like that.

He was a tiger.

Tigers had tiger dignity.

His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraint strap biting deep into the skin of his wrists, leaving red marks across that layer of orange-yellow short fur. That touch made him angry—he, a tiger, was actually bound like this, like livestock awaiting slaughter.

His mouth was sealed. A black rubber ball stuffed in his mouth, fixed with a belt looped over the top of his head. He tried to bite through it, but the rubber was too hard, only making his teeth ache, gums oozing blood threads. That iron-like taste permeated his mouth, mixed with the bitterness of rubber, forming a nauseating odor.

He could only breathe through his nasal cavity, every inhalation carrying that complex smell—the smell of cleaning agent covering deeper foul odors: aged sweat, fear-secreted pheromones, and that smell which made his stomach spasm, the smell of death. The first two were already dead.

That wolf was dying in the corner, his body limp, like a rag doll emptied of its soul. That bear had just been dragged out, his heavy footsteps still echoing in G-891's ears.

Now, it was his turn.

His crime was leaving his post without permission.

One night ago, he left his post to gamble at an underground casino. He thought, just for a while, there wouldn't be a problem. His luck was very good, won several hands in a row, chips piled like a small mountain—then, the alarm rang.

By the time he returned to his post, everything was over.

The intruders had already left, and his absence created a gap in the defense line. Although the final losses weren't large, his unauthorized departure was recorded, becoming evidence for conviction.

"You left your post without permission," the interrogator said, "this is betrayal."

He wanted to argue, wanted to say it was just an accident, that he was just acting on a whim—but the interrogator didn't give him a chance.

"Tiger beastman," the interrogator sneered, "always so arrogant."

That sentence was like a knife, stabbing into his self-esteem.

G-891 opened his eyes, gaze wandering in the darkness. His eyes had adapted to the dimness, able to see the outline of this waiting area. Not far to his left, that black ox G-445 sat quietly, eyes hollowly gazing at the ceiling. His build was thick, black short fur like a mass of shadow in the dim light.

"Hmph—" The tiger emitted a low nasal sound, turned into a threatening muffled noise after being blocked by the mouth-sealing device.

That ox turned his head, looked at him. There was no fear in those eyes, nor anger, only a deep exhaustion and resignation. That gaze made G-891 feel a wave of discomfort—he didn't understand, how could one not be angry at a time like this? How could one not resist?

He turned his head, looking across.

That yellow dog G-203 curled up in the corner, body trembling. He had only been employed for two weeks, a probationary newcomer, who made a mistake due to over-nervousness on his first official mission. He was still a child, eyes full of fear and tears.

G-891 looked down on him.

Tiger pride made him unable to understand this display of weakness. Even in a desperate situation, one should maintain dignity, maintain anger, not tremble like that.

"Wu—" The dog whimpered again.

G-891 turned his head, no longer looking at him.

To his right, that wild boar G-378 restlessly shifted his body. His build was sturdy, brown bristles like a mass of shadow in the dim light. He was the one with the most dishonorable crime in this batch—embezzlement, betraying the company's trust.

"Heng heng—" The wild boar tried to make a sound, turned into a comical nasal noise after being blocked by the mouth-sealing device.

G-891 didn't respond. His gaze passed through the wild boar, looking further—that black panther G-612, sitting quietly, like a black statue. Even in this desperate situation, he still maintained that cheetah-like alertness, body tense, ready to spring up at any moment.

They were of the same kind, all hunters, all possessing innate pride.

But at this moment, they were all just lambs awaiting slaughter.

The metal door emitted a harsh sound.

G-891 raised his head, orange-yellow eyes flashing with angry light. His body instinctively tensed, heart beginning to accelerate, like a war drum thundering in his chest.

That door was opening, light pouring in, like a sharp sword piercing through the darkness.

**Scene-2 The Bear's Remnant**

The door opened.

Two staff members walked in, dragging a limp body.

It was G-556, that brown bear. He had already "performed" and was now back alive—or rather, once alive. His body was limp, like a collapsed mountain, white traces still hanging from the corner of his mouth, lower body a mess.

The staff members pushed the bear into the corner, then turned toward G-891.

"Your turn," one of them said, "Mr. Tiger."

G-891 stared at that bear. In the dim light, he could see the expression on the bear's face—that strange peace, as if finally freed from some heavy burden. That expression made G-891 feel a wave of anger.

How could he be so calm?

How could he accept such a humiliating death?

If it were him, he would rather die in battle than die so humiliatingly.

"What are you looking at?" The staff member pushed him, "Hurry up."

G-891 was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along the white corridor. His legs were powerful, muscles tense; if not for being bound, he could easily break free from these two staff members. But now, he could only be dragged along, like a tamed beast.

That sense of powerlessness made him feel unprecedented anger.

On both sides of the corridor walls, there were faint traces—scratch marks, bloodstains, and that kind unable to be washed away, traces of despair. G-891's gaze swept over those traces, a strange resonance welling up in his heart.

How many hunters had walked this path?

How many tiger-kind like him, had once thought their strength and pride could change fate, ultimately could only become "auction items" in this underground auction house?

His gaze fell on one of the traces—a deep scratch mark, like something with sharp claws had left it. Tiger? Leopard? Or wolf? He couldn't know. That scratch mark was like a silent accusation, a story that could never be told.

"Tiger-kind is trouble," the staff member on the left muttered, "strong, and even worse temper. Last time that tiger-kind, almost broke the frame struggling."

"Don't worry," the one on the right said, "no matter how strong, on stage they'll soften up."

G-891 emitted a low growl, a threatening sound from deep in his throat. But that sound was blocked by the rubber ball, turned into vague whimpering.

"Save it," the staff member patted his face, "on stage, you'll have plenty of time to make noise."

That contemptuous tone made G-891's blood boil. He wanted to tear these people apart, wanted to let them know the power of tiger-kind—but he couldn't do it.

The restraint strap was too tight, the mouth-sealing device too deep, and he could only be dragged along.

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes.

The staff members stopped, adjusted their posture, then pushed open the door.

Light.

Sound.

Smell.

Everything rushed in at once, like a heavy punch hitting G-891's senses.

He was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining directly down, illuminating his body with nowhere to hide. That strong light made him squint, adapting to the sudden brightness.

Then, he saw the surrounding environment clearly.

The audience. Hundreds upon hundreds of gazes fell on him.

Those gazes held curiosity, contempt, anticipation, and that hunting excitement which made him want to vomit.

He raised his head, facing those gazes.

In his heart, there was only a burning anger.

He was a tiger, he shouldn't be treated like this.

**Scene-3 Display and Arrogance**

"Ladies and gentlemen!"

That voice rang out, resonant and cheerful, like an experienced variety show host.

"Today's third auction item is a real 'big shot'!"

The fox host walked out from the side of the stage, his white suit sparkling under the spotlight. His steps were light, face carrying a professional smile, gaze sweeping over the audience, finally landing on G-891.

"G-891, yellow tiger beastman, employed for one year!" The fox's voice was infectious, "One night ago, he left his post without permission to gamble, resulting in a gap in the defense line! What a spectacular 'hobby'!"

A burst of snickering came from the audience.

G-891 glared at the fox. His eyes were full of anger and contempt—even in this situation, he didn't want to let these people see his fear.

"Look at those eyes," the fox seemed amused by his gaze, "so proud! As expected of tiger-kind, refusing to bow even in death."

He walked to G-891's front, beginning to unbutton his uniform.

"Let's take a look at this 'proud tiger's' physical condition," the fox's palm pressed against G-891's chest, forcefully pressing.

That orange-yellow fur trembled slightly beneath the olive-green uniform, pectoral muscle lines clearly visible. Tiger muscles weren't wrapped in fat like bear-kind, but were tight and defined, every block full of power. The fox's fingers sank into that layer of muscle, feeling the power and temperature beneath.

"These pectorals," the fox exclaimed, "firm, full, full of explosive power. Tiger-kind pectorals are truly beautiful, every block like it was carved out."

His fingers slid across that layer of muscle, from collarbone all the way to sternum, feeling the texture of every muscle. G-891 could clearly feel every detail of that touch—the fox's fingers first touching fur, then pressing into muscle, that transition from surface to depth made his skin break out in goosebumps.

"Look here," the fox's fingers stopped above his right chest, where there was a shallow scar, "what's this? From fighting?"

He pressed down hard.

G-891 emitted a muffled hum, filtered by the mouth-sealing device into a low whimper. That scar was from when he was twenty, fighting another tiger for territory. At that time the wound was deep enough to see bone, he thought he was going to die, but finally survived, leaving this scar.

"Hurt a lot, right?" the fox said in a low voice, "this is the price of arrogance."

His hand continued to move, coming to the abdomen.

G-891's abdomen was a typical tiger-kind physique—tight, flat, eight abdominal muscles clearly visible. That layer of orange-yellow short fur covered the muscles, feeling rough but full of texture. The fox's palm covered it, feeling the hardness and temperature of that muscle layer.

"These abs," the fox patted them, "hard as a board, like a shield. Eight blocks, clearly visible. This physique in the arena is an existence that makes people tremble with fear."

His fingers drew circles around the navel, occasionally pressing hard.

G-891's body fiercely trembled. That was one of his most sensitive areas, when suddenly pressed, a strange sensation spread throughout his body.

"Very sensitive here," the fox smiled, "looks like our Mr. Tiger isn't made of iron after all."

Laughter came from the audience.

G-891's eyes burned hot. He felt angry, felt humiliated, but he clenched his teeth, not letting himself show any weak expression.

He was a tiger, he wouldn't beg for mercy.

"Turn around," the fox commanded.

G-891 was forcibly turned around, back facing the audience.

"Look at this back," the fox's palm slid across his spine, from shoulders all the way to waist, "broad shoulders, firm back muscles. Tiger-kind's back lines are truly beautiful, like a cheetah preparing to pounce."

His fingers stopped at the scapula, forcefully pressing.

G-891 emitted a muffled hum. That touch brought not only pain, but also a strange sense of shame.

"There's a scar here," the fox's fingers stopped below his left scapula, where there was a long scar, "what's this? Cut by a knife?"

He pressed down hard.

G-891's body trembled violently. That scar was left by an enemy during a mission, that wound almost ran through his entire scapula at the time. That was an injury he suffered to prove his bravery, a price paid to maintain tiger-kind dignity.

"A medal of bravery?" the fox said with a smile, "too bad, these medals can't save your life."

His hand continued downward, coming to the buttocks.

"Buttocks are even tighter," the fox slapped those two firm mounds of flesh, "no excess fat, all muscle. This physique in the arena is a hot commodity."

His hand continued downward, coming to the thighs.

Tiger-kind thighs were long and powerful, quadriceps and biceps femoris lines clearly defined, like they were carved out. The fox's hands simultaneously gripped his thighs, kneading from the base all the way to the knees, feeling the hardness and power of that muscle layer.

"These legs," the fox exclaimed, "long and powerful, full of explosive power. Run fast, jump high—too bad, ran in the wrong direction."

More laughter.

G-891 closed his eyes. He thought of that casino, thought of those chips, thought of the excitement when winning—then the alarm sound, then panic, then now.

That was his fault.

But he wouldn't admit it.

At least, not in front of these people.

"Finally, let's look at this fur," the fox's hand returned to his chest, grabbing a handful of orange-yellow chest fur, "yellow tiger fur, bright and dazzling, feels rough and powerful. This texture is simply a work of art by nature!"

He let go, turning to the audience.

"Ladies and gentlemen! Such a 'proud work of art,' starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

"1 RUB!" the fox repeated, "anyone want this 'gambling expert'?"

No one raised their hand.

G-891 knew no one would. But he didn't care. He didn't need others to buy him, he—

"Looks like no one wants it," the fox's voice carried mockery, "tiger pride, not worth a cent."

G-891 felt a sharp pain. That sentence was like a knife, stabbing into his self-esteem.

"3! 2! 1!" the fox counted down, "anyone bidding?"

No one.

"Alright—unsold!"

That word fell, and G-891 felt a wave of dizziness.

Finally, it came.

### Story Reflections

This chapter is a story about "arrogance."

Lei Lie's tragedy lies not in his unauthorized departure, but in his arrogance making him unable to face his own weakness.

He tried to arm himself with anger and dignity, but the body's instincts ultimately defeated him. That process of being forced to collapse was more cruel than death itself—because it deprived a person of their self-identity.

Lei Lie's final tears were not because of death, but because he finally realized—before absolute violence, he was no different from others.

Tiger-kind pride, before the system, was not worth a cent.

This is the most profound tragedy.

But perhaps, that final collapse was also a kind of relief.

Because he finally no longer had to pretend to be strong, could finally put down that heavy pride, and leave this world as an ordinary person.

That tear was a farewell to arrogance, and also an acceptance of self.

In this sense, Lei Lie's death was more complex than others' deaths—he lost not only life, but also that carefully constructed self-image.

This is the most profound tragedy, and also the most profound truth.
