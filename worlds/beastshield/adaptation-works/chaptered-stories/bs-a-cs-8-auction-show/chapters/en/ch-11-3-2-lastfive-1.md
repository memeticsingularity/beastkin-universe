## Act 1 The Leopard's Sensitivity

The black leopard was the sixth to awaken.

His consciousness slowly surfaced from the abyss of darkness, like a drowned corpse being pulled by some invisible force, struggling toward the surface of light. When his eyelids finally fluttered, amber pupils gradually focusing, the first thing he felt was a strange stinging—that sensitivity where every pore across his entire body was screaming.

This was leopard-kind's nature. Their nerve endings were denser than ordinary people's; every inch of skin could feel the slightest change in airflow. In the wild, this was an advantage, a hunting weapon. But here, in this sealed basement, it became the cruelest curse.

The man knelt at his side, his posture so reverent it was like worshipping a holy object. His fingers gently parted that short, dense black fur, movements so soft they were nearly pathological.

The texture of that fur was extraordinary—more delicate than silk, softer than velvet, yet carrying a wild roughness. Every hair was like a finely tuned sensor, transforming the slightest touch into intense signals, directly reaching the brain.

"Leopard-kind," the man's voice echoed in the basement, carrying a certain near-reverent emotion. "Your whole body is a sensitive point. Every inch of skin can feel the slightest touch. In the wild, this is your advantage, your hunting weapon. But here... this is your curse."

His finger lightly pressed against the black leopard's neck, feeling that pulsing pulse. "Let me see, a hunter whose whole body is a sensitive point—how much stimulation can he endure?"

The syringe pierced the blood vessel, cold liquid flowing in.

A few seconds later, the black leopard's eyes snapped open. Those amber pupils flickered with alert light in the dim basement, like two lamps burning in the darkness. His body instinctively wanted to spring up, like a cheetah preparing to pounce, but paralytic bonds made him only able to tremble slightly, like a trapped moth.

"Where... where is this..." His voice was hoarse and alert, carrying feline-kind's unique hissing sound.

"Your terminus," the man's hand had already begun wandering across his body, sliding from the side of his neck toward his chest. "Also the final stage of your life. Here, you will display leopard-kind's most sensitive side."

"Let me go!" The black leopard struggled, but that lean body completely refused to obey, only able to make weak tremors. "You madman! Pervert!"

"Madman?" The man laughed, his fingers drawing circles on that tight pectoral. "Perhaps. But I am a madman who knows how to appreciate beauty. And your body... too beautiful. This streamlined physique, this lean muscle, this... sensitivity."

His fingers slid across the black leopard's chest and abdomen, from collarbone all the way downward, passing those six distinct abdominal muscles, lingering around the navel. Every touch made the black leopard's body tremble violently, not pleasure, but pain brought by excessive sensitivity, like countless needles pricking, like electric current running wild through his body.

"Don't... too sensitive... can't endure..." The black leopard's voice already carried a crying tone, that mask of the proud hunter shattering. "Will die... really will die..."

"You can endure," the man's voice was like the temptation of a demon, deep and fatal. "Your body was born for this. Every nerve ending is screaming. Every touch is flame. Let me see, how long can this sensitivity make you last?"

He began to move.

For the black leopard, he didn't need to slowly build up. That body had already been pushed to the limit by drugs and innate sensitivity. His fingers wandered across the black leopard's body, from behind the ear to the side of the neck, from chest to abdomen, from inner thighs to the most private parts.

Every touch brought a bout of violent trembling, a suppressed whimper, a bodily spasm. The black leopard's tears began to flow; that excessive stimulation had already surpassed the category of pleasure, becoming a pure torture.

"The first time."

The wave came fast and fierce, the black leopard's body arched, like a fully drawn bow, a wail mixed with pain and humiliation squeezed from his throat. His body released in violent spasms, but that release brought not relief, but deeper despair.

But the man did not stop.

"The second time."

"Please... too intense... will die... really can't endure..." The black leopard was already bathed in tears, that lean body trembling violently under overstimulation, like a leaf in the wind.

"The third time."

"The fourth time."

Every wave was stronger than the last, more unbearable. The black leopard's nerves were already at the edge of collapse, that whole-body sensitivity characteristic making him with nowhere to escape, nowhere to hide, only able to passively accept the torture brought by every touch.

"The fifth time."

The black leopard's body had already begun to collapse, his heart beating irregularly in violent spasms, like a lamp about to be extinguished. Tears flowed down his cheeks, those amber eyes filled with despair and pleading.

"The sixth time."

When the final wave struck, the black leopard's body had completely collapsed. His heart stopped beating in violent spasms, those amber eyes still open, but pupils dilated, losing all luster.

The man gently closed his eyes.

"The sixth." He walked toward the next prey.

## Act 2 The Boar's Silence

The wild boar was the seventh to awaken.

When he awoke, there was no fear in his eyes, only a near-numb calm. Those small eyes carried a kind of seen-it-all indifference, as if he had long anticipated such an ending.

"You're the buyer," his voice was rough and deep, as if squeezed out from deep within his throat. "The one who bought us at the auction."

"Yes," the man began undoing his uniform, movements mechanical and efficient. "I spent ten gold coins to buy all of you."

"For what? To torture us like this now, then kill us?"

"For this," the man's hand began to move. "To feel your lives draining from your bodies. To collect your final trembling. To... possess you."

The wild boar did not struggle, did not plead. He just lay there, those small eyes carrying a near-indifferent scrutiny, watching the man busying himself upon his body, like watching a performance unrelated to himself.

"You're not afraid?" The man was somewhat surprised, his hand movements pausing slightly.

"What's the use of being afraid?" The wild boar's voice was flat, without any fluctuation. "Dead either way. Dying at the auction house watched by those spectators, or dying here tortured by you alone—what's the difference?"

"At least you can decide how to die. Struggle, or accept."

"I choose silence." The wild boar closed his eyes. "Hurry up and finish it. I'm tired."

The man began to move, mechanical and efficient. His hand wandered across the wild boar's squat body, from thick pectorals to round abdomen, from thick thighs to the most private parts.

The wild boar's body reacted very slowly, but each wave was substantial. That squat body was like an old machine, mechanically responding under that forced stimulation, without any emotional fluctuation.

"The first time."

The wild boar's body trembled slightly, emitting a deep moan, but quickly restored calm.

"The second time."

"The third time."

The wild boar just lay there, eyes fixed on the ceiling, as if thinking about something, or as if thinking about nothing. There was no expression on his face, only a deep exhaustion, the mark left by long-term greed and betrayal.

"The fourth time."

"The fifth time."

"The sixth time."

When the final wave struck, the wild boar's heart stopped beating. There was no fear on his face, no pain, only a relieved peace, like finally freed from some heavy burden, like finally having paid all debts.

The man stood up, looking at those still-open small eyes.

"The seventh." He walked toward the next prey.

## Act 3 The Deer's Gentleness

The white deer was the eighth to awaken.

His white fur shimmered with soft luster under the dim yellow light, like a carefully carved jade statue, like a white lotus blooming in the darkness. Even in the direst straits, his gaze was still calm, carrying a near-compassionate gentleness, as if he had already seen through life and death, transcended fear.

"You're the one who bought us," his voice was soft and gentle, like a breeze brushing across the lake surface. "You're going to kill us, right?"

"I want to feel you die," the man's hand had already begun wandering across his body, caressing that pure white fur. "I want to collect your final trembling, your final sigh, your final... peace."

"The others? Those bought with me?"

"Already gone. You're the eighth."

"I understand," the white deer closed his eyes, a smile even appearing at the corner of his mouth, that smile carrying no resentment, only understanding. "Then let me join them. Let me walk with them toward that unknown world."

"You're not afraid?" The man was somewhat surprised by his calm.

"Afraid," the white deer opened his eyes, those brown pupils flickering with a certain light transcending fear. "But what I fear more is dying with resentment filling my heart. I released those civilians; I chose mercy, even though that violated my duty. I don't regret it. So I hope that in death, my heart can also maintain that mercy."

The man began to move; this time, his movements were surprisingly gentle, like treating a fragile work of art.

"You released civilians," he murmured. "You defied orders, chose mercy. You knew that would cost you your life, but you still did it."

"Yes," the white deer's voice was like a sigh. "I just... didn't want to see innocent people die. Even if that meant I had to bear all the blame."

"You are kind to the point of foolishness."

"Perhaps. But this is the path I chose."

The first wave came gentle and slow, like a light breeze brushing across the sea surface, like a mother's gentle palm. The white deer's body trembled slightly, a near-satisfied low moan squeezed from his throat, that release bringing not shame, but a strange serenity.

"The first time."

"Like waves," the white deer murmured, voice dreamlike. "Like the ocean. Like the lake of my hometown when I was young, peaceful, gentle, embracing everything."

"The second time."

"The third time."

Every wave came gentle and deep; there was no pain on the white deer's face, only a near-transcendent peace. His white fur shimmered with soft luster under the light, like a holy statue.

"The fourth time."

"Thank you," the white deer smiled and said, voice growing lighter and lighter. "For letting me feel such peace before death. Thank you for making my final moments not filled with fear, but filled with... tranquility."

"The fifth time."

When the final wave struck, the white deer's eyes were still open, those brown pupils flickering with a certain light transcending life and death. The corner of his mouth still carried a smile, like finally returning to a mother's embrace, like finally returning to that distant hometown.

The man stood up, looking at that serene face, a strange emotion welling up in his heart.

"The eighth." He walked toward the next prey.

## Act 4 The Lion's Dignity

The male lion was the ninth to awaken.

Even bound, even paralyzed, the moment he opened his eyes, the entire basement seemed to be enveloped by a majestic aura. Those amber eyes burned with the flame of a king, golden mane shimmering like burning flames under the dim yellow light, emitting an inviolable dignity.

"Release me!" His voice was like thunder, echoing in the basement, shaking the walls into slight trembling. "Fight me fair and square! Let me die with dignity! Let me die like a warrior, not like livestock awaiting slaughter!"

The man laughed, that smile carrying genuine appreciation, even a trace of awe.

"Lion-kind's dignity, unwilling to lower its head even in the direst straits. I admire you, truly." His fingers slid across the lion-kind's broad pectorals, feeling that rock-like hardness. "But pride cannot save you. Here, you are just prey. Your dignity, your glory—all will flow away with your life."

"Coward!" The male lion roared, muscles tensing, trying to break free of the paralysis, but the drug's power was too strong; he could only tremble slightly. "If you have the guts, let me go! Let me see what you've got!"

"No," the man began undoing his uniform, revealing that sculpted body. "But I promise, you will die with dignity. At least, more dignified than the others."

He began to move.

The lion-kind's reaction was the strongest, and also the most resistant. Even with his body trembling under that forced stimulation, even with wave after wave striking, the male lion's gaze was still firm, still burning with unyielding flame. He clenched his jaw, refusing to make any sound, refusing to show any expression of weakness before his enemy.

"The first time."

The male lion's body arched, a suppressed low growl squeezed from his throat, but he quickly bit his lip, swallowing the remaining whimper. His golden mane was soaked with sweat, clinging to his neck, but his gaze was still proud.

"Very enduring," the man praised. "But how many times can you endure?"

"The second time."

"The third time."

Every wave was stronger than the last, but the male lion still refused to yield. His body trembled, muscles spasmed, sweat soaking his golden mane, but his gaze still burned with the flame of a king.

"The fourth time."

"You... will not... get... my dignity..." The male lion's voice was broken and weak, but still carried the pride of a king. "I... am a lion... not... livestock..."

"The fifth time."

"The sixth time."

"The seventh time."

By the seventh time, the male lion's body had already begun to collapse, his heart beating irregularly in violent spasms, but his gaze was still firm, still burning with unyielding flame.

"The eighth time."

When the final wave struck, the male lion's eyes were still open, those amber pupils still burning with the dignity of a king, even though life had already left him. His body lay limp on the floor, but that majestic aura still remained, like a king who could never be conquered.

The man stood up, bowing to that body in respect.

"The ninth." He walked toward the last prey.

## Act 5 The Fox's Punishment

The red fox was the last to awaken.

When he awoke, he found himself placed in the center of the basement, surrounded by a circle of already cold bodies. Wolf, bear, tiger, ox, dog, leopard, boar, deer, lion. Nine companions he had once betrayed, now all dead, their eyes mostly open, as if accusing him of his crimes.

"See?" The man's voice rang out beside his ear, warm breath spraying upon his auricle. "They all died because of you. If you hadn't informed on that escape plan at the last moment, they might still be alive."

"No..." The red fox's voice trembled, tears gushing out. "I didn't kill them... Beastshield did... this system did..."

"You betrayed them," the man's fingers slid across the red fox's cheeks, carrying a certain near-cruel tenderness. "You reported the escape plan to the company, thinking you could buy your own safety. You thought you could be saved, but you were wrong. I bought all of you, just for this moment."

"Please..." The red fox's tears flowed down his cheeks, those red eyes filled with remorse and despair. "I'm sorry... I'm truly sorry... I know I was wrong... let me die... let me join them..."

"Sorry?" The man laughed loudly, that laughter echoing in the basement, like mockery from hell. "Sorry won't bring them back. Sorry won't erase your betrayal. But do you know what might make you feel better?"

He bent down, murmuring in the red fox's ear, voice like a whisper from hell:

"Pain."

He began to move.

This time, he used a full two hours. Again and again he pushed the red fox to the edge of his limit, then stopped, letting him fall, then pushing him up again. Over and over, making the red fox wander between hope and despair, struggling at the edge of life and death.

"Please... let me die... let me join them..." The red fox had completely collapsed, voice hoarse, gaze unfocused.

"Not yet," the man's voice was like the temptation of a demon. "First, you must feel everything they felt. All the fear, all the helplessness, all the humiliation. You must look at them, remember them, then die carrying this memory."

"The first time."

The red fox's body trembled violently, a heart-rending wail squeezed from his throat, that pain surpassing the physical level, reaching directly to the soul.

"The second time."

"The third time."

Every wave was accompanied by intense pain, because of excessive stimulation, because of excessive sensitivity, because the body had already been emptied, because the mind had already been destroyed.

"The fourth time."

"The fifth time."

"The sixth time."

When the final wave struck, the red fox's heart finally stopped beating. His eyes were still open, looking at that circle of former companions, looking at the tragedy he himself had caused, that remorse forever frozen upon his face.

The man stood up, looking at the ten bodies, a satisfied smile appearing on his face.

"Perfection." He walked toward the telephone. "Time to clean up."

## Act 6 Cleanup

The garbage truck arrived ten minutes later.

Two workers in gray uniforms walked into the basement, their faces expressionless, as if they had never seen such a scene, or as if they had seen too much and were already numb. They skillfully loaded the ten bodies into black bags, movements mechanical and efficient, without pity, without pause, like moving cargo, not handling lives that once were.

"Another ten," one worker said, voice flat. "This guest has been buying a lot lately."

"He's efficient," the other worker replied. "Not like some guests who torture for several days. He can finish processing in one day."

"A bit of a pity, these goods are not bad quality. That lion, that tiger—if they could be kept..."

"Not our place to judge," the first worker interrupted him. "Our job is cleanup, not evaluation. Clean up and leave."

They worked in silence, loading the bags onto carts, pushing them out the door. The bags made rustling sounds scraping against the floor, like some final sigh.

The man stood at the doorway, watching the truck disappear into the night. That vehicle's taillights flickered in the darkness, like the eyes of some monster, finally completely vanishing into the city's light and shadow.

"Until next time," he murmured, then turned and disappeared into the darkness, as if he had never appeared.

Only cold air remained in the basement, and that lingering scent of death. That scent would stay here for a long time, until the next cleanup, the next batch of prey, the next tragedy.

And in some corner of the city, new prey was being selected, new tragedy was brewing, new cycle about to begin.
