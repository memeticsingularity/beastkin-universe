**Scene-1 Suffocation in the Waiting Area**

**Scene-2 Eye Contact with the Wolf**

Time became distorted under these circumstances.

G-556 didn't know how long he had waited. Perhaps ten minutes, perhaps an hour, perhaps eternity. His thoughts began to drift, returning to that night three days ago.

He remembered that feeling of exhaustion—not ordinary sleepiness, but weariness penetrating deep into the marrow. Three consecutive night shifts, twelve hours each, standing at the warehouse door like a sculpture maintaining vigilance. His eyes were as dry as if ground by sandpaper, his legs as heavy as if filled with lead.

The first night shift, he stood at the warehouse door the entire night, vigilantly watching every passerby. The night wind was cold, blowing through his brown long fur, but he didn't dare leave his post to get a coat.

The second night shift, he began to feel drowsy, using willpower to force himself to stay awake. He marched in place, pinched his thigh with his fingers, even bit through his own tongue tip. The smell of blood permeated his mouth, bringing brief clarity.

The third night shift, exhaustion flooded over him like a tide. He leaned against the wall, closed his eyes, wanting to rest for a while—just a few minutes, just a few minutes.

Then, he fell asleep.

That sleep came too fast, too deep, too heavy. He was like falling into a bottomless abyss, everything around becoming distant and blurred. No dreams, no consciousness, only that void of being completely conquered by exhaustion.

When he woke up, the sun had already risen, the warehouse door pried open, inside completely empty.

At that moment, he knew he was finished.

"Wu—" A weak whimper interrupted his memories.

G-556 turned his head, looking at that dying wolf.

G-724 had his eyes open in the darkness, gray pupils already dilated, but still gazing in G-556's direction. His mouth was slightly open, the corner of his mouth stretched by the mouth-sealing device flowing out more saliva, mixed with blood threads.

They made eye contact in the darkness.

That wolf's eyes no longer held fear, only a deep exhaustion and relief. He knew death was approaching, and he was already prepared.

G-556 saw his own reflection in those eyes—equally exhausted, equally desperate, equally waiting for that inevitable endpoint.

They were the same.

All sacrifices of the system, all abandoned by fate, all prisoners waiting for death in the darkness.

"Wu—" The wolf whimpered again, this time lighter, weaker.

That sound was like a farewell, and also like a blessing. Then, his eyes slowly closed, breathing becoming even weaker, almost inaudible.

G-556 knew that wolf was leaving. His semen had been completely drained, life force flowing away with those white liquids. In a few minutes, perhaps seconds, he would completely stop breathing.

"Go well, brother," G-556 said silently in his heart, "may the starlight of the northern forest illuminate your journey home."

**Scene-3 The Weight Before Going On Stage**

The metal door made a sound again.

This time, it was G-556's number being called.

"G-556," that voice was calm and indifferent, like a piece of ice falling on a hot iron plate, "your turn."

Two people walked in, one on each side holding up his arms. Their hands were strong, carrying calluses from years of holding weapons, but for bear-kind's weight, still seemed somewhat strained.

"So heavy," the one on the left muttered, "bear-kind is trouble. This build, can't even drag it."

"Less nonsense," the one on the right said, "hurry up, don't make the audience wait."

G-556 was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along the white corridor. His legs went soft, but not from fear—he had already accepted his fate. He only wanted all this to end quickly, not to be tortured for too long like that wolf.

On both sides of the corridor walls, there were faint traces—perhaps scratch marks left by previous auction items, or bloodstains that still remained after countless cleanings. G-556's gaze swept over those traces, a strange resonance welling up in his heart.

How many people had walked this path?

How many G-rank grunts like him, had once thought their strength could change fate, ultimately could only become "auction items" in this underground auction house?

His gaze fell on one of the traces—a deep scratch mark, like something with sharp claws had left it. Tiger? Leopard? Or wolf? He couldn't know. That scratch mark was like a silent accusation, a story that could never be told.

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes.

The staff members stopped, adjusted their posture, then pushed open the door.

Light.

Smell.

Everything rushed in at once, like a heavy punch hitting G-556's senses.

He was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining directly down, illuminating his body with nowhere to hide. That strong light made him almost unable to open his eyes, only able to squint, adapting to the sudden brightness.

Then, he saw the surrounding environment clearly.

The audience. Hundreds upon hundreds of gazes fell on him.

Those gazes held curiosity, contempt, anticipation, and that hunting excitement which made him want to vomit.

He raised his head, facing those gazes.

In his heart, there was only a heavy calm.

Finally, it came.

**Scene-4 Display and Humiliation**

"Ladies and gentlemen!"

"Welcome back to the auction! Today's second auction item is a real 'heavyweight' guest!"

G-556 lowered his head. Shame flooded over him like a tide. He would rather they said he failed in battle, said he was cowardly and fled, than let them know—he fell asleep.

That was the symbol of incompetence, the evidence of laziness, the unforgivable negligence.

### Story Reflections

This chapter is a story about "responsibility."

Tieshan's tragedy lies not in his dereliction, but in his self-blame.

He was the kind of person who would take all faults upon himself. Even if those three consecutive night shifts were unreasonable work arrangements, even if anyone in his position might have fallen asleep, he still believed it was his fault.

This excessive sense of responsibility made him unable to forgive himself.

On the auction block, he had no fear, no anger, only calm acceptance.

Because he believed that was the punishment he deserved.

But this thought itself was the victim of institutional violence.

Beast Shield exploited this sense of responsibility, turning an exhausted worker into a "derelict sinner," then executing him in broad daylight.

Tieshan's calm was not relief, but the performance of being domesticated.

Until death, he believed his life was not worth the 50,000 RUB of supplies.

This is the most profound tragedy.
