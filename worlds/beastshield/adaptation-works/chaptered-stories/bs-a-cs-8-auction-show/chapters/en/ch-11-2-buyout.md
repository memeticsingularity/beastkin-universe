**Scene-1 The Agony of Waiting**

The air of the underground auction house was like an overcooked stew—thick, heavy, carrying a certain indescribable sweet, cloying scent.

The audience seats were packed. They wore gorgeous clothing, expensive accessories, held goblets filled with amber liquid, conversed in low voices, waiting for tonight's performance to begin.

No one noticed, in the corner of the last row of the auction hall, a figure sitting quietly.

He wore a dark gray hooded cloak, the hood pulled low, covering most of his face. His build was medium, neither tall nor short, neither fat nor thin, without any eye-catching features. He was like a drop of water merging into the ocean, a grain of sand falling into the desert, perfectly blending into the surrounding shadows.

He held no paddle in his hand, showed no signs of preparing to bid. He just sat quietly, as if waiting for something, as if observing something.

At the entrance of the auction hall, two black panther beastmen in black suits were checking admission tickets. Their eyes were sharp, muscles tense, like two statues ready to pounce at any moment. But even under their strict watch, they found nothing unusual about this gray figure.

He just existed, unnoticed by anyone.

"Ladies and gentlemen!"

The fox host's voice came from the stage, loud and cheerful, instantly drawing everyone's attention.

"Welcome to tonight's auction! I am your old friend, host Hong Yan!"

The audience burst into warm applause and whistles. The gray figure did not clap, only slightly raised his head, revealing a pair of eyes from beneath the hood's shadow.

What kind of eyes were those?

Amber-colored, deep, calm, like two bottomless pools of water, reflecting the dazzling stage lights, yet without any ripples.

"Tonight, we have ten carefully prepared 'auction items,' each with a unique story and 'value'!" The fox deliberately emphasized the word "value," drawing meaningful laughter from the audience, "let's begin!"

The spotlight moved, shining on the side door of the stage. Two staff members carried out a figure.

That was a gray wolf beastman, hands tied behind his back, mouth stuffed with a gag, gray-white fur looking especially glaring under the strong light. His eyes were full of fear and despair, body trembling slightly, like a trapped beast.

"First auction item—G-724! Gray wolf beastman, employed for one year! Three days ago, he encountered an ambush during a mission, squad wiped out, and he—chose to flee!"

A burst of hissing and snickering came from the audience.

"Starting price—1 RUB!"

**Scene-2 The Wolf's Countdown**

"1 RUB! Anyone want this 'deserter'?"

The fox's voice echoed through the auction hall, but no one responded.

G-724 stood on the stage, the spotlight like a heavy punch to his body. He could feel hundreds upon thousands of gazes falling on him, those gazes holding curiosity, contempt, anticipation, and that nauseating excitement of hunters.

His body trembled, gray-white fur standing on end under the strong light. His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraint strap biting deep into the skin of his wrists, bringing waves of stinging pain. His mouth was stuffed with a rubber ball, only able to produce vague whimpers, that inability to speak more unbearable than pain.

"Deserter!" Someone shouted loudly, drawing more laughter.

G-724 lowered his head, shame flooding over him like a tide. He wanted to defend himself, wanted to tell them it wasn't cowardice but survival instinct, wanted to tell them he had tried his best—but he couldn't. He could only stand here, like a commodity being displayed, judged, fate being decided.

"Looks like no one wants him," the fox's voice carried a certain programmed regret, "a deserter's life isn't worth much."

"3!"

G-724 closed his eyes. He didn't want to look anymore, didn't want to face those indifferent gazes, didn't want to feel this humiliation of being displayed.

"2!"

The final images flashed in his mind—mother's smiling face, hometown's snowy plains, and that person he would never see again. Those memories were like distant starlight, flickering with the last warmth in the darkness.

Goodbye, world.

"1—"

A hand was raised.

From the back row of the auction hall, from the shadows.

The hall fell silent.

Everyone's gaze turned toward the source of the sound—the corner of the last row, a figure in a dark gray hooded cloak was slowly raising a hand.

That hand extended from the cloak's sleeve, pale, slender, without any jewelry, without any features. But it was this hand, at the critical moment, that broke the silence.

"1 RUB," that voice rang out again, calm and deep, as if coming from far away, "I buy him."

---

**Scene-3 The Hall Erupts**

The auction hall was in an uproar.

Everyone was whispering, discussing this suddenly appearing buyer. Someone stretched their neck trying to see his face clearly, but the hood's shadow covered his face, only that amber light flickering in the shadows could be seen.

The fox host's expression froze for an instant. His eyes slightly narrowed, obviously surprised by this unexpected event, but quickly restored his professional smile.

"Sir," the fox weighed his words, "are you sure you want to bid 1 RUB to buy this... deserter?"

"Sure," the gray figure's voice held no fluctuation, "1 RUB, now, sold."

His tone was not a question, but a statement. Not a request, but a decision.

The fox was silent for a second, then nodded.

"1 RUB first time!"

No one bid.

"1 RUB second time!"

Still no one bid. All gazes were focused on that gray figure, some curious, some confused, some dismissive.

"1 RUB third time! Sold!"

The gavel fell.

G-724 was bought.

At the starting price, 1 RUB, at the critical moment when the countdown reached "1," by that mysterious gray figure.

G-724 stood blankly on stage, gray-white pupils violently contracting. He didn't understand what was happening, didn't understand why this person wanted to save him, didn't understand why the paddle was raised only at the last moment.

But he was saved.

He wasn't unsold, wasn't executed, wasn't killed in that humiliating way. He was bought, at an almost施舍 price, by that mysterious buyer.

Staff members walked forward, untying the restraint straps on his wrists, removing the gag from his mouth. G-724 moved his sore jaw, took a deep breath, then looked at that gray figure.

That figure had already lowered his hand, re-merging into the shadows, as if he had never appeared.

But G-724 knew, he had seen. At the last moment, those amber eyes met his for an instant.

That instant, what did he see?

Sympathy? Indifference? Or some more complex, more elusive emotion?

He didn't know.

He only knew, he survived.

**Scene-4 The Bear's Countdown**

"Congratulations to this gentleman!" The fox's voice regained its loudness, but listening carefully, a trace of barely detectable tension could be felt, "let's continue! Second auction item—G-556!"

The spotlight moved, the brown bear beastman was brought on stage.

His build was庞大, height close to two meters, the olive-green uniform tight on his body, outlining the thick muscle contours. His hands were tied behind his back, brown eyes full of despair and resignation.

"G-556, brown bear beastman, employed for two years! Three nights ago, he fell asleep while guarding the warehouse, resulting in fifty thousand RUB worth of supplies being robbed!"

The fox began displaying the auction item, palm patting the bear-kind's thick pecs, exclaiming at the unique texture of fat-muscle composition. But G-556 just lowered his head, like a mountain about to collapse, heavy, silent, desperate.

"Starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

"1 RUB! Anyone want this 'sleep expert'?"

No one raised their hand.

G-556 closed his eyes. He remembered that night, remembered the comfortable temperature that made him fall into deep sleep, remembered the fear of discovering the door had been pried open when he woke up. That was his fault, he deserved punishment.

"3!"

His breathing became rapid, chest and belly起伏 under the uniform, forming waves of flesh. He could feel his own heartbeat—heavy, slow, like a war drum being struck, announcing some inevitable doom.

"2!"

He was ready. Ready to face that end, ready to be fixed to the metal frame, ready to ejaculate his last life essence under that irresistible stimulation.

"1—"

"I bid."

That calm voice rang out again.

The hall erupted in uproar.

Everyone's gaze turned toward the back row again—that gray figure was still sitting there, the hood covering his face, only that pale hand extending from the cloak, quietly raised.

"1 RUB," he said, "I buy him."

The fox's expression became more complex. He looked at that gray figure, eyes flashing with vigilance.

"Sir... you have already bought one auction item."

"So?" The gray figure's voice was still calm, "is there a rule in the rules limiting the quantity a buyer can purchase?"

The fox was silent.

Indeed, there was no such restriction in the rules. Because no one had ever thought that someone would want to buy all the auction items.

"...No," the fox finally said, "1 RUB first time!"

"1 RUB second time!"

"1 RUB third time! Sold!"

The gavel fell.

G-556 was bought.

The brown bear stood blankly on stage, brown eyes full of disbelief. He looked at that gray figure, lips trembling, as if wanting to say something, but no sound came from his throat.

He was saved.

At the price of 1 RUB, at the critical moment when the countdown reached "1," by that mysterious buyer.

**Scene-5 The Tiger's Countdown**

The third auction item was brought on stage.

G-891, yellow tiger beastman, orange fur covered with black stripes, even in this situation, his body still emitted a kind of primitive majesty. But at this moment, those amber eyes were full of anger and confusion—he didn't understand what was happening, didn't understand why the first two were bought, didn't understand who that mysterious buyer was.

"G-891, yellow tiger beastman, employed for one year! Left his post to gamble, resulting in a gap in the defense line!"

The fox displayed the tiger-kind's tight muscles, exclaiming at those sculpted abs. But this time, his movements were somewhat stiff, his eyes occasionally drifting toward that gray figure in the back row, as if waiting for something.

"Starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

"1 RUB! Anyone want this 'gambling expert'?"

No one raised their hand.

G-891 glared at the audience, tiger instincts making him want to roar, want to struggle, want to prove his dignity. But he couldn't. He was bound, gagged, displayed, fate being decided.

"3!"

His muscles tensed, like a fully drawn bow, ready to spring up at any moment.

"2!"

His breathing became rapid, orange fur shimmering with wild luster under the spotlight.

"1—"

"I bid."

That voice rang out again, calm and firm.

The hall was no longer in uproar, but some kind of indescribable silence. Everyone's gaze was focused on that gray figure, eyes full of shock, confusion, and a trace of barely detectable fear.

Who was this person?

Why did he want to buy all the auction items?

What did he want to do?

"1 RUB," the gray figure said, "I buy him."

The fox took a deep breath, then struck the gavel.

"Sold."

**Scene-6 The Remaining Seven**

The following auction became a strange ritual.

Fourth auction item—black ox G-445, the wronged honest man. Countdown to "1," "I bid." Sold.

Fifth auction item—yellow dog G-203, the nervous newcomer. Countdown to "1," "I bid." Sold.

Sixth auction item—black leopard G-612, the conceited pursuer. Countdown to "1," "I bid." Sold.

Seventh auction item—wild boar G-378, the greedy embezzler. Countdown to "1," "I bid." Sold.

Eighth auction item—white deer G-891, the kind releaser. Countdown to "1," "I bid." Sold.

Ninth auction item—male lion G-567, the protector who defied orders. Countdown to "1," "I bid." Sold.

Every time, it was at the critical moment. Every time, it was at the starting price of 1 RUB. Every time, it was that calm voice, that pale hand, those amber eyes.

Some in the audience began to leave. They felt this auction had lost its "fun"—no unsold items, no wheel, no exciting execution performance. These ten auction items were all sold at the lowest price, packed away by a mysterious buyer.

This didn't fit the rules.

But this completely fit the rules.

Because the rules didn't prohibit one person from buying all the auction items, the rules didn't prohibit transactions at the starting price, the rules didn't prohibit bidding at the last moment.

The tenth auction item was brought on stage.

Red fox G-734, the informer who betrayed his companions. His red fur was like a ball of burning flame under the spotlight, eyes full of fear and unease.

"G-734, red fox beastman, employed for one and a half years! The crime is attempting to deceive the company and then selling out companions to protect himself!"

The fox host's voice was already somewhat hoarse. He looked at that gray figure, eyes carrying a kind of indescribable complex emotion.

"Starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

Heavier than any previous silence.

"1 RUB! Anyone want this 'betrayer'?"

No one raised their hand.

G-734's body trembled. He looked at that gray figure, looked at those amber eyes, a kind of indescribable fear welling up in his heart.

Who was this person?

Why did he want to buy everyone?

What would he do to them?

"3!"

G-734 closed his eyes. He remembered those companions he had sold out, remembered that rainy night, remembered his choice.

"2!"

He regretted it. He truly regretted it. But regret was already too late.

"1—"

"I bid."

That voice rang out for the last time.

"1 RUB," the gray figure said, "I buy him."

The gavel fell.

"Sold."

**Scene-7 The Mysterious Buyer**

Ten auction items, all sold.

Ten people, all bought by that mysterious gray figure at the price of 1 RUB.

The auction hall fell into dead silence. The remaining audience looked at each other, not knowing how to react. Some wanted to leave, some wanted to ask, but no one dared to step forward to question that gray figure.

The fox host stood on stage, the gavel in his hand still trembling slightly. He looked at that gray figure, took a deep breath, then asked: "Sir... may I know who you are?"

The gray figure slowly stood up.

His movements were slow, steady, carrying an indescribable composure. The dark gray cloak gently swayed on him, the hood covering his face, only those amber eyes flickering in the shadows.

He walked toward the stage, every step like stepping on everyone's heartbeat.

The ten newly bought prisoners stood on one side of the stage, all gazes focused on him. Their eyes varied—gratitude, confusion, vigilance, fear.

The gray figure walked to their front, stopping.

He slowly raised his head, the face beneath the hood finally exposed under the lights.

What kind of face was that?

Ordinary.

Extremely ordinary.

Without any features, without any memorable points, like one of countless faces you would encounter on the street every day. He could be anyone, could come from anywhere, could have any past.

But his eyes.

Those amber eyes.

Deep, calm, like two bottomless pools of water, reflecting everything around, yet without any ripples.

"Who I am is not important," he said, voice deep and calm, "what's important is, you now belong to me."

He extended his hand, pale fingers slicing through the air, pointing at those ten people.

"From tonight, you are no longer Beastshield's G-rank grunts, no longer this auction house's 'auction items,' no longer consumables in anyone's eyes."

He paused, gaze sweeping across every person's face.

"You are mine."

This sentence sounded like a kind of possession, a kind of control, a kind of bondage transferred from one system to another. But in those amber eyes, those ten people saw something more complex.

Not pity.

Not ambition.

But some kind of indescribable... determination?

"Follow me," the gray figure turned, walking toward the auction hall's exit, "leave here."

The ten people looked at each other, then followed.

They didn't know who this mysterious buyer was, didn't know where he was taking them, didn't know what awaited them.

But they knew, they survived.

At the price of 1 RUB, at the critical moment when the countdown reached "1," saved by this mysterious person.

That was enough.

**Scene-8 Departure**

At the auction hall's exit, two black panther security guards blocked them.

"Sir," one of the guards said, "you need to handle handover procedures, sign ownership transfer documents..."

"Not necessary," the gray figure said calmly, "I've already paid."

"But the rules..."

"The rules are yours," the gray figure interrupted him, "not mine."

His voice was still calm, but in that instant, the two guards felt some kind of indescribable pressure. Those amber eyes swept over them, making them feel like they were being stared at by some beast, bodies stiffening.

"Step aside," the gray figure said, "or, become the eleventh auction item."

The guards looked at each other, then stepped aside.

The gray figure led the ten people out of the auction hall, proceeding along the long corridor. At the end of the corridor was a door, outside was the night street, free air, an unknown world.

The ten people followed behind the gray figure, walking silently.

G-724 looked at that back ahead, the dark gray cloak gently swaying in the night wind, like a flag, like a riddle.

"Where... are we going?" G-724 finally couldn't help asking.

The gray figure did not turn back.

He turned, walking toward that door.

The ten people looked at each other, then followed.

They didn't know who this mysterious buyer was, didn't know where he was taking them, didn't know what awaited them.

They only knew, they temporarily didn't have to die.

At the price of 1 RUB, at the critical moment when the countdown reached "1," bought by this mysterious person.

The door was pushed open. Outside was the night, the street, an unknown world.

The ten people followed that gray figure, disappearing into the darkness.

Where did they go?

No one knew.

Maybe a better place.

Maybe a worse place.

Maybe just another auction house, another system, another cage.

But at that critical moment, when the countdown reached "1," when the gavel was about to fall, someone raised his hand.

That's all.

## Story Commentary and Reflections

> *The following commentary is based on this chapter's story content, attempting to understand another possibility of this "what if" world.*

### Salvation at the Critical Moment

This chapter is completely different from the previous Chapter 11 (The Collector).

The collector Shuang Ya is dominant, explicit, using 5000 RUB to smash the system's rules. And this mysterious buyer is obscure, silent, using the starting price of 1 RUB, at the last moment, precisely saving each person.

He didn't come to break the rules—he came to use the rules.

The rules say "starting price 1 RUB," so he bids 1 RUB. The rules say "countdown three times," so he raises his paddle at the last time. The rules don't limit purchase quantity, so he buys all.

He used the system's rules to defeat the system itself.

### The Power of the Anonymous

This mysterious buyer has no name, no identity, no past.

He is like a ghost, a symbol, a personification of "possibility." He could be anyone—a former Beastshield executive, a resistance organization member, a wealthy philanthropist, or just a bystander who couldn't watch anymore.

It is this anonymity that gives his salvation a certain universal meaning.

He is not a specific savior, but proof that "someone can do this." In a world where a human life is only worth 1 RUB, there is still someone willing to pay that 1 RUB, at the last moment, to buy a human life.

That's enough.

### Unresolved Suspense

This chapter gives no answers.

Who is the buyer? Don't know.

Where is he taking these ten people? Don't know.

What awaits them? Don't know.

But this is precisely the charm of this chapter—it doesn't offer cheap hope, doesn't promise a beautiful future, only says: they survived.

The rest is another story.

Maybe they will become resistance fighters, maybe they will build a new home, maybe they will disperse to all corners of the world, each starting a new life.

Maybe, that mysterious buyer is just part of a larger conspiracy, and these ten people just went from a small cage to a bigger cage.

No one knows.

But at that critical moment, when the countdown reached "1," when the gavel was about to fall, someone raised his hand.

That's enough.

That's enough.
