## Act 6 The Struggle

G-724 awoke to a scent.

Not the sweetness of gas, but another scent—a mixture of sweat, fear, and some decaying fishy-sweetness. That scent drilled into his nostrils, activating some alarm buried deep in his genes.

He remembered.

Remembered that night. Remembered that motorcycle. Remembered that dragon beastman's golden eyes. Remembered when he turned and fled, the three dull thuds from behind—the sounds of his teammates falling.

"You fled."

That voice did not come from without, but from within his own mind. That was the interrogator's voice, the voice of judgment, the voice of fate.

"Your life, disposed of by the company."

And now...

He could feel a hand wandering across his body. That touch was very light, like caressing, or like assessing the value of a commodity. That finger slid from the side of his neck toward his chest, lingering there for a few seconds, feeling the rhythm of his heartbeat.

"Wolf-kind," a voice said, very close, carrying a certain pathological interest. "I remember you. G-724. The deserter."

G-724 wanted to open his eyes, but his eyelids were as heavy as if glued shut. He could only emit a hoarse voice: "Please... spare me..."

"Spare you?" That voice laughed. "Just like you spared your teammates?"

That sentence was like a knife, piercing his heart.

That hand began to move. Not rough, but gentle, almost carrying a certain pity. That gentleness made him more fearful than any violence—because it meant the other knew he was afraid, knew he was in pain, yet still chose to continue.

"Your body is very sensitive," that voice continued, fingers threading through that layer of gray-white fur. "Wolf-kind fur is coarse, but the skin is very delicate. That contrast... is quite interesting."

G-724 felt his body betraying him.

Those forced waves surged one after another, washing away his reason, washing away his dignity. He tried to resist, tried to think of other things—the moonlight of that night, the fresh air of the forest, mother's warm embrace—but that stimulation was too intense, too intense to ignore.

"Please... spare me..." His voice trembled, carrying a crying tone.

"Beg me?" The man laughed. "Fine, beg me. Let me hear your pleading."

He began to weep, truly weep, tears blurring his vision. His body was emptied again and again, each time weaker than the last, more painful.

But the man was not impatient. He savored this tug-of-war—the prey's resistance, the prey's shame, the prey's tearing between physiology and psychology.

"How long can you endure in this situation?" The man asked curiously. "One minute? Two minutes?"

G-724's body began to tremble violently. His breathing grew rapid, his chest heaving violently, gray-white fur soaked with sweat.

When the third wave struck, he no longer had any strength to resist.

But this time, the man stopped his movements.

He bent down, murmuring in G-724's ear: "Do you know why you're here?"

G-724 could not answer.

"Because you fled." That voice was like coming from the depths of hell. "You escaped that dragon beastman's blade, but you can't escape my palm. You came back alive, thinking you could be saved—but you only delayed death."

The man's fingers began to move again, but this time more slowly, more torturously.

"I want you to remember this feeling," he murmured. "Remember how your body betrayed you. Remember how your dignity shattered piece by piece. Remember... you can never escape."

When the fourth wave struck, G-724's eyes rolled back.

His body lay limp on the floor, eyes open, but pupils dilated. Those gray pupils' last flash was the moonlight of that night—so bright, so cold, like some freedom forever out of reach.

The man checked his pulse, then stood up.

That body that had once struggled so fiercely was now completely relaxed. The basement air was permeated with a mixture of sweat and despair.

## Act 7 The Indifference

G-378 awoke with no fear in his eyes.

Only a near-numb calm. Those small eyes carried a kind of seen-it-all indifference, as if he had long anticipated such an ending.

"You're the buyer," his voice was rough and deep. "You bought us."

"Yes," the man began undoing his uniform. "I bought you. All of you."

"For what?"

"For this," the man's hand began to move. "To feel your lives draining from your bodies."

The wild boar did not struggle, did not plead. He just lay there, those small eyes carrying a near-indifferent scrutiny, watching the man busying himself upon his body.

"You're not afraid?" The man was somewhat surprised.

"What's the use of being afraid?" The wild boar's voice was flat. "Dead either way."

"At least you can decide how to die. Struggle, or accept."

"I choose silence."

The man began to move, mechanical and efficient. But this time, he adopted a completely different approach—indifference.

His fingers wandered across the wild boar's body, but without appreciation, without marvel, just completing a task. That indifference was more terrible than torture—being ignored, being treated as a lifeless object.

"You're not worth my spending too much energy on," the man said coldly. "You're just a greedy traitor."

The wild boar's body responded mechanically, but the man had no emotional fluctuation. He didn't prolong foreplay, didn't play with edging, just quickly brought the wild boar to release, then started again.

Again and again, mechanically repeating.

The wild boar felt a strange emptiness—not from the body, but from the spirit. He found himself craving attention, craving to be treated as a valuable prey, not abandoned garbage.

"Look at me," the wild boar suddenly said. "At least look at me."

The man stopped his movements, looking into the wild boar's eyes.

"You're begging me to pay attention to you?" The man laughed. "Why? Didn't you not care?"

"I..." The wild boar fell silent.

The man continued his indifference. He used the fastest way to empty the wild boar, again and again, giving no chance to catch his breath.

When the sixth wave struck, the wild boar's heart stopped beating. There was no fear on his face, no pain, only a strange emptiness—he finally got the ending he wanted, yet discovered he had never truly lived.

The man swept one last glance over the wild boar's hollow pupils, his footsteps carrying weariness when he turned.

## Act 8 The Gentleness

G-891 awoke with a gaze as calm as a deep pool.

"You know what I'm going to do?" The man asked.

"I know," the white deer's voice was soft. "You're going to kill me."

"You're not afraid?"

"Afraid. But I hope to die more peacefully."

This time, the man adopted the gentlest approach. He gently undid the white deer's uniform, movements so soft they were like treating a fragile dream.

"You released civilians," he murmured. "You chose mercy."

"Yes."

"Then, I shall give you mercy too."

His fingers wandered across the white deer's body, but not stimulation, rather comfort. He used his warm palm to cover the white deer's chest, feeling that calm heartbeat. He used his lips to lightly kiss the white deer's forehead, as if giving blessing.

"Let me help you," the man murmured. "Let you depart in peace."

He began to move, but the rhythm was the most soothing, like playing a gentle lullaby. The white deer's body slowly relaxed under that gentle touch, that release not forced, but guided, like drifting with the current.

"Like wind," he murmured. "Like clouds."

The man continued his gentleness. He used essential oil to coat the white deer's body, using warm palms to massage every inch of skin, letting that release come slow and deep.

Every wave came gentle and slow, like a light breeze brushing across the sea surface, like a mother's gentle palm.

"Thank you," the white deer smiled and said, voice growing lighter and lighter. "For letting me feel such peace before death."

When the final wave struck, the white deer's eyes were still open, those brown pupils flickering with a certain light transcending life and death. The corner of his mouth still carried a smile, like finally returning to a mother's embrace, like finally returning to that distant hometown.

The man closed the deer's eyelids, like closing a poetry collection finished reading.

That smile remaining at the corner of the mouth gave this dark basement a trace of holiness that shouldn't exist.

## Act 9 The Dignity

G-567 awoke, and the entire basement seemed to be enveloped by a majestic aura.

Even bound, even paralyzed, the moment he opened his eyes, those amber eyes burned with the flame of a king, golden mane shimmering like burning flames under the dim yellow light.

"Release me!" His voice was like thunder, echoing in the basement. "Fight me fair and square! Let me die with dignity!"

The man laughed, that smile carrying genuine appreciation, even a trace of awe.

"Lion-kind's dignity, unwilling to lower its head even in the direst straits. I admire you, truly."

"Admire me then let me go!"

"No," the man began undoing his uniform, revealing that sculpted body. "But I promise, you will die with dignity. At least, more dignified than the others."

This time, the man adopted the most intense approach. He wanted to use the strongest stimulation, the most extreme edging, to test how long this lion's dignity could persist.

He used all tools—drugs, devices, techniques, all skills brought to the extreme. The male lion's body trembled violently under that intense stimulation, but his gaze was still firm, still burning with unyielding flame.

"The first time."

The male lion clenched his jaw, refusing to make any sound.

The man increased stimulation intensity, that pain surpassing pleasure, becoming a pure test.

"The second time."

The male lion's body was in spasms, sweat soaking his golden mane, but his gaze was still proud.

"The third time, fourth time, fifth time..."

Again and again, the man constantly challenged the male lion's limit. He used various ways to stimulate this king's body, trying to break that dignity.

Sixth time, seventh time... The male lion's voice was already broken, that suppressed whimper squeezing out from between clenched teeth, but his gaze was still unyielding.

"You... will not... get... my dignity..." The male lion's voice was broken and weak, but still carried the pride of a king. "I... am a lion... not... livestock..."

Eighth time...

When the final wave struck, the male lion's eyes were still open, those amber pupils still burning with the dignity of a king, even though life had already left him.

The man bowed deeply to that body.

Those eyes were still open, amber pupils freezing pride that refused to extinguish even in death. Even in this filthy basement, this body still emitted the afterglow of a king.

## Act 10 The Torment

G-734 awoke to find himself placed in the center of the basement.

Surrounding him was a circle of already cold bodies—wolf, bear, tiger, ox, dog, leopard, boar, deer, lion. Nine companions he had once betrayed, now all dead, their eyes mostly open, as if accusing him of his crimes.

"See?" The man's voice rang out beside his ear. "They all died because of you."

"No..." The red fox's voice trembled.

"You betrayed them," the man's fingers slid across the red fox's cheeks. "You reported the escape plan to the company, thinking you could buy your own safety. You thought you could be saved, but you were wrong. I bought all of you, just for this moment."

"Please..." The red fox's tears flowed down his cheeks. "I'm sorry... I'm truly sorry..."

"Sorry?" The man laughed loudly. "Sorry won't bring them back. Sorry won't erase your betrayal. But do you know what might make you feel better?"

He bent down, murmuring in the red fox's ear:

"Pain."

This time, the man adopted the most prolonged torture. He wanted to push the red fox to the limit, then stop, let him look at those corpses, let his conscience suffer, then push him up again.

"The first time."

When the wave was about to strike, the man suddenly stopped his movements.

"Look at them," he forced the red fox to turn his head. "Look at those people you sold out."

The red fox's tears gushed out, that spiritual torment more painful than the physical.

The man waited ten minutes, letting that suffering reach its peak, then began again.

"The second time."

Again stopping when about to reach the peak, letting the red fox look at those corpses once more.

"The third time, fourth time, fifth time..."

Again and again, the man constantly repeated this torture. The red fox collapsed under the dual torment of body and spirit, crying echoing in the basement.

He saw hallucinations.

That wolf turned his head, gray eyes full of questioning: "Why?"

That bear quietly looked at him, only sorrow in his gaze.

That tiger roared: "Coward!"

That ox silently watched him, that silence heavier than any curse.

That dog wept and said: "I want to go home..."

That leopard hissed: "You can't escape..."

That boar sneered: "We're all the same..."

That deer gently said: "Why betray?"

That lion majestically said: "You don't deserve to live."

Nine faces, nine pairs of eyes, nine kinds of judgment.

Two hours later, when the sixth wave finally struck, the red fox's heart stopped beating. His eyes were still open, looking at that circle of former companions, looking at the tragedy he himself had caused, that remorse forever frozen upon his face.

The man stood up, looking down at those eyes filled with remorse even in death.

In the red fox's pupils were frozen the reflections of nine faces, a debt he would carry to his grave.

## Final Act The Collector

Ten bodies lay quietly in the basement.

Young blood had cooled, sensitive nerves had fallen silent, heavy fat had stiffened, proud dignity had frozen, hollow compliance had become eternal, struggling souls had rested, indifferent emptiness had been filled, gentle smiles had been fixed, kingly dignity endured forever, betrayer's remorse had frozen.

The man stood in the center of the room, looking at his collection.

The basement air was permeated with a complex atmosphere—sweat, semen, fear, death, and some ineffable, post-life-flowing void.

He walked toward the telephone, calling the cleanup personnel.

"Perfection," he murmured. "Ten different ways, ten different endings."

The garbage truck arrived at three in the morning.

Two workers walked into the basement, their movements mechanical and efficient, without pity, without pause. They skillfully loaded the ten bodies into black bags, like moving cargo, not handling lives that once were.

"Another ten," one worker said.

"He's been buying a lot lately," the other worker replied. "Very efficient."

"A bit of a pity, these goods are not bad quality. That lion, that tiger..."

"Not our place to judge. Clean up and leave."

The bags made rustling sounds scraping against the floor, like some final sigh.

The man stood at the doorway, watching the truck disappear into the night. That vehicle's taillights flickered in the darkness, like the eyes of some monster, finally completely vanishing into the city's light and shadow.

"Until next time," he turned and disappeared into the darkness.

Only cold air remained in the basement, and that lingering scent of death.

And in some corner of the city, new prey was being selected, new tragedy was brewing, new cycle about to begin.
