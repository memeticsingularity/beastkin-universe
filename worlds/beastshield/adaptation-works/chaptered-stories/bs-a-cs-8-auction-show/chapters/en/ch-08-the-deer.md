**Scene-1 Tranquility in the Waiting Area**

In the darkness, G-891 sat quietly, like a white jade statue.

He was a deer beastman, white deer race, with a slender and elegant build. Even in the dim light, one could see his distinctive temperament—not the agility of leopard-kind, not the majesty of tiger-kind, but an innate gentleness and kindness. His figure was slender, about 1.75 meters tall, with a fine-boned frame that didn't appear frail; every line seemed softly sculpted by the spring breeze.

His hands were tied behind his back, but unlike the other auction items, he didn't struggle. He simply sat quietly, his gaze calm, as if accepting fate's arrangement. Those eyes were amber, clear as a mountain stream, capable of reflecting the softest parts of a person's heart.

His mouth was sealed, a rubber ball stuffed inside. He had tried to speak, to explain, but quickly realized it was useless. The bitterness of rubber spread through his mouth, mixed with his own saliva, forming a suffocating silence.

He could smell the odors in the air—the scent of sweat, blood, fear, and that heartbreaking smell of death. But within this mixture, he could still distinguish his own scent—a blend of herbal freshness, morning dew moisture, and gentle warmth, like the purest corner deep within a forest.

The first seven were already dead.

Wolf, bear, tiger, ox, dog, leopard, boar—they had all walked that white corridor, stepped onto that circular stage, and walked toward their end amidst the audience's laughter.

Now, it was his turn.

His crime was mercy.

Two days ago, during patrol, he discovered a captured civilian—an elderly rabbit beastman, arrested for owing a debt to Beast Shield. According to the rules, he should have immediately reported it and sent the rabbit to the detention center.

But he didn't.

He saw the rabbit trembling, saw the fear in its eyes, saw the shadow of his own mother. His heart softened.

He opened the lock and released the rabbit.

Then, he was arrested.

"You released company property," the interrogator said. "Your mercy is a betrayal of the company."

He wanted to explain, to say he simply couldn't bear it, that he couldn't watch an innocent life being tormented. But the interrogator didn't give him a chance.

"In this world," the interrogator said coldly, "kindness is the most useless thing."

**Scene-2 Contrast with Others**

The metal door emitted a harsh sound.

G-891 raised his head, seeing G-378—that wild boar, being dragged out.

His steps stumbled, his body trembling, like a fallen leaf in the wind. The door closed behind him, cutting off all light and sound.

The waiting room fell into silence.

G-891 turned his head, looking at the other survivors.

G-567, that male lion, leaned against the wall, maintaining a kingly posture. Even while bound, his spine remained straight, eyes burning with unyielding flames. That golden mane still flickered with majestic luster in the dim light, like a flame that would never extinguish. His build was robust and sturdy, pectorals full, every muscle like rock tempered a thousand times.

G-734, that red fox, restlessly shifted his body. His eyes wandered everywhere, as if looking for an opportunity to escape—or looking for someone to sell out. His build was much slimmer than lion-kind, his fiery red fur like a dancing flame in the darkness, cunning and restless. His tail swayed uneasily, showing inner anxiety and fear.

G-891 lowered his head, closing his eyes.

He didn't regret it.

Even knowing he would die, he didn't regret releasing that rabbit.

In this world, kindness might be the most useless thing. But without kindness, what meaning would this world have?

He remembered the rabbit's eyes—cloudy and terrified, like a young beast surrounded by hunters. That rabbit was already old, its fur gray-white, ears drooping, its body bony from long-term malnutrition. It shouldn't have been locked in a detention center, shouldn't have become a number on the company's ledger.

It should have returned to its home, to the family waiting for it.

That moment's memory was so clear, as if it had just happened. He remembered that day's sunlight was very soft, shining through the iron bars of the detention room, casting mottled light and shadow on the rabbit's gray-white fur. The rabbit curled up in the corner, its thin body trembling, those cloudy eyes full of despair.

It said: "Please, I still have a little grandson waiting for me at home."

That voice was hoarse and weak, as if coming from very far away. G-891 stood at the door, hand on the lock, struggling inwardly. He knew the rules, knew the price of releasing a prisoner, knew what it meant.

But he also saw the marks on the rabbit's wrists, saw its sunken abdomen from long-term hunger, saw the desire for life in its eyes.

That was the same gaze as his mother.

His mother was also a gentle deer who taught him from a young age to treat every life with kindness. "Lingjiao," mother always said, "our antlers are for protecting others, not for harming them."

Mother passed away when he was fifteen, from a sudden illness. Before dying, she held his hand, those amber eyes still clear: "Promise me, no matter how cruel the world becomes, never lose your kindness."

He promised.

And now, he had fulfilled that promise.

When he opened the lock and released the rabbit, the rabbit looked back at him. That gaze held gratitude, surprise, and a deep confusion—it didn't understand why someone would be willing to take such a risk for it.

G-891 also didn't understand why this world had become like this. Why kindness became a crime, why mercy was seen as betrayal, why one life's freedom needed to be paid for with another life's death.

But he knew, if given another chance, he would make the same choice.

Because that was what mother taught him, because that was the meaning of his existence as Lingjiao.

"Wu—" G-567 emitted a low whimper, like an inquiry, like a sigh.

G-891 opened his eyes, looking at that male lion. Their gazes met in the darkness, like two streams of different natures converging. Lion-kind's eyes held kingly dignity and unyielding spirit, while deer-kind's eyes held only calm gentleness.

They couldn't converse, but in that moment, they seemed to understand each other.

They had both stood here for the sake of protecting others.

G-567 for protecting his subordinates by defying orders.

G-891 for protecting a civilian by betraying the company.

In this cold world, they had chosen the same path—even though the end of that path was death.

**Scene-3 Calm Before Going On Stage**

The metal door made a sound again.

"G-891," that voice rang out, "your turn."

Two people walked in, one on each side holding up his arms. Their hands were strong, but for deer-kind's slender build, seemed somewhat rough. Their fingers dug into the muscles of his arms, that touch reminding him of being caught in a hunter's trap as a child—painful, but tolerable.

"Deer-kind," the one on the left muttered, "so light."

G-891 was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along that white corridor. His steps were light, even while being held up, more elegant than the other auction items. His white short fur flickered with soft luster under the corridor lights, like moonlight falling on snow.

His uniform was dark gray, rough in texture, rubbing against his sensitive skin. Deer-kind skin was naturally delicate, especially sensitive to touch, every inch of fabric's presence could be clearly perceived. The uniform clung tightly to his body, outlining a slender waistline and flat abdomen.

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes.

The staff members pushed open the door.

Light, sound, smell, everything rushed in at once.

G-891 was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining directly down.

He raised his head, facing hundreds upon hundreds of gazes.

Those gazes held curiosity, surprise, and also a trace of imperceptible respect.

He was tonight's most special auction item—not because his crime was so serious, but because he was the only one who didn't regret it.

**Scene-4 Display and Dignity**

"Ladies and gentlemen!"

The fox host's voice rang out, carrying a special tone.

"Today's eighth auction item is a real 'saint'!"

He walked to G-891's front, his fiery red fur shining under the lights.

"G-891, white deer beastman, employed for one year!" The fox's voice was full of cheer, "Two days ago, he released a captured civilian out of 'compassion'! How touching a 'mercy'!"

A burst of snickering came from the audience, but also mixed with some low murmurs.

G-891 calmly looked at the fox. There was no anger in his eyes, no fear, only a gentle firmness. Those amber eyes under the spotlight were like two warm gems, reflecting the madness and absurdity of the entire world.

"Let's take a look at this 'saint's' physical condition," the fox walked to G-891's front, beginning to unbutton his uniform.

"First, the chest."

The fox's palm pressed against G-891's chest, lightly pressing.

Deer-kind pectoral muscles were slender and well-proportioned, covered with a layer of fine white short fur. That fur was soft and smooth, like premium silk, flickering with pearl-like luster under the light. The fox's fingers sank into that layer of down, able to feel the gentle heartbeat beneath the skin—neither hurried nor slow, like a mountain stream flowing.

"Look here," his fingers slid across a light-colored scar above the left chest, "this was from being scratched by a branch as a child, right? What a pure proof!"

That scar was very shallow, like a trace left by a falling feather gently brushing across the skin. It lay across the upper left chest, near the position of the heart, like a gentle guardian.

He lightly pressed it.

G-891 didn't make a sound. He just calmly looked at the fox, not a trace of resentment in his eyes.

"Doesn't it hurt?" the fox asked in a low voice, somewhat surprised.

G-891 slightly shook his head.

That scar was from when he was ten. He was playing in the forest when he saw an injured little bird trapped in thorns. He wanted to save it, but was scratched by the thorns on his chest. But he didn't give up, finally rescuing that little bird.

That scar was the mark of kindness.

The fox's hand continued to move, coming to the abdomen.

"The abs here," the fox patted, "flat, soft. Deer-kind's abdomen is just slender, like a work of art."

G-891's abdomen was flat and firm, skin fair, faint muscle contours visible. That layer of white short fur became even finer here, like covered with a thin layer of frost. The fox's fingers slid across that layer of down, able to feel the warm body temperature beneath the skin.

"Turn around," the fox commanded.

G-891 was forcibly turned around, back facing the audience.

"Look at this back," the fox's palm slid across his spine, "smooth lines, like a sculpture."

Deer-kind's back was slender, the contour of the spine clearly visible, like a string of pearls embedded in white jade. The scapula shape was elegant, gently rising and falling with breath, like a white dove about to spread its wings. That layer of white short fur flickered with soft luster under the light, extending from neck all the way to waist.

His hand continued downward, coming to the buttocks.

"Buttocks tight, thighs slender. This physique under normal circumstances should be the one being protected."

G-891's buttocks were tight and round, thighs slender and well-proportioned, calves slender and elegant. His legs were deer-kind's proudest feature—long, straight, full of power yet not losing softness. White short fur covered his legs, under the light like covered with a thin layer of snow.

His hand continued downward, coming to the legs.

Deer-kind's legs were slender and fine, thighs and calves both slender and well-proportioned. But the fox's gaze fell on his feet—those feet wrapped in white knee-high socks.

That was a pair of specially made white knee-high socks, soft in texture, tightly wrapping his feet and calves. The socks were pure white, without a trace of杂色, like the first snow of winter covering the earth. They fit every curve of his feet, from toes all the way to below the knees, outlining slender ankles and long calf lines.

"Deer-kind's feet," the fox's voice carried a special interest, "are the most sensitive part of the whole body."

He squatted down, fingers gently caressing that layer of white fabric. The sock's texture was fine, touch soft, like stroking a cloud. Through that thin layer of fabric, one could faintly see the outline of the foot—slender toes, arched foot bridge, slender ankle.

"These socks," the fox stood up, saying to the audience, "were specially prepared by us for deer-kind. White, the color of purity, perfectly suited for our 'saint' here."

**Scene-5 Unsold and Fate**

"Ladies and gentlemen!" the fox announced loudly, "such a 'saint,' starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

No one raised their hand.

G-891 knew no one would. He closed his eyes, waiting for that inevitable word.

"Alright—unsold!" the fox announced, "unsold auction items will have their fate decided by the wheel!"

The screen lit up, the wheel spun.

The wheel stopped.

The pointer pointed to a special area.

"Foot sensitive point stimulation!" the fox announced, "looks like our Mr. Deer is going to experience the sensitivity of his feet!"

**Scene-6 Execution**

G-891 was fixed to a metal frame, limbs pulled apart.

His uniform was undone, revealing his slender body. The spotlight shone on his skin, making that layer of white short fur flicker with soft luster. His body under the light was like an exquisite work of art—slender waist, flat abdomen, long legs. Every inch of skin was covered with fine white down, under the light like shrouded in a faint halo.

The staff member first peeled away his white knee-high socks.

Those feet were exposed to the air—slender, elegant, with soft pads between the toes. Deer-kind feet were naturally sensitive, every inch of skin covered with nerve endings. Those feet were milky white, skin as delicate as fine porcelain, toes slender and well-proportioned, toenails a faint pink.

The pads on the soles were soft and sensitive, showing a faint rose color, like cherry blossoms blooming in early spring. The foot bridge arched high, forming a beautiful arc, ankles so slender they seemed to break with a grip.

"Begin," the fox said.

The staff member began to use fingers to press, knead, and lightly stroke those feet—from the soles, to the toes, to the ankles.

G-891 felt all the electricity in his body concentrating on his feet. That stimulation wasn't pain, but a strong, irresistible sensation. His body began to tremble, breathing becoming rapid. He could feel the staff member's fingers wandering on his soles, pressing those sensitive acupoints, kneading the soft pads.

But he didn't make a sound.

"Look," the fox said to the audience, "deer-kind's feet are just sensitive, merely touching produces such a strong reaction."

Foot stimulation continued.

The staff member's technique became more meticulous—using fingers to rub the pads between toes, using nails to lightly scratch the soles, using palms to wrap the entire foot for pressing.

G-891 felt his consciousness gradually blurring. That stimulation was too strong, like countless currents rushing straight from the soles to the brain. His toes involuntarily curled, then spread open, as if making a final struggle.

He remembered that rabbit.

Remembered its cloudy and terrified eyes.

Remembered its trembling body.

Remembered the grateful gaze when it was rescued.

He didn't regret it.

Even in death, he had saved that rabbit.

The staff member's fingers found the most sensitive area on the sole—a pressure point located in the center of the foot bridge. That was one of the places with the densest nerves in deer-kind's whole body, a light press could trigger a whole-body reaction.

Fingers pressed down.

G-891 felt a wave of intense stimulation rushing straight from the soles to the top of his head, his body suddenly arching, like a fully drawn bow. His breathing became rapid and shallow, chest heaving violently, white short fur soaked with sweat, sticking to his skin.

The first ejaculation came suddenly and intensely.

His body spasmed, as if electricity passed through his whole body. Semen shot out, flickering with faint luster under the light. There was no expression of pain on his face, only a strange calm, as if finally completing some mission.

"First time," the fox counted, "looks like our Mr. Deer is very sensitive."

Stimulation continued.

The staff member's technique became gentler, like stroking a precious piece of porcelain. They used fingertips to lightly glide across the soles, used finger pads to press the base of toes, used palms to wrap the entire foot for warm massage.

G-891 felt his life force flowing away with each ejaculation. But his consciousness was exceptionally clear, those sealed memories surging like a tide.

He remembered childhood, mother teaching him how to walk in the forest without startling any life.

Remembered the first time patrolling alone, seeing that firefly under the moonlight, like a star in the darkness.

Remembered after that rabbit was rescued, looking back at him, that gaze holding gratitude, hope, and the most authentic light of life.

Second ejaculation.

His body spasmed again, but already not as intense as the first time. His breathing became weaker, like a candle flame in the wind, about to extinguish at any moment.

"Second time," the fox's voice seemed to come from very far away, "deer-kind's stamina is indeed inferior to other races."

Stimulation became more meticulous.

The staff member began to focus on the toes—that was one of the most sensitive areas. They used fingertips to lightly pinch each toe, from large to small, pressing one by one. They used nails to lightly scratch the gaps between toes, used finger pads to rub the toe pads.

G-891 felt his consciousness gradually drifting away. His body no longer belonged to himself, just instinctively reacting to those stimulations. But his heart remained calm, like the tranquility at the center of a storm.

Third ejaculation.

This time, his body only trembled slightly, like the last fallen leaf in the autumn wind. His breathing became extremely weak, chest almost no longer rising and falling.

"Third time," the fox said, "looks like it's about to end."

But stimulation continued.

The staff member's fingers returned to the soles, finding that most sensitive pressure point. They knew, deer-kind's feet were the most vulnerable place in the whole body, and also the place most able to trigger reactions.

Fingers pressed down again.

G-891 felt a final wave of intense stimulation, like a lightning bolt streaking across the night sky. His body spasmed one last time, then completely relaxed.

Fourth ejaculation.

This time, only a small amount of semen flowed out, like life's final sigh. Then, the heart stopped beating.

On his face, was a satisfied smile.

He didn't regret it.

Even in death, he had saved that rabbit.

**Scene-7 Curtain Fall**

"Confirmed death," the staff member checked, "death by semen exhaustion."

"Thank you for G-891's performance!"

G-891's corpse was untied, dragged away.

On his face, there was still that gentle smile. Those amber eyes were half-open, as if gazing at some distant place—perhaps that forest he had never seen, perhaps the corner where that rabbit he saved was living peacefully.

He was once a deer, kindness, light.

Now, he was nothing.

Just a number, G-891, already cancelled.

But in some corner of the city, that rabbit he released was holding its own grandson, telling a story about kindness.

### Story Reflections

This chapter is a story about "kindness."

Lingjiao's tragedy lies not in his death, but in his kindness finding no reward in this world.

He was tonight's most special auction item—he was the only one who didn't regret. His death is the most profound accusation against this system.

When kindness becomes a crime, when mercy is seen as betrayal, this world has already lost its humanity.

Lingjiao's smile is comfort to all kind people—even if the world is cruel, kindness still has meaning.

Because that rabbit he saved will continue to pass on this kindness.

That is hope.
