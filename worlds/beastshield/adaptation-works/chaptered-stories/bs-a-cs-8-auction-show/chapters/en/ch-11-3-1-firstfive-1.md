## Act 1 The Cage

The sound of the door closing behind them was low and drawn-out, like the sigh of a coffin being nailed shut, like the rumble of the gates of hell slowly closing. That sound echoed in the empty basement, lingering, as if fate itself were pronouncing its merciless verdict upon this enclosed space.

This was a basement of roughly two hundred square meters, its four walls bare concrete without decoration, bearing only the mottled traces left by time and dampness. Several deep cracks ran down the walls, like claw marks left by some colossal beast, like wrinkles carved by time in this forgotten corner. Old fluorescent lamps hung from the ceiling, their tubes buzzing with electrical current, light flickering unsteadily, casting eerie, ghost-like shadows upon the walls.

The air was permeated with an indescribable atmosphere. It was a complex mixture of many odors—the damp smell of mold, the stale scent of old blood, the pungent smell of some chemical agent, and a sickly-sweet, nauseating fragrance like rotting flowers. This atmosphere brought a strange burning sensation when inhaled into the lungs, making one's consciousness hazy and heavy.

Ten bodies lay strewn across the cold concrete floor in various postures, like trees blown down by a storm. Their hands were mostly tied behind their backs; even in unconsciousness, their brows remained furrowed, as if in dreams they were still wrestling with some formless terror.

The man standing in the center of the room slowly removed his hood.

He looked no more than thirty, his complexion pale to the point of near-transparency, as though he had never seen sunlight. His features were ordinary to the point of uncanny—no distinguishing characteristics, no memorable traits, the sort of face that would vanish without a trace in a crowd. But when he raised his head, his eyes betrayed the nature of his soul—deep amber, burning with a pathological, near-mad light, like two bottomless pools whose surface was calm but whose depths concealed whirlpools that could swallow everything.

His build was medium, neither burly nor thin, wearing a dark gray hooded cloak of ordinary fabric without ornament. He simply stood there, fused with the surrounding shadows, as though he had grown from the darkness itself.

But that was precisely what made him terrifying—what twisted soul lay beneath that ordinary exterior? Those eyes, when they swept across the prey on the floor, gleamed with the light of a hunter surveying his trophies, with the obsession of a collector appraising his treasures, and with the sick tenderness of a madman facing his beloved toy.

He paced slowly, the soles of his boots scraping against the concrete with a faint, rustling sound. Each step seemed to tread upon the prey's nerves, stretching the already taut air to the breaking point, as if it might snap at any moment. His gaze swept across the ten people on the floor, like a curator examining his soon-to-be-displayed collection, like a butcher selecting the finest cuts from his block.

The first was the gray wolf.

That body was long and well-proportioned, roughly one meter eighty in height, weighing about seventy kilograms. Even lying on the cold floor, one could still make out the lean, powerful build characteristic of wolf-kind. His gray fur still carried a cold gleam even in the dim light, like the purest snow on the northern plains, like moonlight glinting off a silver blade.

His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraints biting deep into the skin of his wrists, leaving red marks across that layer of gray-white short fur—a cruel decoration. Even unconscious, his brow remained furrowed, as if in dreams he were still fleeing from something, the slight twitch at the corner of his mouth betraying the fear he could not escape even in sleep.

The man knelt down, his fingers gently parting the gray-white fur covering the wolf's face. The muscles beneath the skin still trembled faintly, an aftereffect of the anesthetic, but also instinctive fear. He could feel the warmth at his fingertips—the warmth of life, the warmth he was about to extinguish with his own hands.

"The first," he murmured, his voice so soft it was almost tender, "my gray wolf."

The second was the brown bear.

His massive frame was like a small hill, nearly two meters tall, weighing at least one hundred twenty kilograms. That thick chest and belly rose and fell with heavy breathing, each inhale carrying resonance from deep within his chest, like a war drum being struck, announcing some impending doom. His brown fur was dense and coarse, emitting a mixture of earth and sweat—the scent of a laborer, the scent of a simple, heavy life.

The man's palm hovered above the bear's chest, feeling that slow, powerful heartbeat. It was steady and weighty, like distant thunder, like the pulse of the earth itself. He could imagine how that heartbeat would gradually weaken as this body was emptied, until finally falling silent.

"Such vast life force," he marveled, "enough for me to enjoy for a long time."

The third was the yellow tiger.

Even lying down, the black stripes upon his orange fur remained striking, like nature's most perfect camouflage, like markings from some ancient totem. His muscle lines were distinct, each one carved as if by design, from broad shoulders to tight abdomen to long, powerful limbs—everywhere radiating primal majesty and strength.

Even in unconsciousness, his body maintained a certain tension, as though ready to spring up at any moment, ready to pounce upon prey. That innate kingly aura could not be fully concealed even in the direst straits.

The man's fingers lightly traced the tiger's pectoral muscles, feeling that tightness and power, those sculpted muscle lines. "A proud hunter, even as prey, still unwilling to lower his head. I like that."

The fourth was the black ox.

Sturdy and thick, with a broad frame, short black fur covering firm muscle. His breathing was steady and deep, like a ruminating beast, utterly unaware of the fate approaching. That broad back, round chest and belly, thick thighs—everywhere spoke of a body built for labor, a body squeezed dry by the system and then cast aside.

The man's fingers sank into the ox's thick pectorals, feeling the solid muscle base beneath that layer of fat. "A scapegoat, a sacrifice. Your injustice excites me all the more."

The fifth was the yellow dog.

A young body, still unweathered by time, roughly one meter seventy-five in height, about sixty-five kilograms. His muscle lines were distinct yet tinged with greenness, like a cub not yet fully grown. The corner of his mouth twitched slightly, as if seeing something terrible in his dreams, the corner of his eye still holding an un-dried tear.

The man gently wiped away that tear and placed it in his mouth to taste. "Salty, astringent, young. You will be one of my most exquisite collections."

Further away were five more—the black leopard's lean body like a fully drawn bow; the wild boar's squat build radiating greed; the white deer's pure fur like a holy offering; the male lion's golden mane still dazzling even in the dim light; the red fox's fiery fur like a burning flame.

"Perfection," the man stood up, taking a deep breath, savoring the scent of his prey in the air. "Ten works. Perfection."

He walked to the metal cabinet on the wall. The cabinet door emitted a harsh squeal when opened. Inside were neatly arranged implements—syringes, drugs, ropes, tools. He took out a transparent syringe and several bottles of drugs, the liquid inside refracting a cold, dim light as it sloshed.

"Time to wake up, my prey," he smiled, that smile looking especially uncanny under the dim yellow light. "Let us begin this... private performance."

## Act 2 The Wolf's Awakening

The gray wolf was the first to be awakened.

The man knelt at his side, his posture so reverent it was almost religious. His fingers gently parted the gray-white fur covering the wolf's face, his movements so soft they were almost病态, as though handling a precious piece of porcelain, or caressing a lover's cheek.

That skin was fair and delicate, standing out all the more against the gray-white fur. Wolf-kind fur was divided into two layers—the outer guard hairs were coarse and hard, like armor protecting the body; the inner down was dense and soft, warm and comfortable. The man's fingers threaded through them, feeling that transition from coarse to soft, like reading a book about life.

The syringe pierced the blood vessel at the side of the neck, the needle seeking its target beneath the skin. Cold liquid slowly flowed in, carrying some wakeful component, and also some component that would heighten sensitivity.

A few seconds later, the gray wolf's eyelids began to flutter. First the slight trembling of his lashes, then the rapid movement of his eyeballs beneath his lids, and finally, those gray-white pupils slowly opened, gradually focusing.

He saw the ceiling—gray-white concrete, with a few cracks, traces of water stains. Then he saw the dim yellow light, that light stinging his eyes, forcing him to squint. Finally he saw that face looking down at him—pale, ordinary, yet carrying a certain hair-raising tenderness.

"You..." His voice was hoarse, his throat as if scorched by fire, or ground by sandpaper, every syllable carrying pain.

"Awake?" The man smiled, his fingers still wandering across his face, from forehead to bridge of nose, from cheek to chin. "Don't rush. Your body still needs some time to fully recover. The numbness will slowly fade, but... it won't disappear completely."

The gray wolf tried to struggle, but his limbs felt as though filled with lead, completely refusing to obey. He could feel his hands and feet, could feel their presence, but no matter how hard he tried, he could not make them move. That helplessness was more terrifying than any pain.

He could only lie there, feeling that hand wandering across his body. From neck sliding to chest, pressing against his pectorals through the fabric of his uniform. That touch seemed gentle, yet carried an irresistible possessiveness, like inspecting an item about to be used, or confirming the quality of prey.

"Let me go..." he gasped, voice carrying despair, "please... let me go..."

"Let you go?" The man laughed, that laughter low and pleasant. "I spent ten gold coins to buy you. At the auction house, at the last moment, I raised my hand. From that moment on, you were my property. How I treat my property is my freedom."

His fingers undid the first button of the uniform. It was a metal button, its cold touch making the wolf's skin break out in goosebumps. As button after button was undone, cold air gradually contacted his skin, making the gray-white fur stand on end, an instinctive defensive reaction.

"You're a deserter, right?" The man's fingers slid across the gray wolf's chest skin, feeling the muscle texture beneath that layer of down. "You abandoned your companions during a mission, fled alone. You thought that was survival instinct, but in Beastshield's eyes, that was unforgivable betrayal."

"I didn't... abandon them..." The gray wolf's voice trembled, "I wanted to save them... but..."

"But it was no use," the man took over, "so you ran. You came back alive, and they died. That's your sin."

The uniform was fully opened, the gray wolf's upper body exposed to the cold air. His pectorals were firm and well-proportioned, the gray-white fur forming a thick down at his chest, rising and falling slightly with his breathing. The man's palm covered that patch of down, feeling the violent heartbeat, as if savoring the prey's final fear.

"Do you know?" The man leaned down, whispering by his ear, his warm breath spraying upon the wolf's ear canal, bringing a shudder. "I've been watching you for a long time. From the moment you trembled on the auction block, from the moment you swept the audience with those frightened eyes, I knew... you would be a perfect work."

His hand began to move downward, sliding across the abdomen, stopping at the waist. That was one of wolf-kind's most sensitive areas, the skin around the navel finer and more sensitive than elsewhere.

"Don't..." The gray wolf closed his eyes, tears seeping from the corners, flowing down his cheeks, vanishing into the gray-white fur. "Please... spare me... I don't want to die..."

"No one wants to die," the man's voice was like the temptation of a demon, soft and fatal. "But death is the only end point. At least, I will make your death... valuable. Your body, your trembling, your final whimpers—all will become part of my collection."

His palm enveloped the gray wolf's most vulnerable part, that contrast of burning heat and cold air making the gray wolf gasp. He could feel his blood accelerating, could feel that instinctive physiological reaction occurring, and this made him feel all the more ashamed and desperate.

"Your body has already begun to respond," the man laughed softly. "See? It's more honest than I am. It knows what it wants."

## Act 3 The Wolf's Descent

Time was stretched at this moment, as if twisted by some invisible force, becoming viscous and slow.

The man's movements were slow and deliberate, like performing some ancient ritual, or like savoring a cup of aged wine. His fingers traced every inch of the gray wolf's body, from the throbbing artery at the side of the neck to the sensitive nipples at the chest, from the muscle lines of the abdomen to the soft skin on the inner thighs.

Each touch brought a shudder—not pleasure, but a complex sensation of fear and humiliation intertwined. The gray wolf could feel the trajectory of the man's fingertips across his skin, like a cold serpent, like a dull blade slowly slicing away his dignity.

"Your fur is very soft," the man murmured, his voice echoing in the empty basement. "Softer than I imagined. Like silk, like... a life about to pass."

His palm enveloped the gray wolf's fully erect member, that burning temperature making a satisfied gleam flash in the man's eyes. He began to move, slowly and rhythmically, each slide of his fingers carrying a nauseating tenderness.

"Don't... please..." The gray wolf's voice had become whimpering, broken and desperate. "I don't want to die... I'm still young... I still have..."

"Still have what?" The man asked while moving. "Still have family? Still have dreams? Still have... an unfinished mission?"

His fingers accelerated their rhythm, that stimulation growing stronger, more irresistible. The gray wolf felt his body betraying him, those forced waves surging one after another, washing away his reason, washing away his dignity.

"The first time," the man counted, his voice carrying a pathological satisfaction.

The wave came fast and fierce, the gray wolf's body arching like a fully drawn bow, a suppressed wail squeezed from his throat. That release was not relief, but the beginning of being emptied. He could feel his life force flowing away with that liquid, could feel something essential leaving him.

But the man did not stop.

His hand kept moving, more forcefully, more urgently. The gray wolf felt that sensitivity amplified tenfold by the drug, each touch like a knife cut, like a fire burn, like countless ants crawling upon his nerves.

"The second time," the man's voice seemed to come from very far away, blurred and distant.

The gray wolf could no longer speak. His vision began to blur, gray spots dancing before his eyes like snowflakes. His consciousness wandered between wakefulness and unconsciousness, his body being emptied again and again, each time weaker than the last, more painful.

He could feel his heartbeat accelerating, that irregular rhythm foretelling some dangerous approach. His breathing became rapid and chaotic, his chest heaving violently, the gray-white fur soaked with sweat, clinging to his skin in strands.

"The third time, and the last."

When the final wave struck, the gray wolf no longer had any strength to resist. His body lay limp on the floor, eyes open, looking at the ceiling, but his pupils had already dilated, losing focus. His lips moved slightly, as if saying something, but no sound came out.

The man checked his pulse, his finger pressing against the artery at the side of the neck, feeling that gradually weakening heartbeat, until finally stopping completely. Then he stood up, licking the liquid from his hand, savoring that interweaving of saltiness and bitterness.

"The first one, completed." He walked toward the next prey, his voice sated. "Nine more to go."

## Act 4 The Bear's Steadiness

The brown bear awoke to a feeling of heavy oppression.

Not from without, but from his own body—his build was too massive; even in unconsciousness, the weight of his chest and belly made breathing heavy and difficult. He tried to move, but found his limbs still controlled by paralysis, able only to tremble faintly, unable to make any effective struggle.

The man's hand was wandering across his chest, that touch reminding him of his mother licking him with her rough tongue when he was young, but now that touch brought not warmth, but cold fear.

"Your fat layer is very thick," the man's voice carried marvel, as if admiring a work of art. "Feels like a quilt, soft, warm, with solid power hidden beneath. This is bear-kind's fat-muscle composition, isn't it? Soft fat on the outside, firm muscle on the inside."

His palm sank into that thick layer of fat, pressing the muscle beneath, feeling that transition from soft to hard. That touch fascinated him—the softness on the outside and the hardness within, like some metaphor, like some mockery.

"Let me go..." The brown bear's voice was deep and slow, like a war drum being struck, each word carrying resonance from his chest.

"In such a hurry to end?" The man laughed, his fingers drawing circles on that thick pectoral. "Your body tells me you can endure a great deal. Bear-kind's life force... let me see how tenacious it is."

He began to undo the brown bear's uniform. That massive build made this process difficult, the uniform stretched tight, buttons almost popping open. The man had to patiently undo them one by one, like unwrapping a carefully packaged gift.

When the last piece of fabric was lifted, the man gasped.

The sight before him made him hold his breath—that broad chest was covered in dense brown fur, the pectorals thick and full, wrapped in a layer of fat. The abdomen was round and firm, without obvious abdominal lines, but full of power. The broad back, thick thighs, thick buttocks—everywhere radiated primal power.

"Magnificent," he murmured, his voice carrying genuine marvel. "Such a massive body... how much life force is hidden within? Enough for me to enjoy for a long time, yes?"

His hands began to wander across the brown bear's body, from thick pectorals to round abdomen, from broad back to thick thighs. Every inch of skin was carefully caressed, every hair carefully savored. His fingers threaded through that dense brown fur, feeling that coarse yet warm touch, like stroking a sleeping beast.

"The first time."

The wave came slow and deep, like the bear-kind's own nature. The brown bear's body trembled slightly, his thick chest and belly rising and falling, emitting deep panting. That release was substantial, a large volume of liquid gushing forth, showing this body's vigorous life force.

But the man knew this body could endure more.

"The second time."

"The third time."

Each release took away more life force, but the brown bear's body still responded, still trembled under that forced stimulation. His breathing became heavy and rapid, sweat beginning to seep out, brown fur soaked, sticking to his skin.

"The fourth time."

"The fifth time."

"The sixth time."

By the sixth time, the brown bear's body finally began to collapse. His breathing became rapid and chaotic, his heart like an overloaded machine, pounding wildly in his chest, that heavy beating echoing in the basement.

"The seventh time."

"The eighth time."

The final release was almost dry, just a dry spasm, the body's last struggle, the sign of life force being completely emptied.

"The ninth time. Perfect."

The man stood up, looking at that massive body lying silently on the floor, like a collapsed mountain. That simple face held no fear, only a relieved peace, like finally freed from some heavy burden.

"The second one." He walked toward the next prey.

## Act 5 The Tiger's Pride

The yellow tiger awoke with angry fire burning in his eyes.

Even with his body controlled by paralysis, even with his limbs unable to move, those amber eyes still flickered with unyielding light. He looked at the man looking down at him, his gaze full of contempt and anger, like a caged beast, ready to break free of its cage at any moment and tear the enemy before him to shreds.

"You coward!" he roared, his voice echoing in the basement, carrying tiger-kind's unique majesty and power. "If you have the guts, let me go! Face me in a fair fight! Let me die with dignity!"

The man laughed, that smile carrying genuine appreciation, as if admiring a precious work of art.

"Tiger-kind's pride, unwilling to lower its head even in the direst straits. I admire you, truly." His fingers traced the tiger's pectorals, feeling that tightness and power, those sculpted muscle lines. "But pride cannot save you. Here, you are just prey, a container waiting to be emptied. Your dignity, your glory—all will flow away with your fluids."

"Shut up!" The yellow tiger struggled, muscles tensing, trying to break free of the paralysis, but the drug's power was too strong; he could only tremble faintly, unable to make any effective resistance.

"Struggle," the man's hand began to move downward, sliding across those eight distinct abdominal muscles, stopping at the waist. "Let me see your life force. Let me see how long proud tiger-kind can last at its limit. Let me see how long your dignity can sustain you."

When the stimulation began, the yellow tiger clenched his jaw.

He refused to make a sound, refused to let this man see his weakness, refused to show any expression of pain before his enemy. But the body is the most honest; even with the firmest will, those forced waves would still surge, one after another, washing away his reason, washing away his defenses.

"The first time."

The yellow tiger's body arched, a suppressed low growl squeezed from his throat, but he quickly bit his lip, swallowing the remaining whimper. Cold sweat beaded on his forehead, the orange fur shimmering with wild luster under the dim yellow light.

"Very enduring," the man praised, his fingers accelerating their rhythm. "But how many times can you endure? Your body has already betrayed you."

"The second time."

"The third time."

Each wave was stronger than the last, more irresistible. The yellow tiger's forehead began to ooze more cold sweat, the orange fur soaked, clinging to his skin in strands. His body trembled, muscles spasmed, but he still clenched his jaw, refusing to make any sound.

"The fourth time."

"Don't..." The yellow tiger finally made a sound, a broken, humiliating syllable squeezed from between clenched teeth.

"The fifth time."

Tears flowed from the corners of the yellow tiger's eyes, tears of anger and humiliation, the final unwillingness of proud tiger-kind. He looked at the man looking down at him, that gaze still burning with unyielding flame, even though his body had betrayed him.

"The sixth time."

"The seventh time."

"The eighth time."

When the final wave struck, the yellow tiger's eyes were still open, those amber pupils still burning with unyielding flame, even though life had left him. His body lay limp on the floor, like a tamed beast, but his gaze was still so proud.

The man gently closed his eyes.

"The third one." He walked toward the next prey.

## Act 6 The Ox's Endurance

The black ox awoke with only confusion in his eyes.

"I... I didn't do anything wrong..." His voice was deep and slow, as if coming from the depths of the earth. "I just followed orders... they said what to do, and I did it..."

"I know," the man's hand had already begun to wander across his body, caressing that burly frame. "You were wronged. A scapegoat. A sacrifice. Those who truly made mistakes are safe and sound, while you, this honest, obedient you, must bear all the blame."

His fingers pressed against the ox's thick pectorals, feeling the solid muscle base beneath that layer of fat. "But that is precisely what makes you captivating. Your grievance, your silence, your... resignation. You are like an old ox, working tirelessly until the last drop of blood is squeezed from you."

"Please... spare me... I still have family to support..."

"No." The man's tone was flat, without any fluctuation. "But I will make your death valuable. Your body is more worthy of being remembered than those who are truly guilty. Your endurance, your tenacity—all will become part of my collection."

He began to move, slowly and rhythmically, like playing a mournful melody.

The ox-kind's reaction was the slowest, but also the most enduring. That burly body was like a perpetual motion machine; even emptied again and again, it still responded, still trembled under that forced stimulation. His breathing was steady and deep, even at the strongest waves, without making too much sound.

"The first time."

"The second time."

"The third time."

The black ox simply lay there, eyes fixed on the ceiling, as if thinking about something, or as if thinking about nothing at all. His face held no expression, only a deep exhaustion, the mark left by long labor, the numbness after being squeezed dry by the system.

"The fourth time."

"The fifth time."

"The sixth time."

By the sixth time, his breathing began to grow heavy, that steady rhythm broken.

"The seventh time."

"The eighth time."

"The ninth time."

When the final release came, the black ox's heart finally stopped beating. His face held no fear, only a relieved peace, like finally freed from some heavy burden, like finally able to rest.

The man stood up, looking into those still-open black eyes, those eyes that once held confusion and incomprehension, now forever frozen.

"The fourth one." He walked toward the next prey.

## Act 7 The Dog's Fragility

The yellow dog awoke with tears already streaming down his face.

"I want to go home..." His voice trembled, carrying a crying tone, like a wounded animal. "I want my mom... I'm only twenty-one... I don't want to die... I haven't even been in love... haven't even seen the world..."

The man's hand gently caressed his face, as if comforting a child, but that tenderness concealed cold cruelty.

"Shh, don't be afraid. It won't be long. Your body is very young, very sensitive... it will end soon. You won't endure too much pain, at least, not as much as the others."

"Please... spare me... I'll do anything... I can work for you... I can..."

"It's already too late," the man's hand began to move downward, sliding across those green muscle lines. "But I will remember you. Your young life, your unfinished dreams, your final tears—all will become one of my most precious collections."

The young body reacted very quickly, so quickly it was heartbreaking. That sensitivity was the sensitivity of inexperience, the sensitivity unique to youth; each touch brought violent trembling.

"The first time."

The wave came fast and fierce, the yellow dog's body trembling violently, a suppressed whimper squeezed from his throat. That release brought not pleasure, but deep shame and despair.

"Don't... too sensitive... can't endure..."

"The second time."

The young man was already lying limp on the floor, eyes unfocused, lips moving slightly, unable to speak. His face still carried tear stains, that young, tender, never-suffered expression, forever frozen at the instant of death.

"The third time."

When the final wave struck, the yellow dog's eyes rolled back, his body spasming violently, then falling still. His heart was too young, too fragile, unable to endure three strong impacts.

The man checked his pulse, then stood up, looking at that still-childish face.

"The fifth one." He looked at the remaining five bodies, his voice sated. "First half ended. Second half, continue."

## Act 8 The Interval

The basement was permeated with a complex atmosphere—sweat, fear, death, and an indescribable sweetness.

Five bodies lay silently on the floor, their expressions varied—some peaceful, some painful, some angry, some desperate. The gray wolf's eyes were open, looking at the ceiling; the brown bear's face carried relief; the yellow tiger's mouth still held unyielding stubbornness; the black ox's expression was forever frozen in confusion; the yellow dog's face still carried tear stains.

The man stood in the center of the room, looking at his hands. They still carried the traces of five lives, five different body temperatures, five different stories. He could feel that sticky touch, could smell that mixture of sweat and bodily fluids.

He walked to the wall, where there was a small table with a water jug and cups. He poured a glass of water, slowly drinking it, moistening his parched throat. The water was cool, carrying a hint of metallic taste, but it made him feel sober.

After resting a moment, he looked toward the remaining five people.

The black leopard's lean body like a fully drawn bow, even in unconsciousness maintaining vigilance; the wild boar's squat build radiating greed; the white deer's pure fur like a holy offering; the male lion's golden mane still dazzling even in the dim light; the red fox's fiery fur like a burning flame, awaiting final punishment.

"Don't worry," he smiled, that smile looking especially uncanny under the dim yellow light. "Everyone will have their turn. I promise, you will all die... very valuably."

He walked toward the black leopard, a syringe in his hand, the needle tip glinting with cold light under the light.

"Next, it's your turn."
