**Scene-1 Tension in the Waiting Area**

Darkness draped this narrow waiting area like a heavy curtain, dividing it into several lonely corners.

G-612 leaned against the cold metal wall, his body still maintaining that unique tension—even with nylon restraints cutting into his wrists, even with a gag sealing his mouth, his cheetah-like instincts kept him in a posture ready to spring at any moment.

He was a black panther beastman, 1.8 meters tall, weighing 68 kilograms. Even in the dim light, one could see his distinctive physique—lean, agile, black short fur covering his entire body, muscle lines flowing like a precision machine.

His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraint strap biting deep into the skin of his wrists, leaving red marks across that layer of black short fur. That touch made him feel humiliated—he was a hunter, the wind, a shadow; he shouldn't be bound like this.

His mouth was sealed. A black rubber ball stuffed in his mouth, fixed with a belt looped over the top of his head. He tried to push it out with his tongue, but the rubber ball was wedged too tight. He could only emit vague whimpering, that sound filtered by the gag into a low throaty noise.

His eyes had adapted to the dimness. Not far to his left, he could see G-203—that young yellow dog, curled up in the corner, trembling. He was only twenty-one years old, employed for two weeks, the youngest of this batch. To his right, G-445 the black ox leaned against the wall, eyes hollowly gazing at the ceiling, as if already accepting fate's arrangement. That ox was wronged, G-612 knew—he had heard the guards' conversation.

The air was filled with a complex smell—sweat was the most obvious, coming from ten prisoners about to face death, mixed with pheromones secreted by fear and despair. There was also the smell of blood, from the wounds on G-724 that wolf's body, and from the injuries G-891 that tiger sustained during interrogation. And that smell which made his stomach spasm, the smell of death—the fishy膻味 of semen, the odor emitted when flesh collapsed under extreme stimulation.

G-612 turned his head, looking across.

G-378, that wild boar, restlessly shifted his body. His build was sturdy, brown bristles like a mass of shadow in the dim light. He was the one with the most dishonorable crime in this batch—embezzlement, betraying the company's trust. G-612 looked down on him, but at this moment, they were all in the same boat.

"Heng heng—" G-378 tried to make a sound, turned into a comical nasal noise after being blocked by the gag.

G-612 didn't respond. His gaze passed through the wild boar, looking further—that white deer, G-891, sitting quietly, like a white jade statue. Even in this desperate situation, he still maintained that surprising calm. That lion G-567 sat further away, golden mane still flickering with majestic luster in the dim light.

And in the farthest corner, that red fox G-734 restlessly shifted his body, eyes wandering everywhere. He was the last of this batch, and also the one with the most despicable crime—betrayal, selling out companions.

G-612 closed his eyes, taking a deep breath.

He smelled the scent of fear—coming from that yellow dog, from that wild boar, from that fox. But he wouldn't fear. He was a leopard, a hunter; even if he became prey, he would maintain a hunter's dignity.

But he knew, this dignity would soon be torn to shreds.

The first five were already dead or about to die.

That wolf was dying in the corner, that bear had died peacefully, that tiger had collapsed and been dragged back, that ox had just been dragged out, that dog—that young dog—had also just been dragged out.

Now, it was his turn.

His crime was arrogance.

One night ago, he discovered traces of an intruder. A footprint, a scent, a leaf that had been touched. According to the rules, he should have reported it, waited for backup. But he didn't. He thought, if he could catch that intruder alone, he could prove he wasn't just "agile" but "excellent," he could get promoted early, could get rid of this olive-green G-rank uniform.

He chased after it.

Then, fell into a trap.

That "intruder" wasn't a fleeing civilian at all, but an elite from the Agent Bureau, a hunter who deliberately left traces waiting for him to take the bait.

"You're too arrogant," the interrogator said, "you think your agility makes you invincible?"

He wanted to defend himself, but those words swirled in his throat, ultimately turning into a soundless sigh.

The metal door emitted a harsh sound.

G-612 jerked open his eyes, black pupils flashing with a hunter's light in the dimness. His body instinctively tensed, heart beginning to accelerate.

The door opened, a dazzling ray of light shot in.

Two staff members walked in, dragging a limp body.

It was G-203, that yellow dog. He had already "performed" and was now back alive—or rather, once alive. His body was limp, like a toy with its bones removed, tear stains still hanging from the corners of his eyes, those yellow eyes half-open, as if asking "why."

G-612 looked at that dog, a strange sorrow welling up in his heart.

That dog was too young, too innocent, paying the price of life just for a mistake. And himself, at least had choices, had the thrill of chasing prey, had the freedom of wind brushing through fur.

"Your turn," the staff member said, "Mr. Leopard."

**Scene-2 The Dog's Afterimage**

G-612 was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along the white corridor.

His legs were powerful, muscles tense; if not for being bound, he could have rushed out like the wind, could have disappeared into the darkness like a shadow. But now, he could only be dragged along, like a tamed beast.

That sense of powerlessness made him feel unprecedented anger.

On both sides of the corridor walls, there were faint traces—scratch marks, bloodstains, and that kind unable to be washed away, traces of despair. G-612's gaze swept over those traces, a strange resonance welling up in his heart.

How many hunters had walked this path?

How many leopard-kind like him, had once thought their speed could change fate, ultimately could only become "auction items" in this underground auction house?

His gaze fell on one of the traces—a deep scratch mark, like something with sharp claws had left it. Tiger? Leopard? Or one he was about to leave himself?

"Leopard-kind is fast," the staff member on the left muttered, "but no matter how fast, on stage they'll soften up."

"Last time that leopard-kind, struggled for ten minutes before finishing," the one on the right said, "whole body sensitive, reacts especially strongly when stimulated."

G-612 listened to their conversation, bitter taste welling up in his heart.

Whole body sensitive.

That was leopard-kind's characteristic, their advantage, and also their curse. Every hair could sense the slightest change in airflow, every inch of skin could capture the most subtle touch.

In the wild, this sensitivity made them the most excellent hunters.

Here, this sensitivity would become their greatest torment.

He thought of his father, thought of that equally agile leopard elder. Father once told him: "Speed is your weapon, but also your weakness. Those who are too fast often can't see the traps ahead."

He didn't believe it at the time. He thought, as long as he was fast enough, nothing could trap him.

But now, he finally understood father's words.

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes.

The staff members stopped, adjusted their posture, then pushed open the door.

Light.

Sound.

Smell.

Everything rushed in at once, like a heavy punch hitting G-612's senses.

He was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining directly down, illuminating his body with nowhere to hide. That strong light made him squint, adapting to the sudden brightness.

Then, he saw the surrounding environment clearly.

The audience. Hundreds upon hundreds of gazes fell on him.

Those gazes held curiosity, contempt, anticipation, and that hunting excitement which made him want to vomit.

He raised his head, facing those gazes.

In his heart, there was only a burning anger.

He was a leopard, he wouldn't cry like that dog, wouldn't submit like that ox.

He would maintain dignity until the final moment.

**Scene-3 Display and Scars**

"Ladies and gentlemen!"

That voice rang out, resonant and cheerful, like an experienced variety show host.

"Today's sixth auction item is a real 'agility-type' contestant!"

The fox host walked out from the side of the stage, his white suit sparkling under the spotlight. His steps were light, face carrying a professional smile, gaze sweeping over the audience, finally landing on G-612.

"G-612, black panther beastman, employed for eight months!" The fox's voice was infectious, "One day ago, he discovered traces of an intruder, and then—made a very 'heroic' decision."

He deliberately paused, letting the audience's attention concentrate more.

"He decided to pursue alone!"

A burst of snickering came from the audience.

"And the result?" The fox spread his hands, making a helpless expression, "He fell into a trap, was subdued by the enemy, tied up and left at the scene. When the company's reinforcements arrived, what they saw wasn't a hero, but a black panther tied up like a rice dumpling!"

More laughter.

G-612 felt blood rush to his cheeks, shame flooding over him like a tide. His body trembled under the spotlight, not from fear, but from anger—at himself, at this system, at this absurd "trial."

"Let's take a look at this 'agility-type contestant's' physical condition," the fox walked to G-612's front, beginning to unbutton his uniform.

"First, the chest."

The fox's palm pressed against G-612's chest, forcefully pressing.

That black short fur trembled slightly beneath the olive-green uniform, pectoral muscle lines smooth and firm. Leopard-kind muscles weren't as angular as tiger-kind, but flowed like water, every block carefully polished, forming that cheetah-like streamlined physique. The fox's fingers sank into that layer of muscle, feeling the power and temperature beneath.

"These pectorals," the fox exclaimed, "firm and smooth, like a carefully carved work of art. Leopard-kind pectorals are truly beautiful, feel as smooth as silk."

His fingers slid across that layer of muscle, from collarbone all the way to sternum, feeling the texture of every muscle. G-612 could clearly feel every detail of that touch—the fox's fingers first touching black short fur, then pressing into muscle, that transition from surface to depth made his skin break out in goosebumps.

"Look here," the fox's fingers stopped below his left chest, where there was a bruise, "this is a trace from hitting a branch during the chase. What a heroic proof!"

He pressed down hard.

G-612 emitted a muffled hum, filtered by the gag into vague whimpering. That bruise was from when he was ambushed, that blow had broken two of his ribs at the time, although simply treated, being pressed now still sent sharp pain.

"Hurt a lot, right?" the fox said in a low voice, only G-612 could hear, "this is the price of agility."

His hand continued to move, coming to the abdomen.

"The abs here," the fox exclaimed, "six blocks, clearly visible. Feel hard as a board, but—"

His fingers suddenly pressed hard, pressing around the navel.

"Very soft here, very vulnerable."

G-612's body fiercely trembled. That was one of his most sensitive areas, when suddenly pressed, a strange sensation spread throughout his body—not pure pain, but a complex experience mixed with pain and other feelings.

"Turn around," the fox commanded.

G-612 was forcibly turned around, back facing the audience.

"Look at this back," the fox's palm slid across his spine, from shoulders all the way to waist, "smooth lines, well-proportioned muscle. Leopard-kind's back lines are truly beautiful, like a cheetah preparing to pounce."

His fingers stopped below the scapula, where there was a long abrasion.

"This scar," the fox said loudly, "was scratched by barbed wire, right? A trace from escaping?"

He forcefully slapped that scar.

G-612's body trembled violently, pain making him almost unable to stand steady. A suppressed wail emitted from his gag-blocked mouth.

"Look, trembling so much," the fox said to the audience with a smile, "this is leopard-kind agility—even trembling is so elegant."

His hand continued downward, coming to the buttocks, forcefully slapping.

"Buttocks tight, thighs long. This physique in the wild would be a top hunter."

Then, he grabbed the base of G-612's tail—that was one of leopard-kind's most sensitive areas.

"Tail," the fox said, "leopard-kind's pride, the source of balance, and also—"

He forcefully pinched.

"The most vulnerable place."

G-612 emitted a sharp whimper, body instinctively arching, like a fully drawn bow.

"So sensitive," the fox let go, turning to the audience, "looks like our Mr. Leopard can't wait anymore."

**Scene-4 Unsold and Fate**

"Ladies and gentlemen!" the fox announced loudly, "such an 'agile work of art,' starting price—1 RUB!"

Silence.

No one raised their hand.

G-612 knew no one would. He closed his eyes, waiting for that inevitable word.

"1 RUB!" the fox repeated, "anyone want this 'lone wolf'?"

Silence.

"3! 2! 1!"

"Alright—unsold!"

That word fell, like a heavy hammer.

"Unsold auction items will have their fate decided by the wheel," the fox's voice was still cheerful, "let's see what kind of ending this 'agile hunter' will face!"

The screen lit up, the wheel spun.

G-612 watched that spinning disc, silently praying in his heart—whatever it is, faster, don't be too painful.

The wheel stopped.

The pointer pointed to a special area.

"Multi-area simultaneous stimulation!" the fox excitedly announced, "looks like our Mr. Leopard is going to experience the feeling of whole-body sensitivity!"

G-612 was fixed to a metal frame, limbs pulled apart. His uniform was completely undone, revealing his lean body. The spotlight shone on his skin, making that layer of black short fur flicker with faint light.

"Leopard-kind's sensitive points are spread throughout the body," the fox said, "let's enjoy thoroughly."

Three staff members stepped forward.

The first person used a specially made soft brush, beginning to lightly brush his whole body fur. That gentle touch, for fur-sensitive leopard-kind, was an indescribable experience—not pain, but more unbearable than pain. Every hair was screaming, every inch of skin was burning.

The second person used fingers to press his abdomen, drawing circles on those six abdominal muscles, occasionally pressing hard around the navel. That touch brought sensations like electric current spreading throughout his body.

The third person was responsible for the lower body and rear hole.

G-612 felt his whole body burning.

He had never experienced such a feeling—whole body sensitive points stimulated simultaneously, that feeling wasn't simple superposition, but geometric growth. His consciousness was drifting away, body trembling, that inevitable endpoint was rapidly approaching.

"Look," the fox said to the audience, "leopard-kind's whole body is sensitive points, this is the price of agility."

The stimulation continued.

G-612 felt that accumulation reaching its peak.

Then, release.

His body spasmed on the frame, all pride collapsing at this moment. He ejaculated six times, each time accompanied by painful convulsions. Then, his heart stopped beating.

On his face, was a peaceful relief.

**Scene-5 Curtain Fall**

"Confirmed death," the staff member checked, "death by semen exhaustion."

"Thank you for G-612's performance!" the fox applauded.

G-612's corpse was untied, dragged away.

On his face, that hunter's light finally disappeared, replaced by a peaceful serenity.

He was once a leopard, a hunter, the wind.

Now, he was nothing.

Just a number, G-612, already cancelled.

His pectorals were firm and smooth, black short fur covering his entire body, feeling smooth as silk. Six abdominal muscles on his abdomen were clearly visible, but not as angular as tiger-kind, rather flowing lines. Back muscles were well-proportioned, buttocks tight, thighs long and powerful, calf muscles developed.

His claws were sharp, black eyes always burning with confident light. His ears were keen, able to hear the most subtle sounds.

He always carried a mixed scent of wildness, speed, and confidence—that was the mark of leopard-kind.

Ying Zhao had been fast since childhood.

In the tribe, he was the fastest child, no one could catch up to him. He thought, as long as he was fast enough, nothing could trap him.

At eighteen, he joined Beast Shield.

He thought, if he could prove his speed here, he could rise above others, could become a legend.

Eight months of training made him even more confident. He was the fastest in his cohort, whether in sprints or obstacle courses, no one could beat him. He began to believe he was special, different, destined to become a legend.

He discovered traces of an intruder.

According to the rules, he should have reported it, waited for backup. But he didn't.

He thought, if he could catch that intruder alone, he could prove he wasn't just fast, but excellent. He could get promoted early, could get rid of this olive-green G-rank uniform, could become E-rank, become R-rank, become one of those advanced ranks.

Then, fell into a trap.

That "intruder" wasn't a fleeing civilian at all, nor an ordinary bounty hunter. That was an elite from the Agent Bureau, a hunter who deliberately left traces waiting for him to take the bait.

A heavy blow, darkness, then being tied up, being left at the scene, being discovered by the company's reinforcements, being treated as a "failed actor who acted on his own" rather than a "hero who bravely pursued."

On the auction block, he finally understood—speed can't save everything.

Sometimes, stopping to wait requires more wisdom than blindly chasing.

But he understood too late.

### Story Reflections

This chapter is a story about "arrogance."

Ying Zhao's tragedy lies not in his speed, but in him believing too much in his own strength.

Under this system, no matter how strong individual ability is, it cannot resist the violence of the system. His agility made him an excellent hunter, but also made him easier prey to capture.

Because of confidence, so underestimating the enemy.

Because of underestimating the enemy, so failure.

This is a warning to all arrogant people—before absolute power, individual ability is insignificant.

But Ying Zhao's story is also a story about growth. In the final moment, he finally understood father's words—speed is your weapon, but also your weakness.

Those closed eyes are a farewell to the past, and also a longing for wisdom.

If I could do it over, I would choose to wait.

This sentence is a lesson all young people should remember.
