**Scene-1 Dignity in the Waiting Area**

In the darkness, G-567 sat quietly, like a golden statue.

He was a lion beastman, male lion race, with a burly and majestic build. Even in the dim light, one could see his distinctive temperament—thick golden mane covering his neck and chest, broad shoulders as if capable of bearing the weight of the entire world, those amber eyes that once burned with the flames of a king.

But now, those flames had dimmed, leaving only embers faintly flickering in the darkness.

His hands were tied behind his back, the nylon restraint strap biting deep into the skin of his wrists. For lion-kind, accustomed to commanding others, this binding was a great irony—he was once the one giving orders, now reduced to a prisoner. The rough nylon fibers rubbed against the fine golden down on his wrists; every slight struggle brought deeper marks, like mockery carved into him by fate.

His mouth was sealed, a rubber ball stuffed inside. He had tried to bite through it with his teeth, but the restraint strap prevented his jaw from fully exerting force. He could only emit low whimpering, that sound like the final roar of a trapped male lion echoing in the empty waiting room, yet no one listened.

He could smell the odors in the air—the scent of sweat, blood, fear, and that deathly odor he detested. These smells intertwined, like an invisible net tightly wrapping around him. But within this turbid air, he could still distinguish his own scent—that of sun-drenched grasslands, mixed with the unique musk of lion-kind, a majestic and dignified fragrance belonging to a king.

The first eight were already dead.

Wolf, bear, tiger, ox, dog, leopard, boar, deer—they had all walked that white corridor, stepped onto that circular stage, and walked toward their end amidst the audience's laughter. Every footstep was like a death knell striking his heart, reminding him of the fate to come.

Now, it was his turn.

His crime was disobeying orders.

A week ago, his squad received a suicide mission—attacking an outpost controlled by the Agent Bureau. The mission was clearly a death sentence; even the lowest grunt could see that. The outpost's defenses were tight, its firepower far exceeding his squad's capacity, and the superior's order letter didn't even include a retreat plan. But his superior insisted on executing it because it was a "company decision."

G-567 refused.

He couldn't watch his subordinates march to their deaths. He tried to reason with his superior, tried to fight for a more reasonable plan, but received only a cold response: "Execute the order, or become a traitor."

He chose to protect his subordinates.

Then, he was arrested.

"You disobeyed the company's orders," the interrogator said. "Your 'mercy' is a challenge to company authority."

He wanted to defend himself, to say he just wanted to protect lives, that the company's decision was flawed. But the interrogator didn't give him a chance.

"In this world," the interrogator said coldly, "no one cares about your 'mercy.'"

Those words were like a knife, deeply piercing his heart. Not out of fear, but out of sorrow—sorrow for the cruelty of this world, sorrow that kindness could actually be a crime under this system.

**Scene-2 Eye Contact with the Traitor**

The metal door emitted a harsh sound.

G-567 raised his head, seeing G-891—that white deer, being dragged out. His steps were light, his gaze calm, like a saint walking toward the altar. The door closed behind him, cutting off all light and sound.

Only three people remained in the waiting room.

G-567, G-734, and...

No, now only he and that fox remained.

G-567 turned his head, amber eyes flickering with faint light in the darkness, looking at that red fox.

G-734 restlessly shifted his body, those narrow fox eyes wandering everywhere, as if looking for a crack to escape, or as if avoiding some invisible judgment. He was the last of this batch, and also the one with the most despicable crime—betrayal, selling out companions. To exchange for his own survival, he had pushed companions who tried to escape together into the abyss.

G-567 looked down on him.

As a king, as a protector, what he hated most was betrayal. That fox had sold out companions who tried to escape together for the sake of his own life. This behavior, in the eyes of lion-kind, was the most unforgivable sin. The honor of lion-kind lay in protection, in sacrifice for the group, not in scavenging for survival like a rat in the cracks.

The fox seemed to sense his gaze, turning his head, eyes carrying a trace of pleading, a trace of defense, and a trace of uncontrollable fear.

"Wu—" G-734 tried to make a sound, as if seeking comfort, or as if defending himself. His voice was sharp and trembling, like a candle flame flickering in the wind, about to extinguish at any moment.

G-567 turned his head, no longer looking at him.

He had no need to communicate with such a person.

He was a lion, a king; even in death, he would maintain his dignity. And that fox was merely a rat in the gutter, not even qualified to speak with him.

But even so, a complex emotion welled up in G-567's heart. Not out of sympathy, but out of a deeper sorrow—sorrow that this world could actually breed such betrayers, sorrow that trust and loyalty were so fragile before life and death.

He closed his eyes, banishing the fox's existence from his consciousness.

In his heart, there were only those subordinates he had protected. Their faces emerged one by one—the young wolf lad, always full of energy; the dog-kind girl; the silent but reliable bear-kind strongman... Were they safe now? Did they know he had sacrificed for them?

These questions had no answers, but they gave him his final comfort.

**Scene-3 The King's Body**

G-567 lowered his head, looking at his own body. Even in the darkness, he could feel the existence of this body—this body that once carried countless glories and responsibilities.

His height was close to 1.9 meters, a burly existence among beastmen. But this wasn't that kind of bloated hugeness; it was a sense of power tempered through countless trials. Every inch of muscle was like a work carefully carved by a sculptor, with distinct lines and explosive power.

His golden mane was the most dazzling presence in this room, still flickering with sun-like luster even in the dim light. That mane was thick and rough, extending from his neck all the way to his chest, like the cloak of an ancient king, symbolizing power and majesty. When he was young, his mother had told him that the mane of lion-kind was a gift from the sun; every hair carried the warmth and freedom of the grasslands.

Now, that mane was slightly dampened by sweat, sticking to his skin, losing its former fluffiness and pride. But one could still see its texture—coarse and resilient, like a king's armor, never losing its essence even in a cage.

His shoulders were astonishingly broad, like a mountain, once bearing the trust and dependence of countless subordinates. Those young beastman soldiers had once sought shelter behind him, believing that as long as they hid behind this broad back, no danger could harm them.

His chest was thick and full, pectorals still clearly outlined beneath the uniform's restraint. There was a scar above his right chest, left when he protected a young G-rank grunt. That knife had almost taken his life at the time, but he didn't retreat, because behind him were people who needed his protection.

That scar had healed now, but still protruded, like a winding river, recording his former heroism. He often touched that scar in the quiet of night, not to show off, but to remind himself—protecting others was his responsibility as a king.

His abdomen had six distinct abdominal muscles, every block like carefully cut rock, hard and powerful. That was the crystallization of countless training sessions and battles, a symbol of strength and discipline.

His back was broad and thick, like a city wall, capable of withstanding all attacks. The muscle lines on both sides of the spine were smooth and powerful, extending from the neck all the way to the waist, forming a perfect inverted-triangle physique.

His buttocks were full and firm, thighs thick as pillars, calf muscle lines distinct. This physique on a normal battlefield was an existence that inspired awe—a single charge could tear through the enemy's defenses; a single roar could boost morale.

His tail was bound behind him, one of lion-kind's most sensitive areas, and also the source of balance. The fur on the tail was equally golden, forming an elegant curve at the end.

His skin showed a healthy bronze color, the result of long-term outdoor training and mission execution. The blood flowing beneath his skin was still warm, his heart still powerful, but all this would soon end.

He closed his eyes, taking a deep breath.

The air held the smell of dust, the coldness of metal, the shadow of death. But he could still smell his own scent—that mixture of sunshine, grasslands, and kingly aura, the unique majestic fragrance of lion-kind.

Even in this cage, he was still a king.

Even facing imminent death, his dignity had never faded.

**Scene-4 Echoes of Memory**

Fragments of memory emerged in the darkness, like a lake illuminated by moonlight, sparkling with ripples.

He remembered his childhood, running on the grasslands. The sky was so blue then, the grass so green, the wind carrying the scent of freedom brushing through his mane. He was the youngest cub in the pride, but already showed the potential of a king—brave, just, protecting the weak.

"Golden Mane," his father once called him, "you have the most golden mane, dazzling like the sun. Remember, a true king fights not for himself, but to protect others."

That name—Golden Mane, how long had it been since anyone called him that?

In his two years at Beast Shield, he was just a number, G-567. No name, no past, only orders and missions. But he never forgot his father's teachings, never forgot his responsibility as a king.

He remembered the first time he led a squad on a mission. It was a group of young beastmen, most newly employed, their eyes still carrying greenness and fear. It was he who stood before them, saying in a steady voice: "Follow me, I will protect you."

That sentence was not an empty promise, but a king's oath.

In the two years that followed, he countless times practiced this oath. Every mission, he charged at the front; every danger, he blocked before his subordinates. That scar on his right chest, that burn on his back, that knife wound on his arm—every scar was proof of protection, a mark of responsibility.

He thought the company would see his dedication, would understand his choices.

But he was wrong.

In this system, mercy was a weakness, protection was a betrayal. Company authority was above everything, including life, including justice, including humanity.

When he refused to execute that suicide mission, there was no surprise in his superior's eyes, only cold disgust.

"Who do you think you are?" the superior said. "A G-rank grunt, daring to disobey company orders?"

"I'm not disobeying," he tried to explain, "I just want to seek a more reasonable plan. My subordinates will die; that outpost simply can't be taken—"

"Shut up!" The superior interrupted him. "Company decisions don't need your questioning. Execute, or die."

He fell silent.

Then, he chose protection.

Not because he wasn't afraid of death, but because he couldn't watch those young people who trusted him march to their deaths. They were his responsibility, his oath, the meaning of his existence as a king.

At the moment of arrest, he didn't resist.

Not because of submission, but because he knew resistance would only hurt more people. He calmly accepted the shackles, calmly walked toward the prison van, calmly awaited fate's judgment.

In the interrogation room, he explained again and again, pleaded again and again, hoping the company could understand his original intention. But what responded to him were only cold walls and even colder gazes.

"Your 'mercy' is a challenge to company authority," the interrogator said. "In this world, no one cares about your 'mercy.'"

That sentence was like a knife, cutting his heart into pieces.

Not out of fear, but out of despair—despair at the cruelty of this world, despair that kindness was so powerless before authority.

But even so, he still didn't regret it.

Because a true king is not one who obeys orders, but one who protects the weak.

Even if this world didn't understand him, even if his sacrifice was remembered by no one, he still chose justice.

This was his dignity, his glory, his final persistence as a lion king.

**Scene-5 Dignity Before Going On Stage**

He just calmly stood up, using his own strength to support his body, lightening the burden on those two people. Even on the road toward death, he still maintained a king's demeanor.

He was dragged out of the waiting area, proceeding along that white corridor. The walls on both sides of the corridor were spotless white, like a road leading to heaven, but he knew what awaited at the end was not salvation, but the end.

His steps were heavy and slow, even while being held up, more dignified than the other auction items. Every step was like treading on a drum surface, emitting dull echoes. Those echoes spread in the empty corridor, like a silent elegy, escorting his soon-to-fade life.

At the end of the corridor, that heavy soundproof door appeared before his eyes. Behind the door were hundreds of gazes, mockery, banter, cold observation.

The staff members pushed open the door.

Light, sound, smell, everything rushed in at once.

The blinding spotlight, the noise of the audience, the perfume and sweat permeating the air—all this was like a heavy hammer, striking his senses. But G-567 didn't retreat, didn't blink, just calmly raised his head, facing that light.

G-567 was pushed to the center of the stage, the spotlight shining directly down.

He raised his head, facing hundreds upon hundreds of gazes.

Those gazes held curiosity, awe, and also a trace of imperceptible regret. They looked at him as if looking at a trapped beast, a male lion about to be sacrificed.

He was tonight's most awe-inspiring auction item—not because his crime was so serious, but because he was once a king. His existence itself was a kind of irony toward this absurd auction.

Jin Zong's tragedy lies not in his disobedience, but in his mercy finding no understanding in this world. He was tonight's most awe-inspiring auction item—the only one who sacrificed himself to protect others.

His death is the most profound accusation against this system.

When mercy is seen as betrayal, when protection is seen as disobedience, this world has already lost its justice. But Jin Zong's peace is comfort to all kind people—even if the world is cruel, mercy still has meaning.

Because the subordinates he protected will continue to pass on this kindness.

That is hope.

Jin Zong's story tells us that true dignity lies not in others' recognition, but in inner persistence. Even if humiliated, even if trampled, as long as the heart doesn't waver, one is still a king.

His golden mane will forever shine in the hearts of those he protected.

Like the sun.

Never dimming.
