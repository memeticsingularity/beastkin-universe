Next, he grabbed Little Deer under the armpits and used all his strength to drag this taller deer beastman to the deepest shadow beneath the innermost bed—where the shadows were darkest and not easily visible from the door. He stuffed Little Deer's body under the bed, arranging him in a side-lying posture so his breathing could be smoother.

After doing this, A-Zhong was already drenched in sweat. His blue tight shirt was soaked through, clinging to his skin and outlining his lean but solid muscle contours. He panted, leaning against the bed frame, looking at Little Deer's vague outline in the shadows beneath the bed.

Then he walked back to the sink and began frantically washing socks.

He no longer scrubbed carefully, only shoving the socks into the water, rubbing them roughly a few times, wringing them dry, and throwing them onto the rack. His movements were fast to the point of frenzy, water splashing everywhere, soap bubbles flying all over. His arm muscles tensed, blue veins protruding, every wringing motion using all his strength, as if venting all his anger, fear, and helplessness into these dirty socks.

Sock after sock was washed and folded.

The mountain in the sink rapidly shrank.

A-Zhong didn't know how long he had washed. His mind was blank, only mechanical motions. Wash, wring, fold. Wash, wring, fold. Sweat streamed down his cheeks, dropping into the water. His breathing was heavy, echoing in the silent room.

Until the last pair of socks was wrung dry, folded, and placed on the rack.

The sink was empty.

A-Zhong stopped, both hands propped on the sink edge, head down, gasping heavily. His arms trembled, fingertips wrinkled and white from prolonged soaking. The blue gloves were soaked through, hanging heavily on his hands.

He slowly straightened up and looked at the room.

The floor had been wiped, though some water stains remained, but it was mostly clean. All the dirty socks were washed, neatly folded on the rack. Little Deer's body was hidden in the deepest shadow beneath the bed, hard to spot from the door unless one looked closely.

As for Bear Big and Boar Gang's demand to "dispose of the body"—he couldn't do it. He couldn't throw Little Deer out, nor could he actually kill him.

He could only gamble.

Gamble that Bear Big and Boar Gang wouldn't check carefully. Gamble that they would be satisfied seeing the room clean and the socks washed. Gamble that Little Deer could hold on until they left.

But the stakes were too high.
