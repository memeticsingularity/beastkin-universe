Chapter 11: The Wager on the Tenth Floor
Scene-1: A Filthy Routine
The air on the tenth floor was more dense than downstairs.

Not physically dense, but a viscosity woven from smell, sound, and emotion. The corridor walls were covered in pale blue wallpaper, with recessed ceiling lights every five meters casting cold white illumination onto the floor's occasional stains—possibly oil, possibly some dried liquid. This floor was the O-level supervisor activity zone, the den of the blue uniforms.

At the end of the corridor, turn left, the third door was slightly ajar.

Inside was a twelve-person collective lounge, but currently only four beastmen lived there. The room held four sets of bunk beds. The two lower bunks by the window were piled with clutter: a few unopened instant noodle boxes, some curled magazines, a pile of crumpled green uniforms—used for the occasional disguise. In the center of the room floor lay a few empty aluminum drink cans, their surfaces reflecting the sickly white light from the old fluorescent tube on the ceiling.

The sink was in the innermost corner of the room.

It was a stainless steel single basin, its edges already somewhat yellowed, with a ring of gray-white scale accumulated below the faucet. Currently, a dozen or so white knee-high socks soaked in it, the blue stripes at the cuffs drifting slightly in the water. The water's surface floated a thin layer of oil, gleaming with rainbow colors under the light.

A-Zhong stood before the sink.

He was a German Shepherd beastman, his build medium-to-lean among O-level supervisors—not skinny, but lean. The blue uniform short-sleeve tight shirt wrapped his upper body, showing clear pectoral contours, but not as thick and full as those of bear or ox clans. Rather, it leaned toward well-defined lines. His arm muscles were firm, biceps bulging into an obvious arc when bent. His pants were also blue tight shorts, white vertical stripes on the sides. The waistband hugged above his hip bones, outlining a flat lower abdomen and slightly hollowed waist curves. His calves were tightly wrapped in white knee-high socks, the blue stripe at the cuff sitting two finger-widths below the knee. The socks were new, almost unworn.

A-Zhong was washing socks.

His movements were slow and careful. His left hand fished a soaked sock from the water. His right hand squeezed out a small lump of laundry detergent, dotting the sole, cuff, and toe. Then he began rubbing. His fingers were long, knuckles distinct, the blue veins on the back of his hand slightly protruding when he rubbed. Soap bubbles surged from between his fingers, flowing down his wrist and leaving white trails on his skin.

The water was warm.

The pipe hot water system supplied twenty-four hours a day, an O-level-and-above perk. Warm water dissolved sweat stains better, but it also made the complex stench on the socks evaporate more effectively, filling the air.

A-Zhong didn't frown.

He was used to it. Or rather, he forced himself to be used to it. He wrung the washed sock dry, water droplets falling back into the basin with a patter, splashing small ripples. Then he turned and hung it on a simple drying rack nearby—just a stainless steel pipe stretched between two beds.

Just as he turned, the door was kicked open.

Not pushed. Kicked. The door panel slammed against the wall with a thunderous bang. The fluorescent tube shook with the impact, light and shadow dancing across the walls.

Two figures walked in.

The first was a bear beastman.

He was called Bear Big, number O-5002. His build was one and a half times A-Zhong's width—not pure obesity, but a thick fat layer wrapping rock-like muscle groups. The blue uniform short sleeve on him was stretched to its absolute limit. The armpits and back were already soaked dark with sweat, the fabric pulled into several deep horizontal wrinkles at the full pectorals and broad shoulder blades. His neck was thick as a tree trunk, his Adam's apple prominent, rolling with his heavy breathing.

Bear Big's face was very wide, his nose bridge collapsed, his lips thick. His eyes were small, sunk deep in the shadows cast by his brow ridges, currently squinting as his gaze swept the room and finally landed on A-Zhong.

The second was a wild boar beastman.

Boar Gang, number O-6098. He was half a head shorter than Bear Big, but his lateral volume was no less. The wild boar beastman's defining trait was an exceptionally broad back and shoulders. His trapezius muscles rose like two small hills on either side of his neck, warping the uniform collar. His arms were short and thick, the biceps and triceps almost fused into one mass, the forearms covered with coarse black short hair.

Boar Gang's face was longer, the corners of his mouth habitually downturned, two yellowed tusks protruding from either side of his lower lip, their tips sharp. His nose was a typical pig nose, nostrils flared, currently snorting with heavy breaths.

Both wore blue uniforms, but theirs were different from A-Zhong's—older, dirtier, cuffs and pant hems frayed and stained. The white socks on their feet had turned gray-yellow, the cuffs loosely bunched at their thick ankles.

"Yo, washing?"

Bear Big spoke, his voice low and hoarse, like sandpaper scraping wood. He walked into the room and tossed his shoulder bag onto a bed with a dull thud.

A-Zhong's body visibly stiffened.

He turned around, hands still wet, soap bubbles dripping from his fingertips. He opened his mouth, wanting to say something, but no sound came out. He only nodded.

Boar Gang sneered.

He walked to his own bed by the door—the lower bunk, sheets crumpled into a ball with a few unwashed uniforms on top. He bent down and began removing his shoes.

They were a pair of dark blue work boots, the uppers caked with dust and unknown stains. He untied the laces roughly and pulled his feet out.

A dense smell immediately exploded in the room.

It was the smell of foot sweat fermented inside sealed shoes for over ten hours—strongly acidic, sour, mixed with the musty odor of damp leather and the wild boar beastman's characteristic earthy fishiness. Boar Gang's feet were large, his toes short and thick, his nail beds packed with black grime. He wore white socks, but the original color was unrecognizable. The sole and heel areas were soaked through with sweat, turning a semi-transparent yellow-brown, the fabric clinging tightly to the skin.

He removed his socks.

Slowly, deliberately slowly. The socks slid from heel to toes with a faint, wet friction sound. When the socks were fully off and pinched in his hand, the smell reached its peak, almost physical, slamming into A-Zhong so hard he instinctively held his breath.

Boar Gang pinched that wet, stinking sock and shook it as he walked up behind A-Zhong.

"A-Zhong," he said, his voice sharp and completely mismatched with his build, "do me a favor?"

A-Zhong didn't turn around, didn't answer. He only stopped washing. His spine was perfectly straight, but his shoulders were slightly tucked in.

"My training today was especially heavy," Boar Gang continued, holding the sock almost to the side of A-Zhong's face, "sweated several kilograms. If these socks aren't washed right away, they'll be too stiff to wear tomorrow. Wash them for me, will you?"

Silence for a few seconds.

A-Zhong slowly turned around. His face showed little expression, only his eyes were somewhat hollow. He reached out and took the sock.

The moment his fingers touched it, he felt the warm wetness and unpleasant slickness. The sock was heavy, saturated with sweat.

"Thanks." Boar Gang clapped A-Zhong's shoulder with enough force to make him sway. Then he turned back to remove his other shoe.

Bear Big also walked over and began removing his shoes. His movements were slower, more unhurried, carrying an overbearing sense of superiority. He untied his laces, gripped the heel, and pulled his foot out bit by bit.

Bear Big's feet were even larger.

His soles were broad and thick, his insteps high-arched, his five toes thick as small radishes, their nails thick and yellowed. His socks were slightly cleaner than Boar Gang's, but the ankle areas were already worn through, exposing the skin beneath.

He removed his sock, pinched it in his hand, and looked at A-Zhong.

A-Zhong was still washing Boar Gang's sock.

His hands were shaking. Though only slightly, the soap bubbles were popping faster at his fingertips. He kept his head down, staring at the water in the basin, not daring to look up.

Bear Big walked over and tossed the sock into the sink.

A splash. The sock sank into the water, splashing foam onto A-Zhong's face.

A-Zhong closed his eyes.

The water was warm, slick with soap, and carried that inescapable stench. He raised his hand and wiped his face with the back of it, but the smell had already adhered to his skin.

"Wash them good," Bear Big said, his voice right beside A-Zhong's ear. "Wash and dry them. I'm wearing them tomorrow."

A-Zhong nodded.

His nod was so slight it was almost invisible. Then he reached into the water and fished out Bear Big's sock. That sock was even thicker, the fabric coarser, heavy as a wet rag when soaked.

He began scrubbing.

His fingers pressed hard, knuckles white. Soap bubbles surged from between his fingers, more and more, covering his entire palm. The stench intensified during the scrubbing, the sour foot odor mixing with the soap's chemical fragrance into a nauseating compound smell.

Rustling sounds filled the room. Bear Big and Boar Gang were changing clothes. They peeled off their sweat-soaked blue uniforms—the inner layers wet and clinging to their skin, making sticky peeling sounds as they came off. They stood shirtless, exposing thick, fur-covered pectorals and round bellies. Bear Big's chest hair was dense, spreading from his chest to his abdomen, glistening with oil under the light. Boar Gang's skin was rougher, his pores large, covered with scars of varying depths.

They took clean uniforms from their lockers and began changing.

Throughout this, A-Zhong kept washing socks.

He finished Boar Gang's first sock, wrung it, and hung it. Then Bear Big's first sock, wrung it, and hung it. Then the second. Then the third.

More and more socks accumulated in the sink.

Some were his own. Some had been piled there for days. Some had just been thrown in by Bear Big and Boar Gang. The white socks floated and sank in the water like a school of dead, bloated fish.

The water grew dirtier.

What had been clear water turned grayish-white, the surface floating with tiny bubbles and skin flakes. The drain clogged again, and A-Zhong had to reach in to clear it—sometimes a ball of fur, sometimes a thread shed from the socks.

His arms were aching.

Maintaining the same posture for so long, his shoulder and upper arm muscles began protesting. But he didn't stop. He only slowed his pace. One. Then another. Continuing to scrub.

Then, sounds came from the other end of the room.

The locker door opening and closing. Footsteps.

A-Zhong looked up.

It was Little Deer.

Deer beastman, number O-8261. Promoted at the same time as A-Zhong. His build was on the lean side for an O-level, but his muscle lines were clear and smooth, giving him a lean and capable look. His blue uniform was worn meticulously: pant legs tucked into socks, sleeve buttons fastened, looking much cleaner than Bear Big and Boar Gang. He was putting a few folded uniforms into his locker, then closing the door and turning to leave.

"Stop."

Bear Big's voice. Not loud, but carrying his habitual commanding tone.

Little Deer halted and turned around. His deer-clan eyes were large and clear under the light. He looked at Bear Big without speaking.

"Where to?" Bear Big asked. He had finished changing into his clean uniform and was sitting on the edge of his bed slowly tying his shoelaces.

"On duty. My shift." Little Deer's voice was calm, betraying no emotion.

"What's the rush." Boar Gang interjected. He too had changed and was standing before a small mirror on the wall fixing his hair—or rather, the few stiff tufts he had, which he was combing with a comb. "Come here. Got something."

Little Deer hesitated a moment, then walked over and stopped in the center of the room, a few paces from Bear Big and Boar Gang. He was a head shorter than both, his frame smaller, but he stood perfectly straight.

"What is it?" he asked.

Bear Big finished tying his laces and looked up, his gaze brushing from Little Deer's face to his chest, then down to his lower body, finally stopping at his feet. Little Deer's white socks were snowy, almost blinding, pulled up high with neat edges.

"Socks look clean," Bear Big said, his tone neither praising nor criticizing.

Little Deer didn't reply.

Boar Gang walked over and circled Little Deer once, looking him over like merchandise. "They really are clean," he clicked his tongue. "Look at the soles. Not a speck of dust. What, did you fly today?"

"I changed them before my shift," Little Deer answered.

"Oh—changed them." Boar Gang dragged out the syllable. He walked up to Little Deer, less than half a meter between them. He was taller and broader, a wall of a beastman. "Perfect. I've been wearing this pair all day. Training ground, patrol route, cafeteria, ran all over. Now they're wet and stink. Don't feel like washing them."

He paused, staring into Little Deer's eyes.

"Wash them for me."

Little Deer's breathing stopped for an instant.

His face still showed no expression, but A-Zhong saw his hand clench, finger joints slightly whitening.

"Wash your own socks," Little Deer said, his voice still calm, but with something added—hardness, edges.

The room's air froze.

Bear Big stopped adjusting his shoelaces. The smile vanished from Boar Gang's face. A-Zhong stood by the sink, the sock in his hand falling back into the water with a faint splash.

A few seconds of silence.

Then Boar Gang laughed.

It wasn't a normal laugh, but a phlegmy sneer squeezed from the throat.

"What did you say?" he asked, his voice very light, but each word bitten clearly.

"I said," Little Deer repeated, enunciating each word, "wash your own socks."

Boar Gang said nothing.

He stared at Little Deer, eyes narrowing into slits. In the room, only the fluorescent tube's buzzing current sound and the faint water flow in the pipes could be heard.

Then Boar Gang moved.

His speed was fast, so fast that A-Zhong only saw a blur. His left hand shot out, grabbed Little Deer by the collar, and yanked hard!

A loud rip!

Little Deer's blue uniform short sleeve tore from the collar down to the chest in a long gash. The light brown skin beneath was exposed, the pectoral contours clearly visible.

Little Deer tried to back away, but Boar Gang's right hand had already clamped around his arm.

It was a wild boar beastman's hand, fingers short and thick, palm covered with calluses, grip astonishing. Little Deer's arm was caught like it was in a vise, unable to move.

"Say it again?" Boar Gang asked, his face pressed to Little Deer's, their noses almost touching.

Little Deer said nothing.

His lips pressed into a straight line, eyes burning with anger, or fear, or both.

Bear Big stood up.

He walked over slowly, his heavy footsteps making the floor tremble slightly. He came to Little Deer's other side, and together with Boar Gang they flanked him.

"Newbie," Bear Big rumbled, his voice low as distant thunder, "you think putting on this blue skin makes you equal to us?"

Little Deer didn't answer.

"Let me tell you," Bear Big continued, extending a thick finger and poking Little Deer's exposed chest with dull thuds, "here, seniority is everything. I've been here three years longer than you. I've bled and sweated more than you've drunk water. A freshly promoted runt like you needs to learn to bow your head. Got it?"

Little Deer still said nothing.

But his body was trembling. Not from fear, but from anger forcibly suppressed. A-Zhong could see Little Deer's shoulders slightly heaving, the amplitude of his chest rising and falling growing larger.

"Now," Boar Gang said, releasing Little Deer's arm only to instantly grab his wrist, squeezing until the bone creaked, "apologize, wash the socks, and we call it even."

Little Deer looked at him.

Then, very slowly, very clearly, he spat out a word:

"No."

Boar Gang's expression changed.

It was an instant transformation from mockery to rage. His face flushed red, nostrils flaring, spraying coarse breath. The fingers gripping Little Deer's wrist suddenly tightened, nails almost digging into flesh.

"Good," Boar Gang squeezed the word through his teeth. "Very good."

He turned to look at Bear Big.

Bear Big nodded.

That wasn't a nod of agreement, but a tacit confirmation. The two exchanged a glance, then moved simultaneously.

Boar Gang jerked and flung Little Deer to the ground.

Little Deer's body hit the floor with a dull thud. He tried to get up, but Bear Big was already upon him, raising his thick right leg in its clean sock and stomping on Little Deer's chest.

"Ugh!" Little Deer grunted, his sternum creaking ominously under the pressure. Bear Big's weight plus deliberate force instantly made breathing impossible. He grabbed Bear Big's ankle with both hands, trying to push it away, but that foot was rooted like an iron pillar.

Boar Gang walked back to his bed, pulled a standard-issue Beastshield baton from underneath—black rubber over metal, about forty centimeters long, grip textured for anti-slip. He weighed it in his hand and walked back, crouching beside Little Deer.

A-Zhong stood by the sink, unable to move.

His legs were filled with lead, hands still in the water, fingertips already wrinkled and white from soaking. He watched Little Deer on the floor, watched Bear Big's foot stomping on his chest, watched Boar Gang's baton.

He wanted to say something, wanted to do something.

But his throat was tight, unable to emit a sound. His body was shaking, uncontrollably shaking.

Boar Gang crouched down, the baton's tip pressing against Little Deer's lower abdomen, at the crotch position.

"Last chance," Boar Gang said, his voice very soft, almost a whisper, "apologize, wash socks, or—"

He didn't finish, but the meaning was clear.

Little Deer's breathing became rapid.

His eyes stared at the ceiling, the fluorescent tube's light making his pupils contract. His chest rose and fell with difficulty under Bear Big's stomp, each breath carrying trembling.

Then, he turned his head and looked at A-Zhong.

Their eyes met in the air.

Little Deer's eyes were very bright, even in this situation, still very bright. There was much in them—pain, anger, unwillingness, and a trace of... plea for help?

A-Zhong understood that look.

He was saying: Help me.

A-Zhong opened his mouth.

He wanted to say "stop," wanted to say "enough," wanted to say "I'll wash the socks for him." But those words caught in his throat like a ball of cotton, choking him until he couldn't breathe.

His lips moved, but no sound came out.

The light in Little Deer's eyes dimmed.

Boar Gang saw this eye contact.

He smiled, very happily.

"Looks like nobody's helping you," he said, the baton's tip drawing a circle on Little Deer's crotch, "then don't blame me."

He raised the baton.

Not to strike, but to stab.

The baton's rubber tip aimed at the already slightly bulging area at the center of Little Deer's crotch, then thrust down with force.

"Ugh——!"

Little Deer's body arched sharply.

That was an instinctive reaction, extreme pain exploding from his groin and rushing straight up his spine to his brain. His hands released Bear Big's ankle, turning to clutch his own crotch, but Boar Gang's baton was already lodged there, accurately striking the most vulnerable place through the pants.

Bear Big increased the pressure of his stomp.

His foot pressed down hard, Little Deer's chest emitting faint creaking sounds, ribs protesting under the pressure. Little Deer's breathing was completely disrupted, mouth wide open, wanting to inhale, but chest pressed, unable to draw breath.

Boar Gang began to move.

Not stabbing, but grinding.

He gripped the baton, using the tip to grind left and right, press up and down at Little Deer's crotch. The sound of fabric rubbing against skin was very faint, but clearly audible in the quiet room.

Little Deer's body began to tremble violently.

That wasn't voluntary trembling, but a nervous spasm triggered by pain and mixed stimulation. His legs began kicking, heels scraping against the floor, making "rustle" sounds. His hands tried to grab the baton, but Boar Gang easily brushed them aside.

"Feeling good?" Boar Gang asked, excitement in his voice, "This is way more exciting than sparring on the training ground, right?"

Little Deer couldn't speak.

His face had already flushed crimson, forehead veins bulging, sweat sliding from his temples and dripping onto the floor. His eyes were wide open, pupils scattered, unable to focus.

A-Zhong watched all of this.

His nails dug into his palms, breaking skin, but he felt no pain. His mind was completely blank, only vision and hearing passively receiving information:

Little Deer's coarse gasping.

The sound of the baton rubbing fabric.

The slight vibration of the floor when Bear Big's foot stomped.

And that smell—sweat, foot odor, the scent of fear, mixing together, growing stronger.

Boar Gang continued grinding.

His movements were rhythmic, one after another, like grinding something. Little Deer's crotch area was already wet with a large patch, not sweat, but some darker liquid seeping through, blooming into a dark stain on the blue fabric.

Little Deer's kicking motions weakened.

His strength was draining, his body's trembling changing from violent to faint twitching. His hands lay powerlessly spread, fingers slightly bent, fingertips drawing shallow white marks on the floor.

Boar Gang paused.

He withdrew the baton and looked at the tip—it was stained with some dark liquid, possibly blood, possibly something else. He frowned, then used the side of the baton to pat Little Deer's face.

"Not over yet."

He stood up and walked to the sink.

A-Zhong subconsciously retreated a step.

But Boar Gang wasn't coming for him. He reached into the basin and fished out a sock—his own, just taken off, not yet washed, still wet, covered with soap bubbles and foot sweat.

He pinched the sock and walked back to Little Deer's side.

Little Deer's consciousness was already somewhat blurred.

His eyes half-open and half-closed, breathing shallow and rapid, chest barely rising and falling under Bear Big's stomp. His crotch was completely soaked through, the dark blue fabric turning nearly black.

Boar Gang crouched down, pinching that wet sock with two fingers and holding it above Little Deer's face.

The sock was still dripping.

Water droplets fell one by one onto Little Deer's face. The first drop landed on his forehead, sliding along his brow bone to the corner of his eye. The second drop landed on his nose bridge, flowing along his wing to the corner of his mouth. The third drop landed on his lips; Little Deer's lips moved, but no sound came out.

"Smell it," Boar Gang said, his voice very gentle, terrifyingly gentle, "this is the sock you refused to wash. How is it, fragrant?"

He lowered the sock, the toe touching Little Deer's nose.

It was an extremely dense smell—foot sweat fermented inside sealed shoes for over ten hours, mixed with soap's chemical fragrance, and the sock fiber's own odor.

Little Deer's body jolted violently.

That was an instinctive rejection reaction. His head wanted to tilt back, to avoid, but Bear Big's foot was still stomping on his chest, he couldn't move. He could only be forced to smell that odor, feeling the wet sock pressed against his face.

His throat emitted a sound.

Not speaking, but dry heaving.

"Ugh—cough cough—"

His body convulsed, stomach spasming, but there was nothing in his stomach to vomit, only bringing up some acid water and saliva. Those liquids flowed from the corner of his mouth, mixing with the water on his face, sliding along his cheeks to behind his ears.

Boar Gang smiled.

He moved the sock downward, covering Little Deer's nose and mouth.

That wasn't completely suffocating, just covering. The sock fabric pressed against skin, wetness seeping through. Little Deer's breathing became even more difficult—he needed to inhale, but every breath of air carried that dense foot odor.

His body began to struggle violently.

That was the final eruption of survival instinct. His hands again grabbed Bear Big's ankle, pushing hard, legs kicking frantically on the ground, heels striking the floor with "thud thud" dull sounds.

But useless.

Bear Big's weight plus deliberate suppression made him unable to move. Boar Gang's hand steadily pressed the sock, without the slightest loosening.

A-Zhong looked into Little Deer's eyes.

Those deer-clan eyes, currently filled with bloodshot veins. Pupils dilated, gaze scattered, but still able to see what was inside—pain, fear, and... hate.

Hate for Boar Gang and Bear Big.

Hate for A-Zhong.

Hate for the whole world.

A-Zhong averted his gaze.

He didn't dare look. He lowered his head, staring at the socks floating in the sink, staring at the already grayish-white dirty water. His ears still received sounds: Little Deer's increasingly weak struggling sounds, Boar Gang's suppressed laughter, Bear Big's coarse breathing.

Time became very slow.

Every second was stretched, broken, finely ground. A-Zhong could hear his own heartbeat, feel sweat sliding down his back, smell the increasingly strong blood scent and some fishy odor in the air.

Don't know how long passed.

Little Deer's struggles stopped.

His hands released Bear Big's ankle, powerlessly spread on the floor. Legs also stopped kicking, only faintly twitching. The amplitude of his chest rising and falling became very small, almost invisible.

Boar Gang removed the sock.

Little Deer's face was exposed.

His face was covered with water, saliva, and stains left by the sock. The area around his nose and mouth was red, friction marks from the fabric. His eyes were half-open, pupils dilated, no focus.

Boar Gang checked Little Deer's breathing.

Very faint, but still there.

"Not completely dead yet," he said, tone carrying a trace of disappointment, "worthless, can't even die properly."

Bear Big finally removed his foot.

Little Deer's chest immediately rose and fell, mouth wide open, greedily inhaling, but inhaling very shallowly, only a little each time. His body was still faintly twitching, like a fish out of water.

Boar Gang stood up, threw that wet sock onto Little Deer's body.

The sock landed on Little Deer's chest, wet fabric clinging to skin, then sliding to the floor.

"Clean this up," Boar Gang said to A-Zhong, pointing at Little Deer on the floor, "and these socks, all washed. Before we get back, I want this room clean and the socks hung up."

He paused, adding:

"If it's not clean, you know the consequences."

A-Zhong nodded.

His nod was very mechanical, like a wind-up toy. He didn't even know he was nodding, only a bodily instinct reaction.

Bear Big and Boar Gang began changing socks.

They removed the clean socks on their feet—the ones A-Zhong had just washed and dried—and changed into another pair of clean ones. Then they put on shoes and tied laces.

Throughout this, Little Deer lay on the floor, motionless.

His eyes were still open, but there was nothing in them. His chest was rising and falling, but very faintly. His crotch was completely soaked through, dark liquid spreading into a small puddle on the floor.

Bear Big and Boar Gang tidied their clothes and walked to the door.

"We're going to the rooftop for a smoke," Bear Big said, turning back to look at A-Zhong, "about half an hour. We'll be back in half an hour."

The door opened and closed.

Footsteps faded down the corridor, growing lighter, finally disappearing.

Only two people remained in the room.

A-Zhong and Little Deer.

And the smell filling the room.

Scene-2: The Struggle at Death's Door
The fluorescent tube was still buzzing.

That sound was normally almost inaudible, but in absolute silence, it became exceptionally clear. Every buzz of current was like measuring the passage of time.

A-Zhong stood by the sink, motionless.

His hands were still in the water, fingertips already wrinkled and white from soaking, the skin surface covered with a layer of fine wrinkles. The water had gone cold, temperature dropping from warm to near room temperature, that chill spreading up his arms through his fingertips.

He looked at Little Deer on the floor.

Little Deer lay there, like a corpse.

But his chest was still rising and falling. Very slow, very faint, but it was rising and falling. Every breath, chest slightly rising, then falling. That rhythm was very irregular, sometimes fast, sometimes slow, sometimes stopping for a second or two, then continuing.

A-Zhong's legs could finally move.

He took the first step, foot stepping on the floor, making a faint friction sound. Second step, third step. He walked to Little Deer's side and crouched down.

Up close, he could see more clearly.

Little Deer's face was very dirty. Water, saliva, stains mixed together, drying on skin, leaving trails. His nose and mouth area was red, some places already broken skin, oozing fine blood beads.

His eyes were half-open.

Pupils dilated, no focus, but A-Zhong could see a thin layer of water film covering the eyeball surface. Was that tears, or something else?

A-Zhong reached out, wanting to touch Little Deer's face.

But his hand stopped in midair, not falling.

He didn't dare.

He didn't know if Little Deer was alive or dead now, didn't know what would happen if he touched him. He looked at Little Deer's chest, at that faint rising and falling, at that dark wet stain on the crotch.

Then, he saw something else.

Little Deer's hand.

His hand was spread on the floor, fingers slightly bent. But A-Zhong noticed that Little Deer's right index finger was moving.

Very slight movement.

Only the fingertip, tapping on the floor. One after another, very rhythmic.

Morse code?

A-Zhong didn't understand Morse code. But he could tell it was a conscious movement, not random twitching.

He leaned closer, looking into Little Deer's eyes.

Little Deer's eyeballs moved.

Very slow, very difficult, but they moved. His gaze moved from the ceiling, slowly to A-Zhong's face. Pupils still dilated, but something was inside—consciousness, clarity.

He was still alive.

And, awake.

A-Zhong's throat tightened.

He wanted to speak, but couldn't emit sound. He wanted to ask "how are you," wanted to ask "does it hurt," wanted to ask "what should I do." But those words caught in his throat like a ball of thorns.

Little Deer's lips moved.

No sound came out, only lip shape.

A-Zhong understood.

He was saying: Water.

A-Zhong stood up, walked to the sink. He turned on the faucet, water gurgling out. He cupped some in his hands, water leaking from between his fingers, only a little left. He walked back, crouched down, and carefully brought his hand to Little Deer's mouth.

Little Deer's lips opened a crack.

A-Zhong poured the water in.

Most of it flowed out from the corner of his mouth, sliding along his cheeks to behind his ears. Only a little was swallowed by Little Deer. His Adam's apple rolled once, making a faint swallowing sound.

A-Zhong went to get more.

This time he cupped more, using both hands to hold a handful of water. He knelt down, one hand lifting the back of Little Deer's neck—Little Deer's neck was thin, skin warm, able to feel the vertebrae beneath.

He poured the water into Little Deer's mouth.

Little Deer swallowed, more than last time. His Adam's apple rolled several times in succession, swallowing sounds clear in the quiet room.

After drinking, Little Deer's eyes closed for an instant, then opened.

He looked at A-Zhong, his gaze clearer than before.

Then, his lips moved again.
