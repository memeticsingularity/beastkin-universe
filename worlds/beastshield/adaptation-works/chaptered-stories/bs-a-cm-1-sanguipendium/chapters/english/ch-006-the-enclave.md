## Scene-1: Gathering in the Morning Light

Morning light cut through the gray sky of the White Capital's Seventh District like a blunt knife.

That light was cold white, carrying the sharpness peculiar to a winter morning, pouring down through gaps in the clouds and casting long, sharp shadows on the cement ground. The air humidity was high; when breathing, one could feel that moist, slightly metallic rust taste in the nasal cavity—that was the breath characteristic of an industrial city morning, mixed with the sulfur smell after coal burning, metal steam emitted by distant factories, and the earthy fishiness left by last night's rainwater evaporating from the ground.

Outside the east gate of the Beastshield Combat Troops Camp, thirty-two E-level soldiers had already formed up.

They came from four different camps—the stockiness of the Ox Camp, the agility of the Tiger Camp, the heaviness of the Bear Camp, the nimbleness of the Dog Camp—now mixed together, forming a peculiar sight. Thirty-two male bodies were arranged in four columns in the morning light, each emitting a unique aura, every inch of flesh beneath the uniform trembling slightly in this final calm.

The air was permeated with various scents, layer upon layer, like an olfactory symphony:

The heavy body odor unique to ox beastmen, mixed with fodder and earth, that fermenting scent characteristic of ruminants, rich and warm, carrying a certain pastoral simplicity, yet stained with the saltiness of sweat from long-term physical labor. That smell was like aged haystacks, emitting the sweetness of sun-dried plant fibers under sunlight, or like freshly turned soil, mixed with the fragrance of grass roots and humus.

The slightly fishy-sweet wild scent on tiger beastmen, that smell unique to pure carnivores—iron carried in blood, lactic acid produced by muscle metabolism, and pheromones secreted by glands beneath the fur mixed together. That smell made one think of shadows deep in the forest, the fear of prey when captured, the instant when sharp claws and teeth tore flesh. It was sweet to the point of unease, fishy to the point of alertness, like some danger signal chemically transmitted in the air.

The rich, nearly fermented male hormones of bear beastmen, that heavy scent characteristic of the fat-wrapped muscle physique. The 3-5 centimeter thick fat layer continuously emitted heat beneath the skin, like walking biological furnaces, endlessly releasing the scent of testosterone and other hormones into the air. That smell mixed honey-like sweetness, leather-like agedness, and some deep, primal musk, making one think of the warmth deep in caves, the energy stored before hibernation, the power when bear paws crushed bones.

The various canine beastmen's pheromones, ranging from intense to light, that was the most direct proof of diversity. The mastiff's heavy body odor like a stuffy basement, the greyhound's faint metallic smell like a sharp blade, the German shepherd's cleaner scent like a military supply warehouse, the husky's snowy plain aura like the Arctic cold wind. These scents intertwined in the cold morning air, forming a suffocatingly complex atmosphere, making every breath an information collection, an identity recognition, a premonition of fate.

Niu Xuqiang stood at the left edge of the formation.

At thirty-seven years old, one hundred ninety-five centimeters tall, one hundred fifty kilograms, he was of standard build in the Ox Camp. His grayish-white fur presented an aged luster in the morning light, like fabric faded by time; every hair had lost the oiliness of youth, becoming dry and coarse, like straw sun-dried too many times. His fur color was varying shades of gray, from dark gray on the back to light gray on the belly, forming a natural gradient belt, the most intuitive mark age left on him.

His shoulders were broad, a full seventy centimeters wide; every deltoid was like a thick piece of beef, forming obvious bulges beneath the black uniform. His chest cavity was thick, circumference exceeding one hundred thirty centimeters; every breath was accompanied by the huge rise and fall of the chest cavity, like an ancient bellows being slowly pulled. Although his abdomen had begun to accumulate fat, forming the ox clan's characteristic soft fat layer, his waist and back were still straight—that was the mark left by twenty years of physical labor, the unyielding refusal to bend after countless times of bending to carry heavy objects.

He wore the standard uniform of an E-level combat soldier. That was a black tight short-sleeve shirt, fabric made of special elastic fiber, able to adapt to the muscle expansion of beastmen during strenuous exercise. But that uniform was old, issued three years ago, having lost its initial crispness after countless washes and sweat stains. The white Beastshield logo was printed on the left chest—a shield-shaped mark, edges already somewhat worn, white paint dimmed after multiple washes, like a tired eye gazing into the distance.

The uniform was stretched tight by his body; the black fabric glinted with faint luster in the morning light, highlighting the muscle contours beneath. The chest was stretched full; mammary tissue formed slight bulges beneath the tight fabric—that was the mark left by the ox clan male's characteristic well-developed lactation system, although it would not produce milk, it formed a unique visual curve beneath the uniform. The fabric at the abdomen position was rounded by the fat layer, forming the ox clan's characteristic soft contour, forming a sharp contrast with the tiger clan's leanness.

The white stripes at the cuffs had already yellowed, the result of long-term accumulation of salt and urea in sweat, the chemical traces left by countless training sessions and missions. The fabric under the armpits and on the back was stained white by sweat, forming irregular maps on the black fabric, recording those sweat-drenched days.

The lower body was black shorts, white stripes on both sides, similarly having lost their initial vibrancy. The edges of the shorts were somewhat pilled, traces left by long-term friction, witnesses to countless intimate contacts between skin and fabric. He wore black long socks; the white horizontal stripes at the sock cuffs had become grayish-white, loosely piled on his calves—that was because his calf muscles were too developed, stretching the sock cuffs until they lost elasticity. The black military boots were last year's new model; the soles were still hard, producing dull "thud thud" sounds when stepping on cement ground, every step accompanied by the tremor of weight.

The uniform collar was slightly open, revealing the edge of the gray old-style high-waisted boxer briefs beneath—that was the one he had specially selected last night, the cleanest, most decent piece of underwear. The underwear fabric was pure cotton, already washed white; fibers became soft and fluffy after repeated friction, like pieces of thinned clouds. The elastic band at the waist had lost elasticity, only able to be tied with a thin string; the knot formed a tiny protrusion below the abdomen, rising and falling slightly with his breathing.

That pair of underwear wrapped his stocky hips and perineum; the fabric formed natural folds between the legs, tightening slightly with his standing posture. He knew what day it was today, knew that from this moment on, he would completely bid farewell to his past life. That old underwear was the last relic of past life, the last memento of ordinary days.

His right hand unconsciously stroked the chip at the tip of his left horn. That was a memento from a workplace injury three years ago—a box slipped while moving iron boxes, the edge hitting the horn tip, resulting in only three days of sick leave at the time. Three days, not even enough for interest. The number fifty-two thousand had spun in his mind for too long, so long it had become a kind of background noise, like a sustained, low-frequency tinnitus, constantly reminding him why he was standing here.

That pair of ox horns extended from his forehead, each thirty centimeters long, presenting an ivory-like texture. The horn surface had fine ring-shaped patterns, records of age, each ring representing a year of growth. The horn tip should have been sharp, but the left horn tip had chipped in that accident three years ago, now presenting a blunt, injured form, like some worn weapon, recording those sacrifices for which no one was held responsible.

Morning light glinted on his horn tip, refracting faint light at that chip, as if narrating a story about debt, about survival, about despair.

"Attention all."

An overseer's voice came from the front. That voice propagated in the cold morning air, carrying a metallic sharpness, echoing between the camp buildings, forming an unsettling resonance. That was a tiger beastman, powerfully built, height exceeding two meters, wearing the overseer's characteristic dark gray uniform; the badge on his chest glinted in the morning light—a golden tiger head embedded on a dark gray shield, symbolizing the combination of authority and violence.

Overseer O-127's gaze swept across the formation; those amber eyes held no warmth. His vertical pupils contracted into extremely fine black lines under strong light, like two sharp blades flickering in the eye sockets. That was a predator's gaze, a gaze assessing prey, a coldness ready to cull the weak from the group at any moment. His fur was orange-white mixed, presenting a warm yet dangerous tone in the morning light; the black stripes on his shoulders were thick and clear, each one like carefully outlined with an ink brush, extending from the neck all the way to the waist, forming a natural flow.

"I am your person in charge at the contestant village, designation O-127." His voice rang out again, carrying the tiger clan's unique resonance chamber quality, low and full of deterrence. "From today on, you are no longer ordinary E-level soldiers. You are contestants, participants of the Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament. In the contestant village, you will receive unified management until the tournament ends—or until most of you die."

A slight commotion came from the formation. The sound of someone swallowing saliva—that sound was dry and loud, like a tiny explosion in the silence. The sound of fabric rubbing when someone adjusted posture—black uniform fabrics rubbing against each other, producing a rustling, snake-like sound. The sound of someone's heartbeat accelerating due to tension—Niu Xuqiang could hear the heart in Niu Majun's chest cavity to his right accelerating, producing a "thump thump thump" dull rhythm, like a war drum being urgently struck.

The air was permeated with the chemical traces of fear—that was a slightly sweet, slightly acidic smell, secreted from every contestant's sweat glands, mixed with the cold morning air, forming a suffocating atmosphere. Niu Xuqiang's nasal cavity captured this smell; that was the taste of adrenaline and cortisol, the instinctive reaction produced by the body when facing death threats.

"Now, board the vehicles."

The interior of the transport vehicle was long and narrow, with benches on both sides and an aisle in the middle. Thirty-two people were arranged into four vehicles, eight per vehicle, allocated by faction.

Niu Xuqiang walked into the second vehicle and found seven people already inside—his seven fellow campmates.

The light inside the vehicle was dim, with only morning light coming through the small windows on both sides providing illumination. The air was permeated with a scent characteristic of enclosed spaces—mixed with the aged smell of leather seats, the rust smell of the metal body, and the heavy body odor produced after eight ox beastmen gathered together. That smell was warm, heavy, carrying a certain post-fermentation sweetness, like haystacks heated by the sun, or like the fishy sweetness of freshly milked milk oxidizing in the air.

Niu Sunyong sat in the innermost position by the window. At thirty years old, his light brown fur presented a warm, almost luminous tone in the dim vehicle. That was a healthy color, the mark of youth, the last gift that the age of thirty left on him. Every hair glinted with oily luster, flickering in the faint light, like pieces of carefully cared-for amber.

His build was slightly smaller than Niu Xuqiang's, one hundred ninety-two centimeters tall, one hundred forty kilograms, but his muscles were tighter—nine years at E-level had not left too many fat marks on him. His abdomen was flat and firm, waist and back straight, showing good physical quality and strict self-management. His shoulders were broad; every muscle had clear lines, forming clear contours beneath the black uniform.

He wore a relatively new uniform—issued just last year, the black tight short-sleeve fabric still maintaining its initial crispness and luster. The white Beastshield logo on the left chest was still clear, unworn, reflecting faint light in the dim vehicle, like a bright eye gazing in the darkness. The white stripes on the sleeves were still vibrant, forming a sharp contrast with Niu Xuqiang's yellowed uniform, like a confrontation between new and old, a comparison between youth and aging.

He didn't wear a tactical belt; the white stripes on both sides of the shorts were straight and neat, unobscured by a belt. The cuffs of the black long socks tightly hugged his calves; the white horizontal stripes were clearly visible, showing his firm and symmetrical calf muscles. That was a healthy, vibrant leg shape, contrasting with Niu Xuqiang's slightly loose calves due to age.

The edge of the dark brown high-waisted briefs was faintly visible from his uniform collar—that was his habit when nervous, always unconsciously pulling at the collar, letting the underwear edge be exposed. That underwear was newly bought three months ago; the fabric was pure cotton, still crisp, not having experienced too much washing wear; the elastic band at the waist was still strong, tightly wrapping his waist, leaving slight pressure marks on the skin.

That pair of briefs was cut to fit his body; the dark brown fabric presented a warm tone in the dim light. The waist edge neatly fit below his abdomen, wrapping his perineum and hips, forming a tight and comfortable binding. The fabric formed natural folds between the legs, rising and falling slightly with his breathing; every rise and fall was accompanied by the slight contraction and relaxation of abdominal muscles.

"Brother Xuqiang." Niu Sunyong nodded, voice low. That voice came from deep within his chest cavity, carrying the ox clan's unique resonance, echoing inside the vehicle, producing slight resonance with the vehicle body's metal.

Niu Xuqiang responded with a nod, then sat beside him. The two maintained a distance of one fist—neither too distant nor too close. They were both here to die; too much intimacy would only increase the pain. When Niu Xuqiang sat down, he could feel the slight groan of the seat leather under his weight, could feel the heat emitted by Niu Sunyong's body transmitting through the air, carrying the high temperature characteristic of young males.

The vehicle's vibration transmitted up from the seat, carrying a rhythmic, drowsy cadence. That was the "clickety-clack" sound produced when wheels rolled over road joints, the low rumble of the engine's continuous operation, the slight whistle of the vehicle body rubbing against air. Niu Xuqiang closed his eyes, feeling this vibration—it made him think of the ore transport rail cars in the mining area, think of those days of daily travel between the mine shaft and the surface, think of those times of swaying in darkness, filled with sweat smell and ore dust.

To his right sat Niu Majun. Twenty-nine years old, brown fur, lean and strong build.

Niu Majun's uniform was a four-year-old old model, even older than Niu Xuqiang's. The black tight short-sleeve had been washed gray; the fabric lost elasticity after repeated friction, becoming soft and drooping. The Beastshield logo on the left chest was almost completely worn, leaving only a faint white mark, like a blind eye gazing at the chest. The stripes on the sleeves had broken into several sections, like some faded scar, recording wear that could not be repaired.

He wore a severely worn tactical belt; the belt buckle had lost its luster, edges having scratches left by long-term wear, traces of countless collisions between metal and metal. The belt tightly cinched his waist, forming an obvious pressure mark on the uniform, highlighting his lean waist.

The edge of the dark blue old-style boxer briefs was faintly visible at the uniform collar. That underwear had been worn for at least five years; the fabric was pure cotton, the crotch worn into a thin layer of fuzz, the result of fiber breakage after long-term friction. The edge stitching had long come undone, revealing the grayish-white cotton wadding inside, like a wound that had been torn and then barely stitched. Niu Majun's eyes had been staring out the window, fingers tapping some meaningless rhythm on his knee—that was the gambler's habit, the rhythm of calculating odds, every tap accompanied by the flow of numbers in his mind.

"What are you calculating?" Niu Xuqiang asked.

"Numbers." Niu Majun didn't turn his head, voice dry, as if squeezed from deep in the throat. "Thirty-two people, only one can make it to the end."

"Are you thinking that person might be you?"

Niu Majun finally turned his head, looking at Niu Xuqiang. His eyes were dark brown, with a calculated calmness in the pupils, a mode of thinking formed after long-term dealings with numbers. "I'm thinking, if I lose in the first round, at least I won't have to count the days anymore."

"You afraid?"

"Afraid." Niu Majun's voice was calm to the point of indifference, without any emotional fluctuation. "But being afraid is useless. Already here, no retreat."

Niu Xuqiang didn't answer. He thought of his father still waiting on the kang for money, thought of that fifty-two thousand debt, thought that if he could become champion, everything could be turned around. Those thoughts spun in his mind like a machine that couldn't stop, emitting continuous noise.

The vehicle continued forward, passing through the streets of the White Capital. The scenery outside the window constantly changed in the dim light—dilapidated buildings, rusty fences, and occasionally flashing pedestrian faces. Those faces were blurred and unfamiliar, like ghosts from another world, hurriedly passing by on the other side of the car window, forming two completely different worlds from the eight ox beastmen inside the vehicle who were about to face death.

Meanwhile, on the first vehicle.

Hu Wangwu sat in the center of the vehicle, like an isolated island.

At thirty-two years old, his orange-white mixed fur was still eye-catching in the dim light; that was a warm, vibrant tone—the orange-yellow base color like the sunlight of an autumn afternoon, pure black stripes thick and clear, each about two centimeters wide, edges sharp as ink-brush outlines, extending from the neck all the way to the waist, forming a natural flow. In the dim vehicle light, those stripes presented a dynamic, almost liquid visual effect, flowing with his breathing and slight muscle movements, like a beast sleeping but ready to pounce at any moment.

The white Beastshield logo on the left chest was clearly visible, unworn, reflecting faint light in the dim vehicle, like a vigilant eye gazing ahead. The white stripes on the sleeves were straight and clean, without any yellowing or fading traces, showing the owner's careful maintenance and near-obsessive upkeep.

His gaze swept over the other seven Tiger Camp teammates in the vehicle; those amber eyes glinted with sharp light in the dim light. Twenty-seven years old, dark golden fur, that was a relatively rare fur color among the tiger clan—deeper and closer to golden-brown than the standard orange-yellow, presenting a luxurious and melancholic quality in the dim light. The waist was a gold elastic band, soft and strong, tightly wrapping his waist, leaving slight pressure on the skin, the last remnant of noble life.

Even if fallen, even if reduced to E-level, he still maintained that innate haughtiness, that sense of superiority flowing in his blood.

"Hu Zhaojun." Hu Wangwu took the initiative to speak, voice low and polite. "I've heard of you."

The corner of Hu Zhaojun's mouth curled slightly upward, but that was not a smile. That was mockery, self-mockery, mockery of fate. "If it's you," he said, voice low and clear, every word like squeezed from deep within the chest cavity, "I hope you can let me die with dignity. Not for the family, but for myself—even if fallen, I don't want to kneel on the ground begging for mercy."

Silence fell inside the vehicle. Only the sound of wheels rolling over the road surface, rhythmic, ruthless, like some countdown.

"We're also looking at them." Niu Xuqiang responded, his voice low, beginning to eat on the rough wooden table. His fingers held chopsticks—that was wooden, already used by too many people, surface smooth and greasy.

The brown rice's texture was rough, every grain of rice like a tiny stone, producing a slight "crunch" sound between teeth. That was an aged, stored-too-long smell, carrying some mildewy scent, making one think of warehouse-stored old grain. The rice's temperature was moderate, neither hot nor cold, but that temperature couldn't mask its essence—this was the most basic heat source, the minimum supply to sustain life.

The stewed meat was some cheap synthetic meat, meat texture loose, like a piece of crushed and re-pressed meat mud. Its color was an unnatural pink, carrying traces of some additive. Taste was monotonous and greasy, carrying an unpleasant, plastic-like scent, making one suspect what its composition actually was. Niu Xuqiang tore at it with his teeth, feeling its texture decomposing in his mouth—no meat fiber feeling, no real meat's elasticity and toughness.

The vegetables were boiled too mushy, having lost all crispness, becoming a soft, mud-like substance. Their color was a dim yellow-green, like leaves boiled too long, having lost all vitality. Niu Xuqiang sent them into his mouth, feeling a faint, nearly tasteless sweetness, the last remnant of the vegetables.

But no one complained. They were all E-level soldiers, accustomed to crude food. In the eleven, twelve, or even sixteen years at Beastshield, they had eaten much worse than this. They had eaten moldy bread, eaten rotten meat, eaten various foods of unidentifiable origin. Although this meal was simple, at least it was clean, at least normal, at least wouldn't make them sick.

---

Hu Wangwu led the Tiger Camp's four people sitting at another table.

That table was located in the center of the room, a position that could observe the entire room. Hu Wangwu chose the direction facing the door, a choice any experienced warrior would make—never expose your back to an unknown direction.

His gaze occasionally met Niu Xuqiang's gaze; the two nodded slightly, then each shifted their gaze. That was recognition between the same kind—they were both here to die, both carrying their respective despairs, both waiting for that drawing that would decide fate. In that exchange of glances, there was a silent understanding, a resonance transcending race and camp.

"That old ox." Hu Liyong said in a low voice, his voice pressed extremely low, almost a whisper, but Hu Wangwu's ears could still clearly capture every word. He pointed at Niu Xuqiang, that finger slender and powerful, fingertip slightly curved, as if ready to extend claws and teeth at any moment. "Looks very experienced."

"Thirty-seven years old." Hu Wangwu said, his eyes slightly narrowing, amber vertical pupils contracting into extremely fine black lines under sunlight, "Worked twelve years at E-level. There's a chip on his horn, that's an old injury, should be quite some years old. His uniform is very old, cuffs yellowed, indicating he's worked at E-level for a long time, experienced countless storms."

"You observe very carefully."

"Must observe." Hu Wangwu drank a mouthful of water; that water flowed in his mouth, bringing a cool feeling, but unable to extinguish the inner flame. "Tomorrow we'll know. Who will become the opponent, who will become... memory."

His voice was calm, but every word carried a heavy weight. On this arena, they might kill each other, might become each other's last memory, might become the face that flashed through the mind at the moment of death.

---

Xiong Zhuqiang sat at the Bear Camp's table, his gaze had been staring in the direction of the Dog Camp.

Not looking at Quan Gaozhong, nor at Quan Linyi. He was looking at Xiong Hujun—that opponent from the same camp but different dormitory, currently sitting at another table next to the Dog Camp table, forming some loose alliance with several people from the Dog Camp.

Xiong Hujun's light gray fur presented a soft, almost luminous quality under the incandescent lamp, forming a sharp contrast with Xiong Zhuqiang's dark brown. His sitting posture was upright, back straight, showing good physical quality and confidence. That confidence stung Xiong Zhuqiang, like a thorn stabbed in the heart, making him feel an uncontrollable anger.

"You're still thinking about him?" Xiong Zhouping asked, his voice carrying exhaustion, that exhaustion from long-term living in a tense atmosphere, that weariness of conflict.

"I think about him every day." Xiong Zhuqiang answered, voice low as thunder, coming from deep within his chest cavity, still clearly audible in the cafeteria's noise. "Think about how to defeat him, think about how to prove I'm stronger than him, think about how to let everyone know—power, only power, is the only thing worth pursuing. Technique, speed, flexibility, all meaningless before absolute power."

"But if you two meet on the arena, only one can survive."

"Then let me survive." Xiong Zhuqiang turned his head, staring at Xiong Zhouping, those dark brown eyes burning with a fervent and obsessive flame, like a beast driven into a dead end. "Or let him kill me. But no matter which result, it will prove one thing—who is stronger. In this world, only the strong deserve to live, only the strong deserve to be remembered."

His voice echoed in the cafeteria, attracting the attention of several people around. Hu Wangwu turned his head, looked at him, those amber eyes having an assessment, a disdain for this fervor. Quan Gaozhong's ears rotated, capturing every word of this conversation, analyzing the information contained within.

---

Quan Gaozhong quietly ate, but his ears had been capturing sounds around him.

His hearing was among the best in the Dog Camp, able to distinguish dozens of different sounds, able to separate out every component from mixed sounds. At this moment, he heard Niu Xuqiang and Niu Majun's low conversation—that was a conversation about debt and family, carrying a heavy helplessness; heard Hu Wangwu and Hu Liyong's conversation—that was a conversation about observation and strategy, carrying a calm assessment; heard Xiong Zhuqiang's hostility-filled declaration—that was a roar about power and conquest, carrying a dangerous fervor.

He also heard more—Quan Songliang's frivolous voice trying to tell jokes, that deliberate lightness hiding deep fear; Quan Xiejun's suppressed breathing, that shallow, rapid breathing showing inner tension; and something even more subtle, from the kitchen direction, the sound of knives colliding, that crisp collision between metal and metal, like reminding everyone of the approaching bloodiness.

"What are you listening to?" Quan Linyi asked, his mastiff body pressed close to the gray wolf, body temperature transmitting through fabric, about one degree higher than Quan Gaozhong's, forming a warm, almost comfortable field.

"Everything." Quan Gaozhong answered, his ears erect, slightly rotating, pointing in different directions respectively, like adjusting a precision radar. "And... the sound of fate."

"Does fate have a sound?"

"Yes." Quan Gaozhong raised his head, looking at the cafeteria entrance direction, where a huge poster was pasted—the Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament promotional poster, scarlet background with black text: "Blood as bounty, life as wager." That scarlet color was exceptionally eye-catching under the incandescent lamp's illumination, like a pool of coagulated blood, like an inescapable curse.

"The sound of fate," Quan Gaozhong said, his voice low and calm, like stating an obvious fact, "is the countdown. Every second's passage is a step toward the endpoint. Every bell's ring is death's summons. We are waiting, waiting for that moment that decides fate, waiting for that drawing that will tear us apart."

Quan Linyi fell silent, his dark brown eyes having an understanding, a resonance with Quan Gaozhong's words. They were both beasts driven by fate, both warriors seeking a way out in this cruel system. They both knew, time was passing, the countdown was proceeding, and their fate would be decided tomorrow.

---

After lunch ended, contestants were allowed to move freely in the courtyard.

Niu Xuqiang stood in the central square, feeling the temperature of sunlight shining on his fur. That temperature was real, proof of being alive. He didn't know how many more times he could feel such sunlight, didn't know whether tomorrow, the day after tomorrow, or the day after that at this time, he could still stand here.

He looked around, looking at the other thirty-one people.

Hu Zhaojun stood in the corner, alone using a white handkerchief to wipe something—perhaps a medal, perhaps a ring, perhaps just his own fingers. His posture was elegant and lonely, like a forgotten monument.

Xiong Wugang sat on a bench, eyes hollowly looking at the sky. His dark gray fur presented a nearly black luster under sunlight, but that luster was dim, as if the soul had already left this body.

Quan Xiejun stood before the notice board, looking at the odds table posted on it. His snow-white fur was exceptionally eye-catching in the crowd, the corner of his mouth's "smile" still hanging, but his eyes were deep unwillingness.

Niu Xuqiang took a deep breath, letting air fill his lungs.

Thirty-two people, thirty-two despairs, thirty-two stories waiting to be written.

Tomorrow, the drawing ceremony would decide their fate. Before that, they could only wait, only observe, only sleep beside future enemies in this cage.

The thirty-two of them, standing in the sunlight, but not knowing how long they could still stand.

But in this final calm, in this last moment before the countdown began, they were still alive. Feeling the sunlight, feeling the air, feeling each other's existence—this existence was both comfort and torture.

This was the cruelty of the Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament—it let you live, let you feel the beauty of living, then pushed you toward the abyss of death.

---

### Story Reflections

This chapter depicts the process of 32 contestants arriving at the contestant village, being assigned dormitories, and having their first collective meal. There are no intense conflicts, no bloody battles, only a suppressed, suffocating calm.

Everyone is observing, assessing, waiting. They look at future opponents, calculate win rates, while also inadvertently establishing some subtle connections. The four people in the same dormitory, although knowing they might eventually fight on the arena, still chose some degree of peaceful coexistence at this moment.

This calm is cruel because it creates an illusion—as if these people can coexist peacefully, as if the tournament won't come, as if death won't descend. But everyone knows in their hearts, this is only the calm before the storm.

Niu Xuqiang looks up at the crack on the ceiling, Hu Zhaojun wipes nonexistent dust, Xiong Zhuqiang stares in the direction of his nemesis, Quan Gaozhong captures all sounds. These details form the group portrait of the eve of the Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament—thirty-two souls, thirty-two kinds of waiting, thirty-two stories about to be torn apart by fate.

In this world of only male beastmen, violence and gentleness, competition and coexistence, despair and hope intertwine. They may be opponents, but at this moment, they are also the same kind—souls driven to the dead end by life, wanting to roll the dice, waiting for dawn in the countdown.
