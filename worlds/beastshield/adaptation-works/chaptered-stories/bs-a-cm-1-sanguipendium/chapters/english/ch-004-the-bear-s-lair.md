## Scene-1: Warm Sun

High noon. The sun hung in the center of the sky like a burning, colossal fireball, pouring forth blazing, nearly white radiance into every corner of the White Capital. That light penetrated the thin haze of industrial pollution, casting dazzling, almost scorching patches of light upon the square of the Bear Camp.

The Bear Camp was located at the westernmost edge of the Beastshield Combat Troops camp. Unlike the oppression of the Ox Camp or the sharpness of the Tiger Camp, a unique, languid atmosphere permeated here. It was a feeling as if time itself had slowed its pace—sunlight moved more slowly, sound traveled more slowly, even breathing became long and deep.

This languor was not laziness, but a survival strategy. Bear beastmen knew well that in the long winter, conserving energy was more important than expending it; before unknown threats, patient waiting was wiser than rash action. This wisdom was etched deep in their genes, passed down from ancient ancestors to the present, still functioning even in modern society.

The ground of the square was black basalt—that was the most distinctive feature of the Bear Camp training ground. Not the cement of the Ox Camp, not the rubber mats of the Tiger Camp, but the hardest, most primal basalt. This rock was formed during volcanic eruptions, containing the power of the earth's depths, hard enough to withstand countless heavy strikes.

The ground was covered with a web-like pattern of cracks, crisscrossing, varying in depth, forming a complex map recording countless training sessions and battles. Every crack told a story—a burst of force, a bodily impact, a contest of wills. Some cracks were newly formed, edges sharp, color lighter; some cracks were already ancient, edges rounded, color deep, polished smooth by countless soles.

Sunlight shone upon the basalt ground, was absorbed, then re-released in the form of heat. That ground became warm under the noon sun, even scorching hot, but for accustomed bear beastmen, this temperature was comfortable, reassuring. They liked this warmth coming from the earth, liked this most direct contact with the natural world.

The air was permeated with a unique scent—that complex and rich layered aroma characteristic of the Bear Camp.

The bottom layer was the scent of the basalt itself—a dry, mineral smell, carrying the mystery of the underground depths. The middle layer was the bear beastman's body odor—sweeter than the ox beastman's, warmer than the tiger beastman's, carrying a smell similar to honey and leather mixed together. That body odor came from their well-developed sebaceous glands, secreting an oil rich in pheromones that formed a protective film on the fur while also emitting a unique scent.

The top layer was that distinctive, sweet musk note—the smell of bear beastman estrus pheromones. Even outside of estrus, one could still smell the residual scent from their glands, like some distant, suppressed desire. This scent made the Bear Camp's air even heavier, denser, like a tangible, nearly physical presence.

Xiong Zhouping sat on a large rock at the edge of the square.

That rock was part of the basalt, excavated from deep underground, then cut, polished, and placed here. After countless years of wind and sun, after countless people's sitting and resting, the rock's surface had been worn exceptionally smooth, presenting an oily, nearly black luster. Under sunlight, that surface reflected faint light, like a black mirror.

The rock was approximately fifty centimeters high, eighty centimeters wide, and sixty centimeters deep—just enough to accommodate a bear beastman's hips. The height was precisely designed—allowing the person sitting on it to place both feet flat on the ground, knees bent at a ninety-degree angle, back naturally straight, forming a comfortable and stable sitting posture.

Xiong Zhouping's build was medium-to-large among bear beastmen—two hundred two centimeters tall, one hundred fifty-five kilograms.

This weight was not the heaviest for bear beastmen, but the ratio of fat to muscle was just right—the typical "fat-wrapped muscle" physique. This build was the pride of bear beastmen, the result of millions of years of evolution for survival in nature, their strongest weapon against cold, against hunger, against enemies.

That layer of fat covering the chest and abdomen was thick and soft, a full four centimeters thick.

But this layer of fat was not loose, sagging, useless flab; rather, it was firm, elastic, full of functional protective padding. Beneath the fat layer were steel-hard muscle fibers—pectoralis major, rectus abdominis, external obliques, every piece forged through countless training sessions, every piece containing astonishing power. This "fat-wrapped muscle" physique was the typical characteristic of bear beastmen—fat provided protection and energy reserves, muscles provided power and explosive force, the two combining to form a strong and durable, nearly perfect body structure.

Xiong Zhouping's designation was E-145, joining Beastshield at G-level at nineteen, promoted to E-level after four years, now twelve years at E-level.

At thirty-one years old, he was right at the peak of a bear beastman's physical function—muscles developed, experience abundant, while not yet having entered the decline of aging. His fur was dark brown, presenting a warm, nearly red luster under sunlight. The fur was thick and coarse, covering his entire body from the top of his head all the way to his toes, forming a layer of natural protection.

His chest was full and rounded, mammary tissue well-developed, forming obvious bulges beneath the black tight uniform.

That was the secondary sexual characteristic of bear beastmen, more obvious and prominent compared to other races. Bear beastman mammary tissue was equally well-developed in males, a unique physiological characteristic; its evolutionary significance might be related to energy reserves, body temperature regulation, or nursing young in extreme circumstances. But in modern society, it had become more of a symbol of identity, a mark of race, a physiological reality that could not be ignored.

His nipples were relatively large, dark brown, approximately two centimeters in diameter, surrounded by a circle of lighter-colored areola, exceptionally eye-catching against the bulge of the pectorals. Wrapped in the black uniform, those two bulges were compressed into two huge, hemispherical contours, rising and falling slowly with his breathing. The uniform fabric was tight and elastic, able to change with the body's shape, but simultaneously exerted pressure on the mammary tissue, keeping the nipples in a slightly, persistently stimulated state under the friction of the fabric.

Xiong Zhouping closed his eyes, enjoying the afternoon sun.

That sunlight was warm, direct, shining on his face without any obstruction. He could feel the heat of the sun penetrating his skin, warming his facial muscles and fur. That heat was gradual, penetrating, starting from the skin surface, then going deep into the dermis, finally reaching the subcutaneous tissue and muscles, letting the whole body feel a comfortable, relaxing warmth.

His skin was dark brown, glowing with a warm luster in the sunlight; the fur on the surface formed fine shadows under the light, making his skin look more three-dimensional, more textured. Every hair gleamed in the sunlight, like tiny, golden scales.

His palms were exceptionally broad, fingers stubby, every finger like a small sausage, the knuckles covered with thick calluses.

Those were traces left by long-term training, long-term use, long-term contact with rough surfaces. Every callus was a storage of memory, recording countless strikes, countless grasps, countless supports. His palms rested on his knees, fingers slightly curved, palms upward, revealing that rough, warm, deeply textured skin surface—that was like an ancient map, recording a lifetime's journey.

He was waiting.

Bear beastmen were never impatient. They believed that strength came from accumulation, from waiting, from erupting at the most suitable moment. This belief was etched deep in their genes—in the wild, bears were not the fastest hunters, nor the most agile hunters, but they were the most patient hunters. They could lie by the river for an entire day, waiting for the moment a salmon leaped out of the water; they could wait for hours beneath a beehive, waiting for the instant the bees relaxed their guard.

Waiting was not passive; it was active. It was maintaining alertness in stillness, accumulating strength in silence, preparing to act in seeming inaction.

"Xiong Zhouping, slacking off again?"

A deep voice came from behind. That voice was even lower than an ox beastman's, carrying a chest resonance quality, like thunder rumbling from deep underground, or like thunder heard from very far away. That voice propagated through the air, resonating with surrounding buildings, ground, and objects, forming a deep, sustained echo.

Xiong Zhouping opened his eyes and turned his head, seeing Xiong Wugang standing three paces away.

Xiong Wugang, designation E-112, thirty-two years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at eighteen, promoted to E-level after three years, now ten years at E-level. He was one of the largest in the Bear Camp—two hundred eight centimeters tall, one hundred sixty-five kilograms, almost a moving mountain of flesh.

His figure appeared even more massive before Xiong Zhouping, like a wall, like a mountain, like an insurmountable presence. His shadow was cast upon Xiong Zhouping, blocking part of the sunlight, bringing a cool, comfortable shade. That shadow moved with Xiong Wugang's movement, like a living, touchable entity.

Xiong Wugang's build was an enlarged version of Xiong Zhouping's. His fat layer was thicker than Xiong Zhouping's, a full five centimeters, covering the chest, abdomen, back, thighs, and other areas. When walking, that layer of fat would tremble slightly, producing fine ripples, like wind blowing across water, like the surface of some liquid vibrating. That tremor was rhythmic, slow, almost elegant, showing the thickness and elasticity of the fat layer.

But beneath that fat layer was his astonishing power—his muscle fibers were thicker and denser than Xiong Zhouping's, able to generate more powerful explosive force. That fat layer was not weakness; it was camouflage, protection, energy storage, the source of power.

His palms were broad enough to wrap around an ordinary person's skull, fingers stubby and powerful, every knuckle like a small joint bearing, full of mechanical power. His soles were thick; every step would leave shallow impressions on the basalt ground—that was the result of weight and pressure working together, his signature left upon the earth.

"Not slacking off." Xiong Zhouping said, his voice low and slow, as if vibrating from deep within the chest cavity, every word accompanied by the slow release of breath. That voice propagated through the air, carrying a moist, warm quality, like some thick fabric rubbing in the wind. "Waiting."

"Waiting for what?" Xiong Wugang approached and sat down beside Xiong Zhouping. That massive frame made the rock emit a groan of being overwhelmed—the rock's surface produced fine cracks under heavy pressure, emitting a sound similar to dry firewood breaking, or like some colossal creature sighing.

The rock sank slightly under Xiong Wugang's weight, edges sinking a few millimeters into the ground. This sinking was slow, gradual, showing the elastic deformation of rock and ground when bearing enormous pressure. Xiong Wugang adjusted his sitting posture to make himself more comfortable; the rock emitted another slight groan, then stabilized.

"Waiting for that." Xiong Zhouping pointed to the cast-iron notice board at the end of the square.

The notice board was made of black cast iron, approximately three meters high and one meter wide, with a black anti-rust coating on the surface. Pasted on the notice board was that crimson poster; the poster's red gleamed with eye-piercing light under the sun, like a ball of burning flame, like a warning signal, like a summons of fate.

The smell of ink mixed with the scent of metal, slowly diffusing in the air, forming a unique, slightly irritating odor. That smell became even stronger under sunlight, like a living, breathing entity. Every character on the poster was clearly visible—"Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament," "Thirty-two Strong Contenders," "Winner Takes 24,000 Alone," "Loser Gives All"—those characters were printed in black, bold, blood-written fonts, exceptionally eye-catching against the red background.

"Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament." Xiong Wugang read the large characters on the poster; there was no surprise in his voice, only a deep exhaustion, like a traveler who had walked too long, like an elder who had seen too much, like a gambler who no longer cared about the outcome. "You plan to register too?"

"Mm." Xiong Zhouping nodded, his neck muscles relaxed, the turning of his head on the cervical spine appearing slow and heavy. That motion was characteristic of bear beastmen—not hurried, not sudden, every motion carefully considered, every motion full of weight. "I need money."

"How much?"

"Not much." Xiong Zhouping said, his gaze passing over Xiong Wugang, looking toward the distant training ground. There, several young bear beastmen were swinging stone locks, emitting dull roars. Their muscles tensed during exercise, fat layers trembling beneath the skin, like a layer of active armor. "Ten thousand."

Ten thousand Lu coins. For an E-level soldier with a monthly salary of five hundred, this was a huge sum. Nearly two years of savings without eating or drinking. For Xiong Zhouping, this meant twenty months of blood and sweat, four hundred days and nights of toil, countless dawns and dusks of dedication.

"I owe the underground money house." Xiong Zhouping said, his voice calm, as if narrating something unrelated to himself. His eyes half-open and half-closed, gaze blurred, as if recalling, as if thinking, as if struggling internally. "Last year, my younger brother got ore sickness, mid-stage. Treatment cost needed five thousand. I borrowed five thousand; interest has rolled until now, already became ten thousand."

There was no resentment in his voice, no anger, only a deep, almost transcendent acceptance. He knew this was how the system operated, knew this was the fate of the weak, knew this was unchangeable reality. But he still chose to bear, chose to borrow, chose to trade his own future for his brother's life.

Xiong Wugang fell silent. He looked at Xiong Zhouping, looking at that deep exhaustion in the dark brown eyes.

In those eyes, he saw too many things—responsibility, love, despair, and a kind of almost sacred spirit of sacrifice. That was not weakness; it was strength. Not giving up; it was persistence. Not despair; it was hope.

"What about your brother?" Xiong Wugang asked, his voice becoming even lower, as if coming from a deeper place.

"Cured." Xiong Zhouping said, a trace of warmth finally appearing in his voice, a trace of softness, a trace of something that could be called happiness. "Now working at a ranch, sends me two hundred every month. But the money house can't wait. They gave me a final deadline, fifteenth of next month."

The warmth in that voice was quickly replaced by the coldness of reality. Cured, but the debt remained; alive, but the pressure remained; there was hope, but despair was closer. This was Xiong Zhouping's situation, this was the situation of countless E-level soldiers, this was the cruel reality of this system.

"So you're registering for this tournament?"

"Just win one match, five hundred." Xiong Zhouping said, the corner of his mouth curling slightly upward, revealing a bitter smile. That smile tugged at his facial muscles, forming two shallow lines on his cheeks. "Make top eight, forty thousand. Enough to pay off the debt, with some left for my brother's marriage."

His calculation was precise, rational, almost cold. Win one match, five hundred, could delay the debt; make top eight, forty thousand, could solve all problems. But behind the calculation was enormous risk—thirty-two people, only one could live to the end; every round could be the last round; every opponent could be the finisher.

The knife of the usury lender was already suspended overhead.

That knife was invisible, but its existence was real. It hung above Xiong Zhouping's head, descending an inch with each passing day, becoming sharper with each interest accumulation. On the fifteenth of next month, that knife would fall—either pay off the debt, or...

Xiong Wugang looked at Xiong Zhouping, looked for a long time. His eyes were dark gray, like some ancient rock, surface calm, but depths hiding countless stories. He saw Xiong Zhouping's despair, and also his determination; saw his weakness, and also his strength.

Then, he reached out, that huge palm slapping onto Xiong Zhouping's shoulder, producing a dull "pat" sound—like two thick pieces of meat colliding, like some ancient ritual, like some silent promise.

That contact was warm, heavy, reassuring. Xiong Zhouping could feel the temperature of Xiong Wugang's palm—about one degree higher than his own, heat passing through the uniform fabric to his skin, then seeping into muscle, finally reaching bone. That heat was like an energy, a support, a recognition.

Xiong Wugang's palm remained on Xiong Zhouping's shoulder, not immediately withdrawing. That weight was comfortable, reassuring, like a physical support, an emotional recognition. Xiong Zhouping could feel the texture of the palm—rough skin, thick calluses, powerful grip, and that silent comfort only a fellow kind could understand.

"I'll go with you." Xiong Wugang said.

Those three words were simple, direct, without any embellishment. But in those three words, too many things were contained—companionship, support, sacrifice, and a promise that was almost life-and-death.

Xiong Zhouping turned his head, looking at Xiong Wugang. There was surprise in his eyes, and gratitude; disbelief, and deep emotion. In the Bear Camp, such promises were rare, such friendships were precious, such companionship was priceless.

"Why?" he asked, his voice somewhat hoarse, as if squeezed from deep in the throat.

Xiong Wugang didn't answer immediately. His gaze passed over Xiong Zhouping, looking toward the distant training ground. There, several young bear beastmen were swinging stone locks, emitting dull roars. Their muscles tensed during exercise, fat layers trembling beneath the skin, like a layer of active armor. Sunlight shone upon their bodies, reflecting faint light on their black uniforms.

Xiong Wugang's gaze was hollow, like a dried-up well, bottomless. Beneath that hollowness hid some deep pain, some unspeakable sorrow, some secret only he himself knew.

"I have my reasons too." Xiong Wugang said, his voice becoming even lower, almost a whisper. That whisper propagated through the air, carrying a moist, trembling quality, as if coming from some distant place. "But I don't want to say now."

Xiong Zhouping looked into Xiong Wugang's eyes. In those dark gray eyes, he saw something—a weariness deeper than death, a burden heavier than despair, a deep grief only someone who had experienced great pain could understand.

Xiong Zhouping didn't press further. In the Bear Camp, everyone had their own secrets. Bear beastmen were not as good at expressing themselves as tiger beastmen; they preferred to keep their thoughts buried in their hearts, like silent mountain peaks. They believed that true pain didn't need words to express, true determination didn't need oaths to prove. Sometimes, silence was more powerful than words, companionship more comforting than questioning.

He reached out, covering the palm Xiong Wugang had placed on his shoulder. Two palms stacked together, one large one small, one thick one thin, but both rough, warm, full of power. That contact was brief, only a few seconds, but in those few seconds, more was conveyed than a thousand words.

Sunlight continued to shine, the basalt ground continued to emit heat, the other bear beastmen in the square continued their training. But on that large rock at the edge of the square, two bears sat quietly, shoulder to shoulder, hand over hand, waiting for fate's arrival.

They were no longer alone. No matter what was coming, they would face it together. This was the bear beastman's way—not the fastest, not the sharpest, but the toughest, the most patient, the most understanding of supporting each other in silence.

Xiong Zhouping closed his eyes again, feeling the heat of the sun. But this time, that heat had a different quality—no longer lonely warmth, but shared warmth; no longer hopeless waiting, but waiting with hope.

Because he was no longer waiting alone.

## Scene-2: Eight Mountains

The registration office was located on the first floor of the Bear Camp administration building.

Unlike the Tiger Camp's clamor, the Bear Camp registration office was silent. Eleven bear beastmen stood in line, no one speaking, only heavy breathing sounds and dull thuds of footsteps on the ground. That silence was not awkward, tense silence, but natural, comfortable, almost meditative silence. Bear beastmen didn't need words to fill the void; they could find peace in silence, find strength in waiting.

That breathing sound was special—bear beastmen's nasal passages were short and wide, producing a low, cave-wind-like sound when breathing. Every breath was accompanied by the rise and fall of the chest cavity, that rise and fall obvious, visible to the naked eye, because the thick fat layer and muscle layer formed huge displacement when breathing. When a bear beastman took a deep breath, his chest cavity would expand, abdomen would bulge, fat layer would ripple like water waves; when he exhaled, chest cavity contracted, abdomen receded, fat layer would tremble accordingly.

These breathing sounds intertwined in the corridor, forming a deep, sustained, earth-heartbeat-like echo. That sound was not harsh; it was comfortable. Not tense; it was relaxing. It was like a natural white noise, able to soothe nerves, let people enter a semi-meditative state.

The sound of footsteps on the ground was also special—not the crisp "tap tap" sound, but the dull "thud thud" sound, like some heavy object being repeatedly thrown, like some huge drum being slowly struck. Every footfall made the ground tremble slightly, made the walls produce fine resonance, made dust sift down from the ceiling.

That was the manifestation of weight and power. Every bear beastman's weight was above one hundred forty kilograms; their every step was a declaration of this weight, a slight impact on the earth. Walking in the Bear Camp corridor was like traveling inside some colossal creature—walls were ribs, ceiling was diaphragm, ground was stomach wall, and they were the cells and blood inside this huge creature.

Xiong Zhouping stood in the middle of the line, Xiong Wugang in front of him, and behind him was a young bear beastman—Xiong Zhenghua.

His fur color was light gray, relatively rare among bear beastmen, like brown washed by water, carrying a soft, almost elegant quality. Because of this special fur color, plus the abnormal pallor of the pads on his palms, he got the nickname "Xiong Zhenghua"—Zhenghua, in ancient language meaning "exquisite flower"; for a bear beastman, this was both praise and satire.

Xiong Zhenghua's fat layer was thinner than Xiong Zhouping and Xiong Wugang's, only about three centimeters. This made him appear more "lean," but also meant his protective ability was weaker. On this night, under the mead's effect, in naked honesty, in the exchange of stories, they were no longer just eight desperate warriors. They were a group, a family, a bond deeper than blood.

Tomorrow, the drawing ceremony would decide their opponents. The day after, the tournament would begin; they would fight on the arena, face life-and-death trials. But at this moment, in this late night barracks, they were just eight bears, just eight lives sharing stories and mead.

This was their strength—not from muscle, not from fat, not from technique or experience. But from this connection, from this understanding, from this almost sacred bond of still being able to support each other in despair.

Eight mountains. Before the storm came, they leaned closely together.
