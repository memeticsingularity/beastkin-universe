# Chapter 1 The Crimson Declaration

---

## Prologue

> The night in the White Capital never truly sleeps. Neon light pollution washes over the black glass curtain walls of the Beastshield headquarters, casting blurred, ever-shifting colored shadows onto the ice-smooth surface of the conference table on the top floor. Up here, the street noise is inaudible; only the sustained low-frequency hum of the central air conditioning and the slow spiraling, tearing, and re-fusing trajectories of cigar smoke in the still air. Besides tobacco, the air carries the scent of premium leather, polished wood, and several expensive yet clashing colognes. But beneath all of this, deep within that eight-meter-long table carved from a single ironwood tree, there seems to permeate an even more fundamental, inerasable odor—the cold metallic tang of blood, the rust of settled power, and a nearly odorless yet unmistakably dangerous pheromone left by apex predators marking their territory, refined through countless meetings.

---

## Scene-1: The Silent Wait

Seven people sat in the conference room.

At the far end of the long table, the seat of Chairman "Ya" remained empty, its high-backed chair resembling a black throne. On the table, seven low-angle reading lamps each cast a small pool of warm yellow light, illuminating the cups, documents, or folded hands beside them, while leaving the greater part of each person's face sunk in soft shadow, their full expressions unreadable.

The Minister of Human Resources, the silverback gorilla beastman "Gangu" (Steel Bone), sat in the first seat to the left of the empty chair. His massive frame nearly filled the custom high-backed chair; his thick, gray-furred arms rested on the armrests, knuckles as large and hard as iron walnuts. He wore a tailored black suit, but the muscles of his shoulders and chest stretched the fabric so taut that every seam was visibly straining. He leaned back slightly, eyes closed, as if dozing—only the occasional turn of his thick neck betrayed his impatience. His body odor was heavy, carrying the gorilla's characteristic blend of aged leather and male musk, forming a ponderous top note in the air-conditioned room.

Opposite him sat the Chief Drill Instructor of the Combat Troops, the rhinoceros beastman "Zhongyan" (Heavy Rock). Zhongyan wore no suit, only a skintight black sleeveless tactical vest that exposed his shoulders and arms, muscled and stacked like piles of rock. His skin was a deep blue-gray, thick and coarse, gleaming under the lights with a leather-like luster, its surface covered with the fine scars of age and battle. An old, hypertrophic scar running across his entire forehead made his already fierce face even more intimidating. He was slowly and attentively trimming the end of a cigar with a small knife; wood shavings and tobacco fragments fell onto the gleaming tabletop with a soft rustling sound. His breathing was heavy, each exhalation carrying the moist, distinctive snort of a rhinoceros.

The Minister of Propaganda, the fire-fox beastman "Honglin" (Red Phosphorus), sat beside Gangu. Slender of build, his fiery-red fur was groomed to perfection, flowing like liquid flame under the lights. He wore a well-tailored white suit, and his narrow eyes behind gold-rimmed glasses rapidly scanned data on the tablet before him, his fingertips sliding across the screen in silence. A glass of clear water sat before him, its surface beaded with condensation. His body odor was faint, a dry, slightly sweet fox scent, but in this room saturated with male hormones it was almost completely suppressed. Only when he moved slightly would a trace of something like burnt caramel drift into the air.

The Chief Financial Officer, the hippopotamus beastman "Shentan" (Deep Pool), sat beside Zhongyan, taking up nearly the width of two chairs. His skin was dark and oily, like a waterlogged swamp. His massive head hung low, heavy eyelids drooping as if he were napping. His stubby fingers tapped the tabletop in a rhythmic pattern, producing dull "thud, thud" sounds like distant drumbeats. His body odor was moist and heavy, carrying the algae scent characteristic of aquatic creatures and some deep, silt-like fishiness.

The Minister of Intelligence, the gray wolf beastman "Yingya" (Shadow Fang), sat in the shadows nearest the door, almost blending into the background. Lean of frame, his gray-black fur was short and stiff; a pair of ice-blue eyes glinted faintly in the darkness. He made no unnecessary movements, merely observing everyone in silence. His scent was cold and dry, like metal in winter, nearly imperceptible—a natural gift that served him well as the head of intelligence.

The Minister of Logistics, the wild boar beastman "Jianti" (Iron Hoof), sat opposite Honglin, irritably adjusting his tie. Tall and powerfully built, his dark-brown bristles stubbornly poked out from the collar and cuffs of his suit; two curved tusks pressed against his lower lip. Before him lay a thick paper notebook covered in haphazard symbols and numbers. His body odor was intense and aggressive, carrying the wild boar's characteristic gamy and earthy scent, but currently suppressed beneath cologne into an unpleasant hybrid smell.

There was one more empty seat, belonging to the Director of Field Operations, the cheetah beastman "Jifeng" (Swift Wind), who was absent due to an emergency assignment.

Seven minutes passed in silence. Only the rustle of the cigar cutter, the rhythmic tapping of fingers, the hum of the air conditioner, and several distinctly different, varying concentrations of male beastman scents slowly mixing and settling in the air.

Gangu finally opened his eyes; his cloudy yellow irises swept over the empty throne, then slowly closed again. He raised his left hand and glanced at the heavy mechanical watch on his wrist—large enough to cover half a normal person's forearm—the fluorescent hands glowing green in the dimness.

"The Chairman is late."

Zhongyan didn't look up, his voice grinding like two stones in a deep jar. He finished trimming the cigar, placed it between his lips, lit it, and inhaled deeply. A thick, choking plume of smoke billowed out, instantly altering the local scent composition of the conference room. The smoke carried the pungency of Cuban tobacco and a fermented sweetness, forming a blue haze under the lights.

"He is never late." Honglin set down his tablet and adjusted his glasses; a sharp glint flashed behind the lenses. "He only appears at the most opportune moment."

The moment he finished speaking, the electronic lock on the heavy soundproof door emitted an extremely soft "beep," and the green indicator light came on. The door swung open without a sound.

No footsteps.

A figure walked in.

## Scene-2: The Arrival of Ya

The one who entered was Chairman "Ya."

He was a wolf-like yet not-quite-wolf beastman with white fur. His build was not the largest in the room—indeed, he was leaner than Zhongyan and Gangu—but when he walked into that dim domain woven by the seven reading lamps, the air in the entire room seemed to abruptly drop three degrees in temperature and increase tenfold in density.

He wore no suit, only a crisp black turtleneck tactical shirt and matching trousers, the fabric clinging to his body and tracing the lines of lean, explosive musculature. His fur was pure snow-white, without a trace of impurity; as he moved, that white coat seemed to glow with its own light in the dimness, carrying a cold, unsettling beauty. His facial features were lean and sharp, ice-blue eyes gleaming faintly in the low light, reflecting a cold micro-luminescence. The corners of his mouth turned down naturally, devoid of any expression.

Most striking were his hands. He wore no ornaments; his fingers were long, the knuckles clearly defined, nails translucent keratin with sharp, hook-like tips that gleamed with a faint ivory luster under the lights. When he pulled out his high-backed chair, the legs scraped against the marble floor, producing a teeth-grating, extremely faint sharp sound.

He sat down, his movements economical, without any superfluous motion. His gaze didn't even sweep across anyone present, but fixed levelly on the empty desktop before him.

No greetings, no pleasantries, not even a "let's begin" look.

But the atmosphere in the conference room had completely changed. Zhongyan stopped puffing smoke and rested his cigar gently on the edge of the heavy glass ashtray. Shentan's tapping fingers stilled. Jianti stopped fiddling with his tie. Yingya seemed to straighten slightly in the shadows. Honglin quietly adjusted the angle of his tablet. Gangu slowly sat up, his thick back muscles creating more pronounced wrinkles in his suit.

"Begin." Ya's voice rang out—not loud, not low, without inflection, like a piece of ice-soaked metal scraping across glass. His vocal cords seemed unusual, carrying a strange, cold friction quality; every syllable was piercingly clear, driving straight into the eardrums.

Gangu took a deep breath, his chest swelling, then exhaled slowly. He reached out and switched on a small projector before him. A beam of cold light projected into the air above the center of the long table, forming a suspended, semi-transparent screen.

"E-level Combat Troops, data from the past six months."

Gangu's voice was far deeper than Ya's, but also deliberately lowered, carrying the flat cadence of a work report. Charts, curves, and numbers began scrolling across the screen.

"Fatality rate: twenty-three point seven percent. Severe injury and disability rate: fifteen point four percent. Non-combat attrition rate: eight point nine percent. New recruit replacement rate, net increase after training attrition: eighteen point two percent."

The numbers were cold red and blue. Most curves trended downward or flat.

"The gap is widening; quality is declining." Gangu continued, his thick fingers swiping across the screen to call up another set of comparative data. "Five years ago during the same period, the average service term of an E-level combat soldier was four and a half years, with approximately thirty high-intensity missions executable during that time. Now, the average service term has shortened to three years and two months, with high-intensity mission frequency estimated at no more than twenty-two. Yet training costs have risen by seventeen percent."

Zhongyan snorted, the sound blasting from his nostrils along with thick smoke and dissatisfaction. "The pups these days have soft bones." He brutally crushed the freshly lit cigar between his thick fingers, the impact of fingertip against glass ashtray producing a crisp sound. "In training camp they cry for their mothers; at the sight of blood their legs go soft. The higher-ups want 'efficiency,' want 'cost control'—training time cut again and again, and what comes out are half-finished products! On the battlefield, how could the consumption rate not be high?"

Shentan spoke up slowly, his voice moist and thick, carrying the hippopotamus's characteristic, seemingly ever-sleepy nasal tone, but his words were sharp: "Costs aren't just training. Pensions, medical expenses, equipment wear, indirect losses from morale decline... all rising. Chief Instructor Zhongyan is right—half-finished products have extremely poor cost performance. We're using premium feed to raise a batch of unqualified consumables." As he spoke, his thick lips parted slightly, revealing large, yellowed teeth; a moist, waterweed-tinged breath spread with his exhalation.

"It's not merely a combat effectiveness issue." Honglin took over, his voice clear and rapid, like a fox's claws lightly tapping ice. "The public's 'interest threshold' for conventional conflicts is rising rapidly. Last week's real-time viewership for the Eastern District warehouse suppression operation peaked at only sixteen point three percent, and that was concentrated in the first five minutes of the conflict. People are tired of one-sided crushing. They need... something more stimulating, more unpredictable, something that can mobilize primal emotions." He paused, his narrow eyes glancing toward Ya at the head of the table, and added, "We need a new 'product' to maintain attention, consolidate deterrence, and simultaneously... create new revenue streams."

Ya didn't look at anyone, nor at the screen. His gaze fell upon his own right hand resting on the tabletop. His long fingers curved slightly, those hook-sharp nails tapping the smooth wood grain in an almost inaudible, rhythmic "tap... tap..." sound.

This minute sound, in the sudden silence of the conference room, was so clear that everyone involuntarily tensed their nerves.

"Speak." Ya uttered a single word.

Gangu and Honglin exchanged a rapid glance. Gangu operated the projector again, and the screen's contents changed. No more complex charts—only a few lines of bolded core text, and a crimson, tension-filled logo design sketch: crossed beast claws and a shield, with dripping blood.

The title was: **"Draft Proposal for the First Beastshield Four-Camp Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament."**

---

## Scene-3: The Fangs of the Proposal

"Our proposal is to hold a company-wide, even city-wide public, extreme life-and-death fighting tournament." Gangu's voice dropped, carrying the gravity of presenting a major project, as well as a trace of barely concealed excitement.

Text began appearing on the screen line by line.

"Core elements:

"One: **Minimalist Rules**—one on one, unarmed, no-restrictions combat. The only termination condition: the death of one party. No referee intervention, no rounds, no time limit.

"Two: **Factional Confrontation**—contestants divided by race into Tiger, Bear, Ox, and Dog four camps, eight per camp, thirty-two total. Single-elimination bracket.

"Three: **Homologous Avoidance**—for the first two rounds, contestants from the same camp will not face each other. From the third round onward, this rule is void.

"Four: **Prize Incentive."** Gangu paused, emphasizing his tone. "A dynamic prize calculation formula will be established, tightly binding the prize money to camp survival depth, stimulating collective honor and internal competition."

The screen displayed that complex formula:
`Single Match Prize = 500 Lu Coins × (9 - Pre-match surviving members of own camp) × (9 - Pre-match surviving members of opponent camp)`

Shentan spoke up at the right moment, his moist, thick voice carrying calculator-like precision: "Allow me to explain. In the first round, both camps are at full strength of eight, so the formula is 500 times 1 times 1—that is, five hundred Lu coins. It doesn't look like much. But if one camp suffers heavy losses early on, then as the tournament progresses and their surviving members decrease, this multiplier will increase dramatically. Imagine if we reach the semifinals and both camps have only one member left—the single match prize would be 500 times 8 times 8, thirty-two thousand Lu coins. The champion's cumulative prize would be an amount... sufficient to drive any bottom-rung soldier insane."

His stubby fingers pointed in the air as if manipulating an invisible abacus: "But the key is that the formula's brilliance lies in 'expectation management.' The vast majority of contestants will die in the first two rounds, without winning much. Only a tiny minority can reach the later stages and claim astronomical sums. This both creates the legend of 'overnight riches' to attract applicants, and keeps actual prize expenditures within budget. Moreover," he paused, a gleam flashing in his small eyes, "the scale of the prize pool itself will become the tournament's greatest promotional point and the focal point of gambling wagers."

"Continue." Ya's fingertips still tapped the desktop, the rhythm unchanged.

"Five: **Severe Withdrawal Mechanism."** Honglin took the baton, his voice turning colder. "We call it the 'Fate Lottery.' Contestants may surrender before or during a match, but require their opponent's consent. If surrender takes effect, or if they withdraw before a match, that contestant forfeits all earned prize money and must publicly draw one of eight lots to receive immediate punishment."

The screen listed eight penalties:

- **Lot One: Departure with Funds**—may retain all prize money earned before withdrawal, free to leave (safest but extremely low probability)
- **Lot Two: Goods for Sale**—immediately converted into "captive courier" merchandise, all rights stripped, sold to highest bidder
- **Lot Three: Rank Stripping**—demoted to G-level green-uniform laborer, assigned to most grueling posts
- **Lot Four: Heavy Fine**—must pay a fine equivalent to "prize money that would have been won if victorious"; those unable to pay converted to Lot Eight
- **Lot Five: Unpaid Labor**—forced to perform one month of unpaid high-risk labor
- **Lot Six: Public Execution**—executed in public before the next match day begins
- **Lot Seven: Frontier Exile**—banished to remote outposts, never to return to the White Capital
- **Lot Eight: Forced Participation**—withdrawal request denied, must complete the match; cowardice recorded in permanent file

"The message we want to send," Honglin adjusted his glasses, his gaze sweeping across everyone, "is that stepping onto this ring leaves only two paths: win, or die. Any hesitation, any weakness, will result in consequences more shameful and cruel than death. This not only filters out the weak-willed, but also greatly enhances the tournament's... dramatic tension and topicality."

Zhongyan bared his teeth in a near-feral grin, revealing tobacco-stained, massive molars: "This is good. Now this has some teeth. Raise soldiers for a thousand days, use them for one moment. Whether they're mules or horses, drag them up for a real fight—those who survive are the truly useful ones. The dead, consider them garbage disposal." He looked toward Ya. "I agree. At least it'll leave me with some bones that can actually be used."

Jianti scratched his coarse neck, producing a rustling sound: "What about logistics pressure? Thirty-two E-level troops pulled away, even temporarily, will strain defense everywhere. And there's the venue, medical care, corpse disposal..."

"That is precisely the second part." Gangu switched the projection. "**Tournament Revenue and Risk Hedging Model.**"

Complex bar charts and cash flow projections appeared on the screen.

Shentan's spirits seemed to lift somewhat; he leaned forward, his massive belly pressing against the table edge: "Preliminary calculations. Direct costs: potential pensions (estimated at maximum), tournament operations, venue, publicity, basic medical care. Direct revenue: broadcast rights (city-wide public screens + pay channels), official gambling house cut, sponsor fees, licensed merchandise sales. Conservative estimate: total revenue during the tournament period will be fifteen to twenty times the peak prize pool scale. Net profit will be considerable."

"More important is the indirect revenue." Honglin added, his tone turning somewhat feverish. "This is not merely a tournament. It is a sustained, month-plus city-wide carnival **media event**! It can reignite public awe and discussion of Beastshield's military power, divert internal contradictions, create new stars (or rather, tragic heroes), test new propaganda methods, and pave the way for subsequent possible 'series' or related derivative products. The brand value and deterrence enhancement are difficult to quantify in monetary terms."

Yingya spoke for the first time, his voice low and carrying the wolf's characteristic hoarseness, his pace measured yet like cold wind through a crack: "Intelligence department assessment: risks are controllable. Bottom-rung soldiers' resentment will be diverted by 'opportunity' and 'gambling.' Mid-level officers' resistance can be neutralized with 'recommendation bonuses for successful contestants' or 'camp performance linked to evaluations.' Probability of external interference is low; they are more likely to participate in gambling or attempt to contact promising contestants. The main risk lies in... unexpected casualties during the tournament, or one camp being eliminated too early, affecting entertainment value and gambling balance. Contingency plans will be needed."

Gangu summarized: "In conclusion, we believe the 'Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament' is a highly efficient, multi-functional solution: One, **Screening Mechanism**—using the cruelest and most direct method to eliminate the weak and preserve the strong, forcing out true warriors. Two, **Financial Instrument**—creating massive profits while optimizing long-term manpower cost structure. Three, **Propaganda Weapon**—reshaping corporate image, maintaining public attention and deterrence. Four, **Management Experiment**—testing the applicability of new incentive and punishment models within the company."

The proposal presentation was complete.

The conference room fell into silence once more. Only the soft whir of the projector fan, and Ya's continuous, rhythmic fingertip tapping on the desktop.

All eyes, overt or covert, focused on that expressionless, white-and-black-striped profile.

He stopped tapping.

Slowly, extremely slowly, he moved his gaze from his own fingertips to the suspended lines of crimson title glowing in the air.

His amber vertical pupils seemed to have molten gold slowly flowing within them, refracted by the light.

## Scene-4: The Tempering of Rules

Time seemed to pass for minutes, yet also for only seconds.

Ya finally spoke, his voice still cold and steady, but his questions precisely piercing toward the details.

"Prize formula." he said. "Why is the multiplier base '9'?"

Shentan answered immediately: "For calculation intuitiveness and promotional effect. '9 minus surviving members'—in the first round when survivors are 8, the multiplier is 1. If the base were '8,' then the first-round multiplier would be 0, which doesn't sound good in publicity. '9' allows the numbers to start 'growing' from the first round, creating a sense of accumulation. It's merely a numbers game; it doesn't affect the actual prize calculation logic."

Ya's gaze turned to Honglin: "'Fate Lottery.' The eighth lot, 'Forced Participation.' If one who draws this lot attempts to withdraw again or competes passively, how is it handled?"

Honglin was already prepared: "Treated as serious defiance of company orders and tournament spirit, executed on the spot by the opponent or designated personnel, with all personal property confiscated for public treasury. Their death will not affect tournament proceedings, and their camp's surviving member count will be reduced accordingly. This clause will be written into the contestant handbook's detailed rules, ensuring everyone is informed."

"Camp division." Ya's gaze swept across Zhongyan and Gangu. "Only four? What of other races?"

Gangu replied: "Tiger, Bear, Ox, and Dog are currently the most numerous, most representative, and most easily narrativized-for-conflict groups among E-level combat troops. Other races such as Leopard, Wolf, Wild Boar, etc., are fewer in number or dispersed among camps as support. Forcing them into separate camps would have little meaning and might weaken the confrontation focus of the main camps. They may participate as individuals under their daily affiliated camp. Of course, if the tournament expands in the future, additional camps can be considered."

"Medical standards." Ya looked at Jianti.

Jianti cleared his throat, somewhat nervous: "Only the most basic field-emergency-level treatment will be provided. Hemostasis, bandaging, fracture immobilization, pain relief. No advanced regenerative therapy, organ repair, or rapid rehabilitation services. Two purposes: one, cost control; two, ensuring injuries can genuinely affect contestants' subsequent match conditions, increasing uncertainty and cruelty. The severely injured either fight desperately or... are naturally eliminated."

"Contestant selection." Ya asked finally. "Voluntary?"

"Primarily voluntary." Gangu answered. "Notices will be posted in each camp, with a two-day registration period. At the designated time, volunteers step forward; first come first served, up to eight. If registration is insufficient... the camp commander will randomly draw from remaining personnel to fill the quota. We need to ensure each camp has eight members to activate the camp confrontation narrative and gambling mechanics."

Ya fell silent again. His gaze slowly swept across every face on either side of the long table. Gangu's steadiness, Zhongyan's cruelty, Honglin's shrewdness, Shentan's calculation, Jianti's pragmatism, Yingya's coldness. Finally, his gaze returned to the screen in the air, to that crimson title.

"**Blood-Bounty.**" He spoke these two words, his voice carrying for the first time a trace of something so faint it was impossible to identify—perhaps amusement, perhaps confirmation.

"Yes." Gangu said heavily. "Blood as bounty, life as wager. Simple, direct, impactful."

Ya's body shifted slightly backward, leaning against the high-backed chair. This subtle movement seemed to loosen the invisible pressure in the conference room by a millimeter.

"Approved." He uttered two words.

No excitement, no praise—just a cold authorization.

But the atmosphere in the conference room instantly changed. The corner of Shentan's mouth twitched upward almost imperceptibly. Honglin's fingertips traced lightly along the edge of his tablet. Zhongyan snorted and picked up his cigar again. Gangu exhaled almost invisibly, his thick shoulders sinking slightly.

"Details." Ya continued. "Final prize multiplier range, 1 to 16 times, to be determined by the Board based on champion performance. Specific evaluation criteria—Honglin, you draft the proposal."

"Yes." Honglin nodded.

"Publicity." Ya's gaze turned to Honglin. "Don't only emphasize blood and money. Add packaging of 'honor,' 'proof,' 'Beastshield spirit,' 'the ultimate showdown across races.' The bottom rungs need fantasy; the audience needs stories. Package this massacre as a great trial."

"Understood. We will construct a complete narrative framework, from contestant backstories, to camp grudges, to each match's 'hero's journey' or 'tragic demise.'" Honglin's speech accelerated, his eyes gleaming with the light of planning.

"Logistics and venue." Ya looked at Jianti. "Headquarters basement level three, the abandoned old storage area, converted into arena and contestant closed management zone. It must be sturdy, easy to clean, with sufficient camera positions and spectator seating. Twenty days."

"Twenty days... the timeline is tight, but it can be done." Jianti gritted his teeth and made a fierce note in his notebook.

"Intelligence and security." Ya finally looked at Yingya. "During the tournament period, external security level raised by one. Internally, closely monitor all contestants, staff, and tournament-related financial flows. Any anomalies, handle directly, no need to report."

"Yes." Yingya's response was brief and forceful.

Ya raised his hand and used those sharp nails to lightly tap his own temple, then pointed at the screen.

"This name is good." he said. "Blood-Bounty. Tell all employees, tell the entire city—in Beastshield, your life can be very cheap, or very expensive. Between cheap and expensive, it only depends on how much blood you dare to shed, and what you are willing to trade for it."

He stood up.

His movements were still economical, without any dragging. His tall figure cast a long, pitch-black shadow against the backlight, covering nearly half the conference table.

"Execute."

Having spoken, he turned and left the conference room as silently as he had arrived.

The door closed without a sound, the electronic lock emitting a soft "click."

In the conference room, silence reigned for a full five seconds.

Then Zhongyan erupted in a coarse laugh, relighting his cigar. "Hahaha! Now that's to my taste! Damn, I've long wanted to see those brats tear off their usual fake skins and see how crazy they can really get!"

Shentan leisurely picked up the cool tea before him and drank a large gulp, his Adam's apple bobbing with an audible swallow. "If the profit margin meets expectations, next year we can hold a small quarterly tournament every quarter as warm-up and selection..."

Honglin was already rapidly recording key points on his tablet, muttering under his breath: "Narrative angles... honor and redemption... tragic heroes... family mission... yes, and we need 'unexpected dark horses' and 'rival showdown' script room..."

Gangu turned off the projector; the screen vanished, and the conference room was once again shrouded in dimness and scattered lamplight. He rubbed his brow, his thick fingers pressing against his forehead and leaving faint red marks.

"Alright." His voice regained its usual steadiness and force. "The Chairman has approved. All departments, move immediately according to the division of labor and deadlines just discussed. This is no longer a proposal; it is a company-level project that must succeed. Dismissed."

The others rose one by one; the sound of chairs scraping against the floor, conversations, the rustling of documents being gathered, and low laughter gradually filled the room. A complex scent began to permeate the conference room—a mixture of released pressure, rising ambition, and cold excitement.

They were beginning to meticulously construct a meat grinder.

And drape it in the garments of glory and dreams.

## Scene-5: The Crimson Declaration

Forty-eight hours later.

The giant exterior screens of Beastshield headquarters, the public billboards in the White Capital city center, the notice boards in each camp district, even the sides of buses traversing the city... were simultaneously covered in a glaring crimson.

It was a meticulously designed dynamic logo: the Beastshield claw-and-shield emblem slowly emerging from boiling, viscous blood, then being torn apart by two brutally crossing wounds. The wounds transformed into two dripping characters—

**Blood-Bounty.**

The background sound was a heavy, slow drumbeat like the heartbeat of a colossal beast, mixed with faint, suppressed roars and the sharp clash of metal.

The scene switched, showing rapidly edited clips: a dirt-and-sweat-caked beastman fist clenched tight; a scar-riddled muscular back tensing with exertion; pairs of beast-pupils glinting with resolve or madness in the shadows; a close-up of a blood-stained black E-level uniform.

A provocative, deep, and majestic voice-over sounded, overpowering the heartbeat and roaring:

**"Is your strength merely decoration, or is it fangs?"**
**"Is your life merely a consumable, or is it a chip?"**
**"Who defines your worth?"**

The camera pulled back to reveal a rock arena bathed in spotlights, radiating primal power.

**"Beastshield Corporation proudly presents—"**
**"The First Four-Camp Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament!"**

Soaring, epic music suddenly exploded!

**"Thirty-two slots! Combat to the death!"**
**"The ultimate showdown across races! The winner takes all!"**
**"Prove your existence with blood! Seize your future with victory!"**

The screen rapidly flashed explosive growth animations of prize numbers, finally freezing on that complex and tantalizing prize formula.

**"Registration is open! Details available at camp notices!"**
**"Destiny is in your hands! Blood is your bounty!"**

Finally, the logo appeared again; the two characters "Blood-Bounty" seemed formed from flowing blood. Below them, a line of small text:
**Beastshield glory, passed from flame to flame.**

The promotional video cut off abruptly at its climax.

The screen went black, then returned to normal content.

But in countless corners of the White Capital—before camp notice boards, beneath tavern televisions, in dark alleyways—this declaration ignited something long suppressed, like sparks cast into boiling oil.

Shock. Fear. Fever. Calculation. Disdain. Ambition.

Countless emotions churned and fermented in the chests of countless beastmen.

And on the Ox Camp mudflats beside the old river channel, in the Tiger Camp training grounds at headquarters, in the Dog Camp dormitory district of the old factory building, that crimson poster was being touched, read, and digested by one pair of hands after another.

Thirty-two lives were about to make their choices.

The era of Blood-Bounty had begun.

---

## Epilogue

> The decision was reached coldly in the top-floor conference room suffused with cigar smoke and cologne, transformed into crimson symbols and impassioned sound waves that surged into damp barracks, noisy taverns, and silent streets. It was a beautifully packaged poison, a clearly priced slaughter permit. The abacus beads of the high-levels clicked crisply, while the dice of fate had already been cast high, accelerating their fall toward thirty-two souls who did not yet know, or already sensed, what awaited them. The gears had meshed; the feast was about to begin—only it remained unknown who would be seated at the dining table, and who would be upon the plate.

### Their Final Stories

【Living Entity → Living - High-Level - Chairman - White Wolf Beastman - Ya】

He is the architect of this massacre, yet in this first chapter, he has not even personally killed a single person.

Ya's body is a symbol of power. A lean wolf beastman's physique, smaller than the other beasts in the conference room, yet the sense of oppression is utterly real. His fur is pure snow-white, without a trace of impurity; as he moves, that white coat seems to glow with its own light, carrying a cold, unsettling beauty. His nails—translucent keratin with sharp, hook-like tips—produce a "tap-tap" sound when drumming on the desktop, the metronome of death.

His scent is cold. Not the dry cold of Yingya, but a deliberately suppressed, nearly odorless, apex-predator danger pheromone. This scent causes the other males in the conference room—larger Gangu, more ferocious Zhongyan—to subconsciously tense their nerves.

But beneath this coldness, something flows.

When he asked "why is the multiplier base 9," when he pressed "how is forced participation handled if they attempt to withdraw again," that precise attention to detail exposed something. This is not a leader who only cares about the big picture; this is an artist who enjoys the process of rule design, in a sense.

### Story Reflections

While writing this chapter, I kept thinking about one question: **Who is the true murderer?**

Is it the eventual victor on the arena? Is it those E-level troops who tear each other to death in the octagon? Or... is it these people in suits and leather shoes sitting in the conference room, who won't even stain their hands with a drop of blood?

Ya, Gangu, Honglin, Shentan, Yingya, Jianti, Zhongyan—each of them has their own body, their own scent, their own sexual characteristics. They are tiger, gorilla, fox, hippopotamus, wolf, wild boar, rhinoceros. But in this conference room, these biological differences are erased by something more powerful: **the homogenization of power**.

Ya's body is lean, but his power is inflated. When he sits there, using his nails to tap the desktop and produce "tap-tap" sounds, he needs no muscles, no claws, not even voice—only rhythm, to make a roomful of fierce beasts bow their heads.

Gangu's body is massive, but his power is fragile. He needs data, needs charts, needs a nice-sounding name like "Blood-Bounty" to package his proposal. His muscles are display pieces, not weapons.

Honglin's body is slender, but his power is corrosive. He does not use violence; he uses narrative. He transforms violence into story, death into entertainment, massacre into "a great trial." In this transformation, violence itself is dissolved; what remains is only consumption.

**This is the horror of the Beastshield world.**

It is not simply the law of the jungle. It is the institutionalization, aestheticization, and entertainment of the law of the jungle. The deaths of thirty-two E-level troops are not accidents, not tragedies, but part of "expectation management," reasonable losses in "cost-benefit analysis," necessary plot points in a "media event."

The cologne, cigar smoke, and premium leather in the conference room—these scents constitute a **sterile field** that insulates the decision-makers from the consequences. Ya will not smell the blood on the arena; Honglin will not hear the death throes; Shentan will not see the twitching of toes as corpses are dragged away.

They will only see numbers on reports: viewership, gambling revenue, brand lift.

And this may be more cruel than any direct violence.

In the story that follows, we will leave this conference room and enter damp barracks, pristine training grounds, and dilapidated factory buildings. We will see those thirty-two lives—Niu Gang, Hu Xiao, Quan Zhong, and the others—how they make their choices before that crimson poster.

They do not know about the conversation in the conference room. They do not know their fates have already been decided by a prize formula. They only know that the numbers on that paper may be their only way out.

**Blood as bounty, life as wager.**

This is not a slogan. It is a design from the top floor, a meticulously calculated slaughter permit, a death sentence packaged in Excel spreadsheets and PowerPoint presentations.

And we, as readers, will be forced to witness the full execution of this sentence.

From the moment the poster descends, to the moment the last drop of vital fluid runs dry.

From thirty-two "volunteers" filled with hope or despair, to the final sole survivor, covered in blood yet fabulously wealthy—the "champion."

This is Blood-Bounty.

This is Beastshield.
