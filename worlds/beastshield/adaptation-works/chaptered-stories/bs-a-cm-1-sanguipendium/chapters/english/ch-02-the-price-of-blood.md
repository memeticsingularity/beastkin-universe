## Scene-1: Ink Still Wet

Five-thirty in the morning. The fog of the White Capital had not yet dispersed.

It was a peculiar fog, a grayish-white mixture of industrial exhaust, carrying the astringent scent of metal corrosion. The fog flowed between the high walls of the Beastshield Combat Troops camp like a living entity, sometimes so dense that objects three paces away were invisible, sometimes so thin that morning light could pierce through.

In the central square of the Ox Beastman District, a figure already stood before that rusted cast-iron notice board.

Niu Xuqiang.

Designation E-217, thirty-seven years old, one hundred ninety-eight centimeters tall, one hundred fifty-two and a half kilograms. His fur was grayish-white—not pure white, but a weathered grayish-white mixed with black. The tip of his left horn had a noticeable chip—a memento from a workplace injury three years ago.

He wore the standard uniform of an E-level combat soldier: a black tight-fitting short-sleeve shirt with the white Beastshield logo printed on the left chest; the white stripes on the shoulders had yellowed with age. The uniform was old, issued three years ago, the fabric under the armpits and across the back stained white with salt from sweat.

Niu Xuqiang's gaze fell upon the notice board.

It was a crimson poster. The ink was so thick it seemed capable of bleeding through the paper. In the center were four large characters—"Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament." The edges of the poster bore signs of tearing—someone had tried to rip it down but failed.

He reached out; his thick fingers lightly touched the edge of the poster. The fingertip pads registered a faint stickiness. The ink was still wet.

"Niu Xuqiang, you came too?"

The voice from behind was as low as two rocks grinding in a deep jar. Niu Xuqiang didn't need to turn around to know who it was.

Niu Sunyong. Designation E-189, thirty years old, nine years at E-level. One of the most physically imposing in the Ox Camp—two hundred eight centimeters tall, one hundred sixty-five kilograms.

Niu Xuqiang turned around. Morning light pierced through the fog, falling upon Niu Sunyong's body. Light brown fur, a new uniform, a build even more massive than Niu Xuqiang's. That uniform had been issued just last year; the white stripes on the shoulders were still pristine, forming a stark contrast with Niu Xuqiang's yellowed stripes.

"Taking a look." Niu Xuqiang said, his voice hoarse.

Niu Sunyong's gaze fixed upon the poster, staring directly at the line reading "Champion Prize: 256,000 Lu Coins."

"My younger brother." He spoke, his voice even lower than before. "Diagnosed with ore sickness last month. Early stage. The doctor said, three thousand Lu coins to start."

Niu Xuqiang fell silent. Of course he knew Niu Sunyong's situation. That child was only twelve, an orphan Niu Sunyong had taken in from the slums. He relied on the money Niu Sunyong sent back each month to survive—three hundred a month, three thousand six hundred a year. Fifteen years, fifty-four thousand. Not enough.

"What are you thinking?" Niu Xuqiang asked.

"I'm thinking..." The corner of Niu Sunyong's mouth twitched slightly. "If I can win one match. Five hundred. Enough for his medicine for one month."

He said it lightly. But Niu Xuqiang heard the thousand-jun weight pressing down beneath that lightness.

Five hundred coins. In exchange for a life. Or, in exchange for his own life.

Niu Xuqiang didn't tell Niu Sunyong about his own situation. He wouldn't tell anyone—about that debt that had been rolling in the underground money house for three years, about that figure holding a cleaver that appeared in his dreams every night. Eight thousand principal, fifteen percent monthly interest, compounded. Rolling and rolling until now, it had become fifty-two thousand.

Last month, the money house people had finally found his barracks. Beneath his pillow lay a dagger, its blade bearing dried blood. And a note: "Fifteenth of next month—either pay up, or pay with your life."

Fifteenth of next month. Twenty-three days from now.

And this poster read: Registration deadline, midnight tomorrow.

"Let's go." Niu Xuqiang reached out and clapped Niu Sunyong's shoulder. Palm meeting fabric, he could feel the tension of the muscles beneath. "Let's get something at the mess hall. Today... we still have training today."

The two turned around and saw two more figures emerging from the fog.

Niu Majun stepped out from behind a pillar. Among the ox beastmen, his build was on the leaner side—two hundred two centimeters tall, but only one hundred thirty-seven and a half kilograms. Blue-gray fur, fingers tapping unconsciously in the air as if calculating some rhythm.

Niu Zhuzhi followed in the crowd. Designation E-198, thirty years old, his face always carrying a simple, nearly foolish smile—but not now. His eyes were hollow, as if all emotion had been drained from him. His back was somewhat hunched; there was a slight depression in the uniform over the lower left chest—a remnant of ribs that had fractured and healed incorrectly.

"You guys too..." Niu Sunyong began.

"Owe the gambling house twenty thousand." Niu Majun said directly, the corner of his mouth carrying a faint, almost imperceptible smile. "What's the difference between getting hacked to death in an alley and dying on the arena?"

"I owe the barracks officer ten thousand." Niu Zhuzhi said, his voice hoarse, as if narrating something that had nothing to do with him. "Lost it betting on horses last year. The officer covered it for me, on the condition of paying back five hundred a month for five years. But I can't keep up. Last month I only paid three hundred, and he broke one of my ribs."

As he spoke, he lightly patted his left chest and abdomen—that depressed spot.

"Dead ends either way." Niu Majun looked at the poster. "Might as well choose a death we can decide for ourselves."

The four stood before the poster in silence.

"Heard someone tried to burn the notice board last night." Niu Majun suddenly lowered his voice.

"Who?" Niu Sunyong asked.

"Don't know. But the overseer caught them, threw them in solitary."

Not everyone supported this tournament.

"Count me in."

A voice came from the fog. Niu Huhui walked out of the mist, clutching half a bottle of liquor. Designation E-167, forty-three years old, the oldest E-level soldier in the Ox Camp. Graying fur, his grayish-white coat like frost-covered dead grass.

"Up to my ears in debt—taverns, gambling houses, males..." He took a drink. "Don't want to get sold off by 'captive courier.' Dying on the stage has more dignity than dying in a cargo hold."

Five people stood before the poster.

Footsteps sounded from the fog. A figure in a dark gray uniform approached—the overseer, designation O-127.

"Came early." The overseer swept his gaze across the five, his eyes settling on the poster. "Someone tried to tear this down last night, got thrown in solitary. You guys want to try too?"

The five fell silent.

The overseer's eyes fixed on Niu Xuqiang: "That thing under your pillow, keep it hidden. Don't let others see."

Niu Xuqiang's heart skipped a beat. The overseer knew. But he didn't expose it, only warned.

The overseer turned and left, tossing back one last line: "Signing up is walking to your death. Think carefully."

The five dispersed. Niu Xuqiang was the last to leave, his hand unconsciously moving to his chest—there, beneath the uniform, he could imagine the coldness of that dagger.

In the fog, other figures were approaching the notice board. In a camp of three hundred, they were not the only five with nowhere left to turn.

## Scene-2: Barracks Night Talk

The barracks after lights out.

Moonlight shone through the iron-barred window, casting mottled shadows on the floor. A twelve-man room, two rows of bunk beds, snores rising and falling. But tonight, eight people were not asleep.

Niu Xuqiang lay on the lower bunk, staring at the ceiling. Niu Majun on the upper bunk turned over; the bed boards creaked. Across from him, Niu Sunyong sat on the edge of his bed, clutching that hospital diagnosis in his hand. Niu Zhuzhi lay on his back, eyes open, staring into the darkness. Niu Huhui emitted soft snores from the head of his bed—that was feigned; his hand clutched the liquor bottle tightly.

The door pushed open gently. Three figures slipped inside.

Niu Lingang, designation E-245, lean among the ox beastmen but with clearly defined muscle lines. He swept a vigilant glance around, then walked to Niu Xuqiang's bunk and said in a low voice: "You guys aren't sleeping either?"

Niu Guoming followed behind, designation E-301, thirty years old, his face bearing heavy dark circles, repeatedly rubbing an old pocket watch in his hand. Niu Heping entered last, designation E-233, twenty-two years old, the youngest E-level soldier, his face gloomy, frequently looking toward the door.

Eight people. Eight beds. The same night.

Niu Majun poked his head down from the upper bunk, lowering his voice: "Have you all calculated the odds?"

The group crowded onto Niu Xuqiang's lower bunk, huddling together, speaking in low voices.

"Single match prize = 500 × (9 - pre-match surviving members of own camp) × (9 - pre-match surviving members of opponent camp)." Niu Majun's fingers tapped in the air. "If both sides are at full strength in the first round, that's five hundred. If we can make it to the top eight..."

"We won't." Niu Heping suddenly said, his voice cold. "You think signing up means you'll die? I heard that in the last one someone was crippled, didn't die, got dragged off to be a test subject."

Everyone fell silent.

"I'm being hunted." Niu Lingang broke the silence, turning around to reveal his back—a knife scar ran from his shoulder blade to his waist. "Owed money last month; the creditor wasn't a money house, but someone in the camp. Entering the tournament buys me a few more days of life; not entering means I'm dead tomorrow."

"My mother needs medicine money." Niu Guoming looked at his pocket watch. "She's in the outskirts; I just received word that her condition has worsened. Can't wait any longer."

"The officer is forcing me to sign up." Niu Heping's voice held no inflection. "Said if I sign up, the debt is forgiven. If I don't sign up, tomorrow I'm being transferred to 'captive courier.'"

"I'm forty-three." Niu Huhui finally spoke, the liquor bottle turning in his hand. "Can't beat those youngsters. But at least..." He paused. "Dying on a stage I chose for myself."

Niu Xuqiang didn't speak. His hand beneath the blanket touched the edge of that promissory note—wrinkled, already blurred from sweat. Fifty-two thousand. Fifteenth of next month.

Niu Sunyong didn't speak either. His fingers unconsciously rubbed the edge of the diagnosis, imagining the weight of his brother sleeping on his belly—that layer of soft fat was the most comfortable pillow in his eyes.

"Dead either way." Niu Majun smiled; it was not a smile but a confirmation. "Might as well roll the dice."

The conversation gradually ceased. Each returned to his bed.

Niu Xuqiang lay on his bed, looking at the barbed wire outside the window. The moonlight was cold white, carrying a metallic quality. His fingers clutched the promissory note, nails digging into his palm.

Niu Sunyong closed his eyes; his brother's face surfaced in his mind. That blue-gray patch, spreading from the arm to the forearm.

Niu Majun poured three dice from a toothpaste tube, held them in his palm for a moment, then put them away. He didn't roll. He knew the result didn't matter.

Niu Zhuzhi stared at the ceiling, eyes hollow. He was thinking, after dying, would he no longer have to move crates.

Niu Huhui drained the last of his liquor and stuffed the bottle under the bed. He dreamed he was at a gambling table in a tavern, but this time, he was betting his own life.

Niu Lingang lay on his side, back to everyone, fingers touching that knife scar. The pain was real, proof of being alive.

Niu Guoming pressed the pocket watch to his chest, listening to the ticking of the hands. His mother had given it to him, the year he enlisted.

Niu Heping kept his eyes open until dawn. He had no dreams, no hopes, only a fatalistic calm.

Eight people. Eight lives. Eight reasons they had no choice but to fight.

## Scene-3: Eight Oxen

Six in the morning. Central square.

Three hundred E-level combat soldiers stood in neat formation. Their black uniforms gleamed with a matte finish in the sunlight, like a silent ocean.

Niu Xuqiang stood at the front of the formation. His uniform was old, the white stripes on the shoulders yellowed, the edges of the logo on the left chest worn. But his spine was straight, his gaze level.

Behind him, Niu Sunyong's massive frame was like a mountain. His new uniform gleamed in the sunlight; the white stripes on the shoulders were pristine as new.

Niu Majun stood in the third row, blue-gray fur, fingers tapping lightly at the seams of his trousers. Niu Zhuzhi stood beside him, eyes hollow, shoulders slightly hunched forward.

Niu Huhui stood at the edge of the formation, graying fur, hands trembling slightly, but his back ramrod straight.

Niu Lingang, Niu Guoming, and Niu Heping stood in the rear rows. One vigilant as prey, one haggard as dead wood, one gloomy as a deep pool.

Overseer O-127 stood before the formation, his gaze sweeping across everyone.

"Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament registration confirmation. When your name is called, step forward three paces and fall out."

"E-217, Niu Xuqiang."

Niu Xuqiang took three steps. His boots struck the concrete ground with a dull sound.

"E-189, Niu Sunyong."

Niu Sunyong followed. His gait was heavy; his one hundred sixty-five kilogram weight left shallow impressions with every step.

"E-256, Niu Majun."

"E-198, Niu Zhuzhi."

"E-167, Niu Huhui."

"E-245, Niu Lingang."

"E-301, Niu Guoming."

"E-233, Niu Heping."

Eight people stood before the formation, forming their own line.

The overseer confirmed one by one: "After registration, withdrawal is prohibited; life and death are your own responsibility. Confirmed?"

"Confirmed." The eight spoke in unison.

The overseer's gaze lingered on the eight faces, finally settling on Niu Xuqiang: "Tomorrow at noon, the drawing ceremony."

The eight turned to face the square. The gazes of three hundred comrades fell upon them—some envious, some sympathetic, some fearful, some indifferent.

Tieli stood in the crowd, clenching his fists, ultimately not taking that step. Shidun stood beside him, asking in a low voice: "You think any of them will survive?"

"Don't know." Tieli answered softly. "Maybe none of them will. Maybe... one will."

The shadows of the eight were stretched long by the rising sun, cast upon the ground. Niu Xuqiang raised his head, looking directly at the sun before him. The light stung his eyes, but he did not look away.

He was E-217, a warrior of the Ox Camp, one of the eight oxen.

He was a man who had no choice but to fight.
