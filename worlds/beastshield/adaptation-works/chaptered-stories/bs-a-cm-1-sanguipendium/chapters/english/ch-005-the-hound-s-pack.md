## Scene-1: The Mix

Dusk. The sky was undergoing the most dramatic transformation of the day. The sun had already sunk below the horizon, but its afterglow still burned in the clouds, dyeing the sky in gradients of golden-red, orange-yellow, and purple. The White Capital's industrial pollution made this sunset especially spectacular—the clouds were like ignited cotton, layer upon layer, colors so intense they were nearly artificial.

In the courtyard of the Beastshield Combat Troops Camp · Dog Beastman District, figures had already gathered in twos and threes.

The Dog Camp was the most special of the four camps—because "dog" was not a single race, but a vast category encompassing countless breeds. This diversity stemmed from the wide distribution and artificial selection of canids during evolution—from the snowy plains of the Arctic to the tropical jungles, from the alpine meadows to the city streets, canids had adapted to almost every environment, developing astonishing morphological diversity.

This diversity was fully displayed in the Dog Camp training ground, making it look like some grotesque exhibition, or like some sacred zoo. In the center of the courtyard, a mastiff beastman two meters tall and one hundred forty kilograms was lifting stone locks; the stone locks seemed weightless in his hands, his muscles undulating like waves during exercise, the fat layer forming slight tremors beneath the skin. And not far from him, a greyhound beastman only one hundred seventy-five centimeters tall and ninety-five kilograms was doing stretching exercises; his body lines were smooth and streamlined, every muscle fiber clearly visible beneath the skin, like a statue carved from marble.

This contrast was intense, shocking—the mastiff's stockiness and the greyhound's leanness, like two extremes, or like two sides of the same coin. They represented the two directions of canid evolution: power and speed, endurance and explosiveness, guarding and chasing.

Quan Gaozhong leaned against the courtyard wall, observing all of this.

He was a gray wolf beastman, designation E-157. Joined Beastshield at G-level at seventeen, promoted to E-level after three years, now ten years at E-level. His build was medium-to-large in the Dog Camp—one hundred eighty-eight centimeters tall, one hundred ten kilograms. This weight was just right for a gray wolf—not the heaviest, but muscle density extremely high, every kilogram of weight representing the perfect combination of explosive force and endurance.

His muscle lines were smooth and lean, fat layer extremely thin, abs distinct as rock, every piece clearly visible, like lines carved out with a knife. This build was very common in the Dog Camp—wolf beastmen were usually not as stocky as mastiffs, but they were more agile, more enduring, more adapted to long-distance tracking and protracted battles.

His fur color was standard gray, darker on the back, deep gray near black, lighter on the belly, presenting a silver-white luster, with a clear dividing line between the two, like mountains illuminated by moonlight. Fur texture was coarse and stiff, clinging tightly to the skin; every hair had an obvious medulla—that hollow part in the center of the hair that could store air, providing insulation. This made his pelt look like it had a metallic quality, glinting with faint light in the dusk rays.

His ears stood erect like knives, sharper than most dog beastmen, that was the wolf's unique ear structure. The auricle was triangular, edges having fine hairs, the interior was pink skin with clearly visible blood vessels, like a fine, living map. This ear structure allowed him to capture the most subtle sounds—the rustle of wind through grass blades, the vibration of distant footsteps on the ground, even the faint rhythm of other beastmen's heartbeats. He could rotate his ears, independently adjusting the direction of left and right ears, like a precision biological radar system.

His eyes were amber, glinting with a wild light in the dusk rays. Pupils expanded into circles in dim light; the edge of the iris had a dark outline, making his gaze look deep and mysterious, like two bottomless ancient wells. That gaze was not hostile, not friendly; it was observation, assessment, the vigilance characteristic of a predator.

The E-level combat soldier uniform on Quan Gaozhong's body was black—but on him, this uniform presented a unique quality. Because his build was lean, the uniform appeared more form-fitting, tighter, like a second skin. The white Beastshield logo on the left chest rose and fell slightly with his breathing; the white-gray stripes at the shoulder guards presented a flowing, dynamic visual effect in the dusk light. The uniform clung tightly to his body, tracing the outline of that one-hundred-ten-kilogram frame—broad shoulders, tapered waist, the ravines of abs faintly visible beneath the black fabric.

Quan Gaozhong's os penis (penis bone) had a special structure; this was the typical characteristic of dog/wolf beastmen, and also one of their most obvious physiological differences from other races. This bone was located inside the penis, formed from the ossification of cartilage tissue; when erect it would increase the penis's hardness and support force, allowing it to penetrate the female vagina and maintain insertion. The evolutionary significance of this structure was to ensure mating success, improving reproductive efficiency in the wild environment. But in modern society, it had become more of a physiological marker of canid beastman identity, an undeniable, primal feature.

After erection, that bone would make the penis present an abnormal, almost bendable but not fully foldable hardness, the shape clearly different from a boneless penis, straighter, more directional. After ejaculation, a "tie" phenomenon might occur—the bulbous glands at the base of the penis would swell, locking inside the female, ensuring semen delivery and preventing replacement by other males before mating completion. This "tie" could last fifteen minutes to half an hour, a key link in canid reproductive strategy.

Beneath his uniform, his penis was soft in its normal state, hanging between the legs, but with emotional fluctuations, temperature changes, or fabric friction, it would occasionally become slightly engorged, showing that unique hardness contour with the bone. This physiological reaction was not sexual arousal; it was the body's natural reaction, the normal operation of blood circulation and nervous system.

His specialty was tracking. His sense of smell was among the best in the Dog Camp; he could track a target from a faint scent clue, even if that clue had existed for more than three days. Inside his nasal cavity were one hundred fifty million olfactory receptors, forty times that of humans, twenty times that of cats. He could distinguish one hundred thousand different scents, could separate out every component from mixed smells, could track a target's scent trail left in the city, even if that trail passed through crowds, vehicles, and various other scent interferences.

He closed his eyes, inhaling deeply, letting air fill his nasal cavity. That air contained countless pieces of information—the mastiff's heavy body odor, the greyhound's faint metallic smell, the German shepherd's cleaner scent, the bulldog's saliva smell, and the complex mixture of various pheromones, sweat, urine, and semen.

"Quan Gaozhong, you're interested in this too?"

A voice came from behind. That voice was deep and powerful, like thunder rolling from deep within the chest cavity, carrying the mastiff's unique resonance chamber quality. That voice propagated through the air, resonating with courtyard walls, ground, and buildings, forming a deep, sustained echo.

Quan Gaozhong turned his head, seeing Quan Linyi standing behind him.

Quan Linyi, designation E-089, twenty-eight years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at seventeen, promoted to E-level after three years, now nine years at E-level. He was a pure black mastiff beastman, one of the largest in the Dog Camp—two meters tall, one hundred forty kilograms, almost a moving mountain of flesh.

His muscles were full, skeleton thick and sturdy; although his fat layer was not as thick as bear beastmen, in the Dog Camp it was already considered the "stocky" type. His head was massive, skull width exceeding thirty centimeters, lower jaw strong, bite force astonishing, able to easily crush bones, one of the strongest bite forces in the Dog Camp. His neck had thick skin and muscle, forming natural folds, that was the mastiff breed's characteristic "dewlap," which could protect the neck from fatal injury in battle, like some natural armor.

Quan Linyi's fur was pure black, without any mixed colors, glinting with a deep blue luster in the dusk light—that was the "blue sheen" phenomenon of healthy fur, an effect produced by light refracting on the hair surface. The abdominal fur color was slightly lighter, deep gray-black, forming a subtle contrast with the pure black of the back. His uniform on him appeared tight, especially at the chest and shoulders; the black fabric was stretched full, tracing the thick muscle contours beneath.

"Taking a look." Quan Gaozhong said, his voice low and slightly hoarse, that was the wolf's unique voice—carrying a tremor that could transform into a howl at any moment. "Heard sixty-three people have already registered."

"Competition is fierce." Quan Linyi said, walking to Quan Gaozhong's side, the two leaning shoulder to shoulder against the wall. Quan Linyi's body temperature was about one degree higher than his, heat forming a faint, almost touchable warm field in the air. His uniform glinted with faint luster in the dusk light; the logo on the left chest rose and fell with his breathing. "Eight people can be selected."

"Eight people can be selected." Quan Gaozhong repeated, his gaze sweeping over the other dog beastmen in the courtyard, observing their builds, postures, scents. "In the end, it depends on strength."

"Strength?" Quan Linyi laughed, revealing two huge canines—those canines were thicker than the gray wolf's, presenting an ivory-white quality, length exceeding three centimeters, tips slightly curved, like a primitive, lethal weapon. "In this camp, who doesn't have strength? The question is, who is willing to die for money?"

Quan Gaozhong didn't answer. Of course he had heard about that crimson poster—"Blood-Bounty Elimination Tournament." That poster had already been plastered all over the camp yesterday; the smell of ink still lingered in the air, carrying a chemical pungency and something deeper—the chemical traces of fear, the chemical traces of despair, the chemical traces of hope. He could distinguish every reader's emotional state from those smells—some tense, some excited, some fearful, some resolute.

The door to freedom had been shut tight. Rather than rotting in a cage, better to howl in the arena.

"You're doing it for money?" Quan Linyi asked, his eyes looking directly at Quan Gaozhong, that gaze direct, without any concealment, carrying the mastiff's characteristic frankness.

"No." Quan Gaozhong shook his head; his ears twitched slightly when shaking, capturing changes in surrounding sounds—wind whistling over the wall, impact sounds from the distant training ground, and the breathing of other dog beastmen. "I'm doing it for freedom."

"Freedom?"

"If I win," Quan Gaozhong said, his eyes glinting with a wild light in the dusk rays, that was the light of a caged beast longing to return to the wilderness, "I can demand that Beastshield release me from my contract."

He thought of the northern grasslands, thought of the days of running freely. That was his only way out. He remembered the feeling of wind whistling past his ears, remembered the thrill of prey fleeing beneath his feet, remembered the loneliness and freedom of howling alone under the moonlight. Those were the days before he was captured, a past he could never return to but never forget.

Quan Linyi fell silent. He looked at Quan Gaozhong, looking at that deep longing in those eyes—that longing for freedom, that longing for belonging. Quan Linyi himself also longed for belonging, just in a different way. He once had a home, once had a master, once had a warm fireplace and a soft cushion. But all of that had become the past, completely ended by a single decision of the master.

"Then what if I win?" Quan Linyi asked, his voice becoming lower, as if coming from very far away, as if coming from very deep.

"What do you want?"

"I want a home." Quan Linyi said, his voice becoming even lower, almost a whisper, carrying a deep loneliness, an emptiness after being abandoned. "I am an abandoned mastiff. My former master sold me to Beastshield. Since no one wants me anymore, I might as well find some meaning for my existence on the arena."

There was pain in his voice, anger, but more of a deep, unfillable loneliness. That loneliness was the special emotion of a domestic dog after being abandoned—they once trusted humans, once depended on humans, once were loved by humans, then were personally sent away by humans. This betrayal was more painful than never having had it.

Quan Gaozhong looked at Quan Linyi, looking at that deep loneliness in the dark brown eyes. That loneliness was all too familiar—that feeling of being abandoned, of not belonging anywhere, of feeling lonely even in a crowd. Quan Gaozhong was once a wild wolf, running freely on the northern grasslands until captured by a Beastshield hunting team, forcibly taken from his homeland. Quan Linyi was once a domestic dog, accompanying his master beside a warm fireplace until personally handed over to Beastshield by his master, sold like merchandise. Two different pasts, but the same loneliness, the same torn pain.

"We'll help you." Quan Gaozhong said, his voice very light, but every word exceptionally clear, like a promise, an oath, a bond built in despair.

"Help?" Quan Linyi smiled bitterly, the corner of his mouth pulling to one side, forming a bitter expression, revealing more canines. "This is a tournament. Thirty-one people must die. Only one can live."

"But before that," Quan Gaozhong said, his gaze meeting Quan Linyi's, two pairs of eyes exchanging some silent understanding in the dusk light, some resonance beyond words, "we can be comrades."

Two lonely souls, at this moment, found each other's support. They were not friends, because they had just met; they were not family, because they came from different bloodlines; they were not compatriots, because they had different pasts. But they were of the same kind, similarly caged beasts, similarly trapped beasts longing for something unattainable, similarly warriors seeking a way out in this cruel system.

Quan Gaozhong reached out, palm upward, showing that wolf's unique, slightly slender but powerful hand. Quan Linyi looked at that hand, then extended his own palm—that was a huge, mastiff's unique, thick-padded bear paw. Two palms clasped in the dusk light, conveying a silent support, a wordless understanding, a fragile but real bond built in despair.

They stood by the courtyard wall, shoulder to shoulder, hand in hand, looking at the other dog beastmen in the courtyard, looking at that crimson poster, looking at the approaching, unknown fate.

They were two hounds, two hounds from different bloodlines but equally lonely, two hounds that would rely on each other in the coming storm.

The dusk light gradually dimmed, night fell, stars began to twinkle in the sky. But in the Dog Camp courtyard, by the wall, the silhouettes of the two hounds were still clearly visible, like two lonely statues, like two warriors about to step onto the battlefield, like two wandering souls that had finally found companions.

## Scene-2: Eight Hounds

The registration office was located on the first floor of the Dog Camp administration building.

Unlike the Ox Camp's silence, the Tiger Camp's tension, and the Bear Camp's steadiness, the Dog Camp registration office was noisy, chaotic, and full of vitality. Sixty-three dog beastmen crowded in the corridor, various builds, various fur colors, various scents mixed together, forming a suffocating but also exciting sensory impact. That was the power of diversity, the power of life, the power of seeking order in chaos.

Here was Quan Gaozhong—a one-hundred-eighty-eight-centimeter gray wolf, lean and agile, gray fur glinting with silver light under the lights, like a ghost from the north. Here was Quan Linyi—a two-meter pure black mastiff, stocky and majestic, black pelt glinting with deep blue luster under the lights, like a moving black mountain peak. Here was Quan Heshou—a one-hundred-eighty-five-centimeter German shepherd, back straight as a ruler, ears erect like knives, gaze sharp as an eagle, like an unsheathed military knife. Here was Quan Luoyong—a greyhound only one hundred seventy-five centimeters tall, lean and tall and smooth, body fat percentage extremely low, ribs faintly visible, like a statue of the god of speed carved from marble.

And Quan Liangqiang—a one-hundred-seventy-six-centimeter bulldog, short and stocky, head massive, lower jaw protruding, like a small tank. Quan Songliang—a one-hundred-eighty-centimeter husky, fur color gray-white mixed, eyes the rare ice-blue, like two gems from the Arctic. Quan Zhengwei—a one-hundred-eighty-seven-centimeter Rottweiler, muscles tight, carrying a veteran's vicissitudes, like an old book that had been read too many times. Quan Xiejun—a one-hundred-eighty-two-centimeter Samoyed, fur color snow-white like cotton candy, the corner of his mouth always carrying a "smile," like a warm, glowing cloud.

Eight hounds, eight breeds, eight builds, eight scents, eight predicaments. They stood in the corridor, like a miniature canid exhibition, like a living textbook about diversity, like a story collection about survival.

Quan Gaozhong and Quan Linyi stood in the middle of the line, surrounded by all kinds of dog beastmen, each expressing tension and anticipation in their own way. Some talking loudly, some muttering softly, some pacing back and forth, some standing quietly. The air was permeated with all kinds of smells—sweat, pheromones, chemical traces of fear, chemical traces of hope.

To their left was a German shepherd beastman—Quan Heshou, designation E-269, thirty-one years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at eighteen, promoted to E-level after four years, now ten years at E-level. One hundred eighty-five centimeters tall, one hundred fifteen kilograms, typical German shepherd characteristics: back straight as a ruler, ears erect like knives, gaze sharp as an eagle.

His fur color was black-brown mixed, black on the back, brown on the belly, boundaries distinct, as if carefully painted, like a precise, natural artwork. The black part glinted with a deep luster under the lights; the brown part presented a warm, wood-like texture. This fur color was the German shepherd's mark, the protective color formed during their evolution, allowing them to conceal themselves in grasslands and forests.

Quan Heshou's uniform collar was tightly buttoned; the black fabric was stretched straight by his muscles, the white-gray stripes at the shoulder guards bright and clear. His standing posture was standard military posture—feet shoulder-width apart, back straight, shoulders pulled back, hands hanging naturally at his sides, every line straight as a ruler. This posture showed his training background—German shepherd beastmen in Beastshield were often selected as guards and patrol soldiers; their loyalty and discipline were famous, among Beastshield's most reliable soldiers.

But he discovered that in this world, loyalty only earned orders, not understanding. Ten years at E-level, he had executed countless missions, obeyed countless orders, but never received true respect, never been treated as an independent individual. He was just a tool, a replaceable part, a gear turning within the system.

To their right was a greyhound beastman—Quan Luoyong, designation E-291, twenty-five years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at seventeen, promoted to E-level after two years, now six years at E-level. One hundred seventy-eight centimeters tall, weight only ninety kilograms, one of the smallest in the Dog Camp. But his speed was the fastest in the Dog Camp—a hundred-meter sprint could be completed within ten seconds, an astonishing achievement among beastmen, almost inconceivable to other races.

Quan Luoyong's body lines were the ultimate streamline, without a trace of excess fat, every rib clearly visible beneath the skin, like a precision biological machine, like a statue born for speed. His chest was narrow but deep, providing the maximum expansion space for the lungs, allowing him to inhale more oxygen during high-speed running. His waist was tapered, showing the ultimate streamline figure, like some artwork carved by wind. His legs were long and slender like poles, thigh muscles like steel wire, calf explosive force excellent, every step able to generate astonishing propulsive force.

His uniform on him appeared somewhat loose, because his build was smaller than the standard dog beastman. The black fabric on him presented a different quality—not tight, not a display of power, but flowing, a hint of speed. The logo on the left chest rose and fell rapidly with his quick breathing, showing that heart beating for speed.

But he knew that in this world, there were too many fast people, too few remembered. He was the king of speed in the Dog Camp, but no one remembered his name, no one cared about his story. He was just a fast tool, an existence that could be used and then discarded.

Before them was a bulldog beastman—Quan Liangqiang, designation E-225, twenty-nine years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at eighteen, promoted to E-level after three years, now nine years at E-level. One hundred seventy-six centimeters tall, one hundred thirty kilograms, short and stocky, like a small, unstoppable tank. His head was massive, almost occupying one-third of his entire body height, lower jaw strong, bite force astonishing, teeth exposed, like a primitive, lethal weapon.

Quan Liangqiang's facial skin was loose, with deep folds, that was the bulldog breed's characteristic feature, able to protect them from serious facial injury in battle. These folds presented a layered sense of shadow under the lights, making his expression look even more gloomy, even more angry. His nose was short and flat, producing a low, snoring-like sound when breathing, that was the brachycephalic breed's characteristic breathing method, the price he paid in evolution for battle. His eyes protruded, dark brown in color, gaze stubborn and angry, like a beast driven into a dead end.

His uniform was stretched full by his short and stocky frame, especially at the chest and shoulders; the black fabric was almost about to burst. The logo on the left chest looked somewhat small on his thick pectorals, like a sign submerged in a sea of flesh.

Debt was like a mountain; he could barely breathe. The weight of that debt pressed on his shoulders, making his back slightly bent, making his breathing heavier, making his gaze more desperate.

Behind them was a husky beastman—Quan Songliang, designation E-302, twenty-two years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at seventeen, promoted to E-level after one year, now four years at E-level. One hundred eighty centimeters tall, one hundred kilograms, fur color gray-white mixed, gray on the back, white on the belly, face having typical "mask" markings—black stripes extending from above the eyes to the ears, like a real mask. His eyes were the rare ice-blue, carrying curiosity and playfulness, not as heavy as other contestants, like two gems from the Arctic, glinting with cold light under the lights.

"We're dead either way, might as well die happy." He said, his voice carrying the husky's unique, almost cynical lightness.

And the Rottweiler beastman—Quan Zhengwei, designation E-136, thirty-five years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at eighteen, promoted to E-level after five years, now twelve years at E-level. His fur color was black-brown mixed, black on the back, brown on the belly and limbs, boundaries distinct, like a carefully designed pattern. His muscles were tight, carrying a veteran's vicissitudes, gaze exhausted but firm, like an old book that had been read too many times, every page recording stories of war, death, and survival.

"Twelve years... this is my last chance." He said, his voice carrying a deep, almost desperate exhaustion.

Samoyed beastman—Quan Xiejun, designation E-199, twenty-six years old. Joined Beastshield at G-level at seventeen, promoted to E-level after two years, now seven years at E-level. His fur color was snow-white like cotton candy, whole body pure white without mixed colors, fur texture fluffy and soft, glinting with silver-white luster under light, like a warm, glowing cloud. The corner of his mouth always turned upward, revealing the Samoyed's characteristic "smile," but his eyes hid the stubbornness of not wanting to be mocked, like a misunderstood, gentle but dangerous beast.

"I want them to know, I can bite too." He said, his voice gentle but firm.

Eight hounds, eight breeds, eight predicaments, eight despairs with no retreat. They stood in the corridor, like a story collection about survival, every page writing different suffering, every page writing different hope.

In the registration office corridor, there were also other dog clan soldiers watching. Their eyes held curiosity, sympathy, fear, and also relief—relief that they hadn't reached that step, relief that they still had retreat, relief that they could still choose to live in compromise.

Laohui, a German shepherd beastman, stood at the corner of the corridor. His age was similar to Quan Heshou's, but without Quan Heshou's stubbornness of refusing to admit old age. He looked at Quan Heshou's silhouette, that back straight as a ruler, suddenly feeling a deep sorrow—they were once classmates, promoted together, patrolled together, but now, Quan Heshou chose to fight, while he chose to retreat. That choice had no right or wrong, only different lives, different prices.

"You think any of them will survive?" Laohui asked Fengha beside him, a young husky beastman, only twenty-three years old.

"Who knows." Fengha shrugged, his fur color was the standard gray-white mix, but eyes were dark brown, not Quan Songliang's rare ice-blue. "Maybe that gray wolf can survive, he looks like the best fighter."

"Quan Gaozhong?" Laohui shook his head, "He's been at E-level for ten years, his body's wear and tear is already great."

"But he has experience." Fengha said, his voice carrying a young person's unique worship of the strong.

On the other side of the corridor, several dog clan soldiers who didn't register were discussing in low voices.

"Quan Linyi that big guy, he actually registered too." Tiehao, a thirty-year-old mastiff beastman, his build a circle smaller than Quan Linyi's, but equally stocky. "If he gets serious, who can beat him?"

"But his heart is already dead." Shuangya took over, that was an old wolf beastman, fur already beginning to gray. "He just wants to die with dignity."

"Then what about us?" Tiehao asked, "We just watch like this?"

"What else can we do?" Shuangya sighed, "We don't have their courage, nor their despair. We're just... ordinary people."

The entire Dog Camp had roughly two hundred fifty E-level combat soldiers. Sixty-three registered, eight finally selected. The remaining one hundred eighty-plus were all spectators. They stood in the corridor, in the courtyard, by the windows, watching those eight selected hounds, as if looking into a mirror, the mirror reflecting their own choices—bravely facing death, or cowardly continuing to live in compromise. Choosing dignity, or choosing survival. Becoming warriors, or continuing to be slaves.

Eight hounds stood in the corridor, waiting for fate's arrival. They knew that from this moment on, everything would be different. They would become contestants, become gladiators, become eight survivors (or sacrifices) among the thirty-one who must die. They would fight on the arena, face life-and-death trials, prove their worth—or die.

But they also knew that at this moment, in this corridor, they were no longer alone. They were eight hounds, eight warriors, eight souls that would howl in the storm.

## Scene-3: The Waiting of Eight Hounds

The fall-out ceremony was held at night.

The Dog Camp central square was the messiest of the four camps—the ground had all kinds of paw prints, from small wolf paw prints to huge mastiff paw prints, crisscrossing and overlapping, forming a complex map recording countless runs and battles. The walls were covered with scratches, traces left by various dog beastmen during training, some deep, some shallow, recording different forces and different techniques, recording different breeds and different personalities.

The air was permeated with all kinds of smells: sweat, urine, semen, and various dog beastman-specific pheromones. This mixture of smells was unimaginable in other camps, but in the Dog Camp, this was daily life, a manifestation of vitality, proof of diversity. Every smell represented an identity, a state, an emotion. Quan Gaozhong could read countless pieces of information from these smells—who had just trained, who had just mated, who had just feared, who had just hoped.

Eight selected hounds stood in the center of the square, like a miniature canid exhibition, like a declaration about courage and despair.

Quan Gaozhong stood in the first position. His figure was slender and lean, gray fur glinting with a silver luster under moonlight, like a wolf illuminated by moonlight, like a ghost from the north. His ears stood erect, capturing the most subtle sounds around him—wind whistling over the wall, impact sounds from the distant training ground, and the breathing of the other seven companions. His nose twitched slightly, distinguishing the sources of various scents—the heavy body odor from Quan Linyi, the cleaner smell from Quan Heshou, the faint metallic sweat smell from Quan Luoyong, the saliva smell from Quan Liangqiang, the snowy plain aura from Quan Songliang, the veteran smell from Quan Zhengwei, the soft fragrance from Quan Xiejun.

His uniform presented a deep black under moonlight, forming a sharp contrast with the surrounding silver moonlight. The white logo on the left chest reflected faint light under the moon, like a cold eye watching him. The white-gray stripes at the shoulder guards presented a flowing, almost liquid quality under moonlight. His tail (the dog beastman's tail was an important emotional expression tool) swung gently behind him, showing hidden tension, anticipation and fear of the approaching fate.

Quan Linyi stood in the second position. His build was massive, black fur glinting with an oily luster under moonlight, like a black sculpture, like a silent statue from ancient times. His chest was full, breathing deep and powerful; every breath was accompanied by the huge rise and fall of the chest cavity, the uniform stretched tight on his massive frame, almost allowing the muscle contours beneath to be seen. His eyes glinted with a dark light under moonlight, like two deep wells, bottomless; beneath that darkness hid the pain of being abandoned, the unfillable loneliness.

Quan Heshou stood in the third position. His standing posture was still straight, maintaining a soldier's posture even while waiting, back like a ruler, shoulders pulled back, feet shoulder-width apart. His ears stood erect, slightly smaller than Quan Gaozhong's, but equally sharp, equally able to capture the most subtle sounds. His gaze was sharp, sweeping over the surrounding environment, as if assessing potential threats, as if searching for escape routes, as if preparing to respond to any possible attack. His uniform on him presented a straight, almost rigid beauty, showing his discipline and training background.

Quan Luoyong stood in the fourth position. His figure appeared especially lean and long under moonlight, like a drawn-tight bowstring, like a sculpture born for speed. His ribs were faintly visible beneath the skin, rising and falling with breathing, like a precision biological machine. His eyes were large, dark-colored under moonlight, carrying an anxious alertness, a fear of the unknown, a worry about failure. His uniform on him appeared somewhat loose, because of his ultimate lean build; the black fabric presented a flowing, almost transparent quality under moonlight.

Quan Liangqiang stood in the fifth position. His head appeared especially massive under moonlight, almost occupying one-third of his entire body height; the shadow of his protruding lower jaw was cast upon the ground, forming a strange, almost comical shape. His breathing was heavy, carrying the bulldog's characteristic snoring-like sound, that was the brachycephalic breed's characteristic breathing method, the price he paid in evolution for battle. His uniform was stretched full by his short and stocky frame, the abdominal fat layer forming rounded contours beneath the uniform.

Quan Songliang stood in the sixth position. His ice-blue eyes were exceptionally eye-catching under moonlight, like two gems, like two lakes from the Arctic, glinting with cold light in the darkness. The corner of his mouth still carried that "smile," that husky's characteristic, almost eternal smile, but under the moonlight's reflection, that smile seemed somewhat eerie, somewhat mysterious, somewhat unpredictable. His fur color presented a gray-white, almost luminous quality under moonlight, like a cloud illuminated by moonlight.

Quan Zhengwei stood in the seventh position. His gaze was exhausted but firm, like a veteran who had experienced too much wind and rain, like an old book that had been read too many times. Although his muscles were not as tight as in his youth, he still maintained power, still able to explode at critical moments. His uniform on him presented a vicissitudinous beauty, collar and cuffs somewhat worn, showing his twelve years at E-level.

Quan Xiejun stood in the eighth position. His snow-white fur glinted with silver light under moonlight, like a glowing cloud, like a ball of cotton candy illuminated by moonlight. His ears stood erect, ear tips twitching slightly, showing inner tension, fear of the approaching fate, worry about the unknown. But the corner of his mouth still carried that smile, that Samoyed's characteristic, almost stubborn smile, as if telling the world: even if I'm afraid, I won't show it.

"Eight confirmed." The overseer announced, his voice amplified through the loudspeaker, echoing in the night sky, intertwining with moonlight and starlight. "Tomorrow at noon, the drawing ceremony."

The eight people stood in place, not moving, like eight sculptures, like eight mountain peaks, like eight souls waiting for fate's judgment.

Quan Gaozhong looked at the other seven, using his peripheral vision, using his sense of smell, using his hearing, using that wolf's unique, keen perception. He could feel the weight of loneliness emanating from Quan Linyi, could feel the disciplinary tension from Quan Heshou, could feel the speed-type anxiety characteristic of Quan Luoyong, could feel the oppression of debt from Quan Liangqiang, could feel the cynical lightness from Quan Songliang, could feel the veteran's exhaustion from Quan Zhengwei, could feel the pain of being misunderstood from Quan Xiejun.

Their predicaments were each different—Quan Gaozhong wanted freedom, wanted to return to the northern grasslands; Quan Linyi wanted a home, wanted to find belonging after being abandoned; Quan Heshou wanted loyalty, wanted to prove that ten years of obedience was not in vain; Quan Luoyong wanted to prove that speed was also a value; Quan Liangqiang was in debt, needed money to escape the shackles of debt; Quan Songliang was playful, wanted to find fun before death; Quan Zhengwei wanted promotion, twelve years of waiting needed results; Quan Xiejun wanted to prove that beneath soft fur there were also sharp teeth.

Eight different stories, eight different pains, eight different hopes. But they all had one thing in common: it wasn't that they had no fear, but that they had no retreat. They were all driven to the edge of the cliff, with a bottomless abyss behind them, and unknown battle before them. They had no choice, could only go forward, could only fight, could only wait for fate's arrival on this square, in this night.

Quan Gaozhong took a deep breath, letting the cold night air fill his nasal cavity and lungs. That air was cool, fresh, carrying the night's characteristic tranquility, but also carrying the Dog Camp's unique scent—that mixed, complex, various kind of taste. This scent once represented belonging and companions, the taste of home, the taste of the group, the taste of safety. But now, it represented battle and death, the bloodiness of the arena, the cruelty of the victor living and the loser dying.

He raised his head, looking at the sky.

The moon hung high in the sky, full, emitting silver-white light, like a huge, indifferent eye watching them. Stars twinkled on the black canopy, like countless distant eyes, like countless already dead souls, like countless future possibilities. That starry sky was deep, mysterious, awe-inspiring, making one think of the vastness of the universe, the smallness of life, the unpredictability of fate.

Quan Gaozhong's ears turned toward the moon's direction, as if listening to something, as if in dialogue with that distant, cold light. But what he heard was only the sound of the night wind—wind whistling over the wall, wailing through corridors, and the breathing of the other seven companions—those deep, rapid, calm, or tense breathing sounds, intertwining into a deep, distant-thunder-like echo.

Then, he withdrew his gaze and looked again at the reality before him.

Eight hounds. Eight warriors. Eight lives about to fight to the death on the arena. Eight once-free but captured souls, eight trapped beasts longing for something unattainable, eight warriors seeking a way out in this cruel system.

Eight people with no retreat.

## Scene-4: Grooming and Cleansing

After the fall-out ceremony, the eight hounds came to the Dog Camp cleansing room.

That was a long, narrow room, located on the east side of the barracks, walls embedded with dozens of faucets arranged in two neat rows, like some industrial-era decoration. The ground was slanted cement, tilting toward the central drainage ditch for easy drainage. One side of the room was a huge mirror, almost occupying the entire wall, allowing dog beastmen to clearly see their whole body, every part, every detail.

The air was permeated with the mixed smell of shampoo, conditioner, and various cleaners—that artificial, chemical, but also somewhat comfortable scent. For dog beastmen, this smell represented cleanliness, represented grooming, represented preparation. Before the approaching tournament, they needed to groom themselves to optimal condition, needed to make their fur shine, needed to make their bodies clean, needed to make their spirits focused.

Dog beastmen placed more importance on fur care than other races—their fur was not just protection, but also a symbol of identity, a mark of race, an expression of personality. Different dog breeds had different fur textures, colors, and patterns, requiring different care methods. Mastiff's thick and coarse, greyhound's short and smooth, German shepherd's medium length, husky's thick and fluffy, each needed specific tools, specific techniques, specific patience.

Quan Gaozhong stood before the mirror, naked upper body exposed to the air.

The temperature in the cleansing room was slightly higher than outside, comfortable, suitable for naked bodies. The air was permeated with the moisture of water vapor, making his fur softer, easier to groom. His fur was the gray wolf's typical color—back was deep gray near black, like a forest shrouded by night; belly was silver-white, like a lake illuminated by moonlight; between the two was a clear dividing line, like carefully painted, like nature's most exquisite artwork.

Fur texture was coarse and stiff; every hair had an obvious medulla—that hollow part in the center of the hair that could store air, provide insulation, keeping his fur warm even in the cold north. Under the lights, those hairs glinted with a metallic quality, like tiny, gray metal flakes.

He picked up a special comb—that was a comb made of boar bristles, tooth spacing moderate, able to effectively groom tangled fur without damaging the hair shaft. This comb was the wolf beastmen's favorite, because its hardness could penetrate coarse fur, reach the skin, providing a massaging effect.

He began grooming the fur on his back.

That motion was slow, rhythmic, starting from the neck, along the spine downward, all the way to the tail root. Every stroke of the comb carried away some shed fur and dust, making his fur smoother, more glossy. He could feel the touch of the comb contacting his skin—that slight, somewhat frictioned feeling, like some massage, like some ritual, like some way of dialoguing with his own body.

The comb's teeth pressed against the skin, stimulating the nerve endings beneath, bringing a comfortable, relaxing feeling. That feeling spread from his back to his whole body, relaxing his muscles, soothing his spirit, letting him temporarily forget the approaching tournament, forget that one-in-thirty-two survival chance.

"Need help?"

A voice came from behind, breaking Quan Gaozhong's meditation. That voice was gentle, mild, different from most sounds in the Dog Camp.

Quan Gaozhong turned his head, seeing Quan Xiejun standing behind him.

The Samoyed beastman's naked upper body showed his snow-white cotton-candy-like fur—whole body pure white without mixed colors, fur texture fluffy and soft, glinting with silver-white luster under the lights, like a cloud illuminated by moonlight, like a soft, warm snow. But this beautiful fur also required more care, easily tangled, easily dirty, needing a large amount of time spent daily on maintenance.

"Your back." Quan Xiejun said, his voice even gentler than Quan Gaozhong's, like some warm, flowing liquid. "There are some places in the back that can't be reached."

Quan Gaozhong nodded, handing the comb to Quan Xiejun.

Quan Xiejun took the comb and began grooming the fur on Quan Gaozhong's back. His movements were gentle, careful, as if handling some precious fabric, as if caressing some fragile artwork. His fingers shuttled through Quan Gaozhong's fur, feeling that coarse texture, that wild texture completely different from his own soft fur.

"Your fur quality is very good." Quan Xiejun said, his voice carrying sincere praise. "Wolf clan fur is always so... powerful. Like metal, like rock, like some untamable wildness."

"Yours is also beautiful." Quan Gaozhong said, feeling the temperature of Quan Xiejun's fingers moving on his back. That temperature was slightly higher than his own, heat transmitting through the skin, bringing a comfortable feeling, a feeling of being cared for, a tenderness rarely seen in this cruel world. "Like snow, like cloud, like something pure, something beautiful."

"Beautiful?" Quan Xiejun smiled bitterly, the corner of his mouth still carrying that "smile," that Samoyed's characteristic, almost eternal smile, but there was a trace of bitterness in his eyes, a pain of being misunderstood. "The beauty mocked as a 'pet.' They say I don't look like a warrior, say I look like a plaything kept by humans, say I'm only suitable for pulling sleds in the snow, not suitable for fighting on the arena."

"That's not your fault." Quan Gaozhong said, his voice low, but firm, like a mountain's promise. "It's their ignorance. They can't see your toughness, can't see your determination, can't see the true strength deep in your heart. Beauty is not weakness, softness is not incompetence, a smile is not surrender."

On the other side of the cleansing room, Quan Linyi was using large amounts of clear water to rinse his body.

His fur was pure black, thick and coarse, needing large amounts of water to thoroughly clean. He stood under the faucet, naked body showing the mastiff's characteristic stocky build—muscles full, skeleton thick and sturdy, fat layer although not as thick as bear beastmen, but already considered "stocky" in the Dog Camp. His skin in the naked state presented a deep brown, healthy luster, forming a subtle contrast with his black fur.

His neck had thick skin and muscle, forming natural folds, that was the mastiff breed's characteristic "dewlap," which could protect them from fatal neck injury in battle. Now these dewlaps became softer under the water flow's effect, like layers of wrinkled cloth, like a natural, flowing decoration.

Water flow impacted his back, splashing tiny water droplets, emitting crisp sounds. That water flow's force was strong, like an invisible hand massaging his muscles, washing away a day's fatigue, a day's tension, a day's fear. Quan Linyi closed his eyes, enjoying that clean feeling—that feeling of washing away dirt, sweat, and a day's fatigue, that feeling of making the body become pure again, become ready again.

Quan Luoyong was also cleansing, but his method was completely different.

The greyhound beastman's naked body showed ultimate leanness—fat layer extremely minimal, every rib clearly visible beneath the skin, like a precision biological machine, like a sculpture born for speed. His fur was short and smooth, almost needing no grooming, only needing clear water rinsing, like some carefully designed, low-maintenance, high-efficiency body.

Quan Luoyong stood under the faucet, letting water flow wash over his body. That water flow carried away the sweat smell on his body—that faint, metallic sweat smell, the smell characteristic of speed-type contestants, the metabolic product produced after muscles operated at their limit. His skin in the naked state presented a smooth, almost luminous quality, muscle lines clearly visible, like a statue carved from marble, every muscle optimized for speed, every line designed for efficiency.

"Your fur is really short." Quan Songliang came over, his ice-blue eyes curiously examining Quan Luoyong's body. The husky's fur was thick, needing large amounts of care; he felt curious about the greyhound's short fur, felt envious, felt a different lifestyle that was unattainable.

"Convenient." Quan Luoyong said, his voice concise, carrying the speed-type contestant's characteristic crispness, an efficiency that didn't waste any energy. "Don't need to spend an hour grooming every day. The time saved can be used for training, can be used for resting, can be used for doing anything more meaningful."

"But won't it be cold in winter?" Quan Songliang asked, his eyes carrying genuine concern, warmth from a companion.

"Used to it." Quan Luoyong said, a trace of complex emotion flashing in his eyes—that was memory, pain, nights once trembling from cold, prices once paid for not having enough fat layer. "Besides, cold can make the body more alert, faster, more ready. In extreme states, cold is an advantage, not a disadvantage."

Quan Liangqiang stood in the corner, using a rough towel to wipe his body.

The bulldog beastman's naked body showed a short and stocky build—although height was only one hundred seventy-six centimeters, weight reached one hundred thirty kilograms, every inch of body full of power, every muscle forged for strength. His head was massive, almost occupying one-third of his entire body height, lower jaw strong, bite force astonishing, like a primitive, lethal weapon.

His facial skin was loose, with deep folds, that was the bulldog breed's characteristic feature, able to protect them from serious facial injury in battle. These folds were even more obvious in the naked state, like ravines carved on his face, recording the countless battles he had experienced, countless times being hit, countless times surviving. He used the towel to carefully wipe every fold, ensuring no dirt remained, ensuring his body was in optimal condition.

"Quan Liangqiang." Quan Heshou walked over, his German shepherd build in the naked state showing standard military posture—back straight, muscles symmetrical, every muscle precisely trained, every line conforming to military standards. "You okay?"

"Mm." Quan Liangqiang responded, his voice somewhat muffled because of the protruding lower jaw, but firm, powerful, unyielding.

"The debt matter..." Quan Heshou hesitated, his eyes carrying concern, understanding from a veteran. "If you need help..."

"No need." Quan Liangqiang interrupted him, his eyes looking directly at Quan Heshou, gaze stubborn, unyielding, carrying a dignity that could not be knocked down. "My own matter, solve it myself. This is my own choice, my own responsibility, my own battle."

Quan Zhengwei stood before the mirror, naked body showing the traces of years—muscles although tight, but already beginning to loosen, skin having some old scars, like a map folded too many times. He had worked twelve years at E-level, accumulated too many stories, too many scars, too many memories.

He looked at himself in the mirror, those exhausted eyes looking back at him, as if asking a question, as if waiting for an answer.

"Last chance." He said to himself in a low voice, his voice carrying a deep, almost desperate exhaustion, but also an unyielding, unwilling-to-give-up hope.

Eight hounds in the cleansing room, nakedly cleansing, grooming, caring for their bodies. Everyone had their own story, everyone had their own predicament, everyone had their own body, their own fur, their own way to prepare for the approaching battle.

But at this moment, in this naked honesty, in this common preparation process, they were no longer eight lonely warriors, no longer eight opponents about to fight each other, no longer eight individuals abandoned by fate.

They were eight hounds. Eight hounds that would run, bite, survive or die on the arena. Eight trapped beasts seeking a way out in this cruel system. Eight companions who established some connection in naked honesty.

The water flow in the cleansing room continued to flow, the mirror continued to reflect their silhouettes, the lights continued to shine upon their naked bodies. And tomorrow, at the drawing ceremony, their fates would be decided. They would know what kind of opponent they would face, what kind of battle they would experience, what kind of ending they would welcome.

But at this moment, in this piece of water sounds and steam, they were just eight hounds, just eight naked bodies, just eight lives preparing to welcome fate.

## Scene-5: Night Howl

Late night. The back mountain of the Dog Camp.

Eight hounds stood here naked, looking up at the full moon in the sky.

That was the dog beastman's tradition—at important moments, sending out their own voice toward the moon. This was not mandatory, nor ritualistic, but an instinct, a call from ancient times.

Quan Gaozhong stood at the very front, naked body presenting a silver luster under moonlight. His head tilted back, neck muscles tensed, mouth open.

A long howl issued from his throat.

That sound was piercing, long, carrying a wildness from the wilderness. The sound echoed in the night sky, passing through valleys, traveling toward the distance. That was the wolf's howl, a call to freedom, a memory of the past, an uncertainty about the future.

Quan Linyi followed with a howl.

His voice was different from Quan Gaozhong's—lower, deeper, like thunder rolling from deep within the chest cavity. That was the mastiff's howl, carrying a meaning of guarding, a longing for home, a pain of being abandoned.

Quan Heshou's voice was short, rhythmic, like some military bugle. That was the German shepherd's howl, carrying discipline, carrying loyalty, carrying obedience to and questioning of orders.

Quan Luoyong's voice was sharp, fast, like a gust of wind passing through. That was the greyhound's howl, carrying a sense of speed, carrying agility, carrying resistance to restraint.

Quan Liangqiang's voice was dull, powerful, like a low growl. That was the bulldog's howl, carrying stubbornness, carrying anger, carrying unyielding to fate.

Quan Songliang's voice was undulating, varied, like a song. That was the husky's howl, carrying curiosity, carrying playfulness, carrying optimism about life.

Quan Zhengwei's voice was vicissitudinous, exhausted, like a sigh. That was the Rottweiler's howl, carrying experience, carrying seeing through, carrying final persistence.

Quan Xiejun's voice was gentle, pure, like a prayer. That was the Samoyed's howl, carrying beauty, carrying the sadness of being misunderstood, carrying the desire to prove oneself.

Eight howls intertwined in the night sky, forming a strange symphony.

That was not harmonious, not neat, but it was real. Every voice represented a life, a soul, a story.

After the howling ended, the eight hounds stood silently for a while, naked bodies like eight sculptures under moonlight.

"We will remember this night." Quan Gaozhong said, his voice low, echoing in the night wind.

"Will remember." Quan Linyi responded.

"No matter who survives." Quan Heshou added.

"Will all remember." The other five spoke in unison.

They stood naked under the moonlight, eight hounds, eight voices, eight despairs with no retreat.

But before that, on this moonlit night, they were free. Free to howl, free to be naked, free to exist.

## Scene-6: The Last Supper

After the howling, the eight hounds gathered in the Dog Camp mess hall.

The mess hall was already closed, but they had keys—or rather, they had ways to get in. This was their last night in the Dog Camp; tomorrow was the drawing ceremony, then the tournament.

They sat around a long table, various foods laid out on the table—roast meat, bones, and some internal organs that dog beastmen especially liked. No lights, only moonlight shining in through the windows, casting silver spots on the tabletop.

Quan Gaozhong sat at the head of the table, naked upper body glinting with silver luster under moonlight. Before him was placed a piece of raw beef, that was his favorite food—raw, fresh, carrying the smell of blood.

He tore at the beef with his teeth, feeling the muscle fibers breaking between his teeth. That bloody, metallic taste spread in his oral cavity, bringing a primal satisfaction.

"If one of us meets another on the arena..." Quan Luoyong suddenly spoke, his voice somewhat sharp under moonlight.

"Then go all out." Quan Linyi said, his voice low.

"No mercy?" Quan Xiejun asked, his voice gentle.

"Mercy is an insult to each other." Quan Heshou said, his voice carrying a soldier's crispness. "We are opponents on the arena, but here, we are brothers."

"Brothers..." Quan Liangqiang repeated this word, his protruding lower jaw making this pronunciation somewhat muffled. "I never had brothers."

"Now you have seven." Quan Songliang said, his ice-blue eyes glinting under moonlight.

Quan Zhengwei raised his cup, containing clear water—the Dog Camp didn't allow alcohol, that would affect the sense of smell.

"To eight hounds." He said.

"To eight hounds." The eight spoke in unison, raising cups, producing crisp sounds when colliding.

The sweetness of mead melted on the tip of the tongue, carrying a trace of post-fermentation sourness. Eight hounds sat around the table, no one rushing to speak. Moonlight slanted in through the window, casting silver-white spots on the tabletop.

"I'll start." Quan Gaozhong put down his cup, his fingers gently caressing the cup rim. "The year I was captured, the northern grasslands had just finished the first snowfall..."

His voice was low, narrating that night of being surrounded—the barking of hunting dogs, the sting of the tranquilizer dart piercing muscle, and the memory of seeing the starry sky for the last time before losing consciousness.

After Quan Linyi finished listening, he silently drank a mouthful of wine, then spoke: "My former master... was an old man. He raised me for fifteen years, I thought he would raise me until I died of old age." His voice became hoarse, "But before he died, he sold me to Beastshield. Exchanged for a thousand yuan of medicine fees."

"He didn't look at you?" Quan Songliang asked, his ice-blue eyes glinting under moonlight.

"He turned his head." Quan Linyi said, "But I know, he had no choice. Just like I have no choice now."

Quan Heshou had been keeping his head down; hearing this, he raised his head: "I once thought loyalty would be rewarded. Every mission, I charged at the front. Every corruption, I pretended not to see." He smiled bitterly, "Twelve years, I'm still at E-level. Sometimes I think, those things I chose to be silent about, whether they also pushed me to this table today."

"Don't say these anymore." Quan Luoyong suddenly said, his voice sharp and rapid. "Do you know what it feels like to be called 'skinny dog'? From childhood to adulthood, everyone said I'm thin, said I'm weak, said I can't even move a crate." His fists clenched tight, "So I run. I run desperately. I want everyone to be unable to catch up with me."

Quan Liangqiang had been silently gnawing on a bone; hearing this, he put down the bone: "I owe money. Gambling debt. Twenty thousand." He said very briefly, voice somewhat muffled because of the protruding lower jaw, "The creditor said, either pay back the money, or leave a hand. I think, if I'm going to lose something, better lose it on the arena. At least... can still choose my own opponent."

Quan Songliang smiled, but that smile didn't reach his eyes: "You all think I'm foolishly happy all day, right? 'Silly fool,' 'brainless'... I've heard enough." He lowered his head, "I just think, if even laughter is given up, then there's really nothing left."

Quan Zhengwei was the oldest; he waited until the young people all finished speaking before slowly opening his mouth: "I've been at E-level for twelve years. Seen too many people come and go, some died, some promoted, some... disappeared." His eyes were exhausted, "This tournament, is my last chance. Not for promotion, but to prove... these twelve years, were not lived in vain."

Finally was Quan Xiejun. He had been silently listening, until everyone looked at him.

"I know you all think I look... like a pet." His voice was gentle, "From childhood to adulthood, everyone who saw me wanted to pat my head, say 'so cute.' But I'm a warrior. I have teeth, have claws, have the same blood as them." His eyes became firm, "I want them to remember, this ball of 'cotton candy' can also bite."

Eight stories, slowly flowing under moonlight. No ornate rhetoric, only simple, blood-tainted truth.

In the mess hall under moonlight, eight hounds sat together naked, sharing the last supper.

"After tomorrow," Quan Gaozhong said, his voice low, "everything will be different."

"After tomorrow," Quan Linyi responded, "some of us may no longer be here."

"But tonight," Quan Heshou said, "we are still together."

"Still together." The eight spoke in unison.

They continued eating, drinking, spending this last night under moonlight.

No one mentioned tomorrow's drawing anymore. No one mentioned the fact that thirty-one people would die. They just ate, drank, occasionally saying a few irrelevant casual words, like a group of ordinary soldiers enjoying an ordinary night.

But beneath this ordinary, a silent tacit understanding was flowing. They knew this might be the last time. So every mouthful of wine, every piece of meat, every word, was deeply remembered in their hearts.

On this moonlit night, the eight hounds were alive. Truly alive, nakedly alive, freely alive.

Tomorrow, they would become each other's enemies. But tonight, they were brothers.
