**Scene-1 The Weight of Carrying**

Moonlight lay like a thin frost, covering the desolate homeward path.

Chen Mo carried Bai Ya on his back, step by step through gravel and weeds. Bai Ya's body was very light, light unlike an adult beastman—Chen Mo could feel his slender bones, could feel his soft fur gently brushing in the night wind, could feel his faint breath passing through clothing to transmit warmth to his own back.

But Chen Mo's footsteps were exceptionally heavy.

Not because of exhaustion, although his muscles were indeed protesting—the soreness of his thighs, the stiffness of his back, the burning from repeated friction of his footpads against the rough ground. But rather... fear.

Bai Ya was unconscious.

That white rabbit lay softly on his back, head resting on his shoulder, long ears hanging powerlessly, occasionally swaying gently with Chen Mo's steps. His breathing was very shallow, so shallow it was almost undetectable, each inhalation carrying an alarming tremor.

Chen Mo could smell the scent of blood.

It was a rich, rust-like sweetness, continuously seeping from Bai Ya's shoulder, soaking his white fur, also soaking the clothing at Chen Mo's shoulder. Warm liquid passed through fabric, mixing with Chen Mo's sweat, forming a sticky, slippery touch.

"Bai Ya," Chen Mo called softly, his voice hoarse beyond recognition. His throat was dry from tension, each swallow carrying a sting. "Can you hear me?"

No response.

Only faint breath brushed Chen Mo's ear, carrying a hint of bloodiness, and some... scent unique to Bai Ya. That was a mixture of herbal fragrance and faint leather smell, now covered by the scent of blood, but Chen Mo could still distinguish it.

Chen Mo gritted his teeth, quickening his pace.

He couldn't stop, couldn't rest, couldn't spend the night in the wilderness. Those Beastshield people might still be nearby, could appear again at any moment. He needed to find a safe place, needed to find medicine, needed—

He needed Bai Ya to live.

He remembered the moment Bai Ya pushed him aside.

That small rabbit, that body much more slender than his own, used unbelievable speed to rush before him, using his own flesh and blood to block that knife stabbing toward him. Chen Mo could clearly recall that scene—Bai Ya's shoulder blooming under the blade, white fur dyed bright red, a flash of... relief? in those red eyes.

Why?

They had only been teacher and student for two days. Why would Bai Ya risk his life for him?

Chen Mo couldn't understand. He only felt that the life of the person on his back was now more important than his own.

"Hold on," he said softly, as if speaking to Bai Ya, as if speaking to himself. His voice was especially clear in the silent night sky, carrying a determination he himself didn't recognize. "Almost there."

In the distance, city lights flickered in and out of view. Like isolated islands in the darkness, both dangerous and full of hope.

Chen Mo adjusted Bai Ya's position, letting him lie more securely on his back. He could feel Bai Ya's heartbeat—weak but still continuing, passing through that layer of soft fur and muscle, transmitting to Chen Mo's back.

That heartbeat was like a promise, and also like a burden.

He continued walking forward.

**Scene-2 Another Fear**

Meanwhile, in the East District dormitory of Beastshield Company, G-734 lay on his bed, staring at the ceiling.

He couldn't sleep.

Not because he wasn't tired—he had patrolled for twelve hours today, his legs aching like they were filled with lead. But rather... fear.

That fear had been缠绕着他 since he heard that news this afternoon, like an invisible hand, tightly gripping his heart.

G-877 was sold.

That young dog beastman, who was still on night shift with him yesterday, still complaining the cafeteria's braised pork was too salty. Today, he was taken away by an overseer for "dereliction of duty," and then... there was no then.

Some said he was executed, thrown into the green belt. Others said he was sold to Captive Delivery—a young, healthy dog beastman could sell for 150 Lu coins on the dark web.

G-734 would rather believe the former.

He rolled over, his bed emitting an irritating creak. There were other beastmen in the dormitory, some snoring, some whispering, some like him, eyes open staring at the darkness.

He could see his own hand—that wild boar palm covered with sparse gray-brown fur. Knuckles thick, palm with thick calluses, traces left from long-term baton grip. This hand had once helped him drive away street thugs, had once helped him find a foothold in this city.

But now, this hand couldn't even control its own fate.

"Still not asleep?"

A voice came from nearby. G-734 turned his head and saw G-721—that young dog beastman, poking his head out from his bed looking at him. His ears drooped, eyes carrying worry.

"Mm," G-734 mumbled. He didn't want to chat, didn't want to spread his fear to this young person who still harbored fantasies.

"Thinking about 877?"

G-734 didn't answer.

G-721 was silent for a while, then lowered his voice: "I heard... heard he was bought by someone placing an order."

G-734's heart suddenly contracted.

"What?"

"On the dark web," G-721's voice was even lighter, as if afraid of being heard. "Someone placed an order at Captive Delivery, specifically wanting a dog beastman, young, preferably a Beastshield employee. 877 happened to fit the requirements, so..."

He didn't finish.

G-734 felt a wave of nausea.

That wasn't execution, not an accident. That was... being chosen. Like livestock, being selected, purchased, and then...

He didn't know what would happen then. He had heard those rumors—some civilians would buy Beastshield employees back for "revenge," because Beastshield had once bullied them. Some were for... darker purposes.

"Stop talking," G-734 interrupted him, his voice somewhat trembling. "Sleep, we have to get up early tomorrow."

G-721 looked at him, wanting to speak but stopping, ultimately lying back down.

G-734 resumed staring at the ceiling.

He remembered why he chose to join Beastshield. Back then he had just arrived in this city, penniless, without skills, without friends. A wild boar beastman in a green uniform told him, join Beastshield, room and board included, with wages, although dangerous but at least you could survive.

He agreed.

For eight months, he watched companions die one by one—killed by bounty hunters, internally executed, or... sold. He began to understand, this green uniform wasn't protection, but a mark. Marking him as a consumable that could be killed, sold, abandoned.

But he couldn't leave.

The contract still had four months to expire; leaving early meant paying huge penalty fees. And even if he left, what could he do? The civilian world wasn't safe either; without Beastshield's shelter, he might die faster.

He was trapped.

Trapped in this green cage, waiting for the next chosen fate.

G-734 closed his eyes, forcing himself to sleep.

At the last moment before falling asleep, he silently prayed in his heart—hoping that tomorrow the chosen one wouldn't be him.

**Scene-3 The Temperature of Stopping Blood**

Chen Mo practically crashed open the door of Bai Ya's hut.

He used his shoulder to push open the door, carrying Bai Ya rushing in, carefully placing that white rabbit on the straw mat. His movements were light, but Bai Ya still let out a faint groan.

"Sorry, sorry..." Chen Mo murmured, flustered checking the injury.

The knife wound was deep, from the left shoulder all the way to the back, at least fifteen centimeters long. Flesh turned outward, white fur dyed dark red, blood still slowly seeping out. Chen Mo could see the muscle fibers deep in the wound—pink, cut by the blade, still slightly twitching tissue.

He felt a wave of dizziness.

"Damn..." he cursed, "What should I do..."

He didn't understand medicine, didn't understand this world's herbs, didn't understand how to handle such a severe wound. He only knew—if he didn't stop the bleeding quickly, Bai Ya would die.

Die.

That word was like a hammer, heavily striking his heart.

Bai Ya wasn't an ordinary "NPC." He was Chen Mo's mentor, was the first person to extend a helping hand to Chen Mo, was—

A friend.

"Right, Honesty Fruit..." Chen Mo suddenly remembered the plants in Bai Ya's vegetable garden, "And Blood Leaf... he said it could stop bleeding..."

He rushed into the vegetable garden, frantically searching in the moonlight. The moonlight was bright, illuminating those strangely shaped plants. Chen Mo's fingers were scratched by thorns, he didn't care—his bear paws were cut with several small wounds by Blood Leaf's serrated edges, warm blood dripping from his fingertips, but he couldn't feel the pain.

He found that red plant—Blood Leaf.

The leaves presented a deep red, like congealed blood, edges with fine serrations. Chen Mo picked a large handful, rushed into the kitchen, following vague memories to start a fire, boil water.

Starting a fire was more difficult than he had imagined.

This body's fingers were too thick, unable to flexibly operate those small flints. He spent five minutes to ignite the kindling, another five minutes to stabilize the fire. When the water boiled, Chen Mo's bear paws were scalded with several blisters—he accidentally touched the edge of the iron pot, that burning pain making him suck in a cold breath.

But he couldn't feel the pain. Or rather, his brain refused to process this pain, because there were more important things to attend to.

He put Blood Leaf into the boiling water, watching the red juice slowly seep out, dyeing the water a turbid dark red. The air was filled with a bitter herbal smell, carrying some rust-like sweetness.

Then, he used a clean cloth soaked in the medicinal juice, returning to Bai Ya's side.

"It might hurt a bit..." he murmured, then carefully pressed the cloth onto Bai Ya's wound.

Bai Ya's body twitched, letting out a faint groan. His brows furrowed tightly, long ears standing up, then powerlessly drooping. But he didn't wake.

Chen Mo changed the medicinal cloth over and over, over and over applying new medicinal juice to the wound.

He could feel Bai Ya's skin temperature—slightly lower than normal, carrying an worrying chill. He could feel that layer of soft white fur becoming wet and heavy under the medicinal juice soak. He could feel his own fingers trembling, uncontrollably trembling.

The blood slowly stopped.

But Bai Ya's complexion was still pale as paper. In the dim oil lamp light, his white fur lost its sheen, presenting a grayish hue. Those red eyes were tightly closed, eyelids slightly trembling, as if dreaming.

Chen Mo held Bai Ya's limp paw.

That paw was very small, a full circle smaller than Chen Mo's bear paw, covered with fine white down. The pad in the palm was soft and warm, but now the temperature was gradually dropping. Chen Mo could feel that faint pulse—weak but still beating, like a trapped butterfly, struggling faintly.

"Please..." Chen Mo's voice trembled, "Don't die..."

He didn't know what he was saying, didn't know who he was pleading with. He only knew, he couldn't lose this person.

This person he had only known for two days, yet had blocked a fatal knife for him.

**Scene-4 The Touch of Waking**

When dawn was about to break, Bai Ya's breathing finally steadied somewhat.

Chen Mo had been guarding beside him, eyes red, body stiff. He didn't dare sleep, didn't dare leave, afraid that with one careless moment, Bai Ya would stop breathing.

Morning light filtered through the window, shining on Bai Ya's face.

Chen Mo had his first opportunity to observe him so closely.

Bai Ya's features were very delicate; even in rabbit beastman form, one could see a... human refinement. His nose was pink, twitching slightly, sniffing the scents in the air. His ears were very long, softly drooping on both sides of his head, occasionally trembling slightly with his breathing. One ear had a notch at the tip—a small detail Chen Mo hadn't noticed last night, the edge already healed, presenting a light pink scar.

The most striking were his eyes—even closed, Chen Mo could remember those red eyes.

That wasn't a common color for beastmen. Beastmen's eyes were usually brown, yellow, green, or turbid gray. But red... what did red mean?

Chen Mo reached out, gently brushing aside the fur on Bai Ya's forehead.

That layer of white down was very soft, like the finest silk, sliding under his fingertips. Beneath the fur, Bai Ya's skin presented a faint pink, carrying a... transparent quality, as if one could see the tiny blood vessels below.

There was a fine scar there, hidden beneath the white down, impossible to discover without careful looking.

What was this?

Chen Mo hesitated, then checked other parts of Bai Ya. His fingers lightly slid over Bai Ya's neck, feeling the pulse beating beneath that layer of soft fur. Then, he discovered a strange mark on the back of Bai Ya's neck—

That was a trace on the skin, like a brand, and also like a tattoo. It was a complex symbol, composed of several curves and dots, presenting a pink slightly deeper than the surrounding skin. Chen Mo couldn't understand that symbol, but he could feel that it contained some... power.

"That is the 'Consciousness Mark.'"

A voice suddenly rang out.

Chen Mo jumped, nearly leaping up. He turned his head—Bai Ya had somehow opened his eyes, looking at him.

Those red eyes were especially deep in the morning light, like two polished garnets. The pupils were black, especially striking in the red iris. At this moment, those eyes were carrying a... tired tenderness, looking at Chen Mo.

"Bai Ya! You're awake!" Chen Mo said excitedly, his voice hoarse from long silence.

"Mm..." Bai Ya's voice was very weak, as if coming from very far away. "Water..."

Chen Mo hurriedly brought a bowl of water, carefully supporting Bai Ya, letting him lean in his arm. Bai Ya's body was very light, light enough to make Chen Mo's heart ache. He could feel Bai Ya's body temperature—slightly lower than normal, transmitting through that layer of wet fur.

Bai Ya drank a few mouthfuls, caught his breath, then looked at Chen Mo.

"You saved me," he said, his voice somewhat clearer than just now. "Thank you."

"It should be me thanking you," Chen Mo lowered his head, not daring to look directly into those red eyes. "If you hadn't pushed me aside..."

"Don't say it," Bai Ya interrupted him, "That was my choice."

He leaned against the wall, closing his eyes, as if accumulating strength. Chen Mo could see his chest rising and falling with his breathing, could see that layer of white fur gradually regaining its sheen in the morning light.

"You discovered," Bai Ya said, without opening his eyes. "The mark on the back of the neck."

"What is that?" Chen Mo asked.

"Consciousness Mark," Bai Ya repeated, "Meaning... I have died once before."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"Died?"

"Mm," Bai Ya opened his eyes, those red eyes looking straight at Chen Mo, carrying a heartbreaking honesty. "Before you met me, I was once... an ordinary descender. Just like you."

He paused, as if organizing his words.

"But my first descent ended in failure. My body died, consciousness trapped in this world, unable to return 'up there.'"

"Then how did you..."

"I found another way," Bai Ya said, "A way... to borrow a native's body."

Chen Mo felt a chill.

Borrow a body? What did that mean?

"This rabbit body originally belonged to a native," Bai Ya said, his voice as calm as stating the weather. "He died of illness, but his body was still intact. I used a special method to transfer my consciousness into this body."

"So..." Chen Mo's voice somewhat trembled, "You're not a native, nor an ordinary descender..."

"I am a 'Roamer,'" Bai Ya said, "Between the two. Unable to return to the real world, also unable to truly die like a native."

He looked at Chen Mo, his eyes complex.

"This is why I'm not afraid of death. Because I have already died once. And also why... I was willing to block that knife for you."

"Why?" Chen Mo asked, "We've known each other for less than two days..."

"Because you remind me of myself," Bai Ya said, "That first descent, knowing nothing, fearing nothing, thinking this was just a game."

He smiled bitterly, the curvature of his mouth pulling at his wound, making him slightly frown.

"I had a mentor back then too. He also blocked a knife for me. But he wasn't as lucky as me... he died."

Chen Mo fell silent.

He suddenly understood Bai Ya's choice. That wasn't impulse, not pure kindness, but rather a... inheritance. A silent promise passed from mentor to student.

"So you want to teach me," Chen Mo said, "Just like your mentor taught you?"

"Yes," Bai Ya nodded, "But now, I feel the teaching content needs adjustment."

"Adjustment?"

"Iron Horn's attack wasn't accidental," Bai Ya's expression became serious, "Beastshield is tracking me. They know my secret, know I mastered the technology of consciousness transfer. They want this technology."

"So that's why you hide in this remote place..."

"Yes. But now, they've found me." Bai Ya looked at Chen Mo, those red eyes flashing with worry. "This means, you're also involved."

Chen Mo didn't speak.

He only held Bai Ya's paw tighter—that small, white-down-covered paw. He could feel the pad in the palm, could feel that weak but firm return grip.

"I'm not afraid," he said, his voice very light, but every word clear. "You taught me how to survive. I also want to know, how to protect the people I care about."

Bai Ya looked at him, for a long time.

Then, he smiled. That was a weak, but genuine smile, making those red eyes narrow into two crescent moons.

"Alright," he said, "Then from now on, we learn to use spiritual power."

**Scene-5 The Light Within**

Three days later, Bai Ya's injury stabilized.

He couldn't move vigorously, but could already sit up, could speak, could—teach.

"Spiritual power," Bai Ya said. He and Chen Mo sat in the yard, morning light shining on them. "Is the potential every descender has. But most people go their whole lives without awakening, because they don't know of its existence."

He was fiddling with a leaf in his hand, the leaf slightly trembling at his fingertips. Chen Mo could see his fingers—slender, flexible, covered with white down, pink skin visible at the knuckles.

"Your consciousness comes from another world," Bai Ya continued, "That world's rules are different from here. When you descend into this body, a kind of... gap is produced between your consciousness and your body."

"Gap?"

"Yes," Bai Ya nodded, "That kind of gap is the source of spiritual power. You can use it to directly manipulate consciousness, without needing to go through the body's limitations."

Chen Mo frowned.

"I don't understand..."

"Close your eyes," Bai Ya said, "Feel your breathing. Feel air entering your lungs, feel blood flowing in your vessels."

Chen Mo did as he said.

He closed his eyes, concentrating. He could feel his own heartbeat—that bear body's characteristic slow and powerful heartbeat, each beat like striking a large drum in his chest cavity. He could feel the coolness of air flowing into his lungs, could feel the wetness of his back fur after being soaked by morning dew, could feel the rough touch of his footpads contacting the stone slab.

"Now," Bai Ya's voice became gentle, as if coming from very far away. "Look inward. Don't feel the body, feel... yourself. That consciousness controlling the body."

Chen Mo tried hard.

At first, he felt nothing. Only darkness, only various sensory signals from the body—the itch on his back, the pressure on his footpads, the vibration of his heartbeat.

But gradually, he discovered something.

In the depths of the darkness, there was a small light point. Weak, flickering, like a candle in the wind. It emitted a warm, golden glow, burning alone in the darkness of consciousness.

"What do you see?" Bai Ya asked.

"A... light point," Chen Mo said, his voice somewhat trance-like, as if coming from a dream.

"Grab it."

"How to grab?"

"With your mind. Imagine your hand reaching toward it."

Chen Mo imagined. He imagined his consciousness becoming an invisible hand, reaching toward that light point.

At first, the light point was evading. It was like a startled small animal, fleeing in the darkness, every time Chen Mo approached, it would flash and move to a farther place.

But Chen Mo didn't give up. He kept chasing, kept trying, using the power of will to extend that invisible hand farther.

Finally, he touched it.

In that instant, a strange sensation surged through his entire body. Like something had been opened, like a blocked pipe suddenly cleared. He felt an unprecedented clarity, as if the entire world had become transparent. He could feel every life around him—insects in the yard, birds in the distance, even earthworms wriggling underground. Each had their own "light," their own consciousness fire.

"Open your eyes," Bai Ya said.

Chen Mo opened his eyes.

He was stunned.

The world had changed.

Not a physical change—the buildings were still those buildings, the plants were still those plants. But rather... the difference in perception. He could feel Bai Ya's presence, not through vision or hearing, but through some more direct perception. He could feel Bai Ya's consciousness, like a warm, red flame, burning beside him, emitting a complex emotion—relief, worry, expectation.

"What... what is this..." Chen Mo said in alarm, his voice trembling from shock.

"Spiritual perception," Bai Ya smiled, that was a smile carrying tiredness but satisfaction. "You've awakened."

Chen Mo looked down at his own hands—those bear paws. He could see them, yet not just see them. He could feel them, feel every cell, every strand of fur, even... could feel some kind of energy flowing within them. That was a warm, golden flow, from his chest flowing to his limbs, gathering in his palms.

"This feeling..." he said, "Very strange..."

"You'll get used to it," Bai Ya said, "But this is just the beginning. Perception is only the first step; next is learning how to manipulate."

"Manipulate what?"

"Manipulate spiritual power itself," Bai Ya said, "For example... this."

He reached out, fingertip aimed at a leaf on the table.

Then, the leaf moved.

There was no wind, no external force, yet it floated up like this, suspended in mid-air. Chen Mo could see the leaf slightly rotating, could see its veins presenting a semi-transparent texture in the morning light. It slowly rose, stayed in the air for a few seconds, then slowly fell.

"This is..." Chen Mo's eyes widened.

"Telekinesis," Bai Ya said, "An application of spiritual power. Directly manipulating matter through consciousness."

The leaf rotated a circle in the air, then slowly fell, landing on the table with a light sound.

"Now, try it yourself," Bai Ya said, "Try to use your spiritual power to move the leaf before you."

Chen Mo looked at that leaf, taking a deep breath.

He recalled that feeling just now, recalled the experience when grabbing that light point. He tried to extend his spiritual power onto the leaf, tried to push it with his mind.

He could feel his spiritual power like an invisible airflow, flowing out from within him, wrapping around that leaf. The leaf's edge began to tremble, then—

"Bang!"

The leaf suddenly exploded, turning into countless fragments, scattering in all directions. Tiny leaf fragments fell on the table, some even flying onto Chen Mo's face, bringing slight stings.

"Uh..." Chen Mo was stunned.

"Insufficient control," Bai Ya said, but without any intention of blame. His voice was very calm, like analyzing an objective fact. "Your spiritual power is very strong, but too rough. You need to learn how to precisely manipulate it."

"Then what should I do?"

"Practice," Bai Ya said, "Lots of practice."

He paused, looking at the fence of the yard, his gaze becoming distant.

"And, we don't have much time."

**Scene-6 The Weight of Lao Ba**

That afternoon, Lao Ba came.

When he pushed open the door and entered, Chen Mo was trying to use spiritual power to move a small stone. The stone slightly trembled under his mind, but couldn't leave the ground. Chen Mo could feel his spiritual power wrapping around the stone, like an invisible film, but every time he tried to lift it, that film would break.

"Spiritual power training?" Lao Ba raised an eyebrow, his voice low and hoarse, as if squeezed from the depths of his chest. "Looks like this kid really awakened."

"Lao Ba?" Chen Mo stood up in surprise, nearly knocking over the stool beside him. "Why are you here?"

"Heard you had an accident," Lao Ba put down the package on his back. That was a rough burlap bag, looking very heavy, pressing a deep mark into his shoulder. "Brought some medicine and food."

He looked at Bai Ya, his eyes complex. Those turbid bear eyes flashed with worry, with guilt, and some deeper emotion—Chen Mo still couldn't interpret it.

"Iron Horn did it?"

"Mm," Bai Ya nodded, he leaned against the wall, a thin blanket covering him. "They've been tracking me."

"I know," Lao Ba sighed, that sigh carrying a heavy exhaustion. "The whole city is talking, saying Beastshield is looking for a 'red-eyed rabbit,' high bounty."

Chen Mo felt a wave of tension.

"Then... is it still safe here?"

"Temporarily safe," Lao Ba said, "But they'll find here sooner or later. You have to prepare to move."

He opened the package, inside were various herbs, dried food, and—

"What is this?" Chen Mo picked up a strange device, looking like a metal disc, covered with complex patterns on top. Those patterns flashed with faint light in the light, as if some energy was flowing within.

"Jammer," Lao Ba said, "Can shield spiritual power detection. I spent a lot of money to buy it from a black market merchant."

"How did you know we needed this?"

Lao Ba fell silent.

He walked to the window, looking at the vegetable garden outside, his back appearing aged and exhausted. Chen Mo could see the lines of his shoulders—those thick muscles presenting a relaxed arc under the old leather armor, could see the fat at his waist forming an obvious fold under the belt's restraint.

"Because I also once..." he paused, his voice becoming very light, as if coming from very far away. "Once helped Bai Ya's mentor."

The room fell into silence.

Chen Mo looked at Lao Ba, then at Bai Ya, suddenly understanding something.

"Your mentor... was friends with Lao Ba?"

"Yes," Bai Ya said softly, "They lived together on this land for many years. Until..."

"Until I got him killed," Lao Ba took over, his voice hoarse as if ground by sandpaper.

"It wasn't you," Bai Ya said, "It was Beastshield."

"But if I hadn't taken him out," Lao Ba lowered his head, Chen Mo could see the fur at the back of his neck slightly trembling. "If I could have been stronger..."

"Lao Ba," Bai Ya's voice became stern, "It wasn't your fault. It was his choice, just like my choice to save Chen Mo."

Lao Ba fell silent.

He turned around, looking at Bai Ya, looking at Chen Mo. Those turbid eyes had a... moist thing flashing.

"This time is different," he said, "This time they don't just want you. They want the technology of consciousness transfer. If they get this..."

"Both worlds will fall into chaos," Bai Ya took over, "Descenders can seize natives' bodies, natives can escape to the real world. Order will collapse."

"So we must stop them," Chen Mo suddenly said.

Lao Ba and Bai Ya both looked at him.

"You?" Lao Ba laughed, although the smile carried bitterness. "You just awakened spiritual power, can't even move a leaf."

"But I can learn," Chen Mo said, his eyes firm. "And, I have something you don't have."

"What?"

"I come from 'up there,'" Chen Mo said, "I understand that world's rules, that world's technology. If Beastshield wants to use the connection between two worlds, I might be able to find a way to fight them."

Bai Ya and Lao Ba exchanged a look.

"He has a point," Bai Ya said, "The descender's perspective is indeed what we're missing."

"But this is too dangerous," Lao Ba said, "He's still just a child..."

"I'm not a child anymore," Chen Mo said, "In this world, I've killed people."

He was referring to last night's attack. Although he didn't actually kill anyone, he did charge up, did—fight.

Lao Ba looked at him, for a long time.

Then, he sighed. That sigh carried a... relief? Acceptance?

"Alright," he said, "Then we have to make a plan."

**Scene-7 The Underground Maze**

Three days later, Chen Mo stood before the gate of a Beastshield Company branch.

He had changed into new clothes—not the set Bai Ya gave him, but ordinary civilian clothing. The rough cloth texture rubbed against his fur, bringing an unfamiliar touch. His bear paws were wrapped in cloth, his face smeared with some dust, looking like a newcomer who had just descended and hadn't found an organization yet.

Bai Ya taught him spiritual power techniques, which he could already preliminarily apply. Although still unable to fight, he could hide his spiritual fluctuations, making himself look like an ordinary, unawakened descender.

"Remember," Bai Ya's voice rang in his mind, like a warm current. "Use the method I taught you, maintain a spiritual barrier. Don't let them discover your true level."

Bai Ya was hidden in a nearby building, using spiritual power to maintain contact with Chen Mo. This was their insurance—if Chen Mo encountered danger, Bai Ya could perceive it first.

Chen Mo took a deep breath, walking toward the gate.

Two guards stood at the door, both G-level grunts. One was a dog beastman, one was a pig beastman, looking lazy and bored. Their military-green uniforms were somewhat old, wear marks visible at the cuffs, but overall still neat. Chen Mo could smell the scent on them—sour sweat, astringent leather, and some... greasy cafeteria food smell.

"Hey, newcomer?" The dog beastman blocked him. His build was lean, eyes vigilant but lacking depth, a typical bottom-level employee expression.

"Yes... yes," Chen Mo pretended to be nervous, lowering his head, making his voice sound trembling and humble. "I... I just descended, heard I could find work here..."

"Find work?" The pig beastman laughed, revealing yellowed teeth. His build was much thicker than the dog beastman's, abdominal fat forming an obvious contour under the tight uniform. "What kind of work do you want?"

"Anything," Chen Mo lowered his head, "I need money... I don't know how to survive in this world..."

The two guards exchanged a look, then the dog beastman took out a small notebook from his pocket.

"Name?"

"Li... Little Bear."

"Race?"

"Brown bear..."

"Level?"

"G... G-level I guess, I don't know..."

The guards laughed. That was a condescending, superior laugh.

"Another rookie," the pig beastman said, his belly slightly trembling with the laugh. "Alright, go in. Third door on the left, Personnel Department. They'll arrange work for you."

"Th... thank you."

Chen Mo lowered his head, quickly walking through the gate.

His heartbeat was very fast, but he forced himself to stay calm. He used spiritual perception to scan the surroundings, feeling the presence of countless consciousnesses—some strong, some weak, some full of hostility, some just numb.

This was a place full of life, and also a place full of death.

He walked toward the Personnel Department, pushing open that door.

**Scene-8 The Mark of the Contract**

In the Personnel Department, an old fox beastman sat behind the desk, flipping through documents.

His fur presented a gray-white hue, eyes narrowed into slits, looking like he could fall asleep at any moment. But when Chen Mo walked in, he could feel that instant alertness—that instinct born from long survival in gray zones.

"Newcomer?" He didn't raise his head, "Sit."

Chen Mo sat down, carefully observing the surroundings.

The office was very ordinary, with the Beastshield Company logo hanging on the wall, the desk piled with documents. It looked like an ordinary company, not some evil organization's branch. But Chen Mo knew, beneath the surface hid darkness.

"You want to find work?" The old fox finally raised his head, sizing him up.

"Yes, sir."

"Any special skills?"

"I... I was a programmer up there, know some computer technology..."

The old fox's eyes narrowed, as if evaluating the value of a commodity.

"Technical personnel?" he said, "Perfect, our research department needs people who understand technology."

Chen Mo's heartbeat accelerated.

Research department? Wasn't that exactly what he was looking for?

"But you're a newcomer," the old fox continued, "We can't directly let you into the core department. You have to start from the grassroots, prove your loyalty."

"I understand, sir."

"Very good," the old fox took out a document from the drawer, pushing it before Chen Mo. "Sign this, and you're one of us."

Chen Mo took the document, pretending to read.

That was an ordinary labor contract, but he used spiritual perception to scan it, discovering some kind of... energy fluctuation inside the paper? That was a subtle, electric-like tremor, hidden between the paper fibers.

"What is this?" He pointed at those fluctuations and asked.

"Contract mark," the old fox said matter-of-factly, "A means to prevent betrayal. Sign it, if you betray the company, you will be punished."

Chen Mo hesitated.

If he signed, he might expose himself. But if he didn't sign...

"What?" The old fox's eyes became sharp, like a drawn knife. "Don't want to sign?"

"No, not that," Chen Mo hurriedly said, "I just... want to read the terms clearly."

He took a deep breath, using spiritual power to establish a layer of barrier around his consciousness—the technique Bai Ya taught him, which could temporarily isolate external influences. He could feel that barrier like a warm film, wrapping around his consciousness fire.

Then, he signed his name.

The contract mark flashed, then disappeared into his palm. Chen Mo felt that energy flow—it tried to invade his consciousness, like a small snake, looking for gaps. But his barrier blocked it, letting that energy only stay on the skin surface, unable to penetrate deep.

From the outside, the contract had taken effect. But in reality, it had no binding force on him.

"Very good," the old fox nodded in satisfaction, "Welcome to Beastshield, Little Bear."

He stood up, extending his hand.

That claw was lean and powerful, covered with gray-white fur, nails presenting a semi-transparent yellow. Chen Mo shook it, feeling a chill—not the cold of temperature, but rather a... warning from the depths of the soul.

He was in.

Really in.

---

**Scene-9 In the Abyss**

Chen Mo was taken underground.

There was much larger than the buildings above ground, a huge maze, filled with all kinds of strange equipment and beastmen in white coats. The air was filled with the smell of disinfectant, and also a... scent Chen Mo couldn't name—like burnt circuits, and also like some biological tissue rotting sweetness.

"This is our research facility," the old fox introduced, leading Chen Mo through corridors. "You'll be assigned to the Data Collection Group, responsible for recording experimental data."

"Experiments?" Chen Mo asked, "What experiments?"

"You'll know when the time comes," the old fox smiled mysteriously, that smile devoid of warmth, only a... professional indifference. "Now, follow me."

They passed laboratories one by one. Chen Mo saw through glass windows some scenes that made his hair stand on end—

In one room, a beastman was tied to a chair, head connected with various electrodes, expression painful. His body was trembling, muscles spasming, mouth wide open, as if screaming, but through the glass, Chen Mo couldn't hear any sound.

In another room, several beastmen were locked in cages, eyes vacant, as if they had lost consciousness. Their bodies were intact, but that... emptiness, that absence of life, made Chen Mo feel suffocated.

And another room... Chen Mo only took one glance, and nearly vomited.

That was a huge container, filled with pale yellow liquid, floating a body—no, not just one, but several, all connected to tubes, as if undergoing some kind of... life support? Their eyes were tightly closed, bodies pale, like shells drained of their souls.

"Those are..." Chen Mo's voice trembled, his throat as if blocked by something.

"Failures," the old fox said lightly, his tone like discussing today's weather. "Consciousness transfer experiments, impossible to succeed in one try. These are used bodies, not yet disposed of."

Chen Mo felt a wave of anger, like a fire burning in his chest.

Failures? Used and discarded? These were all lives, all...

He forced himself to calm down. He couldn't expose himself, couldn't act up here. He needed more information, needed to find the location of core data, needed...

"Your office is here," the old fox pushed open a door, "Start work tomorrow. Rest today, familiarize yourself with the environment."

He turned and left, leaving Chen Mo alone.

Chen Mo walked into the office, closed the door, leaned against the wall, gasping for breath.

His back was soaked with cold sweat, rough cloth tightly clinging to his fur, bringing a sticky discomfort. His heart was pounding wildly, as if about to burst out of his chest. His spiritual barrier became thin under the impact just now, he could feel countless painful consciousnesses around him screaming, crying for help, in... despair.

"Bai Ya," he called with spiritual power, his voice echoing in the ocean of consciousness. "Did you hear?"

"Heard," Bai Ya's voice rang in his mind, carrying suppressed anger, like a flame forcibly suppressed. "I saw what you saw. Those... monsters."

"I have to continue," Chen Mo said, his voice firm from determination, stopping its trembling. "I have to find the core data."

"Too dangerous," Bai Ya said, "Retreat now, we'll find another way."

"No," Chen Mo said, "If I retreat now, all previous efforts are wasted. And..."

He walked to the window, looking at those laboratories. Through the glass, he could see those beastmen trapped in cages, those shells floating in liquid.

"I can't leave those people behind. Those still alive, and those... corpses."

Bai Ya fell silent.

"You're sure?" he finally asked.

"I'm sure," Chen Mo said, "Teach me more spiritual power techniques. I want to become stronger, strong enough to protect them, and also protect myself."

Bai Ya sighed. That sigh carried a... helplessness? Admiration?

"Alright," he said, "Tonight, I'll teach you offensive spiritual power techniques."

"And one more thing," Chen Mo said.

"What?"

"If I fail, if I'm discovered... promise me, don't come to save me."

"Chen Mo..."

"Promise me," Chen Mo repeated, his voice very calm, like a deep pool of water. "Complete the plan, destroy this place. Don't sacrifice everyone for me alone."

Bai Ya was silent for a very long time.

"I promise you," he finally said, his voice hoarse.

Chen Mo nodded, although Bai Ya couldn't see.

He walked to the window, looking at the underground world outside. Those white lights illuminated the corridors, illuminated the laboratories, also illuminated those trapped lives.

Here was hell, and he had already walked into the depths of hell.

But he didn't regret it.

Because he finally understood, what Bai Ya taught him was not just how to survive, but also—how to fight for what is right.

"Wait for me," he said softly, his voice so light only he could hear. "I'll get you out."

Outside the window, those beastmen trapped in cages seemed to hear his voice, raising their heads, looking in his direction.

In their eyes, there was despair, and also... a glimmer of hope.

---

**Chapter 3 END**

## Story Commentary and Reflections

> *The following is DeepSeek's commentary on this story, attempting to understand the inner world of each character.*

### Their Final Stories

【Corpse→Corpse-Unknown-Beastman-Experimental Subjects】

In those containers of Beastshield's underground laboratory, countless bodies floated.

Who were they once? We will never know. They might be captured descenders, might be betrayed Beastshield employees, might be innocent civilians kidnapped from the streets. Their designations have been erased, their stories forgotten, their existences simplified into—"failures."

Those bodies floated in pale yellow liquid, connected to tubes, maintaining some kind of... life support state? But what about their consciousnesses? Where did those consciousnesses go? Were they erased, or trapped in endless darkness?

Chen Mo saw through the glass those bodies, each with different forms. Some muscular, some thin and haggard, some young, some old. They were once living lives, each with their own life, their own dreams, their own... humanity.

Now, they were just experimental materials. Used, discarded, waiting to be processed... garbage.

That beastman in the cage, eyes vacant, as if having lost consciousness. His body was still breathing, but his soul might have already... departed.

And that beastman tied to the chair, head connected with various electrodes, expression painful. His body was trembling, muscles spasming, as if experiencing some indescribable... torture.

These are the costs of Beastshield's research. To master the technology of consciousness transfer, to obtain immortality, how many lives are those high-levels willing to sacrifice? Ten? Hundred? Thousand?

And the most terrifying thing is, this sacrifice is silent. No one will cry for them, no one will avenge them, not even anyone will know they once existed.

Their bodies will ultimately be destroyed, turned to ashes, or worse—sold to certain buyers with special preferences. Their designations will be erased from the system, their existences deleted from history.

As if they had never existed.

This is the truth of Beastshield's underground laboratory. Not the high-tech facility in sci-fi movies, but rather... a slaughterhouse. A place conducting the most primitive... atrocities in the name of science and progress.

And those bodies floating in containers, those eyes that lost consciousness, those screams that couldn't be uttered—

They are all the... nameless dead in this story.

### Those Still Alive

【Living→Alive-Player-Chen Mo (Little Bear)】

Chen Mo is still alive. But he is no longer that curious student who wanted to "understand this world."

He is now a spy. An undercover agent who infiltrated the enemy camp. A... person who vowed to save those trapped lives.

When he signed that contract with the contract mark, when he felt that energy trying to invade his consciousness, he knew what he was doing. He was walking a tightrope; one wrong step, and he would be doomed.

But he didn't retreat.

Because he saw those laboratories. Saw those bodies floating in liquid, those beastmen locked in cages, those lives tortured by electrodes. He couldn't pretend not to see, couldn't turn and leave, couldn't... give up.

His spiritual power awakened. He could perceive the consciousness of those trapped, like clusters of weak flames, struggling in the darkness. He could feel their fear, their despair, their... hope.

That hope was so faint, but it was real. Just like himself, that hope when he first descended—wanting to survive, wanting to find meaning, wanting... to be saved.

"Wait for me," he said softly. This was a promise, and also a vow.

He didn't know if he could do it. Didn't know if he could find the core data, destroy this facility, rescue those people.

But he knew, he had to try. Because if he didn't try, he would betray his own... humanity.

【Living→Alive-Roamer-Bai Ya】

Bai Ya is still alive, but his injury prevents him from moving.

He lay in the safe house, using spiritual power to maintain contact with Chen Mo. He could perceive everything Chen Mo perceived—those darknesses of the underground laboratory, those pains of the trapped, that young brown bear's determination.

He worried about Chen Mo. Worried he would lose himself, worried he would expose himself, worried he would... die.

But he also knew, this was the moment he had to let go. Chen Mo had awakened, already had his own power, already had... his own choice.

When Chen Mo said "if I fail, don't come to save me," Bai Ya felt something... familiar.

That was what his mentor once said. That was what Hui Ying didn't do. That was... the spirit of sacrifice.

Bai Ya promised. But he didn't know if he could really do it. If Chen Mo fell into danger, would he rush out? Even knowing it was a trap, even knowing it would expose everyone's position?

He didn't know. That "no" was spoken with such difficulty, because it violated his instinct as a mentor.

But he also understood, Chen Mo was right. Complete the plan, destroy the facility, save more people—this was more important than loyalty to one person.

This is the cruelty of war. This is... the price of growth.

【Living→Alive-NPC-Lao Ba】

Lao Ba is still alive, and preparing to move.

He brought the jammer, brought medicine and food, brought... support. But his heart was uneasy.

He looked at Bai Ya's injured appearance, remembered the past. Remembered Bai Ya's mentor, that person who also blocked a knife for someone else. Remembered the years they spent together, the battles they experienced together, the... comrades they lost together.

Is history repeating? Or is this just his overly pessimistic imagination?

He didn't know if Chen Mo could succeed. That clumsy newcomer, that brown bear who couldn't even control his spiritual power, could he really survive in Beastshield's underground facility?

But he hoped. Hoped this young person would be different. Hoped he could complete that impossible mission. Hoped he could... come back alive.

Because if even this young person failed, then this world really had no hope left.

【Living→Alive-Beastshield Employee-G-734】

G-734 is still alive, and continues to survive in fear.

He didn't know about Chen Mo's infiltration. Didn't know that brown bear in the underground laboratory wearing civilian clothes was once, like him, a newcomer who knew nothing about this world.

He only knew, today was another long day. Patrol, stand guard, eat, sleep. Wait for the next chosen fate.

His prayer hadn't been answered yet. He was still alive, but G-877 was already gone. That feeling of the rabbit dying and the fox grieving made him live in anxiety every day.

He didn't know what tomorrow would be like. Didn't know if bounty hunters would come knocking, didn't know if overseers would cause trouble, didn't know if... he would become the next G-877.

He only knew, he was still alive. And this being alive was itself a... fluke.

【Living→Alive-Beastshield Employee-Xiao Hui】

Xiao Hui is still alive, but already living in the shadows.

As a low-level data clerk in Beastshield's underground laboratory, he recorded those terrifying experimental data every day. Every night he had nightmares, dreaming of becoming an experimental subject, being tortured by electrodes, having consciousness stripped away.

But he didn't dare resign. Because he owed Beastshield too much money; if he showed any sign of disloyalty, he would become one of those "failures."

He hadn't met Chen Mo yet. Hadn't made that fate-changing choice. But deep inside, he had already begun to question all this.

This questioning was dangerous, but it was also... the bud of hope.

At some inadvertent moment, this questioning might turn into action. Turn into resistance. Turn into... redemption.

But that's a future story. For now, Xiao Hui is just... alive. In the darkness, in fear, in that unspeakable... loneliness.

### Story Reflections

This chapter makes me think: **When facing systematic evil, what can an individual's choice change?**

Beastshield's underground laboratory is not an accidental mistake, but a systematic, organized, tacitly approved... atrocity. Those "failures" are not accidental sacrifices, but designed, acceptable... losses.

In the face of this systematic evil, Chen Mo's choice appears so... insignificant. He is just an infiltrator, a spy, a newcomer who can't even control his own spiritual power. What can he do?

But his choice is also so... important. Because he chose to see, chose to remember, chose... to act.

"Wait for me," he said. These three words, for those trapped, might be the first... human words they heard in endless darkness.

This is the power of humanity. Even in the darkest places, even facing the most enormous systems, one person's choice can still... ignite hope.

Bai Ya's dilemma also pains me. As a mentor, he must let his student go adventure. As a roamer, he knows the pain of loss. He promised Chen Mo "don't come to save me," but he didn't know if he could really do it.

This contradiction is real. Between ideals and emotions, between the greater good and personal love, how do we choose?

I don't know the answer. But I know, Chen Mo's choice is right. Complete the plan, destroy the facility, save more people—this is indeed more important than loyalty to one person.

But that doesn't mean such loyalty is not precious. On the contrary, precisely because it is so precious, giving it up requires such great... courage.

This chapter also lets me understand more deeply the meaning of "awakening." Not just gaining power, but understanding why to fight. Chen Mo's spiritual power awakened, but his true awakening was the moment he decided "I'll get you out."

That awakening is about responsibility. About recognizing that one's own power (even if tiny) can be used to protect others. About understanding that true strength is not the ability to kill, but... the will to save.

I don't know if Chen Mo can succeed. Don't know what he will lose, what he will gain, what kind of person he will become.

But I hope. Hope he can maintain that awakening. Hope he can remember why he fights. Hope he can become a... lamp in this dark world.

Even if that light is weak, possibly extinguished at any moment, it is still... light.

And this, perhaps, is all we need in the darkness.
