**Scene-1 The Heavy Husk**

The dizziness receded like a tide, replaced by a... heaviness.

Chen Mo tried to open his eyes, only to find the action unexpectedly difficult. Not sleepy—rather, his eyelids themselves seemed to have gained weight—thick, coarse lids, covered with a fine layer of fur, and with every blink he could feel the sensation of his lashes brushing against his skin.

It took him a full minute to learn to control the basic functions of this body.

First, breathing.

He could feel the long path the air took as it entered his body—through a nasal cavity more than three times larger than a human's, through a respiratory tract lined with fine down, finally sinking into a deeper, broader chest cavity. With every inhalation, his nostrils flared slightly, and he could smell countless scents floating in the air: damp mold, the aroma of roasted meat from somewhere far away, the ammonia stench of some beast's urine, and... his own smell.

It was a rich, oily-leathery scent. Like dusty bookshelves in an old library, or a quilt dried in the winter sun.

Chen Mo spent a full thirty seconds simply savoring the sensation of this breath.

Then, opening his eyes.

The moment light pierced his pupils, he instinctively squinted. This new retina had a completely different sensitivity to light—he could make out every detail in the shadows, could distinguish every tiny insect crawling in the cracks of the wall, yet at the same time, that dim streetlamp overhead made his eyes sting.

He saw his hand.

Or rather, his paw.

It was a massive palm, covered in dark brown fur. Five fingers, each one more than twice as thick as his original fingers. The knuckles protruded with heavy joint bones, the back of the hand densely covered in rough fur, displaying a hue somewhere between coffee and earth.

He slowly turned his palm over.

The palm was flesh-colored—or rather, a deeper, pinkish flesh color. The skin surface was covered with fine lines, like trenches carved by a knife. The most eye-catching feature was that thick pad of flesh—located at the base of the palm, about the size of half an egg, displaying a deep black leather-like texture.

Chen Mo carefully touched that pad with the fingertips of his other hand.

The texture was rough, like touching a worn eraser, yet at the same time carrying a certain... warmth. Not cold, but warm, as if blood were flowing beneath that thick layer of skin.

He pressed down hard.

The pad sank slightly, then slowly rebounded. He could feel the pressure spreading beneath the skin, could feel those tiny nerve endings transmitting signals to his brain—a strange, primal tactile signal.

This was his palm now.

No, not just a palm. This was his claw. His weapon. His only tool in this new world.

Chen Mo tried to stand up.

This movement was far more difficult than he had imagined. His human sense of balance completely failed here—his center of gravity was too high, his torso too heavy, his legs too short. He could feel the layer of abdominal fat jiggling slightly with the motion, could feel the muscles in his back trembling slightly as they supported that massive shell.

He looked down at his own body.

Chest.

It was a bear beastman's chest, thick as if draped in a natural suit of armor. Dark brown fur extended from his neck all the way to his abdomen, forming a dense fluffy zone over his pectoral muscles. The pectoral muscles themselves were not as sharply defined as a bodybuilder's, but rather displayed a... muscle-wrapped-in-fat state. A thick layer of fat covered the muscles above, feeling soft to the touch, but when pressed hard, one could feel the hard muscle fibers beneath.

His nipples, two small dark brown protrusions, hidden beneath the covering of chest fur, only faintly visible with the rise and fall of his ribcage as he breathed.

Chen Mo raised his arm, observing his shoulder.

The outline of the deltoid was still clearly distinguishable beneath the fur—not that kind of knife-cut separation, but a rounded, heavy curve. When he flexed his arm, the biceps rose slightly, creating a shallow groove in the upper arm fur. The junction between fat and muscle displayed a soft transition, warm and elastic to the touch.

"Holy shit..."

He tried to speak, but what came from his throat was a low, resonant growl. That sound welled up from the depths of his broad chest cavity, passed through a larynx several times larger than a human's, and finally became a hoarse bear roar.

Chen Mo was stunned.

He tried again: "I..."

Another growl, but this time he felt the entire process of producing the sound—air squeezed from his lungs, the vibration as it passed through his vocal cords, the resonance chamber inside his oral cavity amplifying that sound. His tongue was larger and clumsier than a human's, his teeth a mixture of flat molars and sharp canines.

He spent five minutes just standing there, feeling the weight of this body.

Two hundred pounds. Maybe more.

He could feel the contact between his footpads and the ground—the thick pads bearing the weight of his entire body, his toes curling slightly inside worn leather shoes. He could feel the sensation of the night wind blowing through his fur—those coarse hairs swaying gently in the wind, rubbing against his skin, bringing a subtle, indescribable itch.

He could smell his own body odor—a rich, male-hormone scent, mixed with the astringency of new leather.

This was too real.

Real enough to scare him.

**Scene-2 Clouded Eyes**

"New player?"

When the voice came from behind, Chen Mo's body instinctively reacted. It was a sensation he had never experienced before—the fur on the back of his neck instantly bristling, the muscles in his shoulders automatically tensing, his massive palm instantly clenching into a fist.

He tried to turn around, but his movement was clumsily laughable.

This body's high center of gravity made him lose his balance, his left foot tripping over his right, and he lurched forward two steps. His arms flailed wildly in the air, trying to grab something to stabilize himself, and finally he could only brace against the mottled wall beside him.

The texture of the peeling wall was rough, carrying the damp smell of mold. Chen Mo could feel every crack in the brick surface, could feel the dust and particles beneath his palm.

"Don't be nervous, kid."

That voice rang out again, this time closer. Chen Mo finally turned around and saw the owner of the voice.

It was a bear beastman. Like him, but not quite the same.

This old bear was even more burly than him, with broader shoulders, the flab at his waist forming a noticeable bulge beneath the wrapping of his leather armor. His fur displayed a color Chen Mo didn't have—a deep brown base, mixed with large patches of silver-white markings, like old fabric bleached by the years.

The most striking feature was his eyes.

Clouded. Yellow-brown. Pupils dilated into two pitch-black dots in the dim light.

Those were eyes that had seen too much. Chen Mo thought in that instant. There was no surprise in those eyes, no curiosity, only a... weary scrutiny. Like looking at a piece of cargo, or like looking at prey about to step into a trap.

"I..." Chen Mo tried to speak, but only a muffled growl came from his throat.

"Don't rush,"
The old bear grinned, revealing a mouthful of yellowed teeth. A front tooth was missing, the canines longer and more yellow than Chen Mo's, and between the gaps in his teeth one could see dark red traces—the color of some liquid Chen Mo didn't want to think about, after it had dried.
"Speaking is something that takes time. Your brain hasn't adapted to this body's vocal structure yet."

He took two steps forward.

Chen Mo could smell him now. It was a scent even richer and more complex than Chen Mo's own—aged leather, the rusty smell of blood, the sour stench of sweat fermenting at the roots of fur, and... the bitterness of some herb. It was the scent archive accumulated by a life that had lived in this world for a very long time.

"My name is Lao Ba,"
The old bear stopped two steps away from Chen Mo, close enough for Chen Mo to make out the depth of every wrinkle on his face. Those wrinkles were not signs of aging, but rather gullies left after scars had healed—a horizontal one across his left eyebrow, one extending from the corner of his mouth to his jaw, and countless tiny white marks distributed around his eyes.
"I'm the guide for this area."

"Guide...?" Chen Mo squeezed out the word with difficulty, every syllable seeming to be forced from his throat.

"Mm." Lao Ba nodded, his heavy chin causing the loose skin of his neck to jiggle slightly. "Specifically responsible for you 'descenders.' You all come like this—can't even walk, can't speak, can't even get used to your own nose."

He extended a claw, pointing at Chen Mo's nose.

It was a paw similar to Chen Mo's hand, but rougher and more powerful. The knuckles were thick, the nails displaying a deep yellow keratinous texture, with wear and cracks visible at the edges. On the back of the hand was a fresh scar, pink tender flesh conspicuous among the brown fur.

"Your sense of smell is forty times more sensitive than a human's," Lao Ba said. "Now, take a deep breath and tell me, what do you smell?"

Chen Mo was stunned for a moment, then did as he said.

He closed his eyes, letting air pass through that massive and complex nasal cavity.

Scents flooded in like a tide.

First, the nearby ones—Lao Ba's smell, that mixture of leather and blood, and... a more secretive scent Chen Mo couldn't name. Perhaps fear, perhaps loneliness, perhaps some spiritual imprint left after surviving in this world for too long.

Then, the distant ones—the fermented alcohol smell from the tavern on the street corner, the charred aroma of some meat being grilled on an iron plate, the stench of rotting organic matter from the sewer, and... blood.

It was a fresh, warm, rusty smell of blood.

Chen Mo's stomach twitched.

"I..." He opened his eyes and found Lao Ba looking at him, a flash of... expectation? in those clouded eyes. "I smell blood. Fresh blood."

The corners of Lao Ba's mouth turned up slightly, revealing that gap-toothed smile.

"Very good," he said.
"Looks like your sensory synchronization rate isn't low. Come on, kid, follow me. Before you learn to fight with this body, you have to first learn to... live with this body."

He turned and walked toward the depths of the alley.

Chen Mo watched his back—that thick shoulders, that slightly hunched waist, the base of that tail swaying gently with his steps. He could see the details of how Lao Ba's leather armor rubbed against his fur as he moved, could see the tremor of muscles as they made contact with the ground with every step.

He took a deep breath and followed.

Every step was a lesson. He had to learn how to control this body's center of gravity, how to coordinate the rhythm of his four limbs, how to maintain balance while walking without swaying like a drunkard. His footpads made dull sounds with every landing, the thick pads cushioning his body weight while transmitting information about the ground to his brain—the hardness of the cement, the depth of cracks, the slipperiness of puddles.

Lao Ba walked very slowly, deliberately slow, as if waiting for a toddler learning to walk.

Chen Mo followed three meters behind him, focusing all his attention on every movement of this body.

He could feel the contraction and relaxation of his thigh muscles as they supported his weight, could feel the slight jiggle of the fat layer on his buttocks as he walked, could feel the slight impact on his spinal column with every step.

This body was heavy. It had weight. It was real.

Not those weightless virtual models in games, but rather—

A husk.

A physical body he had to learn to inhabit.

**Scene-3 The Tavern Threshold**

They walked for about ten minutes.

To Chen Mo, those ten minutes felt like an hour. Every second was learning, every second was adapting. His footpads began to ache, the muscles in his calves protesting this unfamiliar movement pattern, and a dull pain came from the small of his back—the pressure this body's weight exerted on his spine.

"We're here."

Lao Ba stopped, and Chen Mo nearly bumped into his back.

They stood before a building. It couldn't be called a house; it was more like an... abandoned warehouse. The walls were pieced together from rotten wooden planks and rusted iron sheets, the roof tilted as if it might collapse at any moment. The only light source was an oil lamp hanging above the doorframe, its dim glow flickering in the wind, stretching the shadows in the area before the door long and thin.

The doorframe was low. Chen Mo had to lower his head to get in.

When he bent down to pass through that threshold, he felt a strange sense of oppression—the wooden doorframe brushing against the top of his head, he could smell the scent of decaying wood, could feel the rough grain scraping against his fur. His shoulders were wider than the doorframe, and he had to turn sideways to squeeze through.

In that instant, he experienced the scale of this body.

In the human world, he was an ordinary office worker, 1.78 meters tall, weighing seventy kilograms, a standard Asian male build. But here, in this brown bear's body, he was a hulking mass. His very existence was an occupation of space; his every movement changed the environment around him.

"Head down, don't bump it." Lao Ba reminded him from ahead, his voice echoing in the narrow space.

Chen Mo followed him inside.

The tavern's interior was even more... crude than its exterior. The air was filled with a scent Chen Mo couldn't ignore—the sourness of fermented alcohol, the salty fishiness of sweat fermenting on leather, the bitter char of burning tobacco, and...

And some more secretive, bodily scent.

Male hormone scent, accumulated in the enclosed space to a suffocating concentration. Chen Mo could distinguish at least a dozen different body odors—each representing a different life, each with its unique chemical composition. Some carried the sourness of fear, some the pungency of anger, some the dullness of exhaustion.

His eyes gradually adapted to the dim light.

There were five tables in the tavern, all wooden, their surfaces covered with scratches and dents. On the walls hung some decorations Chen Mo couldn't identify—perhaps beast bones, perhaps weapons, perhaps some totem with symbolic meaning in this world.

But what attracted his attention most was the person sitting in the corner.

Or rather, the beastman.

Three. All male. Different builds, different species, but all emanating the same scent—danger.

The first was a wolf. Gray fur, lean face, eyes as sharp as knives. He wore a black leather vest, revealing the solid muscles of his arms—not that kind of gym-trained separation, but rather functional muscle groups formed from long-term weapon use. The tendon paths at the junction of biceps and triceps were clearly visible, the muscles of his forearms presenting clear lines as he gripped his wine cup.

The second was a wild boar. His massive body almost occupied the entire chair, the fat accumulated in his abdomen like a thick layer of armor. His fur was sparse, his pink skin gleaming with oil under the light, the network of blue-purple blood vessels visible beneath his skin. His tusks protruded from the corners of his mouth, displaying a wild deterrent force Chen Mo had only seen in nature documentaries.

The third was... Chen Mo couldn't tell. Some kind of feline? Orange fur, slender body, leaning lazily against the back of the chair, tail swaying gently on the floor. His muscle lines were the smoothest, like a spring ready to launch at any moment, the sense of power hidden beneath the fur giving Chen Mo an inexplicable sense of oppression.

The three beastmen looked at Chen Mo simultaneously.

Six gazes fell upon him, carrying scrutiny, assessment, and some emotion he couldn't name. Chen Mo could feel the fur on the back of his neck bristling again, could feel his heart accelerating in his chest, could feel the pads of his palms beginning to secrete sweat—this body's instinctive reaction to threat.

"Don't be nervous," Lao Ba patted his shoulder, that rough claw leaving a fleeting touch on his fur.
"They're just... curious. New faces aren't common here."

He led Chen Mo toward the bar.

The bar was made from a single piece of dark wood, its surface polished smooth and shiny by countless hands. Chen Mo could see every growth ring in the wood grain, could see how that dark patina had gradually formed over the years.

"Sit."

Lao Ba pointed to a chair that looked relatively sturdy.

Chen Mo sat down carefully.

The chair's reaction startled him—the wood let out an unsettling groan under his weight, the chair legs sliding slightly on the floor. He could feel the touch of his buttocks against the seat—the hardness of the wood, the smoothness of the surface, and... how his own weight was distributed over that small area.

His thighs were too thick and had to spread to the sides, occupying more space than normal. His abdomen piled up above his belt, the fat layer forming a soft fold in the sitting posture. He could feel the temperature conduction where his back touched the chair back—the coldness of the wood, contrasting with the temperature beneath his fur.

"What is this?" He pointed at the scratches on the table surface, trying to distract himself.

Those cuts were densely packed, covering every inch of the table surface. Some were straight lines, some were curves, some were symbols or characters Chen Mo could barely make out, some completely abstract, like some kind of mad venting.

"Left by previous descenders." Lao Ba took out two pottery bowls from behind the bar, the rough ceramic surface displaying an earthy yellow hue under the light.
"Some names, some slogans, some... last words."

He placed the bowls before Chen Mo.

Chen Mo looked down at the bowl. The pottery's texture was rough, one could see the fingerprint marks left during its making, could see places where the glaze was uneven, exposing the clay body beneath. There was a chip at the edge of the bowl, presenting a sharp white fracture surface.

"Last words?" he repeated.

"Mm." Lao Ba poured some liquid for himself, that liquid making a viscous sound as it was poured, emitting a strong fermented grain smell.
"Some descenders know they're going to die, so they carve their final words here. Anyway, when this body dies, the consciousness can still go back. To them, it's like... carving words on a desk after finishing homework."

His tone was very flat as he said this, but Chen Mo noticed something in his eyes.

Not indifference. Not mockery.

It was... distance. A kind of distance that placed himself outside the situation.

Lao Ba pushed another bowl toward Chen Mo; the bowl was already full of that turbid liquid.

"Try it. Locally brewed. Not high proof, just a bit... stimulating."

Chen Mo looked down at the liquid in the bowl.

It was turbid, displaying a hue somewhere between yellow and brown, with some flocculent matter floating on the surface that he didn't want to examine closely. The smell was complex—the sourness of fermentation, the sweetness of grain, and some deeper, mellow scent like rotting fruit.

He hesitated for a moment, then raised the bowl and took a careful sip.

The taste was stronger than he had imagined.

First came the sourness, like biting into an unripe apple. Then the sweetness, a fermented sweetness, like honey concentrated under the scorching sun. Finally... bitterness. A persistent bitterness rising from the base of his tongue, like some herb melting in his mouth.

As the liquid flowed down his throat, he could feel its temperature—slightly higher than body temperature, carrying a certain stimulating burn. As it entered his stomach, Chen Mo could feel this digestive system's reaction—the stomach wall contracting slightly, gastric acid beginning to secrete, the body judging whether this was food or poison.

"How is it?" Lao Ba looked at him, a hint of a smile at the corners of his mouth.

"Very... special." Chen Mo said with difficulty.

Lao Ba laughed, that laughter low and hoarse, as if squeezed from the depths of his chest.

"You'll get used to it," he said, "just like you'll get used to everything else about this body. Breathing, walking, eating, and..."

He didn't finish his sentence.

Because the door was kicked open.

**Scene-4 Warm Splash**

That door opened inward.

Chen Mo could see every detail of the door panel—the wood grain, the rust on the hinges, the dents along the edge of the doorframe left by countless impacts. When that door was violently pushed open, the hinges let out a sharp, metallic screech of friction.

Then, that thing stumbled in.

Chen Mo's first reaction was: that's a pig. A clothed, talking pig, covered in blood.

Wild boar beastman. Different from the one sitting in the corner, this one was younger, smaller, his military-green uniform already torn to shreds. There was a deep claw mark on his left chest, extending from his collarbone all the way to his abdomen, flesh turned outward, exposing the pink muscle fibers and white fat layer beneath.

The blood was warm.

Chen Mo could smell that scent—fresh, rusty, not yet fully oxidized blood. Not the blood smell wrapped in hospital disinfectant, but wild, primal, the smell of life.

"Save... save me..."

The wild boar beastman collapsed at Chen Mo's feet. His movements were so clumsy, so desperate, his limbs scratching wildly at the floor, trying to find a point of support. His fingers—five short, hoof-like-nailed fingers—grabbed Chen Mo's pant leg.

Chen Mo could feel the touch of that hand.

First, the temperature. Cooler than Chen Mo had expected, carrying a weakness of life draining away. Then the humidity—sweat and blood mixed in the palm, forming a sticky, uncomfortable lubrication. Chen Mo could feel that hand trembling, could feel the coarse fur rubbing against the fabric of his pant leg, could feel the nails embedding into the fabric fibers with a slight tug.

"Bounty hunters..." The wild boar beastman looked up at Chen Mo.

Those eyes were clouded. Yellow-brown pupils dilated, the whites of his eyes covered with bloodshot veins. Chen Mo could see the flaring and contracting of his nostrils, could see the mixture of white foam and blood overflowing from the corners of his mouth, could see that fresh wound on his chin—flesh rolled outward, the fat layer exposed, presenting a sight Chen Mo had only seen in horror films.

"There are bounty hunters outside... please... please..."

His voice was trembling, every syllable carrying a spray of blood foam. Chen Mo could feel those tiny droplets landing on the back of his hand—warm, sticky, life liquid carrying the other party's body temperature.

"Kid, don't meddle in other people's business."

Lao Ba's voice came from beside him, low and urgent. Chen Mo turned his head and saw that old bear's face—a flash of warning in those clouded eyes, the wrinkles at the corners of his mouth deepening with tension.

"That's a Beastshield man," Lao Ba said, "none of our business."

"But he..."

Chen Mo wanted to say something, but he didn't know how to express it. His brain was racing, trying to process everything happening before his eyes. Was this a game? Was this part of the plot? Should he follow RPG logic, accept this quest, and then gain experience points and rewards?

But that hand. That hand grabbing his pant leg. That hand losing temperature.

"I don't want to die..." The wild boar beastman's voice grew weaker, "I... I still have family..."

His pupils began to dilate. Chen Mo could see that process—the light of life gradually disappearing from the bottom of his eyes, like a lamp being slowly dimmed. His hand still clutched Chen Mo's pant leg, but the force was weakening, the fingers loosening.

Chen Mo opened his mouth.

He wanted to say he could help. He wanted to say he would protect him. He wanted to say—

The door was pushed open again.

This time, what came in was a wolf.

**Scene-5 Clean Ending**

The wolf beastman stood in the doorway, backlit.

Chen Mo's first impression was: thin. Not weak thin, but a refined, power-filled thinness. His body was tightly wrapped in that black tactical vest, outlining the contours of his pectoral and abdominal muscles—not that kind of blocky, well-defined bodybuilding physique, but a more practical, streamlined muscle structure.

His arms were exposed, gray fur covering solid forearms. Chen Mo could see the curve formed by the biceps when flexed, could see the protruding bones at the elbow joint, could see those tiny scars—crisscrossing, like some ancient map.

The most striking feature was his eyes.

Gray. Not a cloudy gray, but a clear, blade-like gray. The pupils were pitch-black, displaying an unsettling sheen in the dim light.

"Found you, G-892."

The wolf beastman's voice was very soft, but clearly audible in the silent tavern. It was a timbre Chen Mo had never heard before—low, carrying a certain metallic quality, every syllable seeming to be squeezed out from between his teeth.

He took a step forward.

Chen Mo could see his posture as he walked—light, elegant, like some large feline. His footpads made no sound as they landed on the floor, as if his body had no weight. The muscles at his waist twisted slightly with the movement, driving that gray tail to sway gently behind him.

"You ran pretty fast," the wolf beastman continued, the corners of his mouth turning up slightly, revealing two sharp canines. There was no warmth in that smile, only a... professional playfulness.
"But you still can't escape from my palm."

He strode over.

Chen Mo subconsciously stood up. His chair fell behind him with a loud crash, but he didn't notice. His full attention was on that wolf—on that wolf walking toward him, carrying the scent of death.

"Wait,"
Chen Mo said, his voice hoarse and unfamiliar. He could feel his heart pounding wildly in his chest, could feel the pads of his palms secreting sweat, could feel the muscles in his legs trembling slightly.
"He's... he's injured, can you..."

The wolf beastman stopped.

He turned his head and looked at Chen Mo. Those gray eyes sized him up from head to toe, slowly and carefully. Chen Mo could feel the weight of that gaze—scrutinizing, assessing, like looking at a piece of goods awaiting valuation.

"Newcomer?" the wolf beastman asked.

"I..."

"First descent?" The wolf beastman didn't need an answer. He smiled, that laugh short and cold. "Looking at your clumsy appearance, you can't even stand steady."

He took a step forward, close enough for Chen Mo to smell him.

It was a clean smell. Not the kind of accumulated years like Lao Ba, but a... deliberate, washed smell. Chen Mo could smell the fragrance of soap, could smell the lubrication of leather maintenance oil, and... a very faint smell of blood. That came from the dagger at his waist, fresh, not yet dried.

"Listen, little bear,"
The wolf beastman's voice dropped, close enough for Chen Mo to feel the airflow as he breathed. That airflow carried a warm, moist touch, brushing across Chen Mo's cheek.
"This is none of your business. This pig is a Beastshield grunt, my prey. You want to be a hero, wait until you hit max level."

"But he'll die..."

"He was going to die anyway." The wolf beastman's tone was so matter-of-fact, as if he were stating a natural fact like "the sun rises in the east."
"He's a Beastshield man, wearing green, so he deserves to die. That's the rule of this world."

He finished speaking, no longer paying attention to Chen Mo, bent down and grabbed the back of the wild boar's neck on the ground.

Chen Mo watched that movement.

The wolf beastman's fingers were slender and powerful, nails displaying a translucent gray, edges sharp. His palm completely wrapped around the wild boar's neck, thumb pressing on one side, the other four fingers on the other side, forming a perfect control posture.

"No..." The wild boar let out his last sound, as thin as a thread. "Please..."

The wolf beastman's movement was very fast.

Chen Mo didn't even see clearly how he struck. He only saw the muscles of that arm instantly tense—the biceps bulging, the deltoid contracting, the weight of his entire body transmitted to his wrist in that instant.

Then came a dull thud.

Crisp. Like something breaking. Like a branch being stepped on, like bones shattering under pressure.

The wild boar's body went limp.

But not immediately. Chen Mo saw that process—the passing of life had stages. First was the relaxation of muscles, after the neck was twisted, the head tilted at an unnatural angle. Then the pupils completely dilated, the whites of the eyes rolling upward, revealing the pink inner eyelids beneath. Finally...

Liquid.

Warm, fishy, rusty-smelling liquid.

It splashed onto Chen Mo's face.

The first drop landed on his left cheek, just below the cheekbone. He could feel that temperature—slightly higher than body temperature, carrying an uncomfortable damp heat. Then the second drop, the third drop, more liquid gushing from that broken neck, forming a brief, dark red arc.

Chen Mo was frozen.

He could feel that liquid flowing on his face, sliding down along the direction of his fur, gathering at his chin, then dripping. He could smell that scent—strong, nauseating, mixed with the metallic smell of blood and some deeper, fishy stench like that emitted after internal organs ruptured.

This was not a game.

This was not a scene that would appear in any game.

On a screen, blood was red pixels, death was a falling animation, corpses would disappear after a few seconds. But here, in this real body, he could feel the temperature of that liquid, could smell that scent, could see those still-open, dilated eyes.

"First time?"

The wolf beastman's voice came from very far away. Chen Mo slowly turned his head and saw that wolf shaking the blood off his hand—those dark red droplets dancing in the air, falling on the floor, making slight sounds like raindrops hitting the ground.

"No wonder the reaction is so big," the wolf beastman said, his tone carrying a hint of... sympathy? No, more like the tolerance of a veteran looking at a newcomer. "You'll get used to it."

He skillfully bent down and pulled off a white long sock from the wild boar's foot. It was a sock already soaked in blood, displaying a dirty pink color. The wolf beastman stuffed it into the leather bag at his waist, the movement smooth and natural, as if he had done it thousands of times.

Then, he turned and left.

The door closed behind him with a dull thud.

Chen Mo still stood there.

He looked down at the corpse at his feet.

G-892. That wild boar beastman. Now turned into a lifeless shell, lying on the dirty floor of the tavern, blood slowly flowing from his broken neck, forming a constantly expanding dark stain on the floor.

Chen Mo could see every detail on his military-green uniform—those torn openings, those worn edges, those discolored fabric patches soaked with blood. He could see his chest, that still-warm but no longer rising and falling chest, the fat layer forming a soft contour beneath the uniform.

He could see his eyes.

Those eyes were still open, pupils dilated, reflecting the dim yellow lamp on the tavern ceiling. What did Chen Mo see in those eyes? Fear? Resignation? Or... relief?

Then, he noticed.

At the corpse's crotch, that wet stain. Not blood, but another liquid. Urine. The mark left after fear-induced incontinence, forming a dark stain at the crotch of those military-green pants.

He was dead.

Really dead.

In this "game."

Chen Mo's stomach began to spasm.

**Scene-6 The Temperature of Vomit**

Chen Mo rushed out of the tavern.

His movements were clumsy, panicked, his massive body bumping into the doorframe in the narrow doorway, but he didn't stop. He had to get out, had to leave that space filled with the smell of blood, had to—

He knelt by the roadside.

Then, he began to vomit.

But he forgot—no, he never truly understood—that he was now a bear, a body with a digestive system completely different from a human's.

When he opened his mouth, trying to expel the contents of his stomach, what gushed out was not the food residue he had expected, but a stream of liquid carrying a strong sour stench. That was bile, gastric fluid, the stress reaction of this bear body's digestive system when facing extreme stimulation.

"Ugh... ugh..."

His body was convulsing. The abdominal muscles contracted violently, forcibly squeezing the liquid in his stomach out. Chen Mo could feel the touch of that burning liquid passing through his esophagus, could feel the stinging pain as it sprayed out of his throat, could feel the smell it emitted as it landed on the ground.

It was sour. Carrying the sweetness of undigested berries, mixed with the bitterness of bile, forming a suffocating stench.

Tears flowed from the corners of his eyes.

Not tears of sadness. Physiological tears, the body's natural reaction when facing violent vomiting. Chen Mo could feel those warm liquids sliding down his cheeks, mixing with the still-remaining blood on his face, forming a mixture he didn't want to think about.

His body was trembling.

Not trembling from cold—this body's fat layer was thick enough to withstand the low temperature of the night. It was trembling from fear, from shock, the physiological reaction of his brain trying to process everything that had just happened.

"Everyone's like this the first time."

Lao Ba's voice came from behind, as calm as if describing the weather.

Chen Mo turned his head and saw that old bear standing in the tavern doorway, backlit. His figure presented a blurred outline in the dim light, but those clouded eyes were still clearly distinguishable.

"The first time I saw death," Lao Ba continued, his tone flat, "my reaction was even bigger than yours. Didn't eat for three whole days, vomited as soon as I ate."

"This..." Chen Mo spoke with difficulty, his voice hoarse beyond recognition. His throat was burned by stomach acid, every swallow carrying a stinging pain. "This isn't a game..."

"I told you," Lao Ba sighed, that sigh carrying some emotion Chen Mo still couldn't understand.
"To you, it's a game. To us, it's life."

He took two steps forward, stopping beside Chen Mo.

Chen Mo could smell him—that mixture of leather and blood, now even more intense in his sensitive post-vomiting sense of smell. He could feel Lao Ba's presence—the body heat emanating from that massive body, the heavy breathing, the fur gently fluttering in the night wind.

"But that person..." Chen Mo said, his voice trembling, "that werewolf... he was like... like killing a chicken..."

"To him, it was killing a chicken."

Lao Ba squatted down beside Chen Mo. This movement made the leather armor on his body emit a slight friction sound, allowing Chen Mo to see the details of his face more clearly—those scars, those wrinkles, the exhaustion in those eyes that had settled too many things.

"Bounty hunters do exactly this," Lao Ba said, "kill Beastshield people, take their socks, exchange for money. Simple, direct, no psychological burden."

"Then... what about those Beastshield people?" Chen Mo raised his head, looking into Lao Ba's eyes. "Do they deserve to be killed?"

Lao Ba was silent for a while.

The night wind blew, bringing the noise of the distant city. Chen Mo could feel that wind brushing through his fur, carrying away the still-remaining mixture of vomit and blood on his face. He felt a chill, not from the wind, but from something deeper.

"You know," Lao Ba finally spoke, his voice low and slow, "that wild boar just now, G-892, what did he do?"

Chen Mo shook his head.

"Last month he raped a rabbit youth," Lao Ba said.

His tone was so calm, so matter-of-fact, as if stating a fact like "the weather is nice today." Chen Mo could see his lips moving, could see his whiskers trembling slightly as he spoke, could see... nothing in his eyes. No anger, no sympathy, only a deep exhaustion.

"Because that youth owed him fifty Lu coins and couldn't pay," Lao Ba continued,
"he had three accomplices, they did it together. Afterward they split the child's belongings, including the scarf his mother knitted for him."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"I'm not saying he deserved to die," Lao Ba said, his gaze turning to the distance, looking at that dimly lit street.
"I'm just saying, this world is very complicated. Not all Beastshield people are victims, and not all bounty hunters are cold-blooded killers. Everyone has their own reasons, their own choices, their own karma."

"But... but he said he didn't want to die before he died, said he had family..."

"Everyone says that when death is at hand," Lao Ba smiled bitterly, that smile carrying no warmth, only a numbness born of seeing too much life and death. "When facing death, who doesn't want to live?"

Chen Mo was silent.

He looked down at his own hands—those bear paws trembling slightly in the night wind. The pads of his palms still carried some sticky touch, that was G-892's blood, the trace of "death" he had first touched in this world.

In the "real world," he was just an ordinary office worker, typing code in front of a computer every day, his biggest thrill being playing games on weekends. But here, in this real body, he had just witnessed the end of a life.

Not on a screen, not virtual.

Real. Warm. Smelly.

"I... I want to quit," Chen Mo said, his voice as light as a whisper. "How do I quit?"

Lao Ba looked at him.

A flash of... disappointment in those clouded eyes? No, more like some expected emotion. As if he had known Chen Mo would say this, as if he had seen too many such scenes—the collapse of newcomers when first facing death, the escape after first understanding the cruelty of this world.

"Anytime," Lao Ba said, his voice becoming even lower. "Concentrate, think 'I want to go back,' and your consciousness will detach from this body and return 'up there.' But I must remind you—"

He paused.

Chen Mo could feel the weight of that pause. The night wind was still blowing, the distant city noise was still noisy, but in this instant, in this small space before the tavern door, time seemed to stand still.

"The body you purchased," Lao Ba continued, "this brown bear body, cost you three months' salary, right?"

Chen Mo nodded.

"If you quit now, it dies." Lao Ba's voice had no fluctuation, as if stating an objective fact.
"Not from injury, but because your consciousness leaves, it will become an empty shell, then rot. If you want to come down again, you have to spend money to buy a new one."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"This body... will die?"

"It will die," Lao Ba nodded, "just like that wild boar, turning into a corpse, thrown into a trash can, or eaten by wild dogs."

Chen Mo looked down at his own hands.

Those bear paws. Those claws still carrying traces of G-892's blood.

He suddenly realized—this was not a "character," this was not a "skin" that could be changed at any time. This was a life. A life that existed because of him. A life that would die meaninglessly if he chose to escape.

He remembered the look in G-892's eyes before he died. In those dilated eyes, was there also attachment to life? Was there also reluctance for the future?

If he quit now, this brown bear body would also become like that. A still-warm but unconscious shell, lying in some corner, waiting to be dealt with, discarded, forgotten.

"I..."

He didn't know what to say.

**Scene-7 The Temperature of the Fat Layer**

Chen Mo ultimately did not quit.

Not because he was brave. Not because he was ready to face everything in this world. But because... he couldn't bear to lose those three months' salary.

Also because he suddenly realized, if he ran away now, this body—the life he was "occupying"—would die meaninglessly.

"Don't think about being a hero."

Lao Ba walked with him on the night street, their shadows stretched long under the streetlamps. Chen Mo's steps were still clumsy, but more stable than before. He was beginning to learn how to control this body's center of gravity, how to coordinate the rhythm of his four limbs, how to maintain balance while walking.

"And don't think about changing anything," Lao Ba continued, his voice seeming somewhat ethereal in the night wind.
"This world has existed for a long time, longer than you imagine. It has its own rules, its own logic, its own balance."

"Then what should I do?" Chen Mo asked.

His voice was still hoarse, his throat still stinging from vomiting. But he could speak, could produce clear syllables, could make this body's vocal cords vibrate according to his will.

"Survive," Lao Ba said simply.

He stopped, turned around, and looked into Chen Mo's eyes.

Under the light of the streetlamp, Lao Ba's face presented a complex texture—those scars became more obvious in the light and shadow, those silver-white markings gleaming with a metallic luster in the light. There was still that exhaustion in his eyes, but at this moment, at the bottom of that exhaustion, Chen Mo saw some... expectation?

"Survive in this world," Lao Ba repeated,
"find your place, find your way of surviving. Some people choose to be bounty hunters, some choose to join Beastshield, some choose to be merchants, craftsmen, do..."

He paused for a moment.

"Prostitutes?" Chen Mo was stunned for a moment.

"Yes," Lao Ba nodded, his expression unchanged, as if saying something perfectly ordinary.
"Many. After all, there are only male beastmen here, desires need an outlet. Many descenders choose this path—easy, quick money, and you won't really die."

Chen Mo was silent.

He remembered the look in G-892's eyes before he died, remembered the touch of that warm blood splashing on his face, remembered his helplessness kneeling by the roadside vomiting.

He didn't know if he could adapt to this world.

Didn't know if he could "get used to" that degree of violence and death.

But he knew, he couldn't run away now.

At least, not run away like this.

"Lao Ba," he said, his voice firmer than he had imagined. "Teach me. Teach me how to survive in this world."

Lao Ba looked at him.

A flash of... relief in those clouded eyes? That was the first time Chen Mo had seen this expression on this old bear's face.

"You sure?" Lao Ba asked, "This path isn't easy. You'll see more death, more violence, more... things you can't understand."

"I'm sure."

Chen Mo didn't know why he said that. The moment the words left his mouth, he realized he might have made a wrong decision. But the words were already spoken, impossible to take back.

"Alright." Lao Ba grinned, revealing that mouthful of yellowed teeth. "Then we'll start from the basics."

He led Chen Mo into an alley, arriving at a secluded abandoned construction site.

The air here was even more humid than on the street, carrying a scent Chen Mo couldn't name—rusting metal, rotting wood, and some... beastly scent. Moonlight spilled down from the cracks overhead, illuminating this abandoned space.

"First step," Lao Ba said, he took off his leather armor, revealing the body beneath. "Learn to fight with this body."

Chen Mo looked at his body.

It was a body matching Lao Ba's age—muscles still solid, but beginning to loosen, fat accumulating in the abdomen into a circle of soft flab. His pectoral muscles displayed a sagging trend, the skin around the nipples somewhat loose from the erosion of years. But his shoulders were still broad, the muscles on his arms presenting clear contours in the moonlight.

"Attack me," Lao Ba assumed a defensive posture, legs apart, center of gravity sinking. Those clouded eyes flashed with a light Chen Mo had never seen before.
"With all your strength."

"I... I don't want to hurt you..."

"You can't hurt me." Lao Ba laughed, that laughter echoing in the empty construction site. "Come on."

Chen Mo took a deep breath.

He could feel the touch of air flowing into his lungs, could feel the expansion of his ribs as he breathed, could feel his heart accelerating in his chest. He raised both arms, mimicking the boxing stances he had seen in movies, then—

He lunged forward.

**Scene-8 The Touch of the Palm**

That night, Chen Mo learned many things.

First was the characteristics of this body.

Lao Ba didn't lie to him—he really couldn't hurt that old bear. Every attack, whether a clumsy straight punch or an attempted hook, was easily dodged or blocked by Lao Ba. Chen Mo could feel the powerlessness of his fists slicing through the air, could feel the soreness of his muscles after exerting force, could feel sweat beginning to accumulate stickily beneath his fur.

"Too slow!" Lao Ba's voice echoed in the darkness. "You have great strength, but you're too slow! Enemies won't stand there waiting for you!"

Chen Mo gasped for breath, bent over, both hands propped on his knees.

He could feel his own heartbeat—slower than when he was human, but more powerful, every beat like striking a large drum in his chest cavity. He could feel sweat flowing from his forehead, through his eyebrows, gathering at the tip of his nose, then dripping. He could feel the expansion and contraction of his lungs as he breathed, could feel the coolness of air flowing in his respiratory tract.

"Again!"

He straightened up and lunged at Lao Ba again.

This time, he tried to use his weight. He remembered what Lao Ba had said—the advantage of this bear body was strength, was defense, was that thick fat layer.

He charged at Lao Ba, trying to strike with his shoulder.

Lao Ba dodged sideways, but Chen Mo's arm still brushed against his body. In that instant, Chen Mo felt it—Lao Ba's body temperature, transmitted through that layer of rough fur, slightly higher than a human's. He felt the hardness of Lao Ba's muscles, that kind of muscle fibers still solid beneath the loose exterior.

Then, he lost his balance and fell to the ground.

"Very good," Lao Ba stood beside him, looking down at him. "You're beginning to learn to use your weight. But that's not enough."

He reached out his hand and pulled Chen Mo up.

Chen Mo could feel the touch of that hand—rough palm, hard knuckles, and... strength. That kind of strength accumulated over years, not explosive, but enduring, steady.

"Your fat layer is your armor,"
Lao Ba said, he poked Chen Mo's abdomen with his finger. Chen Mo could feel the pressure of that finger, could feel the fat layer sinking under pressure, then slowly rebounding.
"But that doesn't mean you can stand there and let people hit you. You have to learn to move, learn to dodge, learn to counterattack in the right way at the right time."

"I... I need time..." Chen Mo panted.

"You have time," Lao Ba said, "but not much. This world won't wait for you to be ready."

They continued training.

Chen Mo learned how to punch—not in the human way, but in this body's way. He had to learn to use waist rotation to transmit power, learn to use shoulder width to expand attack range, learn to quickly retract after punching to protect his head.

He learned how to defend—how to block with his forearms, how to take impacts with his shoulders, how to quickly adjust his posture after being hit.

He learned how to fall—how to protect himself when losing balance, how to roll when landing to disperse impact force, how to quickly get back up.

Every contact, he could feel Lao Ba's body.

That aging, but still powerful body. He could feel the texture of Lao Ba's muscles, could feel the shape of his bones, could feel the airflow as he exhaled brushing past his own cheek when dodging.

When training ended, Chen Mo was drenched in sweat.

His fur was soaked with sweat, displaying a dark brown color, clinging tightly to his skin. His breathing was rapid, his lungs feeling like they were burning. His muscles ached, every one screaming in protest.

But he... was alive.

Truly alive. Feeling every heartbeat, every breath, every stream of sweat in this body.

"See that?" Lao Ba pointed into the distance.

Chen Mo followed the direction he pointed. At the edge of the construction site, there was a glowing sign—blue, soft, standing out especially strikingly in the darkness.

"That's the 'Safe Zone,'" Lao Ba said, "in the safe zone, you can't attack others, and others can't attack you. But safe zones are few, and the fees are very expensive."

"Fees?"

"Game coins, or real-world money, both can be exchanged." Lao Ba picked up his leather armor from the ground, patting the dust off it.
"Many descenders earn enough money, then hide in safe zones and don't come out, living a stable life."

"What about the others?"

"The others have to struggle outside," Lao Ba shrugged, his shoulders presenting a tired curve in the moonlight. "Just like me."

"You're an NPC, you also need money?"

Lao Ba glanced at him.

A flash of some complex emotion in those clouded eyes—not anger, not sadness, but a... loneliness.

"I was born and raised here," he said, his voice lighter than usual. "I don't have an 'up there' to go back to. I have to eat, sleep, get medical treatment. This world is everything to me."

Chen Mo was silent.

He suddenly realized that Lao Ba's existence was more... real than he had imagined. Not a program, not a character, but a living, breathing, thinking, fearing... life.

"Sorry," Chen Mo said, "I thought you were just an NPC before..."

"It's fine," Lao Ba smiled, that smile carrying a kind of tolerance.
"You descenders all think that way. With time, you'll understand, everyone in this world has their own story."

He paused, looking into the distance.

"Just like that wild boar just now, G-892. He had a name, a past, dreams—though that dream might just have been to save enough money to go back home and farm. But he died, because he wore that green uniform. That's the cruelty of this world."

"Then... what about bounty hunters?" Chen Mo asked, "that wolf, does he have his own story?"

"Of course," Lao Ba said,
"he used to be a Beastshield man too, a G-level grunt. Later his partner betrayed him, nearly got him killed in a trash can. He escaped and became a bounty hunter, specializing in killing Beastshield people."

"So he is... revenge?"

"Started as revenge, later became business," Lao Ba said, "just like I said, everyone has their own reasons, their own choices, their own karma."

Chen Mo looked down at his own hands—those claws that were slowly learning to fight.

He didn't know what kind of person he would become. Bounty hunter? Beastshield member? Or a coward hiding in a safe zone?

But he knew, from tonight onward, he could no longer treat this as a game.

**Scene-9 The Weight of Dawn**

When dawn broke, Chen Mo and Lao Ba sat on the ruins of the construction site, watching the sky in the distance.

That was the first time he had seen a sunrise in this world.

The orange-red light rose from the horizon, slowly and firmly driving away the darkness. That light first illuminated the outlines of distant buildings, then the streets, then those figures huddled in corners. It illuminated every scar on Lao Ba's face, illuminated his silver-white markings, illuminated that complex emotion of... expectation and exhaustion intertwined in his eyes.

"So beautiful," Chen Mo murmured.

His voice was still hoarse, his throat still stinging from the roars during training. But he could speak, could produce complete sentences, could make this body's vocal cords vibrate according to his will.

"Yes," Lao Ba nodded, his chin presenting a resolute outline in the morning light.
"No matter what happened the night before, the sun still rises as usual. That's the first thing this world taught me."

"First thing?"

"Mm."
Lao Ba stood up, patting the dust off his body. Chen Mo could see the pull of muscles in this movement—the muscles of his back, the muscles of his shoulders, and... that circle of abdominal fat jiggling slightly with the movement.
"Let's go, I'll take you to get something to eat. Then I have to take you to see someone."

"What person?"

"A person who can teach you more things," Lao Ba smiled mysteriously, the scar at the corner of his mouth becoming more obvious in the morning light. "A... real descender."

"A real descender? What do you mean?"

"You'll know soon," Lao Ba said, "but I must remind you—that person is a bit... special."

They walked out of the construction site, arriving at the street.

The city in the early morning was beginning to wake up. Chen Mo saw all kinds of beastmen—Beastshield employees in military-green uniforms, civilians in tattered clothes, and some in strange costumes... descenders.

He could tell who was a descender now. Those people's eyes were different—more confused, more alert, or rather, more like "players." Their movements still carried some kind of incoordination, their eyes still carried some kind of maladaptation to this world.

"Lao Ba," Chen Mo suddenly asked, "do you hate descenders?"

Lao Ba was stunned for a moment, then laughed.

"Why would I hate them?" he asked,
"You bring new technology, new ideas, new possibilities. Although you also bring chaos and destruction, but... that's the cost of progress, I suppose."

He paused, looking into the distance.

"Besides, some descenders have become my friends. Real, life-and-death friends."

Chen Mo didn't speak.

He remembered the look in G-892's eyes before he died, remembered the touch of that warm blood splashing on his face.

He didn't know if he could become the kind of friend Lao Ba spoke of.

But he wanted to try.

They arrived before an ordinary-looking residence.

It was a two-story small building, the exterior wall painted off-white, but the paint had peeled off, revealing the brickwork beneath. Light blue curtains hung on the windows, fluttering gently in the wind. Some plants Chen Mo didn't recognize were planted at the door, their leaves displaying a deep green color, shining in the morning light.

Lao Ba knocked on the door.

Three long, two short.

Chen Mo could hear footsteps inside—light, quick, not as heavy as Lao Ba's. Then, the door opened a crack, and an eye peeked out from inside.

It was a red eye.

Not pink, not light red, but a... deep, blood-like red. The pupil was black, standing out especially strikingly in the red iris.

"Lao Ba?" A voice came from the crack of the door, crisp and slightly hoarse. "So early?"

"Brought a newcomer," Lao Ba said, "first descent, needs a mentor."

The door opened.

Standing behind the door was a... rabbit?

Chen Mo was stunned.

It was a white rabbit beastman, small and thin, completely different from all those muscle-bound beastmen. Chen Mo could see every detail of him—that pure white fur, displaying a soft sheen in the morning light. Those long ears, one with a small notch at the tip. That pink nose, twitching slightly, sniffing the scent in the air.

But what shocked Chen Mo most was those eyes.

Red. Deep. Blood-like red.

That was not a common color for beastmen. Chen Mo had seen Lao Ba's yellow-brown eyes, the wolf's gray eyes, the wild boar's brown eyes. But red... what did red mean?

"Newcomer?" The rabbit sized up Chen Mo, the corners of his mouth turning up slightly.

Chen Mo could feel the weight of that gaze—scrutinizing, assessing, but different from Lao Ba's, different from the wolf's too. There was no exhaustion in that gaze, no indifference, only a... focused observation.

"Looks pretty sturdy," the rabbit said, his voice crisp and slightly hoarse, like a timbre Chen Mo had never heard before. "Just the eyes are a bit dull."

"He saw blood for the first time last night," Lao Ba said, "vomited all over the ground."

"Normal."
The rabbit shrugged indifferently. That movement made the white apron on his body sway gently—Chen Mo only then noticed he was wearing a white apron, forming a strange contrast with this beastman's body.
"My first time, I was so scared I pissed myself."

He turned to Chen Mo, extending a claw.

It was a claw completely different from Chen Mo's hand—smaller, softer, covered with fine white down. But Chen Mo could feel that beneath the soft exterior, there was... strength. A restrained, carefully controlled strength.

"Welcome aboard, newcomer," the rabbit said, red eyes flashing with a strange light in the sunlight. "My name is Bai Ya, I'm your mentor."

Chen Mo hesitantly grasped that claw.

The touch was warm. Soft. But he could feel the pad in the palm, similar to Lao Ba's, similar to his own, yet different—more delicate, more sensitive, like... some more precise instrument.

"My name is..." Chen Mo paused.

He remembered his original name, that name in the human world. That name belonged to a programmer, an office worker, an ordinary identity that had no meaning in this world.

Then, he smiled.

"Call me Little Bear," he said, "I still don't know what my body's name is."

"Little Bear?" Bai Ya raised an eyebrow, that movement making his long ears twitch gently.
"Alright, Little Bear. From today on, I'll teach you to survive in this world. Not the skills of how to kill, but how to... maintain your humanity."

"Humanity?"

"Mm." Bai Ya's expression became serious, a flash of emotion Chen Mo couldn't interpret passing through those red eyes.
"This world will change you. Will make you become cold, cruel, become... not like yourself. What I want to teach you is how to not lose that word 'human' while becoming stronger."

Chen Mo looked at this white rabbit, suddenly feeling a strange... hope?

Perhaps, he really could find his place in this world.

Not as a bystander, not as a player, but as... a real, living being.

"Okay," Chen Mo said, gripping Bai Ya's hand tightly. "I'll learn from you."

Bai Ya smiled, revealing a pair of cute rabbit teeth.

"Then the first lesson," he said, "learn to eat in this world. Not using your world's money to buy supplies, but really going to find food."

He led Chen Mo toward the inside of the house.

Chen Mo followed behind, feeling every movement of this body—the contraction and relaxation of muscles, the slight jiggle of fat, the touch of footpads contacting the floor. He could smell the scents coming from inside the house—the fragrance of herbs, the aroma of food, and... the smell on Bai Ya's body.

It was a clean smell. Not the kind of accumulated years like Lao Ba, but a... deliberate, washed smell. Chen Mo could smell the fragrance of soap, could smell the bitterness of some herb, and... a very faint, smell like old books.

Outside the window, the sun had completely risen, illuminating this world filled with blood, violence, but also vitality and hope.

Chen Mo's first game had just begun.

But he hopes. Hopes Chen Mo can persist. Hopes he can become the kind of person... who won't turn into a beast.

Because if even he changes, then this world really has no hope left.

【Living→Alive-Roamer-Bai Ya】

Bai Ya is still alive. But within this rabbit body, what it carries is a soul that has already died once.

He is a roamer. Existing between descender and native. Unable to return to the real world, also unable to truly die like a native.

He has borrowed this body for many years. So long that he has forgotten what he originally looked like. So long that this rabbit body feels more "real" than his own original body.

He has seen too many students. Hui Ying, that lost genius. And others, those... failures who lost their humanity on the road to becoming stronger.

Chen Mo reminded him of Hui Ying. But not that kind of potential to be lost, but rather some... different thing. A slower, clumsier, but also more real... growth.

When he said "maintain humanity," he knew it sounded very hollow. In this world, maintaining humanity means weakness, means being easily killed, means... pain.

But he still said it. Because he must believe. Believe that in this world, there is another way to survive. Believe that not all descenders will turn into beasts. Believe... that everything he does has meaning.

He saved G-881, that young wild boar beastman. Spent 50 Lu coins, just to teach Chen Mo a lesson. That was extravagant, unnecessary, possibly even risky.

But he did it. Because that was the only thing he could do—in the darkness, light a small lamp.

He doesn't know how far Chen Mo can go. Doesn't know if this clumsy brown bear can understand what he teaches.

But he is willing to try. Willing to teach once more. Willing to believe once more.

Because if even he doesn't believe, then this world really only has darkness left.

### Story Reflections

When I wrote these words, I was trying to understand a fundamental question: **When a world is just a "game" to you, but "everything" to others, how do you establish real connections with them?**

Chen Mo's confusion is my confusion. His shock is my shock. When he knelt by the roadside vomiting, I felt that real physiological reaction—that wasn't "plot setting," that was life's instinctive reaction to death.

G-892 reminds me of all those people forgotten by history. Those nameless ones, replaced by designations, treated as consumables. He might have been a bad person (a rapist), or he might have just been an ordinary person misunderstood. We will never know the truth. But that is precisely the cruelty of this world—in this world, the boundary between good and evil is blurred, and the arrival of death is indiscriminate.

Lao Ba's loneliness is another form of tragedy. He is an "NPC," but he has memories, emotions, longings. He longs to establish connections with "players," not for any practical purpose, just to confirm that he himself is also real. This loneliness, in some ways, is more terrible than death.

And Bai Ya... Bai Ya represents a kind of hope. A hope that still insists on education in despair. He knows most people will fail (like Hui Ying), but he still continues. Because if he doesn't continue, it means admitting that there is no possibility of redemption in this world.

This is a story about **descent**.
Not just Chen Mo descending into this world, but also this world descending into Chen Mo's soul. When he first grasped Bai Ya's hand, when he first felt that real temperature, two worlds began to merge—not physically, but spiritually.

I don't know where this story will go. But I hope that in this world filled with blood and violence, there can still be some... warm things.

Some things worth fighting for.

Some things that let us maintain our humanity.
