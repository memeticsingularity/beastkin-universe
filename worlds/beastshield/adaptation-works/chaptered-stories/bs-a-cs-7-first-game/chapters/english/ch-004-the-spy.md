**Scene-1 Morning Light in the Data Collection Room**

Chen Mo had been working in Beastshield's underground laboratory for seven days.

Every morning at six, he woke up on time. The dormitory bed was a simple iron frame bed, the mattress so thin he could almost feel the shape of the iron bars beneath. When he turned over to get up, the bed frame emitted an irritating creak—that sound echoed in the silent corridor, like some ancient, tired sigh.

He put on that set of gray work clothes. That was synthetic fiber texture, rough, non-breathable, but durable enough. The fabric rubbed against the fur on his back, bringing a dry itch. Chen Mo scratched his back, feeling the touch of nails scraping through fur—those dark brown hairs were longer and denser than when he first descended, emitting a scent belonging to himself, a mixture of sweat and fiber.

He walked toward the wash area. That was a communal space, over a dozen beastmen sharing a few rows of faucets. Chen Mo turned on the faucet, ice-cold water flow washing his bear paws. He could feel the bone-piercing water temperature, could feel the pressure of water flow impacting his palms, could feel those tiny water droplets flowing, gathering, dripping between the fur.

He raised his head, looking at himself in the mirror.

That was a brown bear beastman—dark brown fur covering a thick face, eyes deep brown, carrying a fatigue he had never seen before. His beard was longer than seven days ago, presenting a rough texture. His build was still burly, but that fat-over-muscle contour appeared more... heavy under the wrapping of gray work clothes.

He washed his face, feeling water droplets flowing down the fur on his cheeks, gathering at his chin, dripping. Then he walked toward the data collection room, beginning a new day.

The corridor was very long, the lighting that kind of stark white fluorescent light, emitting a slight buzzing sound. Chen Mo's footsteps echoed in the empty corridor—dull, rhythmically stable footsteps, from the contact between his thick footpads and the cement ground.

The data collection room was a long, narrow room, various instruments arranged on the walls, emitting regular beeping sounds. Chen Mo's workstation was in the corner, a metal desk, a plastic chair, and a pile of record books and electronic devices.

He sat down, the chair emitting a groan. His thighs were too thick, forcing him to spread to the sides, occupying more space than normal. His abdomen piled up above his waistband, forming a soft fold.

He picked up the first record book, opening it.

That was a thick, rough-paper-made book. The edges of the paper were somewhat worn, the pages covered with various handwriting—some his own, some from previous record keepers. Chen Mo's fingers brushed over those characters, could feel the slight凸起 formed after ink permeated the paper.

"G-1172, Experiment Day 43."

This was the first subject he had to record today.

He looked toward the other end of the room, through a glass window, could see the laboratory next door. There a wolf beastman was fixed on an experimental platform, body connected with various electrodes and tubes. That wolf beastman was very young, looking not even twenty years old, build lean, gray fur sparse and dull.

His eyes were vacant, as if already drained of all hope.

Chen Mo picked up the pen, beginning to write data in the record book. His bear paw clumsily gripped that slender pen, pen tip scratching across the paper, emitting rustling sounds.

"Time: 06:30. Body temperature: 38.2 degrees. Blood pressure: 140/90. Brain waves: abnormal fluctuations. Spiritual power index: 85%. Status: stable, no awakening signs. Suggestion: continue observation."

He wrote these numbers, hand very steady, as if he was just an ordinary data recorder.

But his heart was trembling.

Because he could see that wolf beastman—G-1172—his eyes. That vacancy, that numbness, that... giving up on life. Chen Mo recorded his data every day, watching him gradually change from a responsive individual into a walking corpse.

This was Beastshield's "research."

They weren't looking for some scientific truth; they were—extracting. Extracting spiritual power, extracting consciousness, extracting life itself.

"Little Bear, come here for a moment."

A voice came from the door. Chen Mo raised his head, saw a fox beastman in a white coat standing there.

That was Hu Lin, his direct superior.

Hu Lin's build was smaller than Chen Mo's, but had a... lean temperament. His fur was orange-red, neatly combed, forming a sharp contrast with his white coat. His eyes were amber, carrying a depth Chen Mo couldn't interpret.

"Yes, sir." Chen Mo put down the pen, stood up, following Hu Lin out of the data collection room.

His footsteps echoed in the corridor, contrasting with Hu Lin's light steps. Hu Lin walked almost without sound, like some feline, while Chen Mo's every step was as heavy as walking on stilts.

They passed through several corridors, arriving at a room Chen Mo had never entered.

Three words were written on the door: "Awakening Room."

**Scene-2 The Limits of the Awakening Room**

The light in the room was very dim, only a chair in the center was illuminated by a spotlight.

That chair was metal-made, surface with various restraint devices—wrists, ankles, waist, head. A young bear beastman was tied to the chair, the same race as Chen Mo, but smaller in build, looking like he had just reached adulthood.

That bear beastman's fur was light brown, still carrying a youth's characteristic soft sheen. His body was trembling, chest and abdominal fat layer slightly trembling from fear. His eyes were deep brown, currently filled with fear and confusion.

"This is..." Chen Mo's voice tightened, his throat as if blocked by something.

"New experimental subject," Hu Lin said lightly, pushing up the glasses on his nose bridge. That was a pair of gold-rimmed glasses, appearing especially突兀 in this world. "We need to test his spiritual power potential. You're responsible for recording his reactions."

Chen Mo approached that bear beastman. He could smell the scent on the other party—young, carrying a soap fragrance, and... the sourness of fear. That sourness seeped from skin pores, mixed with sweat, forming an unsettling scent.

The bear beastman raised his head, looking at Chen Mo. Their gazes met; Chen Mo saw what was in those eyes—that longing for life, fear of the future, ignorance of this world.

"I... where am I?" The bear beastman asked, voice trembling, carrying a youth's voice-changing-period characteristic hoarseness. "What is this? I shouldn't be here... I just... just logged into the game..."

Chen Mo's heart suddenly sank.

This was a descender. A new player, just like himself seven days ago.

"Recording begins," Hu Lin said, picking up a strange device, aiming it at the bear beastman's head. That device looked like a helmet, covered with electrodes and glowing crystals. "First stimulation, intensity 10%."

The device emitted a buzzing sound, like a swarm of angry bees. The bear beastman let out a scream, body violently convulsing. Chen Mo could see his chest and abdominal muscles spasming, could see his finger claws extending from pain, could see tears gushing from his eyes.

"Spiritual fluctuations... weak," Hu Lin looked at the readings on the instrument, his tone like reading a weather forecast. "Continue, intensity 20%."

"Wait!" Chen Mo couldn't help saying, his voice louder than he had imagined. "He... he's just a newcomer, this is too..."

"Too what?" Hu Lin turned his head, eyes icy. Those amber eyes in the dim light presented an unsettling sheen. "Too cruel?"

He smiled, that smile making Chen Mo remember the wolf bounty hunter from Chapter 1—equally icy, equally matter-of-fact. The corners of Hu Lin's mouth turned up, revealing a mouthful of neat but yellowed teeth, canines sharper and longer than a human's.

"Little Bear," Hu Lin said, "Do you know why we do this?"

Chen Mo shook his head, clenching his fists. He could feel his nails embedding into the pads of his palms, bringing a sharp sting.

"Because only through extreme stimulation can spiritual power awakening be triggered," Hu Lin said, his tone like explaining a math problem. "Every descender has potential, but most people go their whole lives without awakening. We're helping them."

"Helping them?" Chen Mo's voice somewhat trembled.

"Of course," Hu Lin nodded, his ears lightly twitching when he spoke. "Awakening spiritual power means having true power in this world. We are giving them a gift."

He turned back to the bear beastman, increasing the device's power. Chen Mo could hear the buzzing of electric current becoming sharper, could see the bear beastman's body violently trembling under electric stimulation, could see white foam overflowing from the corners of his mouth.

"Intensity 30%."

The bear beastman's screams echoed in the room, like a knife, cutting Chen Mo's heart. That sound penetrated Chen Mo's eardrums, echoing in his mind, making him remember G-892's dying wail.

Chen Mo lowered his head, writing in the record book:

"Experimental subject G-1173, violent spiritual fluctuations, no awakening signs detected, suggestion..."

He paused.

"Suggestion: continue stimulation."

He wrote these four words, hand very steady, but tears nearly fell.

Because he knew, if he didn't write, Hu Lin would suspect him. If Hu Lin suspected him, he couldn't complete the mission, couldn't rescue these people.

So he wrote. Dancing with monsters, first you must learn—pretending to be a monster.

**Scene-3 Fear from Another Perspective**

Meanwhile, on another floor of the underground laboratory, Xiao Hui was trembling at his workstation.

He was a rat beastman, small in build, gray fur sparse and dull. His eyes were black, very small, always carrying a frightened look. His nose was pink, constantly twitching, sniffing every trace of danger in the air.

He had been working at Beastshield for three months—or rather, forced to work here for three months.

Xiao Hui was originally a descender, from the world "up there." Five years ago, he logged into this game, thinking it was just an ordinary VR experience. Then he owed Beastshield money, a lot of money—because he wanted to buy a better set of equipment, and was scammed.

Now, he worked here, recording those terrifying experimental data every day, having nightmares every night.

His workstation was in a corner, near the ventilation opening, so always a bit cold. He wore a set of gray work clothes, two sizes larger than his original size, looking loose and baggy on him. His fingers were slender, clumsily operating a data terminal.

The screen displayed today's experimental records: G-1172's spiritual power data, G-1173's stimulation response, and... a pile of cruel numbers he didn't want to look at.

Xiao Hui's stomach was twitching. He had grown accustomed to this feeling—the fear upon waking every morning, the nausea upon entering the laboratory every day, the despair before sleeping every night.

But he couldn't resign. Because he owed Beastshield too much money; if he showed any sign of disloyalty, he would become one of those "experimental subjects."

He remembered yesterday in the cafeteria encountering that newcomer—Little Bear, a brown bear beastman. That person's eyes had something, something different from other Beastshield employees. That was a... unease? A humanity not yet worn smooth by this world?

Xiao Hui wasn't sure. But he felt some... resonance.

"Xiao Hui, is G-1173's data organized?"

A voice came from behind. Xiao Hui was so startled he nearly jumped up, tail trembling violently behind him.

"D... done, sir," he stammered, fingers randomly tapping on the keyboard. "I'll send it to you right away."

"Hurry up," that was a blue-uniformed overseer, looking at him impatiently. "Mr. Hu Lin wants this data today."

"Yes... yes..."

After the overseer left, Xiao Hui let out a long breath. His heart was still pounding wildly, as if about to burst out of his chest.

He looked at the data on the screen, looked at those numbers representing lives. G-1173, that young bear beastman, was currently suffering torture in the "Awakening Room." And all he could do was record this data, organize it into a report, then... turn a blind eye.

His fingers stopped on the keyboard, trembling.

He remembered when he first arrived here, still trying to help those experimental subjects. Secretly bringing a piece of bread to G-1172, passing a message for another experimental subject. But later he was discovered, warned, and... punished.

Now, he learned silence. Learned to turn a blind eye. Learned to be... an accomplice in this hell.

But this silence had a price. Every night lying in bed, he could hear those screams echoing in his mind. Those he had recorded, those he had ignored, those he had... betrayed.

Xiao Hui lowered his head, looking at his own hands—those slender, gray claws. This pair of hands, once used for typing, writing, creating. Now, they were used to record death.

He hated this pair of hands. But what he hated more was... himself.

Because he knew, if it happened again, he would still choose silence. Because he was afraid, because he was cowardly, because... he wanted to survive.

This self-loathing eroded him every day. Making him thinner and thinner, paler and paler, more and more... unlike a living life.

But he was still alive. In some way.

In the cafeteria, when he looked at that brown bear Little Bear, he felt some... hope? That hope was very faint, like a match swaying in the wind. But it existed.

Perhaps, just perhaps, that person would make a different choice. Perhaps he would resist, would escape, would... change something.

Xiao Hui didn't know. He only knew, when he looked into Little Bear's eyes, he saw a light he thought had disappeared.

That newborn light that hadn't yet learned to be cold.

**Scene-4 Late Night Training**

At ten at night, Chen Mo finally finished work, returning to his dormitory.

That was a very small room, only a bed, a table, and a chair. The walls were gray cement, emitting a damp scent. But for Chen Mo, this was the only sanctuary.

He closed the door, locked it, then sat on the bed, closing his eyes.

His body was protesting—back soreness, leg stiffness, and that spiritual exhaustion. His work clothes were soaked with sweat, clinging tightly to his fur, bringing a sticky discomfort.

"Bai Ya," he called with spiritual power, "Are you there?"

"Here." Bai Ya's voice rang in his mind, carrying a trace of fatigue, as if coming from very far away. "How was today?"

"Very bad," Chen Mo said, he opened his eyes, looking at the cracks on the ceiling. Those cracks were like a net, covering the entire room. "They caught a new descender, subjected him to extreme stimulation. He... he was in great pain."

"You can't save him," Bai Ya said, "At least not now."

"I know," Chen Mo clenched his fists, could feel the pads of his palms deforming from pressure. "So I need to become stronger. You promised me, teach me offensive spiritual power techniques."

Bai Ya was silent for a while.

"Alright," he said, "But you have to be mentally prepared. Offensive spiritual power is different from control-type. It will change you."

"I've already changed," Chen Mo said, looking at his bear paws—these hands wrote "suggestion: continue stimulation" today, "I can't turn back."

"Then let's begin."

Bai Ya's voice became serious.

"First, recall the feeling when you awakened. That light point, that core of consciousness."

Chen Mo did as he said. He closed his eyes, finding that light point in the darkness—now it was brighter than before, like a tiny star, flickering in the depths of his consciousness.

"Now," Bai Ya said, "Don't just perceive it, but—push it."

"Push?"

"Imagine your spiritual power is a knife, that light point is the handle. What you have to do is stab this knife out."

Chen Mo imagined.

He imagined his consciousness becoming a sharp knife, aimed at that chair in the room—that wooden, worn chair that creaked when sat on.

"Stab!"

An invisible force erupted from him. Chen Mo could feel that force surging from his chest, passing through his arms, then—

The chair trembled slightly, but didn't move.

"Not concentrated enough," Bai Ya said, "Your spiritual power is too scattered. Like a real knife, concentrate all power at one point."

Chen Mo took a deep breath, trying again.

This time, what he imagined wasn't a knife, but a needle. A needle extremely fine, extremely sharp. He concentrated all his spiritual power at the tip of this needle, aimed at one leg of the chair—

"Stab!"

"Crack."

A slight sound. A small crack appeared on that leg of the chair.

"Very good!" Bai Ya's voice carried praise, "This is Spiritual Piercing. The most basic offensive technique."

Chen Mo opened his eyes, looking at that crack, a strange feeling welling up in his heart.

Not joy, but rather... fear.

He had just, truly used consciousness to harm an object. If that was a chair, then what if it was a person?

"Continue practicing," Bai Ya said, "But don't overdo it. Spiritual power consumption is more dangerous than physical exhaustion."

"I know," Chen Mo said, "And... Bai Ya, I discovered something."

"What?"

"Hu Lin, my superior," Chen Mo said, "He is also a descender."

Bai Ya fell silent.

"You're sure?"

"Sure," Chen Mo said, "Today when he used that stimulation device, I perceived his spiritual fluctuations. That fluctuation... different from natives. More... chaotic, more like our own."

"A descender," Bai Ya's voice became icy, "Yet helping Beastshield torture other descenders."

"Yes," Chen Mo said, "And he seems to have been in this world for a very long time. His spiritual power... is very strong."

"Stronger than me?"

"I'm not sure," Chen Mo said, "But he's definitely much stronger than me."

Bai Ya was silent for a very long time.

"Be careful of him," he finally said, "This kind of person is the most dangerous. They have completely adapted to this world's rules, even begun to enjoy them."

"I will," Chen Mo said, "And... I might need more time. I still haven't found the location of the core data."

"No rush," Bai Ya said, "Safety first. If you're discovered..."

"I know," Chen Mo interrupted him, "I won't let that happen."

He lay on the bed, looking at the ceiling.

Seven days. Every day he collected information, every day he learned, every day he—pretended.

Pretending to be a loyal Beastshield employee, pretending those pains had nothing to do with him, pretending he hadn't seen those desperate eyes.

But this pretending was eroding him.

He began to grow accustomed to those screams, began to be able to write cruel data with a straight face, began to see those experimental subjects' faces in dreams—then wake with a start, covered in cold sweat.

What was he becoming?

**Scene-5 Encounter in the Cafeteria**

On the eighth day, Chen Mo encountered an unexpected person.

Five years.

Chen Mo remembered Bai Ya. Bai Ya had also said similar things—his body died, consciousness trapped here.

Chen Mo's heartbeat accelerated. He could feel his heart pounding wildly in his chest, blood surging in his vessels.

"Because..." Xiao Hui's voice trembled, "Because I've had enough. Because I want... want to do the right thing, even if just once."

He raised his head, looking at Chen Mo, eyes carrying something Chen Mo was very familiar with—that was what he had seen in the mirror.

"You're not an ordinary person, right?" Xiao Hui said, "I can feel... you're different from others."

Chen Mo didn't deny it.

"Where is the core data room?" he asked.

"Underground seventh floor," Xiao Hui said, "The deepest place. But to enter, you need three high-levels' biological features—fingerprint, retina, and spiritual power signature."

"Is that so?" Hu Lin smiled, that smile not reaching his eyes. "Then you continue. But remember—"

He lowered his voice, close enough for Chen Mo to smell some... medicinal scent in his breath. "Here, some topics are not suitable for discussion in the cafeteria."

But his words, like a knife, hung over Chen Mo's head.

**Scene-6 Hu Lin's Test**

The next day, Chen Mo was called to Hu Lin's office.

"Research spiritual power and consciousness transfer, sir."

"Yes," Hu Lin leaned back in his chair, his white coat slightly opening with the movement, revealing the exquisite shirt inside. "But do you know why?"

Chen Mo shook his head.

"For immortality," Hu Lin said, his tone as flat as discussing the weather. "The human body is too fragile, it ages, it dies. But if consciousness can be transferred into a body that never ages..."

He looked at Chen Mo, eyes carrying a fanaticism. That fanaticism made his amber eyes flash with a sickly light.

"Imagine, Little Bear. Forever young, forever strong, forever alive. This is not a dream, this is a reality about to be realized."

Chen Mo was silent.

He remembered Bai Ya. Bai Ya had said similar things—consciousness transfer, borrowing bodies. But Bai Ya used it to survive, while Hu Lin... used it to pursue eternity.

"You're also a descender, right?" Hu Lin suddenly asked.

Chen Mo's heartbeat missed a beat.

"I..."

That young wolf beastman. That youth with vacant eyes, who had already lost all hope.

But his footsteps were very heavy, as if every step was stepping on a knife tip.

He sat on the bed, looking at that document, looking at that experimental subject about to be "stripped"—G-1172.

**Scene-8 The Price of Rescue**

The next morning at seven, Chen Mo arrived at the laboratory early.

G-1172 was tied to the experimental platform, body connected with various electrodes and tubes. His eyes were vacant, as if already accepting his fate. Chen Mo walked to his side, softly saying: "Don't be afraid, I've come to save you."

The wolf beastman's eyes flashed, but quickly dimmed again. He didn't believe. In this place, no one would save him.

Chen Mo began to act.

He used spiritual power to perceive the surroundings—no one else, only the surveillance camera at the end of the corridor. Xiao Hui had promised, would cut the power to that area at seven-thirty, giving him five minutes.

Five minutes. Save a person, escape from Beastshield.

"Xiao Hui," he called with spiritual power, "Ready?"

"Ready," Xiao Hui's voice trembled, "Three, two, one—"

The lights flickered, then went out. The entire area sank into darkness.

Chen Mo immediately acted. He used spiritual power to condense into a needle, stabbing toward the restraint belt binding G-1172. The restraint belt broke, emitting a slight tearing sound. The wolf beastman gained freedom.

"Follow me," Chen Mo pulled him up, could feel the thinness and trembling of the other party's arm, "Quick!"

They ran in the darkness. Chen Mo's spiritual perception fully opened, able to "see" everything around—walls, doors, and... approaching threats.

"Someone's coming," Chen Mo said, "Three people, from the left corridor."

He pulled G-1172 turning right, passing through a maintenance channel Chen Mo had previously scouted. The channel was very narrow, very dark, emitting the smell of machine oil and rust.

"You... why save me?" G-1172 asked, voice weak.

"Because I can't watch you die," Chen Mo said, "Because I'm also a descender."

They ran in the channel for who knows how long, finally saw light—that was the exit leading to the surface.

But before the exit stood a person.

Hu Lin.

"I knew it," Hu Lin said, face carrying a regretful expression. His white coat was especially glaring in the morning light. "You have that smell... the smell of a rebel."

Chen Mo stopped, shielding G-1172 behind him.

"Step aside," he said, "I don't want to hurt you."

Hu Lin smiled.

"Hurt me?" he said, "Little Bear, you think that bit of spiritual power can threaten me?"

He extended his hand, a powerful spiritual pressure surged toward Chen Mo.

Chen Mo felt his consciousness as if grabbed by an invisible giant hand, compressing, squeezing, almost about to shatter. His head violently ached, as if countless needles were piercing into his brain.

"I've been here for twelve years," Hu Lin said, "My spiritual power is not something you newcomers can imagine."

Chen Mo knelt down, bleeding from all seven orifices. He could feel warm liquid flowing from his nose, ears, eyes, dripping onto the ground.

But he didn't give up.

He remembered everything Bai Ya taught him. Remembered that light point, remembered Spiritual Piercing, remembered—

"Xiao Hui!" he shouted with his last strength, "Now!"

A figure rushed out from the shadows of the channel, holding a scalpel in hand—it was Xiao Hui.

Hu Lin was unprepared. His attention was fully concentrated on Chen Mo, completely not noticing Xiao Hui's existence.

The scalpel stabbed into Hu Lin's back.

"Ah!" Hu Lin let out a scream, spiritual pressure instantly disappearing.

Chen Mo seized this opportunity, pulling up G-1172, rushing past Hu Lin, running toward the exit.

"You... can't escape..." Hu Lin knelt on the ground, voice weak, "Beastshield... will find you..."

"Maybe," Chen Mo looked back at him, "But at least, today, you lost."

They rushed out of the exit, arriving at the surface. Sunlight shone on them, like some sacred baptism.

Chen Mo gasped for breath, looking at the sky. That was the first time in seven days he saw sunlight—warm, bright, full of... hope.

He did it. He really did it.

---

**Scene-9 The Weight of Loss**

They shuttled through the city, avoiding Beastshield's pursuit.

Chen Mo's spiritual power was almost exhausted, his head splitting with pain, every step like stepping on cotton. But he couldn't stop, couldn't let G-1172 fall into Beastshield's hands again.

"Ahead... turn left..." he guided with a weak voice, "There's a... safe house..."

That was the location Bai Ya told him, a secret stronghold of the resistance.

They finally arrived. It was a grocery store that looked very ordinary, but when Chen Mo knocked out a specific code, the door opened, an old cat beastman let them in.

"Bai Ya has already notified us," the old cat said, "You're safe."

Chen Mo let out a breath, almost collapsing to the ground.

But at this moment, he discovered something wrong.

"Where's Xiao Hui?" he asked.

G-1172 was stunned. They looked back, toward the door—Xiao Hui hadn't followed.

"He... he was in the channel," Chen Mo recalled the last scene, "He stabbed Hu Lin... then..."

Then he was gone.

"He was caught," the old cat said, voice heavy, "Beastshield's people found him before us."

Chen Mo felt a wave of dizziness.

No, no, no...

Xiao Hui saved him. Xiao Hui risked his life, helped him cut the power, helped him divert attention, finally... also stabbed Hu Lin.

And now, he was caught.

"I want to go back and save him," Chen Mo said, struggling to stand up.

"You can't," the old cat blocked him, "If you go back now, it's suicide. And..."

He paused.

"And what?"

"And Xiao Hui..." the old cat's eyes carried sadness, "He might already be dead. Beastshield's punishment for traitors... is very cruel."

Chen Mo knelt down.

He rescued G-1172, but lost Xiao Hui.

Was this victory? Or failure?

He remembered what Xiao Hui said in the cafeteria: "I want... want to do the right thing, even if just once."

He did it. He did the right thing, even if just once.

And the price was his life.

Chen Mo lowered his head, tears finally flowing down. Those warm liquids slid down his cheeks, passing through his fur, dripping onto the ground.

He didn't know who he was crying for—for Xiao Hui, for G-1172, for himself, or for this cruel world.

He only knew, from now on, whenever he remembered that small rat beastman, remembered his拘谨 smile, remembered him saying "I know where the core data room is" with nervousness—

He would remember, someone paid the price of their life for him.

And this price, was something he could never repay.

## Story Commentary and Reflections

> *The following is DeepSeek's commentary on this story, attempting to understand the inner world of each character.*

### Their Final Stories

【Living→Dead-Data Clerk-Xiao Hui-Rat Beastman】

His real name was no longer important. In Beastshield's system, he was "Data Clerk-Gray," a low-level employee responsible for recording experimental data. But at the last moment of life, he became some kind of more... real existence.

Xiao Hui was a small rat beastman, gray fur sparse and dull, always carrying a frightened look. His work clothes were two sizes too big, looking loose and baggy on him, unable to cover his thin body. His fingers were slender, once used for typing, writing, creating, later used to record death.

He worked in this underground laboratory for five years. For five years, every day he witnessed hell, every day he had nightmares, every day he... betrayed his own conscience.

He owed Beastshield money, a lot of money. So he couldn't resign, couldn't resist, couldn't even show any sign of disloyalty. He could only be silent, could only turn a blind eye, could only be... an accomplice in this system.

But this silence had a price. Eroding him every day, making him thinner and thinner, paler and paler, more and more... unlike a living life.

Until he met Chen Mo.

That brown bear's eyes had something, something he thought had disappeared... humanity. That unease, that weakness not yet learned to be cold, that... desire to do the right thing.

That conversation in the cafeteria changed his fate.

"If... someone wanted to destroy this place, would you help?"

He spoke that dangerous secret: "I know where the core data room is."

Then he regretted, was afraid, wanted to take it back. But when Hu Lin appeared, when that dangerous aura enveloped them, he realized—there was no turning back.

So he chose to continue. Chose to help Chen Mo cut the power, chose in that dark channel, to use a scalpel to stab at Hu Lin.

That stab, used all his courage. That was the first time in five years he did the "right thing."

Then he was caught.

We don't know what his final moments were like. Don't know if he suffered torture, if he maintained dignity, if at the last moment of life, he still believed his choice was right.

But we know, at that instant, when he charged at Hu Lin, he was no longer that poor wretch who owed Beastshield money and was forced to work.

He became a true—person.

He used his life, proving that even in the darkest places, there are people willing to do the right thing.

Even if just once.

---

### Those Still Alive

【Living→Alive-Player-Chen Mo (Little Bear)】

Chen Mo is still alive, but he is no longer the infiltrator from seven days ago.

He rescued G-1172, but he lost Xiao Hui. This interweaving of victory's joy and failure's pain made him feel an unprecedented... weight.

He drank that bottle of spiritual power enhancer, felt that scorching power flowing in his body. His spiritual power greatly awakened, perception range expanded, able to understand this world's... essence.

But this power had a price. Every awakening accompanied loss. G-892, Xiao Hui, and those lives he couldn't save.

He began to understand what Bai Ya said: "Saving one person often means losing another."

But he didn't accept it. He didn't accept this cruelty. He wanted to become stronger, strong enough to not need to make this kind of choice.

Was this possible? He didn't know. But he was willing to fight for it.

For Xiao Hui. For those who couldn't be saved. For... maintaining humanity.

【Living→Alive-Experimental Subject-G-1172-Wolf Beastman】

G-1172 is still alive. But he is no longer that vacant-eyed experimental subject.

He was rescued by Chen Mo, brought to the safe house. His body was weak, spirit exhausted, needing time to recover. But in his eyes, there was... light again.

That longing for life, hope for the future, gratitude for freedom.

He didn't know who Chen Mo was, didn't know why he wanted to save him, didn't know what the future would be like.

But he knew, from that moment on, his life no longer belonged only to himself.

It belonged to that person who risked danger for him. Belonged to that person who lost a friend for him.

Belonged to that person who proved... even in hell, there is redemption.

【Living→Alive-Roamer-Bai Ya】

Bai Ya is still alive, waiting for Chen Mo in the safe house.

When he heard the news of Xiao Hui being caught, his heart sank. Another one. Another person who sacrificed for justice.

He remembered his mentor, that gray cat beastman also called "Xiao Hui." Was history repeating? Or was this just his overly pessimistic imagination?

But when he saw Chen Mo's appearance—spiritual power overdrawn, covered in blood, eyes vacant—he saw some... change.

That clumsy newcomer was becoming a true warrior. Not for killing, but for saving.

He gave Chen Mo that bottle of spiritual power enhancer, knowing it was dangerous, but knowing it was necessary.

Because what they had to face was Beastshield this huge machine. They needed power, needed speed, needed... everything.

"Welcome to true power, Chen Mo."

This sentence was both for Chen Mo and for himself.

Because they both knew, from now on, the war truly began.

【Living→Alive-Descender-Hu Lin】

Hu Lin is still alive, but severely injured.

Xiao Hui's knife stabbed into his back, although not hitting a vital point, but enough to make him bedridden for several weeks. More importantly, his pride was shattered.

He, an old player who had been in this world for twelve years, was actually defeated by two newcomers—a clumsy brown bear, a timid mouse.

This humiliation was harder to endure than physical pain.

"Beastshield will find you..." that sentence he said kneeling in the channel was not a threat, but... a promise.

He wouldn't let Chen Mo go. Wouldn't let Xiao Hui go (if he was still alive). Wouldn't let any rebel go.

Because he had already chosen. Chose to become part of this new world, chose to pursue immortality, chose... to become a monster.

And now, someone was trying to challenge his choice.

This was absolutely intolerable to him.

### Story Reflections

This chapter pains me. Because it raises an unanswerable question: **To save one person, are you willing to lose another?**

Chen Mo chose to save G-1172. This choice led to Xiao Hui being caught (possibly dead). From the result, this was a "cost-effective" transaction—saved one person, lost another person.

But this "cost-effective" is cruel. Because Xiao Hui is not a number, he is a life. He has his own fears, his own weaknesses, his own... courage.

At the last moment, he chose to do the right thing. He used his life, proving that even in the darkest places, there are people willing to light a lamp.

But the price of this proof was his life.

This makes me think of those nameless sacrificed people in history. Those who gave up their lives for the greater good, for a better future.

Their names won't be remembered, their stories won't be told, their... existences will be erased from history.

But their sacrifices shaped the future.

Xiao Hui's sacrifice shaped Chen Mo. Let him understand, some battles must have someone stand up. Let him resolve to become stronger, strong enough to not need to make this kind of choice again.

This pain of growth is real. It's not like in games where it can be reset, archived, loaded. It's permanent, irreversible, and... the essence of life.

Hu Lin represents another choice. Adapting to this cruel world, becoming part of it, even enjoying it. He believes this is the "smart" choice, the "realistic" choice.

But Xiao Hui proved, there is another possibility. Even at the most desperate moment, even in the darkest place, there are people who choose—humanity.

I don't know which choice is right. Perhaps in this world, Hu Lin's choice is more "realistic," more able to ensure survival.

But I hope, choices like Xiao Hui's won't disappear. Because it is precisely these choices that define our essence as "people."

This chapter also lets me understand Chen Mo's growth more deeply. He is no longer that curious student who wanted to "understand this world." He became a warrior, a person willing to take risks for others... a hero.

But the price he paid was heavy. Xiao Hui's death (or capture) will become a permanent scar in his heart. This scar will drive him to become stronger, but will also... change him.

What kind of person will he become? A stronger savior, or another Hu Lin?

I don't know. I only know, from his tears, I saw... hope.

That hope of not wanting to give up humanity even in pain.

And this, perhaps, is all we need in the darkness.
