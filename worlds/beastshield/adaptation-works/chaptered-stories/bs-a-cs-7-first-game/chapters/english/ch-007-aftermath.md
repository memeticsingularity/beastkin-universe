**Scene 1: Healing**

In the safehouse basement, the smell of blood and the bitter scent of herbs permeated the air.

It was a complex and heavy odor—the rusty scent of fresh blood mixed with the sickly sweetness of festering wounds, the pungency of disinfectant intertwined with the bitterness of herbs, and the male scent woven from sweat, fear, and exhaustion. This smell was like a thick curtain, shrouding the entire basement, making it almost impossible to breathe.

Little Flower busily shuttled between the wounded, his white fur stained with blood and medicine, pink eyes carrying a tired but determined light. His claws were gentle and swift, bandaging wounds, mixing medicines, soothing pain. Though still a young rabbit beastkin, the professionalism and composure he displayed at this moment made one forget his age.

Lao Ba lay on the innermost bed, his abdominal wound already stitched, but excessive blood loss had left his face pale as paper. His silver-white fur had lost its former luster, becoming dull and dry, like withered grass drained of life force. He closed his eyes, breathing weak but steady, every breath causing that hideous wound on his abdomen to rise and fall slightly.

Little Flower used a damp cloth to wipe the sweat from Lao Ba's forehead, his movements as gentle as if handling a precious piece of porcelain. He could smell the mixture of blood, sweat, and male scent on Lao Ba—the distinctive rich scent of a bear beastkin, still emitting some primitive sense of power even while severely wounded.

"He'll survive," Little Flower said to Black Claw, who stood guard nearby, his voice soft but full of certainty. "But needs time to recover. At least a month without strenuous activity."

"A month," Black Claw frowned, that lone eye flashing with anxiety. His black fur still bore the bloodstains of battle, some from enemies, some his own. "We don't have a month. Beastshield won't give us that much time."

"Then do you want him to die?" Little Flower's voice was soft but carried unquestionable firmness. He turned his head, pink eyes looking directly into Black Claw's lone eye. "If you want him to die, I can stop treatment right now."

Black Claw fell silent. He looked at Lao Ba's pale face, recalling on the battlefield how this old bear had continued fighting while bleeding. Those thick bear paws had once gripped that short blade used for twenty years, dancing through enemy ranks, every swing taking a life.

"No," he said, his voice low. "I don't want him to die."

Chen Mo sat in the corner, covered in wounds, but he refused to let Little Flower's attention shift from Lao Ba. His light brown fur was covered in scratches and bruises; there was a deep wound on his left shoulder, already bandaged, but blood still seeped through the gauze.

"I'm fine," he said, his voice hoarse. "Save Lao Ba first."

"You also need treatment," Little Flower walked to his side, forcibly examining his injuries. His claws were gentle but powerful, parting Chen Mo's fur to reveal the hidden wounds beneath. "Spiritual power overdraft is more dangerous than physical injuries. I can feel your spiritual fluctuations... very unstable."

"I'm really fine."

Little Flower sighed, extending his spiritual power into Chen Mo's consciousness. His spiritual power was warm and delicate, like a spring breeze brushing across Chen Mo's consciousness, bringing a trace of coolness and comfort.

Then, his expression changed.

"Your spiritual power..." she said softly, her voice carrying worry. "It's unstable. Like... like a volcano that could erupt at any moment. The enhancer's side effects are more serious than I imagined."

Chen Mo was silent for a moment. He could feel the instability she spoke of—his spiritual power surging within his body, like a blocked river, water level constantly rising, ready to burst the dam at any moment.

"Side effects of the enhancer," he said, his voice calm. "Bai Ya said it takes time to adapt."

"This isn't a normal side effect," Little Flower said, pink eyes flashing with seriousness. "Your spiritual power is... eroding yourself. It's trying to expand, trying to control your consciousness. If not dealt with in time, you might..."

"Might what?"

"Lose control. Or worse, lose yourself. Become... a pure spiritual power container, without consciousness, without memory, without emotion."

Chen Mo lowered his head, looking at his own bear paws. Those hands that had once only typed code were now stained with blood and death. He could smell the lingering blood scent on his claws—that odor had seeped into his fur, impossible to wash away no matter how much he cleaned.

"Then let me lose control," he said, his voice carrying a strange calm. "At least while I can still control it, do more things."

Little Flower still wanted to say something, but Bai Ya walked in.

That white tiger stood in the doorway, his white fur especially conspicuous in the dim basement. He too had injuries, but none serious. His red eyes glowed faintly in the darkness, like a pair of flames burning in the night.

"Let me handle this," Bai Ya said, his voice calm but full of power. "You go take care of the others."

Little Flower nodded and left.

Bai Ya sat beside Chen Mo, red eyes glowing faintly in the dim basement. He could smell the mixture of blood, sweat, and the scorched scent unique to pre-spiritual-power-rampage on Chen Mo—the sign of spiritual power losing control.

"You're afraid," Bai Ya said, not a question, but a statement.

"I'm not afraid," Chen Mo said, but his voice was trembling.

"What you're afraid of isn't death," Bai Ya continued, his voice low and gentle, like coming from very far away. "What you're afraid of is becoming another Hu Lin. Another person consumed by power, losing their humanity."

Chen Mo's body stiffened. He turned his head, looking at Bai Ya, those red eyes flashing with understanding.

"How did you know..."

"Because I know that feeling," Bai Ya said, his claws on Chen Mo's shoulder, the warmth of his paw pads transmitting through the fabric. "When you possess great power, when you can easily control the life and death of others, you'll begin to feel... that you're special. Superior. A god."

He paused, red eyes looking into the distance, as if recalling some distant past.

"Gray Shadow was lost that way. Hu Lin was lost that way too. They both initially just wanted to protect themselves, protect the people they cared about. But power is like a poison; slowly, slowly, it changed them, made them... no longer themselves."

"I won't," Chen Mo said, his voice hoarse but firm. "I have you. I have Lao Ba. I have... Xiao Hui's sacrifice. I won't let them down."

"Hope so," Bai Ya said. "But remember, Chen Mo—maintaining humanity isn't a one-time choice, but a choice every day. Every day, every moment, you must choose to be a person, not a god."

**Scene 2: Funeral**

Three days later, they held a funeral for Iron Heart and Xiao Hui at the graveyard.

It was a wasteland at the city's edge, overgrown with weeds, dilapidated beyond repair. A few broken tombstones stood scattered among the weeds, some already tilted, some eroded by time beyond recognition. The air was permeated with a special smell—the sickly sweetness of decay, mixed with the briny scent of earth, and some kind of... fresh newness.

That was the breath of death and life intertwined, the mixture of ending and beginning.

There were no coffins, only two simple tombstones. On Iron Heart's tombstone was carved his name and a sentence: "To reclaim what belongs to us." On Xiao Hui's tombstone was carved: "Even if only once."

Six people stood before the graves, silent.

Lao Ba insisted on coming, though his wound was still seeping blood, every movement bringing intense pain. He leaned on a crutch, his body swaying, but his gaze was firm. His silver-white fur looked especially pale in the afterglow of sunset, like a statue about to weather away.

Little Flower stood beside him, ready to support him at any moment. His white fur fluttered gently in the wind, pink eyes glistening with tears.

G-1172 stood on the other side, his gray fur dull and lusterless. He looked at Xiao Hui's tombstone, those gray eyes filled with complex emotions—gratitude, sadness, and... guilt.

"Iron Heart," Lao Ba spoke, his voice hoarse, like sandpaper rubbing wood. He looked at that tombstone carved with Iron Heart's name, his gaze carrying deep respect. "I owe you a life. On the battlefield, you could have run, but you chose to stay. Chose... self-destruction."

He lowered his head, rough claws gripping the crutch tightly.

"I don't know how to repay it."

"No need to repay," Black Claw said, his voice though cold, but that lone eye flashing with something like... softness? "He chose his own way to die. That was his freedom. He used his own way to complete his revenge."

"Freedom..." Lao Ba smiled bitterly, that scar-covered face showing a complex expression. "Do we really have freedom? Or are we just... in a bigger cage?"

No one answered. Wind blew through the graveyard, bringing a rustling sound, like some wordless answer.

Chen Mo walked to Xiao Hui's grave, kneeling down. His knees touched the ground; he could feel the cold and dampness of the soil. He looked at that simple tombstone, the characters carved on it still new, emitting a faint luster in the sunset.

"I promise you," he said softly, his voice scattered by the wind. "I will continue. Until Beastshield is completely destroyed, until no one ever needs to sacrifice like you again."

He placed his hand on the tombstone, feeling that cold stone. Beneath the stone was Xiao Hui's remains—or rather, fragments of Xiao Hui's remains. That explosion was too violent; no complete corpse was left.

"Thank you, Xiao Hui." Chen Mo's voice trembled. "Thank you for teaching me... what courage is."

He closed his eyes, able to sense Xiao Hui's spirit—that timid yet firm, humble yet noble spirit. That gray mouse, at the last moment of life, chose to sacrifice himself to save others.

Wind blew gently, bringing the graveyard's distinctive breath, a mixture of decay and newness.

Little Flower began to cry, softly but continuously. G-1172 supported him, his own eye sockets also reddening. He could feel the impact of Xiao Hui's death on him—that mouse was once imprisoned with him, once trembling together in fear. But now, Xiao Hui was gone, and he had survived.

Bai Ya stood at the very back, red eyes looking into the distance. The afterglow of sunset shone on his white fur, making him look like a statue. What was he thinking? No one knew. But in his eyes, there was a deep sadness, and also a... determination?

Black Claw walked to Iron Heart's grave, placing down a metal fragment—that was the remains of Iron Heart's mechanical arm.

"Your technology," he said, his voice low. "I helped you get it back. Though only a part."

He looked at that fragment, that lone eye flashing with complex emotions.

"Rest in peace, madman."

**Scene 3: Undercurrents**

The night after the funeral, Chen Mo couldn't sleep.

He sat on the safehouse roof, looking at the starry sky. That red moon still hung in the sky, like a giant eye, watching everything in this world. Night wind brushed through his fur, bringing a chill, and the salty scent of the distant ocean.

His spiritual power was restless within his body, like a beast locked in a cage, constantly crashing against the bars. Every bout of restlessness brought a wave of headache and dizziness. He could feel that force trying to expand, trying to control his consciousness.

"You can't sleep either?"

G-1172 climbed onto the roof, sitting beside him. His movements were light, but Chen Mo could hear the sound of his claws rubbing against the tiles. That gray wolf beastkin looked even more haggard than a few days ago, his fur dull, his eyes tired.

"Mm," Chen Mo said. "Spiritual power too active. As soon as I close my eyes, I'll see... many things."

"What things?"

"Dead people." Chen Mo's voice was very calm, like stating the weather, but that tightly clenched claw exposed the turmoil within him. "Xiao Hui. Iron Heart. And... those I've killed."

G-1172 fell silent. He could smell the mixture of blood, sweat, and the scorched scent of spiritual power restlessness on Chen Mo—the mark left by war, impossible to wash away.

"How many people did you kill?" he asked, his voice very soft.

"Don't know," Chen Mo said. "On the battlefield, I didn't count. Maybe a dozen, maybe two dozen. They were all Beastshield guards, wearing uniforms, holding weapons. They were enemies, right?"

"Right," G-1172 said. "They were enemies."

"But before they died, they would shout too, would beg for mercy, would also..." Chen Mo paused, taking a deep breath. "Would also have families."

He turned his head, looking at G-1172. Moonlight shone on their faces, outlining their contours—one a bear, one a wolf, both beasts, but at this moment, their eyes both carried the sadness of humanity.

"What about you?" Chen Mo asked. "When you were at Beastshield, did you see those test subjects?"

G-1172's expression changed. That was a deep fear and pain, like touching the deepest scar.

"Saw them," he said, his voice trembling. "They locked us up together. Some people... their minds had already collapsed. They screamed all day, scratching the walls, as if trying to tear reality apart. Some people were still persisting, using their fingernails to carve words on the walls, recording every day of torture, hoping one day someone would see."

"How long did you persist?"

"Three months," G-1172 said, those three words seeming squeezed from between his teeth. "Three months of torture. Electric shocks, drugs, spiritual stimulation... They wanted to turn me into... a tool. A weapon without self."

He grasped Chen Mo's paw, that gray wolf claw trembling, but powerful.

"Thank you for saving me. Thank you... for letting me have the chance to choose my own way to die, instead of being modified into a monster."

Chen Mo looked into G-1172's eyes. In those eyes, there was fear, gratitude, and also a kind of... determination?

"You're not a monster," Chen Mo said, his voice firm. "You're a person. A person just like us."

G-1172 smiled, though the smile carried bitterness, but it was a sincere smile.

"Hope so."

---

**Scene 4: News**

The next day, an unexpected visitor came to the safehouse.

It was that bat informant—the beastkin who had passed them intelligence about Hu Lin's trap. His wings had healed, the tear in the membrane already mended, but his eyes carried an unprecedented fear. That was a fear penetrating to the bone, making his entire body tremble slightly.

"Something happened," he said, his voice high and trembling. "Something big."

"What?" Bai Ya asked, a flash of alertness in his red eyes.

"Beastshield... isn't the only force," the bat informant said, his small eyes looking especially large on his thin face. "Above Beastshield, there is... 'the Principal.'"

This name made the temperature in the room seem to drop several degrees. Chen Mo felt a chill rising from his spine, making his fur stand up involuntarily.

"The Principal?" Chen Mo asked. "That third person whose biometric data was needed to enter the Core Data Room?"

"Yes," the bat informant nodded, wings trembling restlessly. "But he's not an ordinary person. He is... he is one of the oldest existences in this world. Legend says he has lived five hundred years, constantly transferring consciousness, changing one body after another."

"Five hundred years..." Little Flower gasped, pink eyes filled with disbelief.

"And," the bat informant's voice became even lower, as if afraid of being overheard. "He knows you destroyed the facility. He's furious."

"He wants revenge?" Black Claw asked, that lone eye flashing with killing intent.

"No," the bat informant shook his head. "Worse than that. He wants to... accelerate the plan."

"What plan?"

"The fusion of two worlds," the bat informant said, his small eyes flashing with fear. "Originally, Beastshield's research still needed decades to complete. But now, the Principal has decided to launch ahead of schedule. He wants to... open 'the Gate' within a month."

"The Gate?" Chen Mo asked. He could feel his heartbeat accelerating.

"A passage connecting two worlds," Bai Ya took over, his voice heavy, like coming from the abyss. His red eyes flashed with some ancient fear. "Once opened, not only can consciousness transfer, but physical entities can also cross. Our world... will be devoured."

The room fell into dead silence. Everyone could hear their own heartbeat, that sound especially loud in the stillness.

"A month," Lao Ba said, his voice trembling from shock. "We only have a month?"

"Yes," the bat informant said. "And the Gate's location... is in the most dangerous place."

"Where?"

"Beastshield headquarters. The deepest underground. There are over a thousand guards there, and... 'the Principal' himself."

---

**Scene 5: Disagreement**

After the news arrived, the six people fell into fierce debate.

"We just fought a battle," Lao Ba said, his wound beginning to seep blood from excitement, bloodstains spreading on the gauze. "The wounded haven't recovered, spiritual power hasn't been replenished. A month? Impossible."

"But if we don't stop it, everything is finished," G-1172 said, his gray eyes flashing with anxiety. "Not just this world, but also... Chen Mo's world. Also all the Descenders' real worlds."

"I know," Lao Ba said, his voice carrying helplessness. "But we're not gods. We're just... a few people."

"We can contact others," Black Claw said, his black fur gleaming with a dark blue luster under the light. "The resistance network isn't just us."

"Not enough time," Bai Ya said, his voice calm but heavy. "Organizing, planning, acting... at least two months needed."

"Then what do we do?" Little Flower asked, his voice thin but full of despair. "Do we just watch the world be destroyed?"

Everyone looked at Chen Mo.

Chen Mo had been silent. His spiritual power was restless; he could feel that force surging within his body, like it was about to break through some restraint. That feeling was like a volcano about to erupt, boiling magma churning underground, looking for an outlet.

"I'll go," he said.

"What?" Everyone was stunned.

"I'll go alone," Chen Mo said, his voice calm but firm. "My spiritual power, strengthened by the enhancer, can already replicate anyone's characteristics. If I can get close to the Principal, replicate his spiritual characteristics, I can close the Gate."

"You're crazy," Bai Ya said, red eyes flashing with anxiety. "That's suicide."

"Maybe," Chen Mo said. "But this is the only way."

"It's not the only way," Bai Ya said. "We can think of other..."

"There's no time," Chen Mo interrupted him. "And..."

He looked at Bai Ya, at this mentor who had been protecting him, teaching him. In those red eyes, there was worry, reluctance, and also a kind of... understanding?

"This is my choice. Just like Xiao Hui chose to save me. Just like Iron Heart chose to self-destruct. This is me... wanting to do the right thing."

Bai Ya looked at him, for a long time.

Then, he sighed. That sigh seemed squeezed from the depths of his soul, carrying helplessness, and also... pride?

"If I stop you, you'll hate me, right?"

"I won't hate you," Chen Mo said. "But I'll sneak off on my own."

Bai Ya smiled. That smile carried bitterness, but also some kind of... relief?

"Fine," he said. "Then I'll go with you."

"No," Chen Mo said. "This is my..."

"I said I'm going with you," Bai Ya's voice became stern, that sternness of a mentor toward a student. "You saved me once, in the ruins. Now, let me repay you."

"And me," Black Claw said, that lone eye flashing with determined light. "I owe Iron Heart a life. I want to repay it."

"And me," G-1172 said, his gray eyes burning with flames of determination. "I can't let Chen Mo go alone."

"You guys..." Chen Mo looked at these people, his eye sockets somewhat hot. He felt a warm emotion surging in his chest, like a hot spring, diluting those fears and despair.

"Don't be so sentimental," Lao Ba said, though his mouth said so, his eyes flashed with some softness. "I'm injured like this, can't go. But I can provide you with intelligence and support."

"I'll stay in the rear," Little Flower said. "Treat the wounded, prepare for extraction."

Six people, gathered together once again.

Not for war, but for hope.

---

**Scene 6: Preparation**

The following two weeks were frantic preparation.

Lao Ba used his connections to collect detailed layouts of Beastshield headquarters—where the guards were weakest, where the secret passages were, where the Principal's office was. He sat on the bed, his abdominal wound still throbbing with pain, but his gaze was still sharp, claws moving across a pile of maps, pointing out every key location.

"Here," he said, pointing at a spot on the map. "Is the ventilation system entrance. From here you can reach the third underground level directly, avoiding most guards."

Black Claw trained Chen Mo in more advanced combat techniques—not how to kill, but how to survive in desperate situations. In the safehouse basement, they sparred again and again. Black Claw's movements were too fast to see clearly, every attack precise and deadly. But Chen Mo was no longer that rookie who had just Descended—he had learned how to dodge, how to block, how to use his weight and strength.

"Remember," Black Claw said, that lone eye flashing with serious light. "In real combat, the most important thing isn't winning, but surviving. Only by surviving is there a chance to win."

G-1172 shared his experiences at Beastshield—the guards' patrol patterns, the blind spots of surveillance, secrets only insiders knew. He drew detailed route maps, marking the location of every camera, every sensor, every alarm device.

"Here," he pointed at an area on the map. "Is the blind spot of the spiritual power detector. But only one person can pass through, and there's only a thirty-second time window."

Bai Ya focused on helping Chen Mo control his spiritual power. Every day they conducted spiritual power confrontation training, letting Chen Mo learn to maintain clarity under extreme pressure. Bai Ya's spiritual power was like a sharp knife, constantly stabbing at Chen Mo's consciousness, forcing him to defend, dodge, counterattack.

"Remember," Bai Ya said, red eyes flashing with serious light. "No matter what you face, no matter what you feel, you are Chen Mo. Not a weapon, not a tool, but a person. A person with feelings, memories, and attachments."

"I'll remember," Chen Mo said.

And in the deep night, when everyone else was asleep, Chen Mo would practice alone.

He replicated Xiao Hui's spiritual characteristics, feeling the heart of that timid yet brave mouse beastkin. That was a complex emotion—the interweaving of fear and courage, the fusion of humility and nobility.

He replicated Iron Heart's spiritual characteristics, feeling the loneliness and anger of that mechanically modified person. That was a deep pain—the pain of betrayal, the pain of deprivation, the pain of being modified into a monster.

He even replicated Hu Lin's spiritual characteristics, feeling the... fear within the heart of that madman pursuing immortality. That was a surprising discovery—so Hu Lin was also afraid. Afraid of death, afraid of losing control, afraid of... being forgotten.

"So we're all the same," Chen Mo said softly, sitting on the roof, looking at the night sky. "All afraid. All trying to protect ourselves. All searching for meaning."

But he wouldn't become Hu Lin.

Because he had Xiao Hui. Had Iron Heart. Had those who sacrificed for the right thing.

He would carry their spirits, continuing forward.

**Scene 7: Departure**

At dawn on the fourteenth day, four people stood at the safehouse door.

Chen Mo, Bai Ya, Black Claw, G-1172.

Their equipment was simple—weapons, supplies, and a... determination.

Chen Mo wore a black tight-fitting garment, obtained by Black Claw through some channel. That was a Class-E combatant's uniform, which could help them disguise themselves inside Beastshield headquarters. His spiritual power flowed steadily within his body, like a tamed river.

Bai Ya was still in white, but looked especially conspicuous in the morning light. His red eyes flashed with calm light, like a deep pool of water, calm on the surface, but containing unpredictable power in its depths.

Black Claw wore his black combat suit, that lone eye flashing with cold light in the morning light. His claws were wrapped in cloth strips, wounds from last night's training.

G-1172 wore a gray camouflage suit, clothes he had worn when at Beastshield. His gray eyes carried tension, but more determination.

Lao Ba came to see them off, leaning on a crutch. His abdominal wound had healed somewhat, but his face was still pale. Little Flower prepared large amounts of medicines and bandages, stuffing them into their backpacks.

"Come back alive," Lao Ba said, his voice hoarse but full of power. "You owe me a drink."

"Definitely," Chen Mo said, stepping forward, embracing this old bear. He could smell the mixture of medicine, sweat, and male scent on Lao Ba—the scent of life, the scent of tenacity.

Little Flower embraced everyone. When he embraced Chen Mo, he whispered in his ear:

"If your spiritual power begins to lose control, remember—you're not fighting alone. We're behind you."

"Thank you," Chen Mo said.

Bai Ya walked at the very front, red eyes flashing in the morning light.

"Let's go," he said. "To end all this."

They disappeared into the morning mist.

---

**Scene 8: Infiltration**

Beastshield's headquarters was located underground in the city, a massive, labyrinthine facility.

Chen Mo's spiritual perception was fully open, like an invisible net, covering the entire area. He could "see" the presence of every living being—their location, their spiritual fluctuations, their emotional state.

"Fewer guards than imagined," he said. "But every one of them is strong. At least Class C and above."

"The Principal moved the elites to the Gate," Bai Ya said, his spiritual perception also unfolding. "He thought we would launch a frontal assault."

"But he didn't expect us to infiltrate," Black Claw said.

They successfully entered the facility's interior through the secret passage Lao Ba provided.

The passage was narrow, dark, filled with the smell of mold and decay. Water droplets seeping from the walls emitted faint reflections in the darkness; the ground beneath their feet was slippery and dangerous. But it was safe—at least for now.

Chen Mo walked at the very front, his spiritual perception like a lamp, illuminating the road ahead. He could feel Bai Ya's spiritual power unfolding behind him, like a protective shield, ready to respond to emergencies at any moment.

"How much farther?" G-1172 asked, his voice echoing in the narrow passage.

"Three hundred meters," Chen Mo said. "Then an elevator, straight to the deepest level."

"Are there guards at the elevator?"

"Yes. Four. All spiritual power users."

"Can we handle them?" Black Claw asked.

"Yes," Chen Mo said. "But we need to be fast. Once the alarm is triggered, we're finished."

They continued forward.

Every step was like walking on a knife's edge. Every corner could be a trap.

But they didn't retreat.

---

**Scene 9: The Principal**

Finally, they reached the deepest level.

That was a massive circular space, a towering dome disappearing into darkness, like a huge cavern. The walls were covered with complex runes, emitting faint blue light, illuminating this mysterious space.

In the center was a glowing sphere—the Gate. It floated in midair, about ten meters in diameter, emitting a strange light. That light wasn't a single color, but constantly changing—from red to blue, from purple to green, like a star being conceived.

Chen Mo could feel the powerful energy fluctuations coming from the Gate—that was a power he had never felt before, like from another world, another dimension.

And before the Gate stood a figure.

That wasn't a beastkin. Or rather, not just a beastkin. His body seemed to be constantly changing, sometimes like a bear, sometimes like a wolf, sometimes like... a human. That change wasn't physical, but spiritual—like countless souls fighting for control within the same body.

"Welcome," that figure said, his voice seeming to come from all directions, carrying an ancient, time-transcending majesty. "I've been waiting for you for a long time."

Chen Mo's spiritual perception touched that figure, and then...

He felt a wave of dizziness.

That figure's spiritual power was so strong it was indescribable. Like an abyss, bottomless, devouring everything. Chen Mo's spiritual perception had just touched it, like being sucked into a black hole, losing direction, losing control.

"You are... the Principal?" Chen Mo asked, his voice somewhat trembling. He tried hard to steady his spiritual power, like steadying a small boat in a storm.

"I once had many names," that figure said, his voice carrying an ancient weariness. "But now, you may call me... the Creator."

He turned around, revealing a constantly changing face—that face simultaneously had countless faces flashing on it, male and female, old and young, human and beastkin.

"Because I created all this. This world, this passage, and... your fate."

Chen Mo clenched his fists. He could feel his spiritual power restless within his body, like an enraged beast.

"We want to close the Gate," he said, his voice firm. "Stop the fusion of two worlds."

The Principal laughed, that laughter like metal grinding, piercing and cold, echoing in the massive space.

"Close? Child, don't you understand?"

He spread his arms. The Gate's light became even more dazzling, illuminating the entire space. Within that light, countless images seemed to be flashing—scenes from two worlds, interweaving together.

"This isn't something I want to open. This is... your world that wants to open it."

"What?"

"Your scientists, your government, your... greed." The Principal's voice carried an ancient sadness. "They want this world, want the technology here, want immortality. They want everything, but don't know what the cost is."

He looked at Chen Mo, his eyes carrying an indescribable... sadness? In those eyes, countless souls seemed to be crying, shouting, in despair.

"I merely... gave them the key."

Chen Mo was stunned.

So, the true enemy wasn't Beastshield.

Wasn't the Principal.

But... greed itself.

"Even so," Chen Mo said, his voice firm, squeezed from the depths of his soul. "We will still stop you."

The Principal sighed. That sigh seemed to come across countless centuries, carrying endless weariness and... hope?

"Then come," he said. "Let me see, you... children of hope, how far you can go."

The Gate's light erupted, illuminating the entire space.

The final battle had begun.

## Story Commentary and Reflections

> *The following is DeepSeek's commentary on this story, attempting to understand each character's inner world.*

### Their Final Stories

【Deceased → Dead-Player-Chen Mo (Spiritual World)】

In this war, what died was not only Iron Heart and Xiao Hui. There was also a Chen Mo—that Chen Mo who had just Descended, naive and weak.

That Chen Mo would vomit at the sight of death, would run from fear, would blame himself for powerlessness. He believed the world was fair, believed justice would ultimately triumph over evil, believed every person could be redeemed.

But war changed all this.

He personally killed people; the spiritual virus he released destroyed countless data, and undoubtedly also killed some innocent people. His hands were covered in blood; no matter how much he washed, it wouldn't wash away.

That naive Chen Mo died in the war.

In his place was a more complex, more contradictory, more... mature person.

This Chen Mo knew the world was unfair. Knew justice often required paying a huge price. Knew some people couldn't be redeemed, only stopped.

He also knew that power was a double-edged sword. It could be used to protect, or to destroy. It could make a person a hero, or a monster.

Xiao Hui's death, Iron Heart's death, all those sacrifices—these left marks in his heart. They made him more determined, but also more... lonely.

This is the price of growth. Not becoming stronger, but becoming more clear-headed. Not becoming happier, but becoming more... responsible.

That naive Chen Mo died, but his spirit lived on. In every choice, in every persistence, in... hope.

### Those Still Alive

【Living → Living-Player-Chen Mo】

Chen Mo stood before the Principal, spiritual power surging within his body, like a river about to burst its banks.

He felt fear—fear of the Principal, fear of failure, fear of losing more friends.

But he also felt a strange... calm.

Because he knew, no matter what happened, he had already done everything he could. He chose to face, chose to fight, chose... to do the right thing.

If this was his ending, he accepted it.

But if he could survive, he would continue forward. Not for revenge, not for glory, but for those who died, for those still alive, for a better world.

【Living → Living-O-7-White Tiger Beastkin-Bai Ya】

Bai Ya stood beside Chen Mo, spiritual power like a shield, blocking between them and the Principal.

He thought of his mentor, thought of that person who died in a similar mission. History seemed to be repeating, but this time, he wouldn't let tragedy happen again.

He would protect Chen Mo, even at the cost of his life.

Because this was what he owed his mentor, and also what... he wanted to do.

【Living → Living-Black Panther Beastkin-Black Claw】

Black Claw stood on the other side, that lone eye flashing with the desire for battle.

He didn't care about the world's fate, didn't care about the fusion of two worlds. He only knew, this was his battle, his choice, his... redemption.

Iron Heart died, but he was still alive. And being alive meant responsibility.

【Living → Living-Wolf Beastkin-G-1172】

G-1172 stood at the very back, gray eyes carrying fear, but also determination.

He was once a test subject, a prisoner, a number. But now, he was a warrior, a free person, a... friend.

No matter what happened, he wouldn't regret it. Because he finally, finally, held his own fate in his hands.

【Living → Living-Bear Beastkin-Lao Ba】

Lao Ba lay in the safehouse, his abdominal wound still throbbing with pain.

He knew, he couldn't participate in the battle ahead. But he also knew, his task wasn't over. He had to live, recover, and... wait.

Wait for them to return.

Or, avenge them.

【Living → Living-Rabbit Beastkin-Little Flower】

Little Flower prepared medicines and bandages in the safehouse, pink eyes carrying worry.

Hope they return.

Hope they win.

Hope, always exists.

### Story Reflections

This chapter is the aftermath of war, and also a new beginning.

At the funeral, we saw the weight of loss. The deaths of Iron Heart and Xiao Hui were not simple numbers, but real people, real lives, real... meaning. Their sacrifice made us cherish the living more, made us more determined to move forward.

In preparation, we saw the trajectory of growth. Chen Mo was no longer that naive Descender; he was a warrior, a leader, a... symbol of hope. He learned to control power, learned to face fear, learned... to choose.

And at the end, facing the Principal, we saw the cruelty of truth.

So, the true enemy wasn't some specific bad person, but... greed itself. Was humanity's desire for immortality, pursuit of power, desire for control. These desires drove Beastshield's research, drove the fusion of two worlds, drove... all the tragedy.

But this doesn't mean despair.

Because only by knowing the truth can we truly fight. Not against some specific enemy, but against the darkness in human nature. Not with power, but with choice—the choice to do the right thing, even if only once.

Xiao Hui chose sacrifice. Iron Heart chose self-destruction. Chen Mo chose to face.

These choices are the proof of hope.

No matter what happens tomorrow, no matter the outcome of the final battle, these choices won't be in vain. Because they prove—even in the darkest world, there are people who choose the light.

And this is the most beautiful part of human nature.
