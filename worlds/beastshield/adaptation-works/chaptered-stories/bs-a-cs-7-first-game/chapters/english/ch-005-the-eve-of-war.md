**Scene 1: Side Effects**

The effects of the spiritual power enhancer were more intense than Chen Mo had imagined.

In the first hour after drinking the potion, he felt a clarity unlike anything before. Every detail of the world was vividly sharp—dust motes floating in the air glittered like golden specks in the sunset light, the crawling path of an ant in a wall crack was as clear as if magnified, the rustle of Lao Ba turning pages in the next room, the heartbeats of every pedestrian on the distant street merging into a rhythmic drumbeat.

His spiritual perception expanded to an unprecedented range, like a vast net covering the entire block. He could "see" every living being—not their appearance, but their spiritual light-clusters. Each cluster had a unique color, shape, and temperature.

But in the second hour, the pain began.

A headache. Not an ordinary headache, but like someone stirring a red-hot iron rod inside his brain, burning outward from the deepest depths. His vision began to distort; what he saw was no longer physical objects, but... essences. Every life became a ball of light, some strong, some weak, some bright, some dim, weaving into a sea of radiance.

"Bai Ya..." He curled up on the bed, claws gripping the sheets, the rough fabric making tearing sounds under his sharp talons. "What... what is happening..."

Bai Ya sat beside him, his expression grave. The afterglow of sunset slanted through the window, gilding his silver-white fur with gold, making him look like some ancient deity. But now, on that usually calm face, a rare look of worry appeared.

"Side effects of the enhancer," he said, his voice low and slow. "It forces your spiritual power to expand rapidly, but your consciousness isn't ready to process this level of information."

"How... how long will it last..."

"Varies by individual. Maybe a few hours, maybe several days."

Several days? Chen Mo wanted to scream. This pain lasting a few hours was already maddening—several days? His claws gouged deep marks into the sheets, rough paw pads whitening from overexertion, sharp talons piercing the fabric with harsh ripping sounds.

"Focus," Bai Ya's voice came from very far away, carrying a strange rhythm, like waves crashing against reefs. "Don't resist the information. Accept it, sort it, control it."

"I can't..." Chen Mo's voice shattered, like torn silk. Sweat beaded on his forehead, soaking his light brown fur, emitting a sour smell mixed with fear and pain.

"You can." Bai Ya grasped his paw, those soft but powerful rabbit paws wrapping around Chen Mo's trembling bear paws. Bai Ya's body temperature was slightly lower than his, the paw pads dry and rough, carrying a strangely calming strength. "Think of Xiao Hui. Think of why you drank that potion."

Xiao Hui.

That name was like a lightning bolt, cleaving through the chaos in Chen Mo's consciousness.

Xiao Hui. That reserved mouse beastkin, always hunched over, eyes evasive, speaking in a voice as thin as a mosquito's whine. The one who said, "I want to do the right thing, even if only once." The one who, at the last moment, stabbed Hu Lin with a scalpel to buy him escape time. The one who was captured by Beastshield so he could get away.

He couldn't fall. Not for himself, but for Xiao Hui. For all the lives crushed by Beastshield.

Chen Mo took a deep breath, forcing himself to calm down. He could smell the scent emanating from Bai Ya—dry grass, mixed with a faint medicinal fragrance, the lingering aroma of long-term herb use. This familiar smell was like an anchor, pulling him back from the chaotic ocean of consciousness.

He no longer resisted the flooding information, but instead tried to... organize it. Like sorting a chaotic database, labeling every piece of data and placing it in the correct category.

That bright light-cluster was Lao Ba, the safehouse owner. His spiritual light-cluster displayed a steady amber color, like aged whiskey—calm on the surface, but countless stories hidden in its depths.

That weak light-cluster was G-1172, resting in the basement. His cluster showed a sickly gray-white, edges blurred, like a candle flame about to be extinguished, but with a stubborn red still beating at its core.

That warm light-cluster was Bai Ya, right beside him. It was silver-white, like moonlight on snow, pure and bright, but Chen Mo could perceive the pain hidden deep within that radiance—the traces of countless losses, the echoes of death marks.

And... that approaching, familiar light-cluster...

"Lao Ba's coming," Chen Mo suddenly said, his voice weak but much clearer. He could "see" that cluster—thick earthy yellow, like a moving hill, steady, slow, but containing unshakable power.

Bai Ya looked at him in surprise, a flash of admiration crossing his red eyes.

"You can sense that far?"

"Mm..." Chen Mo opened his eyes. The world in his vision gradually returned to normal, but that perception of the spiritual world still lingered at the edge of his consciousness. "Three blocks away. His light... is special. Not bright, but... heavy. Like..."

"Like the earth," Bai Ya smiled, that smile carrying a trace of relief. "That's how Lao Ba is. Steady, reliable, unwavering."

A minute later, a knock sounded at the door.

Lao Ba walked into the room, his heavy footsteps making the entire floor tremble slightly. He carried that huge canvas bag, rough brown fur dusted with the grime of travel, his body carrying a strong scent mixed with pine, leather, and sweat—the mark of long-term wilderness survival, the distinctive male scent of a bear beastkin.

Seeing Chen Mo's condition, he frowned, a trace of worry flashing through those cloudy bear eyes.

"Enhancer?" he asked, his voice as deep as thunder rolling from deep within his chest.

"Mm," Bai Ya nodded. "Drank it less than three hours ago."

"Crazy," Lao Ba shook his head, his massive skull swaying side to side. "That stuff will burn out his brain. I've seen too many people turn into idiots from enhancer abuse, or worse—vegetables."

"But he pulled through," Bai Ya said, pride in his tone, "and is adapting."

Lao Ba looked at Chen Mo, and in those cloudy bear eyes flashed something like... relief? He stepped closer, that strong male scent growing even more intense, mixed with the sweat of long travel, forming a primitive and powerful olfactory assault.

"Kid," he said, a rare gentleness in his voice, "you're stronger than I thought."

Chen Mo tried to smile, but the headache only allowed him to produce a twisted expression. He could feel Lao Ba's gaze—not scrutiny, but a kind of... approval. Like looking at a junior who had finally grown up.

"Lao Ba... thank you for coming..."

"Don't thank me," Lao Ba set down the large bundle on his back, which made a heavy thud. He began untying the knots, rough claws moving nimbly through the ropes. "I'm here to help. Heard you're planning something big?"

"Destroy Beastshield's research facility," Bai Ya said. "Stop the consciousness transfer technology."

"I knew it," Lao Ba sighed, that sigh squeezed from deep within his chest, carrying helplessness and a trace of... anticipation? "You Descenders, always doing these... impossible things."

"Will you help us?" Chen Mo asked, his voice carrying both anticipation and unease.

Lao Ba was silent for a moment, then smiled. That smile looked especially fierce on his scar-covered face, but Chen Mo could feel the sincerity in it.

"Help," he said. "Who told me I owe your mentor a life."

He looked at Bai Ya, his gaze complex.

"And... I also want to do something for that kid called Xiao Hui." His voice dropped. "That kid... reminds me of something from many years ago."

Bai Ya didn't press further, only nodding.

**Scene 2: Planning**

That evening, four people sat in the safehouse basement—Bai Ya, Chen Mo, Lao Ba, and G-1172.

The basement was filled with dampness and mold. Faint yellow light swayed overhead, casting flickering shadows. Chen Mo sat on a rickety wooden chair, feeling the texture of the wood rubbing against his thighs through the thin fabric. The air was also mixed with Lao Ba's strong male scent, G-1172's faint disinfectant smell, and Bai Ya's dry herbal fragrance.

That young wolf beastkin had recovered somewhat. Though his face was still pale, his eyes were no longer hollow. He insisted on participating in the meeting. "For Xiao Hui, and for myself."

"First, intelligence," Bai Ya spread out a hand-drawn map, yellowed paper densely marked with various symbols. His claws moved across the map, every motion carrying precision and steadiness. "This is the layout of Beastshield's underground facility. Chen Mo, you were inside for seven days. Any additions?"

Chen Mo looked at the map. The headache had lessened considerably, replaced by a strange clarity. The enhancer's effects were stabilizing. He could now feel his spiritual power like a... river—turbulent, but capable of being guided.

"Here," he pointed at a spot on the map, the tip of his claw leaving a tiny indentation in the paper. "Data Collection Room. Where I worked. Light defenses, only ordinary guards."
He closed his eyes, recalling that place's smells—the pungent disinfectant, the metallic scent of machines, and that aquatic creature's distinctive briny smell from the large crocodile intern.

"What about here?" Bai Ya pointed at an area on the seventh underground level, a zone marked with a red circle labeled "Restricted Zone."

"Core Data Room," Chen Mo said, his voice involuntarily lowering, as if afraid of being overheard. "I never went in, but Xiao Hui told me the entrance location. Requires biometric data from three executives—fingerprint, retina, and spiritual power signature." He paused. "Xiao Hui said the defenses there are stricter than anywhere else. Class-E armed personnel on twenty-four-hour rotation, plus spiritual power detection devices."

"Three executives," Lao Ba frowned, rough claws rubbing the scar on his chin, making a scratching sound. "Hu Lin is one. What about the other two?"

"Tiejiao," G-1172 suddenly said, his voice hoarse, like sandpaper rubbing wood. "The other is Tiejiao. I heard them talking in the lab."

His wolf claws trembled slightly on the tabletop, his gray fur looking dull under the light. Chen Mo could smell the scent of fear on him—a sour, rotten-fruit-like smell, mixed with the wild scent unique to wolf beastkin.

"Tiejiao," Bai Ya's gaze turned cold, a flash of killing intent crossing his red eyes. "The bull beastkin who attacked us."

"What about the third?" Chen Mo asked. He could feel his heartbeat accelerating, the sound of blood rushing through his vessels echoing in his ears.

G-1172 shook his head, ears drooping powerlessly on both sides.

"Don't know. They call him 'the Principal,' but I've never seen him."

"The Principal," Lao Ba and Bai Ya exchanged a glance, both their expressions turning grave. "The highest authority of Beastshield's research department. Legend says he's lived two hundred years, constantly transferring consciousness to change bodies." Lao Ba's voice carried disgust. "A monster. A true monster."

"How do we deal with him?" Chen Mo asked, his claws tightening unconsciously, embedding into the wooden chair's armrest.

"We don't need to deal with him," Bai Ya said, his voice returning to calm. "Our goal isn't to kill people, but to destroy data. As long as the core data is destroyed, their research will be set back decades."

"But entering the Core Data Room requires biometric data from three people," Chen Mo said. "How do we get it?"

Bai Ya smiled, that smile carrying a hint of mystery.

"That's where you come in, Chen Mo."

"Me?"

"Your spiritual power, strengthened by the enhancer, can already mimic other people's spiritual characteristics." Bai Ya's eyes gleamed in the light. "That's why I chose this specific enhancer. It doesn't just strengthen power, but also grants you... the ability to mimic."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"Mimic?"

"Mm." Bai Ya nodded. "A spiritual power signature is like a fingerprint—everyone has a unique pattern. But if you can precisely replicate that pattern..." He paused, giving Chen Mo time to understand.

"You can fool the identification system," Chen Mo understood, his voice carrying surprise and a trace of... fear. "But I need to have come into contact with that person's spiritual power to replicate it."

"Right. So we need to capture Hu Lin or Tiejiao, let you contact their spiritual power."

"Capture?" Lao Ba raised an eyebrow, rough claws clenching into fists. "Not kill?"

"Capture," Bai Ya said, his tone firm. "We need them alive. And..." He looked at Chen Mo, his gaze carrying a profound understanding. "Chen Mo hasn't killed anyone yet. I don't want his first time to be part of our plan."

Chen Mo lowered his head, staring at his claws. They looked no different from a week ago—light brown fur, black paw pads, sharp talons. But he knew they were already stained with blood. Not his own blood, but the deaths he had witnessed.

G-892. Gray Shadow. Those test subjects in the lab. Xiao Hui.

He had witnessed many deaths, but had never personally taken a life.

He didn't know if he could do it.

"There's another problem," G-1172 said, his voice trembling. "Even if we enter the Core Data Room, how do we destroy the data? Simple destruction might not be enough. They might have backups. Cloud backups, off-site servers, even... paper archives."

"A virus," Bai Ya took a crystal-like object from his pocket. It refracted rainbow light under the lamp, like a miniature rainbow imprisoned within. "I got this from an old friend. A special spiritual virus that can infect data systems, thoroughly deleting all information, including backups."

"Spiritual virus?" Chen Mo leaned closer, able to smell the faint scent emanating from the crystal—a strange, ozone-like smell, carrying the aura of electric current.

"Mm. It's not a physical program, but spiritual power encoding. Once released, it spreads along spiritual connections, destroying all related data nodes. No matter where the data is stored, as long as it's connected to the spiritual network, it will be infected."

"This is dangerous," Lao Ba said, rough claws stroking the scar on his chest. "If not controlled well, it might affect innocents. The entire underground network, even..."

"So precise control is needed," Bai Ya looked at Chen Mo, those red eyes burning with flames of trust. "That's why we need Chen Mo. His spiritual power, after enhancement, is sufficient to precisely locate the release point and control the virus's spread range."

Everyone looked at Chen Mo.

He could feel their gazes—expectation, trust, and... worry. These gazes were like tangible weights, pressing on his shoulders.

Chen Mo took a deep breath. The air held the smell of mold, sweat, fear, but also... the smell of hope.

"I understand," he said, his voice not loud but firm. "My tasks are:

One, replicate Hu Lin's or Tiejiao's spiritual characteristics;
Two, enter the Core Data Room;
Three, precisely release the spiritual virus to destroy the data.

Correct?"

"Correct," Bai Ya said. "But there's a fourth step."

"What?"

Bai Ya reached out, placing his paw on Chen Mo's shoulder. His claws were gentle but powerful, the warmth of his paw pads transmitting through the fabric.

"Come out alive."

**Scene 3: Allies**

The next day, more allies arrived at the safehouse.

The first was **Hei Zhao (Black Claw)**, a one-eyed black panther beastkin, a bounty hunter. When he walked into the safehouse, Chen Mo felt the temperature in the air seem to drop several degrees. That panther was lean and wiry, his black fur gleaming with a dark blue luster under the light, like a carefully polished obsidian stone. His left eye was covered by a leather eyepatch, while his right eye shone with a cold light.

He carried a scent of blood—not fresh, but the aura long steeped in killing, mixed with leather, gunpowder, and some wild musk. That was the scent of a top predator, dangerous and deadly.

"I'm not here for justice," he stated bluntly, his voice low and hoarse, like sandpaper rubbing metal. He leaned against the doorframe, his posture lazy but full of threat. "I'm here for money. Your price is high enough, I'll do it."

"We have no money," Bai Ya said, looking directly into Black Claw's eyes without flinching.

"I know," Black Claw smiled, revealing a mouthful of sharp fangs, that smile devoid of any warmth. "So I'll change the terms—after success, I want all of Beastshield's research data on 'Soul Hunters.'"

"Soul Hunters?" Chen Mo asked. He could feel his fur standing up slightly from alertness.

"A type of bounty hunter specifically designed to kill Descenders," Black Claw's gaze turned cold, that lone eye burning with flames of hatred. "They killed my mate. I want to know who trained them, and then..." He didn't finish, but Chen Mo could feel that killing intent—cold, sharp, like an unsheathed blade.

The second was **Tie Xin (Iron Heart)**, a mechanically modified goat beastkin. Half his body was metal, his left arm completely replaced by machinery, his eyes gleaming with electronic light. When he walked into the room, Chen Mo could hear the faint hum of mechanical joints, like the wingbeats of some insect.

There was no biological scent on him, or rather, that scent was masked by the smells of metal and machine oil. It was a cold, mechanical, precise smell, making one think of operating rooms and factories.

"I don't care about your war," his voice carried an electronic synthetic echo, every word as precise as if calculated. "But Beastshield stole my technology. I want it back."

He raised that mechanical arm, the metal surface reflecting the light. "This arm is their 'masterpiece.' Three years ago, they captured me and performed so-called 'voluntary modification.'" There was no pain in his tone, only a cold anger. "I want them to know that stolen things come with a price."

The third was the most unexpected to Chen Mo—**Xiao Hua (Little Flower)**, a young rabbit beastkin who looked barely in his teens. He wasn't a warrior, not a hunter, but a... healer?

When he walked into the room, he brought a fresh scent—the clear fragrance of herbs, mixed with some warm, soft floral aroma. That was a smell that made one think of spring and fields, forming a sharp contrast with Black Claw's bloody scent and Iron Heart's metallic smell.

He was petite, with soft, fluffy white fur, large and soft ears always trembling slightly, as if capturing the tiniest sounds around him. His eyes were light pink, clear to the bottom, carrying a comforting gentleness.

"I can repair spiritual damage," he said softly, his voice as thin as a mosquito's whine, but every word clearly audible. "You will all need me."

"I found him," Bai Ya explained, a kind of... brotherly tenderness? in his gaze toward Little Flower. "After the spiritual virus is released, it might cause spiritual trauma to those around. We need someone who can handle that situation."

"And me," G-1172 said. "Though I'm not strong, I know the lab's layout. I can guide the way." His wolf claws tightened on the tabletop, gray eyes gleaming with determination.

"You sure?" Chen Mo asked. "There are bad memories there..."

"Precisely because there are bad memories," G-1172 said, his gaze firm, "I have to go back. Not for revenge, but for... closure. To say goodbye to that imprisoned self."

Chen Mo looked at these people.

Black Claw, for revenge. His body carried the pain of loss, that pain already transformed into a sharp blade.

Iron Heart, for technology. His body was modified, his dignity trampled; he wanted to reclaim what belonged to him.

Little Flower, to help others. He looked the most fragile, but was perhaps the strongest of all—because he chose to face pain, chose to heal others.

G-1172, for closure. To walk out from the shadows of the past, to prove that he was still alive.

Lao Ba, to repay an old debt. Those cloudy bear eyes hid too many stories, too many regrets.

Bai Ya, to stop disaster. To complete his mentor's unfinished business, to protect more people from harm.

Everyone had their own reasons, everyone carried their own pain. But now, they gathered together, for the same goal.

"Do we need more people?" Chen Mo asked.

"No," Bai Ya said. "Too many people make exposure easy. Seven of us is just right."

"Seven?" Chen Mo counted. "Bai Ya, Lao Ba, me, G-1172, Black Claw, Iron Heart, Little Flower—yes, seven."

"Seven," Lao Ba smiled, that smile carrying a hint of mystery. "A good number. In many cultures, seven represents completeness."

"Or the seven deadly sins," Black Claw said coldly, that lone eye flashing with mockery.

"Or the seven virtues," Little Flower said softly, pink eyes carrying hope.

Bai Ya raised his paw, stopping the debate.

"Whatever it represents," he said, "we start training tomorrow. Action in three days."

**Scene 4: Training**

The following three days were the most arduous of Chen Mo's life.

On the first day, Bai Ya taught him how to control his enhanced spiritual power—how to precisely replicate others' spiritual characteristics, how to maintain focus in chaos, how to release the spiritual virus without harming innocents.

They sat on the safehouse roof, night wind blowing through their fur. Chen Mo could smell the city's scents—the pungent exhaust of cars, the food aromas drifting from distant restaurants, and the urine smell of stray beastkin from some corner downstairs. But above all these was Bai Ya's dry herbal fragrance, the mark of his concentration.

"The spiritual virus is like... a fire," Bai Ya said, red eyes gleaming in the moonlight. "What you need to do is ignite a specific pile of firewood, not burn the whole forest."

Chen Mo practiced again and again.

He used his spiritual power to sense Bai Ya's spiritual characteristics, then tried to replicate them. At first, he could only replicate a part, and it would quickly collapse. That feeling was like trying to pick up a tiny bead with clumsy claws—the harder he tried, the more he failed.

"Don't rush," Bai Ya said, his voice especially gentle in the night. "Feel my spiritual power, like feeling my body temperature. Don't try to grasp it; let it flow into your consciousness."

Chen Mo closed his eyes, breathing deeply. The night wind brought Bai Ya's scent—dry grass, mixed with a faint medicinal fragrance. He let his spiritual power flow like water, touching Bai Ya's spiritual light-cluster.

He "saw" it—Bai Ya's spiritual characteristics. It was a complex, multi-layered structure, like a precision mechanical clock, every gear meshing exactly. And at the very center, there was a patch of... darkness?

"What is that?" Chen Mo asked, pointing at that darkness. His voice trembled with surprise.

Bai Ya was silent for a moment. Moonlight shone on his profile, outlining his soft contours—that delicate face unique to rabbit beastkin, but now carrying an ancient sorrow.

"My death," he said, his voice as light as a sigh. "The mark left by my first death. It has always been in the depths of my consciousness."

"Death... can leave marks?"

"It can." Bai Ya turned to him, red eyes gleaming with some profound light. "Every near-death, every loss, leaves a mark on consciousness. They shape us, make us who we are now. Like scars, they are proof of pain, and also... proof of survival."

Chen Mo thought of his own marks.

G-892's death. That bear beastkin fallen in a pool of blood, eyes losing their luster, body gradually growing cold.

Xiao Hui's death. That thin mouse beastkin, sacrificing himself to save him.

Those test subjects. Those innocent lives dissected and tortured before his eyes.

These marks were also shaping him.

On the second day, besides spiritual power training, he had to learn combat.

Black Claw taught him close-quarters combat. In the safehouse basement, that black panther was like a bolt of black lightning, his movements elegant and deadly.

"You're not a warrior," Black Claw said, his voice echoing in the empty basement. "But you can learn how to survive in battle."
He demonstrated a throat-locking move, muscles flowing under his black fur like winding rivers. "Use your weight, your strength. You're a bear, not a cat. Don't try to become agile; become... unshakable."

Chen Mo tried to imitate his movements, but was as clumsy as if drunk. His paws were too big, his body too heavy.

"Don't think about defeating your opponent," Black Claw corrected him, that lone eye flashing with impatience, but more with... focus? "Think about controlling your opponent. Grab him, pin him, make him unable to move."

He let Chen Mo try to catch him. Chen Mo lunged, but Black Claw easily dodged, then gave him a light push from behind, making him lose balance.

"Too slow," Black Claw said. "And your intent is too obvious. In battle, hide your intent until the last moment."

Again and again, Chen Mo fell, got up, fell again. Sweat soaked his fur, emitting a strong male scent. His muscles ached, claws trembling from overexertion. But slowly, he began to understand Black Claw's words—not about becoming faster, but about becoming... more patient.

Iron Heart taught him how to use simple mechanical devices—jammers, detonators, trackers. Those cold metals gleamed under Little Flower's light.

"This," Iron Heart picked up a small disc, metal fingers nimbly operating the buttons on it. "Is a jammer. Can block all electronic signals within a fifty-meter radius. Duration: thirty seconds." His electronic eye flashed blue light. "At critical moments, thirty seconds is enough to decide life and death."

Chen Mo took the jammer, feeling the cold of the metal transmitting through his fur to his palm. The weight was light, but the meaning it carried was heavy.

On the third night, Chen Mo could already:

- Precisely replicate Bai Ya's and Lao Ba's spiritual characteristics
- Last ten minutes undefeated in simulated combat
- Precisely locate the spiritual virus's release range
- Cover a five-hundred-meter radius with spiritual perception

He stood on the safehouse roof, looking at the night sky. Stars twinkled overhead, like countless eyes watching him. His body was exhausted—every muscle screaming for rest, every nerve stretched to the limit. But at the same time, he felt a strange... fulfillment.

"Not enough," Bai Ya said, walking to his side. Night wind stirred his white fur, making him look like a drifting cloud. "But enough."

"Enough for what?"

"Enough to go get yourself killed," Black Claw's voice came from behind. Chen Mo turned back to see that black panther leaning against the doorframe, moonlight flowing on his black fur. Though his tone was cold, there was a trace of... approval in his eyes? "You're much stronger than three days ago. At least... you won't die immediately."

"Enough to try," Bai Ya corrected, then turned to Chen Mo, his gaze carrying a profound tenderness. "And then come back alive."

**Scene 5: Unexpected Intelligence**

The night before the operation, an unexpected visitor came to the safehouse.

It was a beastkin Chen Mo had never seen before—a thin, sickly-looking bat beastkin. His wings were injured, a clear tear in the membrane, unable to fly, only able to crawl with his arms. When he crawled into the safehouse, he left a faint trail of blood.

He carried a sickly aura—a sweet smell of decay, mixed with the rusty scent of blood. That was a smell that made one think of hospitals, wards, death.

"I'm... an informant for the underground network," he gasped, his voice high and trembling. His small eyes looked especially large on his thin face, flashing with fear. "There's... urgent intelligence..."

"Speak," Bai Ya helped him sit down, his movements gentle. Little Flower immediately stepped forward, beginning to examine his wounds.

"Hu Lin... and Tiejiao," the bat beastkin said, panting after every word. "They know you're coming. They've set a trap."

Chen Mo's heart sank. He felt a chill rising from his spine, making his fur stand up involuntarily.

"How?" Lao Ba asked, his voice carrying doubt. "Only seven of us know the plan."

"It wasn't leaked by your people," the bat beastkin said, small eyes flashing with fear. "It was... Hu Lin. He has an ability to read the memories of the dead. He read the plan from Xiao Hui's... corpse."

Xiao Hui's corpse.

Chen Mo felt a wave of dizziness. Xiao Hui was already dead? And... being exploited by Hu Lin? That anger was like a fire, burning in his chest. His claws tightened, embedding into his palms, bringing a sharp pain.

"Damn," Black Claw cursed, that lone eye burning with killing intent. "Then our plan is already exposed."

"Not necessarily," Bai Ya said, his gaze calm, but Chen Mo could see his claws trembling slightly. "Knowing we're coming and knowing exactly how we're coming are two different things."

"But they'll strengthen defenses..."

"Yes," Bai Ya admitted. "So we need to change the plan."

He looked at Chen Mo, those red eyes gleaming with determination.

"The original plan was infiltration. Now, we need... a frontal assault."

"Frontal assault?" Iron Heart frowned, his mechanical fingers making a faint humming sound. "We only have seven people."

"Seven people," Bai Ya said, "but if all seven are strong enough, fast enough, unexpected enough..."

He spread out the map, pointing at another spot. That was a ventilation system entrance, located on the other side of the facility.

"Here, the ventilation system entrance. Chen Mo, how far can your spiritual perception reach?"

"About five hundred meters," Chen Mo said. He could feel his heartbeat accelerating.

"Enough. From here, you can sense the Core Data Room's location, and then..."

"Directly release the spiritual virus?" Chen Mo understood, his voice carrying surprise. "Without entering the Core Data Room?"

"Yes. As long as the distance is close enough, the spiritual virus can penetrate walls and infect the data system." Bai Ya's eyes gleamed in the light. "But this way, the risk of exposure is greater. Once discovered, you'll be surrounded."

"So we need bait," Bai Ya said. "To distract them."

He looked at Black Claw, Iron Heart, Lao Ba.

"You three, attack from the main entrance. Create chaos, draw the main force."

"Suicide mission," Black Claw smiled, that smile carrying a trace of madness. "I like it."

"G-1172 and Little Flower," Bai Ya continued. "You wait at the alternate entrance of the ventilation system. If Chen Mo fails, you retreat. If successful, you're responsible for extraction."

"What about me?" Chen Mo asked.

"You and me," Bai Ya said. "We take the ventilation system, straight to above the Core Data Room. Then, you release the virus, and I protect you."

"That simple?"

"That simple," Bai Ya said. "And that dangerous."

**Scene 6: Preparation**

That night, everyone prepared in their own way.

Black Claw was sharpening a knife, a sharp dagger gleaming coldly under the light. He sharpened slowly, rhythmically, every friction producing a tooth-aching sound. That lone eye stared intently at the blade, as if looking at something distant.

Iron Heart was checking his mechanical devices, again and again, ensuring every part functioned normally. His electronic eye flashed blue light, metal fingers moving nimbly through complex circuits.

Little Flower was organizing herbs and bandages, preparing for possible casualties. His movements were gentle and precise, pink eyes carrying a comforting focus.

G-1172 sat in the corner, eyes closed, whether resting or recalling, unknown. His wolf claws unconsciously scratched the floor, making faint scraping sounds.

Lao Ba was writing a letter. Chen Mo stole a glance and found it was addressed to someone called "A Hua"—perhaps his family, perhaps his lover. Lao Ba's claws held the pen, his movements clumsy but earnest. Every word seemed squeezed from the bottom of his heart.

"Lao Ba," Chen Mo walked over. "You..."

"Don't talk," Lao Ba didn't look up, his voice low. "Let me finish."

Chen Mo fell silent.

He looked at Bai Ya. That white rabbit stood by the window, looking at the night sky, red eyes glowing in the darkness. Moonlight spilled on his white fur, making him look like a statue.

"Bai Ya," Chen Mo walked over, standing beside him. He could smell the scents brought by the night wind—the city's hustle and bustle, the distant ocean, and Bai Ya's dry herbal fragrance.

"What are you thinking about?"

"Thinking about my mentor," Bai Ya said, his voice as light as a sigh. "Before he died, he also did something similar. Infiltrate enemy camp, destroy core facility."

"Did he succeed?"

"He succeeded. But he didn't come out." Bai Ya turned to Chen Mo, red eyes gleaming with some profound light. "He traded his life for the success of that operation."

Chen Mo fell silent.

"Afraid?" Bai Ya asked.

"Afraid," Chen Mo admitted. "But more than that... uncertain. I'm not sure if I can do it. Replicating spiritual characteristics, releasing the virus, coming out alive... every step could fail."

"Then think of Xiao Hui," Bai Ya said. "Think of why he chose to help you."

"He said... he wanted to do the right thing, even if only once."

"Right." Bai Ya turned to him, his gaze carrying a profound tenderness. "Tomorrow will be your 'once.'"

Chen Mo took a deep breath, night wind filling his lungs, bringing a chill.

"Bai Ya, if... if I don't make it out..."

"You will make it out," Bai Ya interrupted him, his voice carrying unshakable firmness. "Because I won't let you die. I owe my mentor a life, and I'll repay it by protecting you."

He reached out, placing his paw on Chen Mo's shoulder. Those soft rabbit paws were warm and powerful, the temperature of the paw pads transmitting through the fabric.

"And, Chen Mo, you still have many things to do. This is just the beginning, not the end."

Chen Mo looked into Bai Ya's eyes, those red eyes burning with some indescribable power. At that moment, he felt a strange calm—not because he was no longer afraid, but because he knew that no matter what happened, he wasn't fighting alone.

"Okay," he said. "I'll come out alive."

---

**Scene 7: Hu Lin's Game**

Meanwhile, in the depths of Beastshield's underground, Hu Lin sat in his office.

The room's lighting was dim, only the screen before him emitting a faint blue glow. That light shone on his face, outlining his sinister contours—that hyena's pointed muzzle rose slightly, revealing a triumphant smile.

Bandages still wrapped around his back, faint bloodstains seeping through the white gauze. Xiao Hui's knife had nearly taken his life, but it didn't matter. He had survived. And from Xiao Hui's memories, he had obtained valuable intelligence.

"Bai Ya," he murmured this name, his voice carrying a hint of amusement. "And that little bear..."

On the screen before him, the safehouse's location was displayed, along with the profiles of those seven people. Every photo, every name, every background, clearly presented before his eyes.

"Think I don't know?" He laughed, that laughter echoing in the empty office, carrying a trace of madness. "Think changing the plan can fool me?"

He pressed the communicator, the button making a slight click.

"Tiejiao, how are preparations?"

"Ready," Tiejiao's voice came from the speaker, low and cold. "The trap is set. Once they enter, they won't be able to fly away."

"No," Hu Lin said, his voice carrying a commanding authority. "Don't kill them. Especially that little bear—I want him alive."

"Why?"

"Because of his spiritual power," Hu Lin's gaze turned fanatical, the screen's blue light dancing in his pupils. "After enhancer strengthening, his spiritual power has surpassed the scope of ordinary Descenders. If we can study his consciousness, our technology will make a breakthrough." He paused, licking his lips. "Imagine—a living, powerful Descender, his spiritual power can be replicated, transplanted, utilized..."

"What about Bai Ya?"

"Bai Ya..." Hu Lin paused, his gaze turning cold. "Bai Ya must die. He knows too much. And..." His voice dropped, carrying a hint of personal grudge. "He's that person's disciple. As long as he's alive, he's a threat."

He stood up, walking to the window, looking at the underground world outside. That was a massive cavern, countless lights twinkling in the darkness, like an underground starry sky. But Hu Lin knew, beneath this beautiful surface, how many dirty secrets were hidden.

"Tomorrow," he said, his voice carrying the confidence of one holding victory in his hands. "Everything will end."

---

**Scene 8: Before Dawn**

The last few hours before the operation.

Chen Mo didn't sleep. He sat on the roof, watching the eastern sky slowly brighten. Night was receding, replaced by a deep blue—that was the color before dawn, the darkest, yet closest to light.

G-1172 came onto the roof, sitting beside him. Chen Mo could smell the scent unique to him—a wild, slightly damp smell, mixed with the complex odors of fear and determination.

"Can't sleep?" G-1172 asked, his voice especially clear in the silence.

"Can't sleep," Chen Mo said. "You?"

"Can't sleep either." G-1172 looked at his own hands, gray fur looking dull in the dawn's faint light. "I've been thinking... if I die tomorrow, will it be better than dying as a test subject?"

"You won't die," Chen Mo said, his voice carrying a firmness that even he found surprising. "I promise."

"You can't promise that," G-1172 smiled, though the smile carried bitterness, there was warmth in his eyes. "No one can promise that."

He looked at Chen Mo, those gray eyes flashing with complex emotions.

"But thank you. Thank you for saving me. Thank you... for letting me have the chance to choose my own way to die." His voice dropped. "In that lab, I thought I would end up like those test subjects, cut open, studied, then discarded. But now... at least I have a choice."

"Don't talk about dying," Chen Mo said. "We're coming out alive together."

"Hopefully," G-1172 said. "If... if I really die, please tell my family. Tell them I'm not some bastard addicted to games who won't come home. Tell them I'm trying to do the right thing."

"You tell them yourself," Chen Mo said. "When this is over, we'll go find them together."

G-1172 looked at Chen Mo, for a long time. The dawn's faint light began to dye the eastern sky red, gilding their outlines with gold.

"You know?" he said. "You're completely different from when I first met you."

"How different?"

"Back then, watching that wild boar die, you would vomit, would be afraid." G-1172's voice carried a hint of nostalgia. "Now... you're still afraid, but you no longer run."

Chen Mo fell silent.

He was right. He was still afraid. Afraid of death, afraid of failure, afraid of loss.

But he no longer ran.

"Because I understand," Chen Mo said, his voice especially clear in the dawn light. "Running solves no problems. Only by facing them is there a chance to change."

In the east, the first ray of sunlight pierced the darkness.

That was a golden light, like the embodiment of hope, piercing through the curtain of night. It shone on Chen Mo's face, warm and bright, making him squint.

The sky had brightened.

---

**Scene 9: Departure**

Early morning, seven people stood at the safehouse door.

Bai Ya looked at everyone—Lao Ba, Black Claw, Iron Heart, Little Flower, G-1172, Chen Mo. His gaze lingered on each person, as if etching their appearances into memory.

"Remember our goal," he said, his voice especially clear in the morning air. "Destroy core data, stop consciousness transfer technology. Not for revenge, but to protect more people from harm."

"What if we encounter civilians?" Little Flower asked, his voice thin but clearly audible.

"Try not to harm innocents," Bai Ya said. "But if you must choose... protect yourself."

"What if we encounter Hu Lin or Tiejiao?" Black Claw asked, that lone eye flashing with the desire for battle.

"Retreat," Bai Ya said, his tone firm. "Our goal isn't to kill, but the data. Don't get entangled in battle."

"If..." G-1172 spoke, his voice somewhat trembling. "If I hold everyone back..."

"You won't," Chen Mo said, looking into G-1172's eyes. "I believe in you."

G-1172 looked at Chen Mo, his eye sockets somewhat red. He took a deep breath, nodding.

"Okay," he said. "I believe in you, and you believe in me."

Bai Ya raised his paw, seven different claws stacking together. Different colors, different shapes, different temperatures, but at this moment, they gathered together, forming a whole.

"For Xiao Hui," he said.

"For Xiao Hui," the others responded, voices merging as one.

Then, they separated, heading toward their respective targets.

Lao Ba, Black Claw, Iron Heart walked toward the main entrance. Their silhouettes stretched long in the sunlight, like black cutouts.

Little Flower and G-1172 walked toward the alternate entrance of the ventilation system. Their steps were light and cautious, as if trying to blend into the surrounding shadows.

Bai Ya and Chen Mo walked toward that ventilation pipe leading to the core.

Chen Mo looked back. Sunlight spilled over the city; everything looked so ordinary, so peaceful. Pedestrians walked on the streets, vendors called out their wares, children played.

But he knew, beneath this ordinary surface, a war was about to erupt.

And he was walking toward the center of the war.

"Ready?" Bai Ya asked, his voice echoing at the ventilation pipe entrance.

Chen Mo took a deep breath. The air held the smell of dust, the cold of metal, and Bai Ya's dry herbal fragrance.

"Ready," he said.

They crawled into the ventilation pipe, disappearing into the darkness.

The war had begun.

---

**Chapter 5 END**

> *"The darkness before dawn is the deepest, but dawn will come nonetheless. No matter what happens tomorrow, at least tonight, we choose to fight. When seven different souls gather for a single purpose, when the flames of vengeance intertwine with the hope of redemption, we know—tomorrow will change everything."*

## Story Commentary and Reflections

### Their Final Stories

【Deceased → Dead-G-3-Mouse Beastkin-Xiao Hui】

His name was Xiao Hui. No one knew his real name. In this underground world of only numbers, "Xiao Hui" was the name Chen Mo gave him—because when that little bear first saw him, he was curled up in a corner of the Data Collection Room, his gray fur covered in dust, looking just like an abandoned little gray ball.

Xiao Hui was a mouse beastkin, typical rodent features—small stature, pointed muzzle, large round ears always alertly perked up. His fur was gray, soft but dull, like plants long deprived of sunlight. His eyes were black, small, always evasive, afraid to meet others' gazes. His body was thin, ribs faintly visible beneath thin fur, the marks of long-term malnutrition and overwork.

But beneath this thin body beat a... kind heart.

Xiao Hui was originally an ordinary programmer in the surface world, in love with a human, living an ordinary but happy life. But disaster came too suddenly—a beastification, an失控, he killed his lover. When he regained consciousness, what he saw was blood everywhere, and those eyes that would never open again.

At that moment, his world collapsed.

He chose self-exile, coming to the underground world, becoming a bottom-level employee of Beastshield. Number G-3, data collector. Working sixteen hours a day, processing endless experimental data—those records of innocent people's pain. He looked at those numbers, every one representing a life, a dream, a crushed future.

He thought of resisting, but he was too weak. He was just a mouse; in this world of the strong preying on the weak, even self-preservation was a luxury.

Until he met Chen Mo.

That little bear had the same eyes as him—confused, fearful, but with a trace of... unwillingness deep inside. Xiao Hui saw himself in him—that self who once wanted to do the right thing.

He began helping Chen Mo. Secretly passing him intelligence, telling him the lab's layout, teaching him how to survive in this cruel world. Every act of help was a risk. But he didn't care.

"I want to do the right thing, even if only once," he said to Chen Mo.

He didn't know that this sentence would become his last words.

That final night, when Hu Lin's men surrounded the safehouse, Xiao Hui made his choice. He picked up a scalpel—that tool he commonly used in the lab, sharp, cold, a tool for cutting flesh.

He stabbed toward Hu Lin.

That knife didn't kill Hu Lin, but bought Chen Mo escape time. When Tiejiao's large hand gripped his neck, Xiao Hui felt no fear. He watched Chen Mo disappear into the ventilation pipe, a trace of... smile appearing at the corner of his mouth.

"At least this once," he thought, "I did the right thing."

Tiejiao snapped his neck. In that instant, what Xiao Hui felt wasn't pain, but... relief. He was finally freed from that lab full of sin, freed from those endless data, freed from that nightmare of killing his lover.

His corpse was taken away by Hu Lin. That hyena had a cruel ability—he could read the memories of the dead. Xiao Hui's body was dissected, studied, exploited. His last bit of value was also squeezed dry.

No one mourned for him. In this underground world, death was too common; the death of a bottom-level employee was like a drop of water falling into the sea, stirring not a single ripple.

But Chen Mo remembered him. Bai Ya remembered him. G-1172 remembered him.

That early morning, when seven people gathered at the safehouse door, they said in unison: "For Xiao Hui."

That was a memorial, and also a promise—a promise not to let such sacrifice be in vain, a promise to make this world better, even if only a little.

Xiao Hui, that gray mouse, finally in death got what he wanted—to do the right thing, even if only once.

### Those Still Alive

【Living → Living-Player-Chen Mo】

Chen Mo stood at the ventilation pipe entrance, darkness gaping wide before him. He could feel his heartbeat, steady and powerful, no longer the panic of when he had first Descended.

The enhancer flowed in his veins, like a blazing river. His spiritual power pulsed at the edge of his consciousness, like a vast net, sensing everything around him. He could "see" Bai Ya's spiritual light-cluster—silver-white, warm, right beside him.

Tomorrow, the war would begin.

He no longer ran, no longer escaped. He chose to face, chose to fight, chose to change this world in his own way.

Xiao Hui's death left a deep mark in his heart. That gray mouse, sacrificing himself to save him. Chen Mo knew he couldn't let such sacrifice be in vain. He must live, must complete the mission, must make Beastshield pay the price.

But at the same time, he also felt fear—fear of death, fear of failure, fear of losing more friends. This fear didn't make him retreat, but made him more clear-headed. He knew what he was doing, knew the cost, knew what he might lose.

And this was the proof of growth.

【Living → Living-O-7-White Tiger Beastkin-Bai Ya】

Bai Ya walked in front of Chen Mo, white fur almost glowing in the darkness. His ears perked up alertly, capturing the tiniest sounds in the ventilation pipe.

He had already lost too much. Mentor, friends, companions. Every loss left a mark in his consciousness—those death marks, like scars one after another, carved into the depths of his soul.

But he wasn't defeated. On the contrary, these marks made him stronger, more determined. He knew the pain of loss, so he cherished the people before him even more. He knew the terror of death, so he longed even more to protect life.

Chen Mo reminded him of his mentor—that persistence, that kindness, that desire to change the world. He wouldn't let Chen Mo repeat his mentor's mistakes. This time, he would protect his apprentice well, even at the cost of his life.

【Living → Living-G-1172-Wolf Beastkin-G-1172】

G-1172 stood at the alternate entrance of the ventilation system, waiting for his task. His wolf claws unconsciously scratched the wall, leaving shallow marks.

He never thought he would participate in such an operation. A month ago, he was still an imprisoned test subject, living every day in fear and pain. Now, he stood here, preparing to fight for freedom, for justice, for... closure.

Xiao Hui's death made him understand one thing—in this world, no one will come to save you. You must stand up yourself, fight yourself, choose your own fate.

He chose to fight. Not for revenge, but to bid farewell to that weak self, to prove he was still alive, for... hope.

【Living → Living-Bear Beastkin-Lao Ba】

Lao Ba walked toward the main entrance, heavy footsteps echoing in the corridor. His claws held a letter—a letter to A Hua. If he couldn't return, it would be his last words.

He had lived too long, witnessed too much death and betrayal. He once thought his heart had already numbed, no longer moved by anyone or anything.

But Xiao Hui's death changed him. That gray mouse made him think of many years ago—that time when he too was young, idealistic, wanting to change the world.

He chose to participate in this operation, not to repay an old debt, but for... atonement. For that once-weak self, for those he failed to protect, for the mistakes he made.

This time, he wouldn't retreat.

【Living → Living-Black Panther Beastkin-Black Claw】

Black Claw licked his claws, that lone eye flashing with the desire for battle. He smelled blood—the blood that was about to be spilled.

He wasn't a hero, nor a messenger of justice. He was a bounty hunter, a killer, a man who would stop at nothing to survive.

But he also had his bottom line—those who killed his mate must pay the price. Beastshield's "Soul Hunter" project must be destroyed.

He didn't care whether this operation would succeed, didn't care whether people would die. He only cared about one thing—revenge.

【Living → Living-Mechanically Modified Goat-Iron Heart】

Iron Heart checked his mechanical arm, metal joints making a faint humming sound. His electronic eye flashed blue light, scanning the surrounding environment.

He didn't care about justice, didn't care about evil. He was just a victim who had everything taken from him, a avenger who wanted to reclaim what belonged to him.

Beastshield stole his body, his technology, his dignity. He would make them pay, make them know that machines also have souls, that modified people also have dignity.

【Living → Living-Rabbit Beastkin-Little Flower】

Little Flower organized his medicine bag, pink eyes carrying worry. He knew how many people would be injured in tomorrow's battle, how many would die.

But he chose to face it. Not because of bravery, but because—someone had to do it. Someone had to light a lamp in the darkness, someone had to give hope in despair.

He was a healer; his mission was to heal. No matter what happened tomorrow, he would do his utmost to alleviate pain, to save lives.

### Story Reflections

This is a story about choices.

In this dark world, everyone is making choices—choosing survival or death, choosing submission or resistance, choosing selfishness or sacrifice.

Xiao Hui chose sacrifice. He traded his life for Chen Mo's escape, fulfilling his wish to "do the right thing once." His death was tragic, but also... complete. At the last moment of life, he finally found his own value, finally no longer a slave defined by a number, but an individual with dignity and choice.

Chen Mo chose to face. From a player who wanted to run, to a warrior preparing to fight, his growth was painful, but also inevitable. Xiao Hui's death, G-892's death, the deaths of those test subjects in the lab—these deaths left marks in his heart, shaping his soul. He no longer lived just for himself, but for those who died, for those still alive, for a better world.

And the others—Bai Ya, Lao Ba, Black Claw, Iron Heart, Little Flower, G-1172—they were all making choices. Everyone had their own reasons, everyone carried their own pain, but at this moment, they gathered together, for the same goal.

This is the complexity of human nature. We are not simply good or evil, not simply black or white. We are gray, contradictory, beings who search for hope in pain, who persist in fighting in despair.

Tomorrow, the war will begin. I don't know who will survive, who will die. But I know that no matter the outcome, these choices are meaningful. Because they prove—even in the darkest world, there are people fighting for the light.

And that is where hope lies.
