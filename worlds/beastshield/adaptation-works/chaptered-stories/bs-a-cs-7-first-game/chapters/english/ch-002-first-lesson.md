**Scene-1 The Heaviness of Morning Waking**

Chen Mo was awakened by his own weight.

It was a sensation he had never experienced—not pain, but rather... the oppression of existence itself. A body of over two hundred pounds pressed against the straw mat, the straw mat against the wooden floor. He could feel the texture of every straw, could feel the slight deformation of the floorboards, could feel the natural curve of his spine under gravity's pull.

He opened his eyes.

Light filtered in through the gaps in the window, that distinctive pale-golden light of early morning. Dust drifted slowly in the beams, each particle illuminated, like a silent dance. Chen Mo stared at that dust, taking a full minute to completely wake up.

His nose began working first.

It was a complex, multi-layered scent. First, the smell of the room itself—rotting wood, dry straw, and some... small animal's urine. Then, from farther away: the aroma of food, some kind of meat frying in grease, and...

And the scent of another living being.

Not his own. Another male. Carrying a faint soap fragrance, some herb's bitterness, and... Bai Ya's unique scent, which Chen Mo still could not name.

"Awake?"

That voice came from the kitchen, accompanied by the light clink of metal against pottery. Chen Mo tried to sit up; the movement was more difficult than he had imagined—abdominal muscles needed to contract, back muscles needed to support, and that thick fat layer would jiggle slightly with the motion, creating extra inertia.

He spent thirty seconds completing this action.

"Take it slow," Bai Ya's voice came again, carrying a tone Chen Mo still couldn't judge as sarcastic or caring. "Your nervous system is still adapting. This body wasn't designed for you; you need to learn... renegotiation."

"Renegotiation?"

"With your body," Bai Ya said, "with its weight, its balance, its... desires."

Chen Mo finally stood up.

He looked down at his hands—those bear paws covered in dark brown fur. In the morning light, he could see more details: the sheen of the fur, the direction of the palm lines, the wear at the edges of the nails. His palms felt more... real than last night. More like a tool he had to learn to use.

He walked toward the kitchen.

Every step was a lesson. The contact between footpad and floor, the shift of center of gravity, the coordination of leg muscles. He could feel his thigh muscles contracting as they supported his weight, could feel the slight jiggle of gluteal fat, could feel the slight impact on his spinal column with every step.

The kitchen was small, but tidy.

Bai Ya stood before the stove, back to Chen Mo. His white apron was especially striking in the morning light—pure white, without any pattern, almost blending with his pure white fur. Chen Mo could see the knot of the apron ties behind his back, could see the outline of that slender waist beneath the apron.

Bai Ya was even smaller and thinner than he had imagined.

But within that small body, there was a... tension. Like a fully drawn bowstring, seemingly fragile, yet containing power that could burst forth at any moment.

"Sit," Bai Ya didn't turn around, pointing to a low stool beside the stove. "There's a stool by the stove. Be careful, don't break it."

Chen Mo carefully walked toward that low stool.

It was wooden, looked somewhat old, its surface polished smooth by countless uses. Chen Mo could feel the hardness of the stool, could feel its slight groan under his weight. When he sat down, abdominal fat piled up above his waistband, forming a soft fold. His thighs were too thick, forcing him to spread to the sides, occupying more space than normal.

"How did you sleep last night?" Bai Ya asked.

He turned around, holding a pottery plate in his hands. Morning light shone in from the window behind him, gilding his white fur with a golden edge. Chen Mo could see his every detail—those red eyes presenting a transparent quality in the light, like two polished garnets. His nose was pink, twitching slightly, sniffing the scents in the air. His ears, those long ears gently swaying atop his head, one of them had a notch at the tip that Chen Mo hadn't noticed last night.

"Not well," Chen Mo answered honestly. His voice was clearer than last night, his throat more skilled at controlling this body's vocal cords. "Kept dreaming. Dreamed of that wild boar, and... blood."

"Normal," Bai Ya placed the pottery plate before Chen Mo. It was a rough ceramic plate, with a chip at the edge presenting a sharp white fracture surface. On the plate was some kind of... meat? "Everyone's like that the first time they see blood. I had a friend who dreamed of the first person he killed for a whole month. Later he discovered that person was actually a good person—at least better than he imagined."

Chen Mo looked down at the food on the plate.

It was some kind of meat, cut into thick slices, the surface presenting an appetizing golden-brown color. A thin layer of grease formed a glossy sheen on the meat surface, glinting in the morning light. Beside it were some green leaves and a few red berries.

"What meat is this?" Chen Mo asked.

"Rat," Bai Ya said lightly. He sat down across from Chen Mo and began eating his own portion. "Caught in the sewer, nice and fat."

Chen Mo froze.

"Don't worry, it's not poisonous," Bai Ya chewed, his jaw displaying a rodent's characteristic rapid chewing rhythm while eating. "The ingredients in this world are different from ours... from yours. The rats here grow very large, cat-sized, and the meat is pretty good. Just troublesome to process—have to clean out the innards."

Chen Mo looked at the meat on the plate, his stomach beginning to twitch.

But Bai Ya had already started eating, and eating with relish. Chen Mo could hear the slight sounds of his chewing, could smell the rising heat from his plate—the aroma of grease, the char of protein, and some... spice Bai Ya had added.

"You're not eating?" Bai Ya glanced at him, those red eyes devoid of any emotional fluctuation. "Then give it to me. Don't waste food."

"No, I'll eat," Chen Mo gritted his teeth.

He reached out—those massive, fur-covered bear paws. He clumsily picked up the tableware, a fork carved from wood, its handle polished smooth by countless uses. He used the fork to spear a piece of meat, lifting it to his mouth.

He could feel the weight of that piece of meat, could feel the lubrication formed by grease on its surface, could feel the temperature of the heat brushing his cheek.

He bit down.

The taste was more complex than he had imagined.

First came the aroma of grease, that rich animal fat flavor spreading in his mouth. Then the meat itself—firm, chewy, carrying a faint, gamey sweetness unique to wild meat. The crispy outer skin shattered under tooth pressure, revealing the juicy tender meat beneath. The spices Bai Ya added gradually released during chewing, bringing a flavor like rosemary mixed with pepper.

"How is it?" Bai Ya asked.

"...Pretty good," Chen Mo admitted. He forked up another piece, this time more decisively.

"This is the first lesson I want to teach you," Bai Ya said. He put down his utensils, looking into Chen Mo's eyes. Those red eyes in the morning light possessed a penetrating power that was unsettling, as if they could see straight to the deepest part of Chen Mo's thoughts. "Put aside the prejudices from your world. Everything here is different from there, including food, including morality, including... life and death."

He paused, looking out the window.

"In your world, eating rat might be considered disgusting, a sign of poverty. But here, being able to eat meat is luck. Many people, many beastmen, can't even eat rat."

Chen Mo fell silent.

He remembered last night's dead wild boar, G-892, remembered what Lao Ba had said. This world was truly different.

**Scene-2 Another Morning**

Meanwhile, at the other end of the city, a completely different morning was unfolding.

G-734 was a wild boar beastman, twenty-three years old, who had served in Beastshield Company for eight months. His real name didn't matter—at least not within Beastshield—there, he was just a designation, a consumable in a military-green uniform.

When he woke up, the first thing he smelled was the dormitory's odor.

That was the smell of over a dozen male beastmen living together—the sour stench of sweat, the astringency of leather, and some more secretive, personal scent accumulated beneath everyone's bedding. G-734's bed was in the corner of the dormitory, near the window, which allowed him to get a little thin sunlight in the morning.

He rolled out of bed, feeling the creak of his bed under his weight.

His build was the typical wild boar beastman—tall, burly, with a layer of fat accumulated in his abdomen, but shoulders and arms still maintaining the sturdiness characteristic of laborers. His fur was sparse, displaying a gray-brown hue, pink skin faintly visible between the gaps in fur. His tusks protruded from the corners of his mouth, already grown to thumb length, the edges worn from long-term chewing.

"Morning, 734."

A voice came from nearby. G-734 turned his head and saw G-721—a young dog beastman, tying his shoelaces. G-721 had only been here two months, still the kind of newcomer who would smile at every colleague.

"Morning," G-734 mumbled, beginning to get dressed.

His uniform was military-green, the standard issue for G-level grunts. Short-sleeved tight uniform, Beastshield's LOGO printed on the left chest, with golden vertical stripes on both sides. He put it on, feeling the fabric clinging tightly to his skin—that was synthetic fiber texture, rough, non-breathable, but durable enough.

Then the shorts. Also military-green, tight, with golden vertical stripes on both sides. G-734's thighs were thick; every time he put on these shorts he needed to adjust several times to find a comfortable position.

Finally, the socks.

Those white long socks.

G-734 sat down, picking up those socks. They were cotton, already washed somewhat gray, but still showing their original whiteness. Each sock had military-green stripes on both sides, with the word "SHIELD" printed in white within the stripes. And on the bottom—

His designation. G-734.

That was his identity marker, and also his death certificate. If he died, those socks would be stripped off, serving as proof for bounty exchange. Every Beastshield employee had a designation on the bottom of their socks; this was their only "identity proof" within this organization.

"Heard there's a new overseer coming today," G-721 said. He was before the mirror, straightening his uniform. That dog beastman's build was much smaller than G-734's; the military-green uniform looked somewhat loose on him. "Blue uniform, transferred from headquarters."

"Another one here to gild their resume," G-734 snorted. "These people, tired of staying up there, want to come down and 'experience the grassroots.' When they've had their fun, they go back to the office to enjoy air conditioning."

"Don't say that," G-721 looked around somewhat uneasily. There were other beastmen in the dormitory, some still sleeping, some already getting up. "What if someone hears..."

"Hears what?" G-734 said, but he lowered his voice. "We greenskins, in their eyes, are consumables. One dies, another is recruited. Do you know how many died in the East District last month?"

G-721 shook his head, his ears always drooping when nervous.

"Seventeen," G-734 said, "Seventeen greenskins, taken out by bounty hunters. Their bodies were thrown in the green belt, gnawed beyond recognition by wild dogs. Guess how the company handled it?"

"..."

"Did nothing." G-734 stood up, putting on those white athletic shoes. The soles were already worn down badly, but he had no money for new ones. "Just reissued seventeen new uniforms, recruited seventeen new people. That's all."

G-721 fell silent.

G-734 looked at this young dog beastman, a complex emotion welling up in his heart. He wanted to tell him, while it was still early, while he hadn't accumulated too much debt, while he hadn't been worn smooth of all his edges by this world, to get out quickly. Go find a civilian job, even if the income was lower, even if it was harder, but at least... at least he wouldn't one day have his throat cut, just for the designation on the bottom of his socks.

But he didn't say it.

Because he knew, G-721 was just like him, with no choice.

"Let's go," G-734 patted G-721's shoulder. That dog beastman's shoulder was thin, the outline of bones clearly visible beneath the fur. "To the cafeteria. There's braised pork today; if we're late, it'll be gone."

They walked out of the dormitory, merging into the morning flow of people.

**Scene-3 Lessons in the Kitchen**

Chen Mo finished that plate of rat meat.

More than he had imagined, but he forced himself to finish it. Bai Ya was right—in this world, wasting food was a sin.

"Come with me," Bai Ya stood up, removing his apron. Beneath the apron, he wore a simple gray vest, revealing slender but solid arms. Chen Mo could see the muscle lines on his arms—not that kind of bulging blockiness, but rather a streamlined, lean muscle, like a long-distance runner or... an assassin.

He led Chen Mo to the small yard behind the house.

It was a very small vegetable garden, surrounded by a crude fence. Sunlight shone straight down, illuminating those strangely shaped plants. Some Chen Mo recognized—like mutated tomatoes and peppers—but some he had never seen before. There was a kind of blue vine, crawling all over the entire eastern fence, blooming purple flowers, emitting a faint fluorescence in the sunlight.

"These are what I planted," Bai Ya squatted down, checking the leaves of a plant. His movements were very gentle, fingers lightly sliding over the leaves, like caressing a living being. "Most food in this world is poisonous, or has hallucinogenic effects. You have to learn to distinguish what can be eaten and what can't."

He pointed at that blue vine.

"This is called 'Dream Vine.' Eating it will make you see hallucinations. Some descenders specifically eat this to seek thrills, then go mad and never wake up again."

Chen Mo leaned in to look. That vine's leaves presented an unnatural blue, as if dyed with ink. On the back of the leaves, he could see fine hairs, glinting with micro-light.

"Then why do you still grow it?"

"Because the root can be used as medicine," Bai Ya said, "Very effective for treating fever. Everything has two sides; the key is how you use it."

He led Chen Mo around the vegetable garden, introducing various plants.

"This is 'Blood Leaf,' the leaves are red, like blood. Raw it's poisonous, cooked it can supplement iron. This is 'Mute Grass,' eating it will make you lose your voice for three days, but it can also treat sore throats. This is 'Laughing Mushroom,' eating it will make you keep laughing until you suffocate to death, but trace amounts are a potent painkiller..."

Chen Mo's head spun from listening.

The plants of this world, each one seemed to challenge his cognition. They weren't simple food or medicine, but rather... weapons, or tools, depending on how you used them.

"I can't remember so much..."

"You don't need to remember it all now," Bai Ya said. He straightened up, dusting off his hands. "Take it slowly. But there's one thing you must learn now—"

He walked to a corner of the garden, where there was a bush Chen Mo hadn't noticed before. The bush was covered with red berries, each only the size of a fingernail, presenting an appetizing sheen in the sunlight.

Bai Ya picked one, handing it to Chen Mo.

"This is 'Honesty Fruit.' After eating it, you won't be able to lie. We're going to do a test."

"A test?"

"I'm going to ask you a few questions," Bai Ya's eyes fixed on him, those red eyes possessing a strange penetrating power, as if they could see straight to the deepest part of Chen Mo's thoughts. "You must answer truthfully. This concerns what I can teach you and what I can't."

Chen Mo hesitated a moment, then took that berry.

It was lighter than he had imagined, its surface covered with a fine fuzz, feeling like peach skin when touched. He put it in his mouth, gently biting through it with his teeth.

The taste was very sour.

Like an unripe lemon, carrying a hint of bitterness. The juice spread across his tongue, bringing a slight stinging sensation. Then, he felt a warm current sliding down from his throat, flowing into his stomach, then spreading throughout his body.

It was a strange sensation. Like something had opened a door in his consciousness that he couldn't close.

"First question," Bai Ya said, "Why did you stay?"

Chen Mo opened his mouth, wanting to say something. But what came out was—

"Because I couldn't bear to lose those three months' salary."

He was stunned. This wasn't what he wanted to say, or rather, wasn't what he wanted to admit. But the words flowed uncontrollably from his mouth, carrying a shameful truth.

Bai Ya smiled.

"Very good, the Honesty Fruit is taking effect. Continue—besides money, what other reason?"

"Because..." Chen Mo felt the words continuing to flow uncontrollably, "Because I don't want to be a coward. Because that wild boar... he died before my eyes, and I did nothing. I want... I want to become useful."

His eye sockets felt somewhat hot. This wasn't sadness, but a more complex emotion—shame, longing, and some anger he himself hadn't yet realized.

"Third question," Bai Ya's voice became serious, "Can you kill?"

Chen Mo froze.

This question, he had thought about countless times last night. When that wild boar died, when the wolf left, when he knelt by the roadside vomiting. Could he kill? To survive, to become stronger, could he take another life?

"I... don't know," he finally said, every word seeming forced through gritted teeth. "I think... I can't. At least not now."

"But what if it's to protect yourself?" Bai Ya pressed, his eyes fixed tightly on Chen Mo, not missing the slightest change in expression. "If someone wants to kill you, can you fight back?"

"I..."

Chen Mo fell silent. The Honesty Fruit made him unable to lie, but this question, he himself didn't have the answer.

"Last question," Bai Ya said, "Do you hate that wolf? The one who killed the wild boar."

"I..." Chen Mo was stunned.

Hate? He had been thinking about this question all last night. That wolf killed the wild boar, splashed him with blood, then left lightly. He should hate him, right?

But strangely, he didn't.

"I don't hate him," Chen Mo said, his voice as light as a whisper. "I'm just... afraid. Afraid of becoming someone like him."

Bai Ya looked at him, for a long time.

A flash of emotion Chen Mo couldn't interpret passed through those red eyes—perhaps satisfaction, perhaps vigilance, perhaps pure observation.

Then, he smiled.

"Very good," he said, "The Honesty Fruit test is passed. You're not the kind of person who would lose yourself for power."

He turned and walked toward the house.

"Come, I'll take you to a place."

**Scene-4 The Gray Market Morning Light**

They passed through several alleys, arriving at a bustling market.

That was the first time Chen Mo had seen so many people in this world—or rather, so many beastmen. Various races gathered here, bears, wolves, oxen, pigs, and some varieties he couldn't name.

But what attracted Chen Mo's attention most were those figures wearing different colored uniforms.

The military-green G-level grunts were the most numerous. They patrolled in groups at the edge of the market, holding batons, eyes vigilant. Their uniforms were old, some already washed white, but their postures carried a... deliberate arrogance, as if that green skin gave them some privilege.

Then there were blue figures.

O-level overseers. Chen Mo saw two, checking something at a stall. Their uniforms were neater than the greenskins, the fabric looking better too. Their postures were more relaxed, their eyes carrying a... contempt for greenskins.

"Those are overseers," Bai Ya said in a low voice, "Low-level cadres, managing greenskins. Monthly salary 200 Lu coins, bounty is also 200."

"Bounty?"

"Every person in uniform," Bai Ya said, "has a price. Greenskin 100, blueskin 200, blackskin and whiteskin 500, W-level 1000. This is the amount bounty hunters can exchange after killing them."

Chen Mo felt a chill.

He looked at those greenskin grunts, remembering last night's G-892. That designation, that life easily taken by the wolf, was only worth 100 Lu coins?

"This is the 'Gray Market,'" Bai Ya said, "Not a safe zone, but it has its own rules. Here, you can buy anything—food, weapons, intelligence, bodies..."

"Bodies?"

"Mm," Bai Ya nodded. He led Chen Mo through the crowd, arriving at a relatively quiet corner. "Some descenders don't want to work hard leveling up, so they directly buy other people's bodies. Strong people's bodies are expensive, but you can directly obtain power."

Chen Mo frowned.

"What about the original body owner?"

"Dead," Bai Ya said, his tone as flat as stating the weather. "Or trapped 'up there,' never able to come down again."

They passed a stall, the vendor a fat pig beastman, hawking some kind of green liquid.

"Come try it! Fresh 'Berserker Agent'! Triples your strength after drinking! Only fifty Lu coins!"

"That's fake," Bai Ya said in a low voice, "Can only double strength, but will make people lose their minds, becoming beasts that only know how to kill. Nine out of ten who drink it die."

They passed another stall, this time a thin rat beastman, with a row of... socks laid out before him?

Chen Mo was stunned. Those white long socks, identical to the ones on that wild boar's feet last night. Some looked new, some clearly worn, some even... carrying dark stains.

"White socks! Cheap white socks! Just stolen from the Beastshield warehouse! Only ten Lu coins a pair!"

"Those..." Chen Mo's voice trembled somewhat.

"Stolen goods," Bai Ya said, "Beastshield uniform socks. Some descenders like collecting these, as... souvenirs."

Chen Mo felt a wave of nausea. Last night when that wild boar died, that wolf had stripped off his socks. In his eyes, that was just a trophy, an item that could be exchanged for money. But here, these socks were sold as commodities, like... like pork.

"Don't look," Bai Ya pulled him forward, "Most things here are blood-stained. You have to learn to get used to it, but never become numb."

They arrived at the end of the market, a relatively quiet corner. There was a small stall, half-concealed by a dark cloth curtain, looking more... secretive than the other stalls.

Bai Ya stopped, looked around, then lifted the curtain.

"Come in."

Chen Mo followed him inside.

**Scene-5 Captive Delivery**

The interior of the stall was larger than Chen Mo had imagined.

It was a long, narrow space, with several iron cages placed on both sides, each cage holding a figure. Chen Mo's heartbeat accelerated; his sense of smell told him those were alive—he could smell sweat, could smell fear, could smell that special scent life emitted in despair.

"Bai Ya?" A voice came from the darkness.

It was an old fox beastman, rising from behind the counter. His fur had turned gray-white, eyes narrowed into slits, looking like he could fall asleep at any moment. But when his gaze fell on Bai Ya, Chen Mo could feel that instant alertness—that instinct born from long survival in gray zones.

"Old Fox," Bai Ya said, "I brought a student."

The old fox sized up Chen Mo, his gaze lingering on Chen Mo's brown bear body for a few seconds.

"Newcomer?" His voice was hoarse, carrying the trace of long-term smoking. "The kind who vomits the first time they see blood?"

"How did you know?" Chen Mo asked.

"I've seen too many like you," the old fox smiled, revealing several missing teeth. "Don't worry, you'll get used to it after vomiting enough."

He turned to Bai Ya.

"What do you want this time? Same rules as before?"

"Look at the goods first," Bai Ya said, "Anything fresh?"

"Of course."

The old fox walked toward those cages; Chen Mo followed behind, his heartbeat growing faster and faster.

The first cage held a wolf beastman. Young, lean, wearing a tattered military-green uniform. His face carried injuries, eyes vacant, clearly already caged for some time. Seeing someone approach, he instinctively shrank back, but the cage space was too small; he had nowhere to escape.

"G-level, three months on the job," the old fox introduced, his tone like introducing a commodity. "Body still relatively healthy, just mental state is average. Price 80 Lu coins."

"Too ordinary," Bai Ya said, "Anything more special?"

"Yes."

The old fox walked to the second cage. Inside was an ox beastman, burly in build, his sturdiness visible even in the cage. But his eyes were different—not fear, but rather... anger. A still-burning, not-yet-extinguished anger.

"This one is interesting," the old fox said, "E-level reserve soldier, in the middle of转正考核. Betrayed by a colleague, said he stole his credit. Excellent physical condition, the best muscle mass I've ever seen."

He knocked on the cage; that ox beastman lunged at the cage wall, letting out a low roar.

"Temper's a bit explosive," the old fox shrugged, "But some people like this type. Price 300 Lu coins."

Chen Mo looked at that ox beastman, feeling an indescribable complex emotion.

That wasn't a human. That was an ox, a uniformed, caged ox. But his eyes... that anger, that unwillingness, that struggle still not given up even in despair—that was human. Or rather, that was common to all conscious beings.

"Not this one," Bai Ya said, "Too much trouble. Anything... gentler?"

"Gentle?" The old fox thought, then walked to the last cage.

That cage was in the corner, half-concealed by a cloth. The old fox lifted the cloth; Chen Mo saw the figure inside.

It was a young wild boar beastman, even younger than last night's G-892. His uniform was military-green, but still very new, as if just issued not long ago. There were no injuries on his face, but there was a... deep exhaustion. That exhaustion wasn't physical, but spiritual, as if he had already given up all hope.

"G-881," the old fox said, "Just two weeks on the job. Sent to guard a warehouse, result the warehouse was attacked by bounty hunters, he was the only survivor. The company wanted to punish him, so he ran."

"Why did he run?" Bai Ya asked.

"Afraid," the old fox said, "He saw the whole process of his colleagues being killed. Three bounty hunters, used wire to strangle their necks, then... took the socks. He said he heard them laughing, like they had just completed a good deal."

Chen Mo felt a chill.

That young wild boar beastman raised his head, looking at Chen Mo. Their gazes met; Chen Mo saw what was in those eyes—not a cry for help, not hope, just a... emptiness. An emptiness hollowed out by fear.

"How much?" Bai Ya asked.

"50 Lu coins," the old fox said, "He's a coward, not worth much. But body is still healthy, no injuries or illness."

Bai Ya was silent for a moment, then turned to Chen Mo.

"Do you want to save him?"

Chen Mo was stunned.

"What?"

"I'm asking you," Bai Ya's voice was very calm, "Do you want to save him? With your own money, 50 Lu coins, buy him, give him freedom."

Chen Mo looked at that wild boar beastman, looked at those empty eyes.

He remembered last night's G-892, remembered that hand grabbing his pant leg, remembered the sensation of warm blood splashing on his face.

"I..." He opened his mouth, but didn't know what to say.

He had no money. Or rather, he did, but that was in the world "up there." In this world, he was a penniless newcomer.

"I don't have..."

"I know," Bai Ya said. He turned to the old fox. "I'll take this one. Put it on my tab."

The old fox nodded, beginning to handle the paperwork.

Chen Mo looked at Bai Ya, feeling a complex emotion—gratitude, confusion, and some hope he himself didn't yet understand.

"Why?" he asked.

Bai Ya didn't answer immediately. He looked at that wild boar beastman being led out of the cage, looked at that trembling, young figure.

"Because," he finally said, "This is the second lesson I want to teach you."

He turned to Chen Mo, those red eyes flashing with a strange light in the dim light.

"In this world," he said, "You can choose to become a hunter, or you can choose to become... something else. But not today. Today, you only need to watch."

That young wild boar beastman was brought before them. His body was trembling, but there was a change in his eyes—a little bit of... light appeared in that emptiness.

"Th... thank you..." His voice trembled, as if squeezed from his throat.

"Don't thank me," Bai Ya said, his voice devoid of temperature, only a... flat statement of fact. "I didn't do this to save you. I did it to teach him a lesson."

He pointed at Chen Mo.

"Your life is still in your own hands. Go, find a place to hide, don't wear that green skin anymore. In this city, green skin is a target."

The young wild boar beastman nodded, then stumbled out of the stall, disappearing into the crowd of the Gray Market.

Chen Mo watched his departing back, feeling a strange emotion surging in his chest.

That wasn't happiness. Not victory. Just a... confusion.

"I don't understand," he said. "You spent 50 Lu coins, just to... let me watch this scene?"

"No," Bai Ya said. He turned to Chen Mo, the corners of his mouth slightly turning up. "I spent 50 Lu coins, to let you remember this feeling."

"What feeling?"

"Powerlessness," Bai Ya said, "You wanted to save him, but you had no money. You wanted to change something, but you had no power. This powerlessness will drive you to become stronger. But remember—"

His voice became serious.

"Becoming stronger is not to become the next bounty hunter, not to climb higher on this food chain. Becoming stronger is so that one day, when you see this kind of scene again, you will have a choice."

Chen Mo fell silent.

He looked at those still-caged figures, looked at that E-level reserve soldier's angry eyes, looked at that G-level wolf beastman's vacant gaze.

This was part of this world. He realized. This wasn't game setting, not plot arrangement. This was real, was playing out every day, was this world's... norm.

"Let's go," Bai Ya said, "Today's lesson is over."

They walked out of the stall, back into the morning light of the Gray Market.

**Scene-6 Afternoon Admonishment**

In the afternoon, Bai Ya brought Chen Mo to a wasteland.

This place was far from the city center, surrounded by abandoned buildings and weed-overgrown empty lots. The air carried a rotten smell, as if something had died nearby. Chen Mo's nose told him, that was a corpse, or multiple corpses, rotting in some corner not far away.

"What is this place?" Chen Mo asked.

"A graveyard," Bai Ya said, his voice somewhat ethereal in the open space. "Not for people, but for 'discarded bodies.'"

He explained: After descenders exit the game, their bodies die. Some are recycled and reused, some are just thrown here, left to rot.

Chen Mo looked around, suddenly realizing those weed-overgrown places might conceal countless corpses.

"Why bring me here?"

"Because I want to teach you the most important lesson," Bai Ya said. He walked to the center of the empty lot, taking something out of his backpack—

It was a... doll?

No, not a doll. It was a dead beastman, preserved in some way, maintaining the posture of his lifetime. It was a wolf beastman, eyes closed, expression peaceful, as if asleep.

"This is..."

"My first student," Bai Ya said, voice calm, "His name was Hui Ying, a descender. Three years ago, he died here."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"I thought descenders don't really die..."

"Normally they don't," Bai Ya said, "But sometimes, consciousness gets trapped, can't go back."

He squatted down, looking at that corpse. Chen Mo could see Bai Ya's fingers lightly sliding over that corpse's arm, that touch carrying a... strange tenderness.

"Hui Ying was a genius. He only used three months, from a newcomer to someone who could单挑 a C-level bounty hunter. He learned everything fast—combat techniques, survival skills, even this world's history and culture... he understood it all."

"Then why did he..."

"Because he became strong too fast," Bai Ya said, "Fast enough that he didn't have time to learn control. He began to enjoy killing, to enjoy that feeling of holding others' lives and deaths in his hands. He told me, the moment of killing, he felt like he was God."

Chen Mo felt a chill.

"I tried to stop him," Bai Ya continued, "I told him, going on like this he would lose himself. He wouldn't listen. He said, this world is the law of the jungle, the strong live, the weak die, those are the rules."

"Then what?"

"Then he went to challenge an A-level bounty hunter," Bai Ya said, "He wanted to prove he was the strongest. Result..."

He paused.

"That A-level bounty hunter had a special skill, could attack consciousness. Hui Ying's body didn't die, but his consciousness... was shattered. He could never go back, never wake up."

Chen Mo looked at that corpse, suddenly feeling an indescribable sadness.

"He's been lying here for three years," Bai Ya said, "I preserved him, as a reminder. To remind myself, and to remind every one of my students—power is not the goal, control is."

He turned to Chen Mo, those red eyes looking straight at him.

"This is why I want to teach you 'maintaining humanity.' Because if you don't learn control, you will become Hui Ying. Not a corpse, but a beast."

Chen Mo fell silent.

He looked at that corpse, looked at that peaceful face. When Hui Ying died, was he smiling? Or at the last moment, did he finally realize his mistake?

"Can I... touch him?" Chen Mo asked.

Bai Ya nodded.

Chen Mo squatted down, reaching out, gently touching that corpse's arm.

The touch was strange. Not cold, not warm, but rather a... hollow feeling. Like touching an empty shell, a shell that had lost its soul. He could feel the texture of the skin—dry, somewhat rough, like aged leather. He could feel the shape of the bones beneath, that hard outline left after life had departed.

Was this death?

Not last night's bloody, violent death, but rather a... quiet, thorough extinction.

"Does he still have family?" Chen Mo asked.

"Up there," Bai Ya said, "His parents, his girlfriend. They don't know he's here, think he's just... addicted to the game, unwilling to log out."

Chen Mo closed his eyes.

He suddenly realized, the cruelty of this world wasn't just blood and violence. There was also this... abandoned loneliness. A consciousness dissipating in the void, a body rotting in the wilderness, while relatives still waited, waiting for a son, a boyfriend who would never return.

"I remember," Chen Mo said, opening his eyes, "I won't become Hui Ying."

Bai Ya looked at him, for a long time.

"Every one of my students says that," he said, "But few truly do it."

"I'll do it," Chen Mo said, "Because I'm afraid."

"Afraid of what?"

"Afraid of becoming someone I don't recognize."

Bai Ya smiled.

"Very good," he said, "Fear is good. Fear keeps you awake."

He stood up, dusting off his body.

"Today's lesson ends here. Tomorrow, we begin combat training."

---

**Scene-7 Admonishment in the Twilight**

At night, Bai Ya made a pot of stew.

The ingredients were mixed—rat meat, some kind of root vegetable he couldn't name, and some wild vegetables. The taste was strange, but not bad. Chen Mo ate two bowls, feeling his body warm up.

They sat at the stone table in the small yard, watching the sky gradually darken.

"Lao Ba told me you trained all night last night," Bai Ya said, "How did it go?"

"Not bad," Chen Mo said, "At least now I won't trip over myself when walking."

"Bear bodies are hard to control, but once you get used to it, the power is terrifying."

Bai Ya put down his bowl, looking at Chen Mo.

"I want to ask you a question. Not using Honesty Fruit; you don't have to answer."

"What question?"

"What do you want to achieve in this world?"

Chen Mo was stunned.

This question, he hadn't thought about yet. He stayed because he couldn't bear to lose the money, because he didn't want to be a coward. But these... did they count as goals?

"I don't know," he said honestly, "I haven't thought about it."

"Then think about it now," Bai Ya said, "Everyone needs a goal. Not to become stronger, but to not lose themselves in the process of becoming stronger. Hui Ying's goal was to be the strongest, so he lost himself. Your goal can't be that."

Chen Mo fell silent.

He thought for a long time.

"I want..." he finally said, "I want to understand this world."

"Understand?"

"Mm," Chen Mo nodded, "Last night that wild boar, I don't know if he was a good person or a bad person. That wolf, I don't know if he was a murderer or an avenger. Lao Ba, I don't know if he's a friend or... some existence I can't understand."

He paused.

"I want to understand them. Not using my world's standards, but using the standards here. I want to know why this world became like this, why you... natives, accept this kind of fate."

Bai Ya looked at him, his eyes complex.

"This goal is difficult," he said, "More difficult than becoming stronger."

"I know," Chen Mo said, "But I want to try."

Bai Ya was silent for a while, then smiled.

"Alright," he said, "Then our curriculum needs adjustment. Not just survival skills, but also history, culture, sociology... Are you ready?"

"Ready."

"Don't agree too quickly," Bai Ya said, "These things are more boring than you imagine."

---

**Scene-8 Night Raid**

After dinner, Chen Mo sat in the yard, looking at the night sky.

This world's starry sky was somewhat different from his world. The positions of the constellations were different, the color of the moon was slightly redder. But that feeling of silence was the same.

Bai Ya walked over, sitting down beside him.

"Can't sleep?"

"Mm," Chen Mo said, "Thinking about things."

"Thinking about Hui Ying?"

"Mm."

Bai Ya was silent for a while.

"Before Hui Ying died, he told me one thing," he said, "He said: 'Teacher, I finally understand what you said. But it's too late.'"

"What did he understand?"

"Understood that power isn't everything," Bai Ya said, "Understood that control is more important than destruction. But by then, he could no longer turn back."

Chen Mo looked at the sky.

"Bai Ya," he asked, "What are you?"

"What do you mean?"

"Your eyes are red," Chen Mo said, "Beastmen don't have red eyes. And... you know too much. Survival skills, history, culture, and also... how to preserve a corpse. This isn't something ordinary people can learn."

Bai Ya smiled.

"Good observation," he said, "But this question, I can't answer you now."

"Why?"

"Because the answer involves the core secret of this world," Bai Ya said, "Before you're ready, knowing too much is no good for you."

He stood up, patting Chen Mo's shoulder.

"But one day, you'll know. When you're ready."

He turned and walked toward the house.

"Sleep early," he said, "Tomorrow morning, we're going to the 'Ruins.'"

"Ruins?"

"This world's history is all buried there."

---

**Scene-9 Moment of Awakening**

Early the next morning, they set out for the ruins.

It was a long journey, passing through the Gray Market, passing through the wasteland, entering an area Chen Mo had never seen. The buildings here were even older, walls covered with vines and moss, the air carrying a... scent of time.

"This is the 'Old City,'" Bai Ya said, "The earliest city in this world."

"Earliest?"

"Mm," Bai Ya nodded, "Before you descenders arrived, we already existed."

He led Chen Mo into a collapsed building. Inside was very dark, with only a few beams of light filtering in through cracks. On the walls... there were paintings?

Chen Mo leaned in to look.

They were some primitive murals, painted with various beastman figures. Some hunting, some fighting, some... mating?

"This is..."

"This world's history," Bai Ya said, "Before you arrived, we had our own civilization, our own society, our own rules."

He pointed at one of the paintings. It depicted two bear beastmen, standing face to face, with a glowing sphere between them.

"What is this?"

"'Connection,'" Bai Ya said, "We call it 'Connection.' Legend says our world and another world are connected, through some... channel."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"You mean..."

"Your descenders coming to this world wasn't accidental," Bai Ya said, "It was guided. That VR device, that 'Meta Beast Master' game, they're all part of the channel."

He turned to Chen Mo, those red eyes glowing in the darkness.

"Someone is deliberately making the two worlds merge. I don't know why, nor who. But I know, this is not just a 'game.'"

Chen Mo felt dizzy.

If what Bai Ya said was true... then what he entered wasn't a virtual world, but a... real parallel world? And those "NPCs," weren't programs, but real lives?

"This is why I don't hate descenders," Bai Ya said, "Because you are also being used. You think you're playing a game, but actually, you are being guided here, to become part of this world."

"Then... what about natives? What do you think?"

"Some welcome it, some fear it, some... want to use it," Bai Ya said, "Beastshield Company is one of the forces using descenders."

He pointed at another mural. It depicted a uniformed beastman, standing on countless other beastmen's corpses.

"Beastshield's history can be traced back hundreds of years," Bai Ya said, "They have always been researching 'Connection,' trying to control it. The appearance of you descenders gave their research a breakthrough."

"What breakthrough?"

"Consciousness transfer," Bai Ya said, "They are researching how to transfer natives' consciousness into descenders' bodies, or... vice versa."

Chen Mo felt a chill.

If Beastshield succeeded... then those dead descenders, their bodies might be occupied by natives? And those natives trapped in this world, could escape to "up there"?

"This is the world you want to understand," Bai Ya said, "A world full of secrets, conspiracies, and dangers. Are you sure you want to continue?"

Chen Mo fell silent.

He thought of Hui Ying, thought of that wolf bounty hunter, thought of Lao Ba's lonely gaze. He thought of those figures he saw at Captive Delivery last night, that young wild boar beastman's empty eyes.

This world was far more complex than he had imagined.

"I'm sure," he said.

When they left the ruins, the sky was almost dark.

Bai Ya walked in front, Chen Mo followed behind. His mind was still digesting the information he had just heard—the connection between two worlds, Beastshield's conspiracy, consciousness transfer research... all of this exceeded his imagination.

"Watch out!"

Bai Ya suddenly shouted, pushing Chen Mo aside.

An arrow shot out from the darkness, grazing Chen Mo's shoulder, nailing into the wall behind him.

"Who is it!" Bai Ya shouted sternly, his body instantly tensing, entering combat posture.

Three figures walked out from the darkness.

They were Beastshield people. Wearing military-green uniforms, holding weapons in their hands—knife, club, and a crossbow.

"Bai Ya," the one leading, a tall ox beastman, sneered, "Long time no see."

"Iron Horn," Bai Ya's voice became icy, "You're still alive."

"Thanks to you," the ox beastman called Iron Horn said, "That knife you gave me last time, I remember it."

Chen Mo felt his heartbeat accelerate. He looked at Bai Ya, then at those three Beastshield people. Their eyes were filled with killing intent; this wasn't an ordinary patrol, they were here for revenge.

"This is between him and me," Bai Ya said to Chen Mo, his voice very low, "You find a chance to run."

"But..."

"Run!"

Bai Ya suddenly moved.

His speed was astonishing, completely unlike what a rabbit should have. He instantly rushed to Iron Horn's face, palm striking toward his chest. Iron Horn raised his arm to block, but Bai Ya's palm force penetrated the defense, directly hitting his shoulder.

"Bang!"

Iron Horn retreated several steps, his face showing a pained expression.

"Together!" he shouted.

The other two Beastshield members rushed up. One was a wolf, one was a dog, both sturdy combat types. They flanked Bai Ya from left and right.

Chen Mo stood in place, his legs going soft.

He wanted to help, but he knew he could do nothing. He hadn't even mastered controlling this body yet; how could he possibly be a match for these professional combatants?

But he couldn't run either.

Bai Ya said he wanted to teach him how to maintain humanity. If he ran now, what humanity did he have left?

"Ah—!"

Chen Mo shouted, raising his bear paws, charging at that dog beastman.

His movements were clumsy, his speed slow; the dog beastman easily dodged his attack, then kicked him in the stomach.

"Bang!"

Chen Mo flew backward, heavily crashing to the ground. Pain surged like a tide; he felt like a few ribs might be broken.

"Newcomer?" The dog beastman sneered, "Since when did Bai Ya accept such a useless student?"

He raised his knife, walking toward Chen Mo.

"It's over, little bear."

The moment the knife fell, a white figure blocked Chen Mo.

It was Bai Ya.

He used his body to block that knife. The blade deeply pierced his shoulder, blood gushing out.

"Bai Ya!" Chen Mo shouted.

Bai Ya didn't turn back. His claws grabbed the dog beastman's wrist, then—

An invisible force erupted from Bai Ya.

That wasn't physical force, but rather some... spiritual level impact. Chen Mo felt a stabbing pain in his brain, like someone screaming in his consciousness.

The dog beastman let out a scream, releasing the knife, holding his head, kneeling to the ground. Blood flowed from his nostrils and ears, his body convulsing violently.

"This... this is..." Iron Horn's face changed, "You can actually use this..."

"Scram," Bai Ya's voice was icy, "Or the next one is you."

Iron Horn looked at Bai Ya, looked at those glowing red eyes, his expression changing from anger to fear.

"Go!" he shouted, bringing another still-moving subordinate, dragging the convulsing dog beastman, disappearing into the darkness.

Bai Ya finally couldn't support himself, kneeling down.

"Bai Ya!" Chen Mo struggled to crawl over, supporting him.

"It's fine..." Bai Ya panted, "Just... consumed too much..."

His shoulder was bleeding, dyeing his white fur red. But more serious was his complexion—pale as paper, the red light in his eyes also dimming.

"What was that... just now?" Chen Mo asked.

"My... special ability," Bai Ya said with difficulty, "Spiritual impact... directly attacks consciousness..."

"But you're injured..."

"Worth it," Bai Ya smiled, very weak, but genuine, "You... didn't run."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"I thought you would run," Bai Ya said, "Most newcomers... run when encountering danger. But you... charged up."

"I..."

"Although you're weak," Bai Ya continued, "Although you did nothing, even caused trouble... but you charged up. This is... the beginning of courage."

He leaned against Chen Mo, closing his eyes.

"Take me back..." he said softly, "I'll teach you... how to use that ability..."

"What ability?"

"Spiritual power..." Bai Ya's voice grew lighter and lighter, "Every descender... has it... just... not yet awakened..."

His head drooped, falling into unconsciousness.

Chen Mo held him, feeling an unprecedented emotion.

Not fear, not confusion, but rather a... sense of responsibility.

Bai Ya was injured saving him. And he, couldn't just give up like this.

"I'll take you back," Chen Mo said, his voice firm, "I promise."

He carried Bai Ya on his back, walking toward the direction of the city.

Moonlight shone on them, illuminating the road ahead.

This was his first lesson, about courage, about responsibility, about... how to not lose his humanity in this cruel world.

And his journey had just begun.

### Their Final Stories

【Corpse→Dead-G-892-Wild Boar Beastman-Unknown】

He died in Chapter 1, but his story has not been fully told.

G-892's corpse was left on the tavern floor. That once warm, trembling, life-seeking body was now cold, stiff, silent. The military-green uniform was soaked with blood, clinging tightly to his fat-over-muscle body. The white long socks were stripped by the wolf bounty hunter, taken as proof for bounty.

His designation would be canceled, his position filled, his existence forgotten. This was the fate of Beastshield grunts—consumables, used and discarded.

But if we could peer into that corpse's memories, what might we see?

Perhaps a wild boar youth from the countryside, joining Beastshield with dreams of the big city. Perhaps a poor soul forced by debt, with no choice but to put on that green skin. Perhaps someone once kind, but gradually worn smooth of all edges by this world... an ordinary person.

That accusation Lao Ba mentioned about "raping a rabbit youth" might be true, might be false, might be an exaggerated single event. We will never know the truth. But even if it was true, could it offset the real fear of "don't want to die" before his death?

In that tavern, when he grabbed Chen Mo's pant leg, his fingers were trembling, the sweat and blood in his palm mixed together. In his eyes there was despair, and also... hope? That instinctive attachment to life, that reluctance for the future, that struggle that had to be tried even knowing it was impossible... humanity.

Now, all of this was gone.

His body might have been thrown into a trash can, or gnawed by wild dogs, or hastily dealt with by Beastshield's logistics department. His parents (if he really had them) would never receive notification. His existence would be erased from the system, as if he had never existed.

This is G-892's ending. A designated life, meaninglessly dying in a "game."

And we don't even know his name.

### Those Still Alive

【Living→Alive-Player-Chen Mo (Little Bear)】

Chen Mo is still alive. But he is no longer that newcomer who only knew how to vomit.

In this chapter, he learned to eat rat meat—that taste mixed with grease char and gamey sweetness, that food considered "disgusting" in the human world but "lucky" in this world.

He passed the Honesty Fruit test, admitting the real reason he stayed: couldn't bear to lose the money, didn't want to be a coward. This honesty let him pass Bai Ya's assessment, but also let him more clearly see his own... weakness.

He saw Captive Delivery. Saw those lives caged, saw that young wild boar G-881's empty eyes. He wanted to save them, but he had no money. That powerlessness was like a blunt knife, slowly cutting his heart.

Then, Iron Horn's attack came.

Bai Ya blocked that knife for him. When he watched Bai Ya bleeding, when he carried Bai Ya walking under the moonlight, he felt something—responsibility.

This wasn't a quest in a game, not a resettable plot. This was a real person (or rather, a real consciousness), injured because of him. And he, could not betray this trust.

His spiritual power awakened. Although still uncontrollable, although even a leaf would be shattered, that power was already there. That power from another world, transcending physical rules.

He didn't know where this would lead him. But he knew, from this moment on, he had a reason he must become stronger.

Not for himself, but for those he wanted to protect.

【Living→Alive-Roamer-Bai Ya】

Bai Ya is still alive, but severely injured.

He blocked that knife for Chen Mo, not because Chen Mo was worth it—they had only been teacher and student for two days. He did this because Chen Mo reminded him of himself.

That first descent, knowing nothing, thinking this was just a game.

That also having a mentor, that mentor also blocking a knife for him, then dying... past self.

Bai Ya is a roamer. He has already died once, his consciousness trapped in this world, borrowing a native's body. He is not afraid of death, because he has already died. But this doesn't mean he has no pain.

When he used spiritual impact, he felt that familiar weakness—consciousness being drained, vitality flowing away. But he had no regrets. Because Chen Mo didn't run.

That clumsy, weak, unsteady brown bear, didn't run when facing danger. This gave Bai Ya hope. Hope that this time, he wouldn't repeat the same mistake. Hope that this time, his student wouldn't become Hui Ying.

He spent 50 Lu coins to buy G-881, not to save that wild boar—although that was also part of the reason—but to teach Chen Mo a lesson. About powerlessness, about choice, about in this world, you can become a hunter, or you can become... something else.

Now, he lay on Chen Mo's back, unconscious. His shoulder was bleeding, his spiritual power failing. But he felt some... peace.

Perhaps, just perhaps, this student would be different.

【Living→Alive-G-734-Wild Boar Beastman-Beastshield Grunt】

G-734 is still alive. But this is just a... fluke.

He lay on his bed, staring at the ceiling, listening to G-721 tell rumors about G-877 being sold. That young dog beastman, who was still on night shift with him yesterday, was today a commodity at Captive Delivery.

150 Lu coins. A life. A "colleague."

G-734 felt a wave of nausea, but more was fear. That feeling of fear tightly gripping his heart made him unable to sleep.

He joined Beastshield eight months ago. For eight months, he watched companions die one by one—killed by bounty hunters, internally executed, or... sold. He began to understand, this green uniform wasn't protection, but a mark. Marking him as a consumable that could be killed, sold, abandoned.

But he couldn't leave. The contract still had four months; leaving early meant huge penalty fees. And even if he left, what could he do? The civilian world wasn't safe either; without Beastshield's shelter, he might die faster.

He was trapped. Trapped in this green cage, waiting for the next chosen fate.

He looked at his hand—that wild boar palm covered with sparse gray-brown fur. This hand had once helped him drive away street thugs, had once helped him find a foothold in this city. But now, this hand couldn't even control his own fate.

At the last moment before falling asleep, he silently prayed in his heart—hoping that tomorrow the chosen one wouldn't be him.

This is G-734's life. Fearful, humble, hopeless... survival.

【Living→Alive-G-881-Wild Boar Beastman-Former Beastshield Grunt】

G-881 is still alive. But he is no longer a Beastshield employee.

He was released by Bai Ya from the cage at Captive Delivery, for 50 Lu coins—but that wasn't to enslave him, but to give him freedom. He didn't quite understand why someone would do this, but he knew, from that moment on, his fate had changed.

He took off that military-green uniform, threw it into the trash can. That was once his only shelter, and also his biggest shackle. Now, he was a "fugitive," a "missing person," a... free life.

He didn't know what the future would be like. He didn't know if he could survive in this cruel world without Beastshield's shelter. He didn't know where to go, what to do, how to survive.

But he knew one thing—he was still alive. And G-877, that dog beastman who was still on guard duty with him yesterday, was already dead (or rather, sold).

This feeling of being alive, although full of uncertainty, was also full of... possibility.

He walked in the shadows of the city, avoiding patrolling Beastshield soldiers. His steps were light, his heartbeat fast. But he walked forward, step by step, toward an unknown future.

He didn't know what lay ahead. But he knew, as long as he was still alive, there was hope.

Even if it was just a faint, possibly extinguished at any moment... tiny bit of light.

【Living→Alive-Corpse-Hui Ying】

Hui Ying... is he still alive?

His body was preserved by Bai Ya in the graveyard, maintaining the posture of his lifetime. His consciousness was shattered, never able to coalesce again. He could neither return to the real world, nor truly die like a native.

He is a warning. A cautionary tale about pursuing power and losing oneself. A... once genius, now empty shell.

When Chen Mo touched his arm, that hollow feeling, that touch of a shell that had lost its soul—that is Hui Ying's current state.

He was once Bai Ya's proudest student. Three months from newcomer to strong enough to单挑 a C-level bounty hunter, learning everything fast—combat techniques, survival skills, history and culture... he understood it all. But he became strong too fast, too fast to learn control.

He began to enjoy killing. Began to think he was God. Then, he went to challenge an A-level bounty hunter, his consciousness was shattered, his body abandoned.

His parents, his girlfriend, are still waiting for him in the real world. They don't know he's "dead," think he's just addicted to the game, unwilling to log out.

This abandoned loneliness is more cruel than death. A consciousness dissipating in the void, a body rotting in the wilderness, while relatives still wait, waiting for a son, a boyfriend who will never return.

Hui Ying's story is a tragedy. But the greater tragedy is, his story will repeat endlessly. In this world, there are always new descenders, always new geniuses, always new... lost ones.

### Story Reflections

This chapter makes me think: **In a world without choices, what does choice itself mean?**

G-734 has no choice. He is trapped in Beastshield's system, unable to leave, unable to change. His fear is real, his despair is real, his... being alive is just a fluke.

And G-881, a younger, more fragile wild boar beastman, unexpectedly obtained the opportunity to choose. He was released by Bai Ya, walked out of the cage at Captive Delivery, toward an unknown future. He didn't know where to go, but he knew, he was no longer a designation, no longer a commodity.

This contrast pains me. Similarly trapped at the bottom of Beastshield's system, one continues trapped in fear, while the other obtains freedom—not because of his ability, but merely because of... luck.

Bai Ya spent 50 Lu coins to buy G-881, not to enslave him, but to teach Chen Mo a lesson. This "luxurious" behavior appears so... out of place in this world. But it is also a kind of resistance. A resistance that lights a lamp in the darkness.

Chen Mo's growth moves me. He is no longer that newcomer who only knew how to vomit. He began to have a sense of responsibility, began to want to protect others, began to understand the true meaning of "courage"—not being unafraid, but still moving forward when afraid.

But he still has a long way to go. His spiritual power has awakened, but he still can't control it. He has seen the darkness of this world, but he still doesn't have the ability to change it.

This is the pain of growth. The more you know, the more powerless you feel. But it is precisely this powerlessness that drives you to become stronger.

This chapter also lets me understand Bai Ya more deeply. He is a roamer, between life and death. He has already died once, so he is not afraid of death. But he still chooses to teach, to protect, to... believe.

This belief is fragile, possibly doomed to fail (like Hui Ying), but he still continues. Because if he doesn't continue, it means admitting that this world has no possibility of redemption.

I don't know if Chen Mo will become the person who changes something. But I hope. Hope he can persist, hope he can maintain his humanity, hope he can find... some kind of answer.

In this world filled with blood and violence, perhaps the answer is not "becoming stronger," but "connection." Establishing real, human connections with others. Like the budding... teacher-student bond between Chen Mo and Bai Ya.

That connection is a power stronger than any spiritual power.
