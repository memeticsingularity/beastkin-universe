## Scene-1 Contours in the Fog

Li You didn't like ports.

Not because of the smell — although it was indeed permeated with the saltiness of seawater, the oxidized odor of rust, the pungent sting of unburned diesel, and some deeper, corrupt sweetness seeping from accumulated years of garbage and sludge. Nor because of the sounds — those metallic groans, the monotonous rhythm of waves slapping concrete shores, the occasional blurred steam whistle from distant ocean freighters. He didn't even hate the damp cold here, the kind that could penetrate the thickest coat and sink directly into bone marrow.

What he hated was the visibility.

Fog was alive. It rolled in searchlight beams like living gray-white cotton, devouring light, turning container yards twenty meters away into blurred gray shadows. Water vapor condensed on rusted steel beams, gathering into thin streams that made irregular "drip, drip" sounds when falling, interfering with judgment of footsteps. Water puddles everywhere reflected no complete images, only fragmentarily reflecting scattered lights like pairs of disoriented eyes.

But tonight, this place was very suitable.

Li You squatted on the second-floor platform of an abandoned watchtower. The platform was made of iron grating, its edges severely rusted, making slight "creaking" sounds when bearing weight, but the structure was still relatively stable. He wore dark gray urban camouflage, rough light-absorbing material that almost merged with shadows in dark places. His face was painted with similarly colored greasepaint, revealing only a pair of eyes that were currently slowly scanning the crisscrossing passages below through a low-light night vision device.

His breathing was steady and long; each exhalation condensed into a brief white mist in the cold air, quickly dissipating. His body maintained a relaxed yet ready-to-explode posture — knees slightly bent, center of gravity on the forefeet. His right hand hung naturally at his side, only ten centimeters from the modified anesthetic pistol in his waist holster. His left hand steadily supported the night vision device.

In the night vision device's field of view, the world was monotonous green. But the contrast was clear enough. The edges of containers, cracks in damaged roads, scattered discarded tires, clumps of grass shivering in the night wind... every detail was magnified, endowed with a surreal clarity.

He was searching for a specific shade of green.

According to intelligence, tonight between midnight and 2:00 AM, a low-level patrol soldier of the Beastshield Company would be solely responsible for security inspection of Berths D7 to D12. This area was basically abandoned; only a few warehouses storing unsold building materials and expired chemical raw materials still occasionally had vehicles coming and going. Patrol was more form than substance, a consumption of bottom-level employees by the company — making you feel like you have something to do, while in reality no one cares whether you actually fulfill your duties.

Li You needed that number, and that sock.

At the edge of the green-lit field of view, something moved.

Li You's fingers instantly tightened on the night vision device, the movement so subtle that only an almost imperceptible contraction of shoulder muscles. He slowly moved his gaze, focusing on that area.

It was a tall silhouette, walking out from between two rows of containers. Even in the distorted green field of view, one could see the heaviness of its build. Broad shoulders, almost forming an exaggerated contrast with the width of the containers. Walking posture carried a large-animal-specific, somewhat clumsy sway, each step solid and heavy; even across this distance and through the iron grating, Li You seemed to feel an extremely faint vibration from the platform beneath his feet.

The target wore a military green uniform. Even under low-light night vision, that color was particularly jarring — a cheap, synthetic-fiber-textured green. Short-sleeve tight fit, tightly wrapping the upper body, clearly outlining the blocky contours of pectoral and abdominal muscles, but also revealing some relaxed curves around the waist and abdomen — that was the fat layer. Typical "fat-wrapped muscle," a body where strength and laziness coexisted. The lower body was matching tight shorts, stretched tight at the base of the thighs, inner thigh muscles rubbing against each other with each step.

The target wore a matching peaked cap on his head, brim pressed very low. On his feet were a pair of military green athletic shoes with thick soles, making "slap, slap" muffled sounds when stepping on damp concrete. Most eye-catching were those long socks, white with military green stripes on the sides, appearing in an eerie bright white in the night vision device's green field of view. The socks were pulled very high, almost reaching below the knee, wrapping around the swollen gastrocnemius muscles of the calves.

The target's right hand carried a black rubber baton, tapping his outer thigh from time to time. His left hand held a cigarette, the red tip a glaring bright white spot in the green field of view, flickering with his lifting and inhaling motions.

Li You turned off the night vision device. Too much electronic assistance sometimes interfered with the most instinctive judgment. He narrowed his eyes, adapting to the dim light with naked vision, observing that slowly moving green figure in the fog.

The target stopped, leaning against a rusted fire hydrant. He took a deep drag of the cigarette, tilting his head back to exhale a long string of gray-white smoke toward the fog-obscured night sky. By the dim light of a fortunately intact sodium lamp in the distance, Li You clearly saw his profile.

It was a typical grizzly bear beastman's face. Protruding muzzle, covered in dark gray short fur, nose tip moist and black. Small eyes hidden under the double shadows of brow ridge and cap brim, gaze somewhat unfocused, revealing fatigue and boredom. Ears were round, standing on both sides of the head, occasionally twitching to shake off condensed water droplets. Strength type, but probably lacking agility, and currently with extremely low alertness. Emotional state: bored, tired, possibly also a bit cold. Because he saw the target unconsciously shrug his shoulders, shrinking his neck into a nonexistent collar.

A standard, even somewhat templated G-level grunt.

Li You gently exhaled a breath, white mist dispersing before him. He began planning the route in his mind. Down from the back of the watchtower, using a row of abandoned pipes as cover, circling to the container yard behind and to the side of the target. He needed to get closer — close enough to smell the other's body odor, to clearly see the texture of the Beastshield logo on the uniform chest, to hear the rhythm of his breathing.

Then, the real beginning.

Li You peeled off the iron grating platform like a shadow falling from a tree, landing on soft, rotting-leaf-filled mud without making a sound. He crouched for a moment, ears capturing all sounds brought by the wind — the target's footsteps, the light sound of baton tapping, the subtle "hiss" of smoking, and the more distant port background noise.

He moved, hugging the rusted pipes, body pressed very low, each step precisely calculated, avoiding gravel and puddles on the ground. The rough fabric of the camouflage rubbed against his skin, bringing a tangible sensation reminding him of his own existence. Cold, salty, rust-flavored air inhaled into his lungs, keeping his mind clear.

He maintained about fifty meters distance from that slowly moving green mass, like a patient spider beginning to weave its approaching web.
