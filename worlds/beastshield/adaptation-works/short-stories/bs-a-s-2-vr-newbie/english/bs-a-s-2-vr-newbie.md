> *The First Game*  
> When he first put on the VR headset, he thought this was just an ordinary game. But when that warm, fishy liquid splashed onto his face, he understood—this isn't a game, this is another real world.

---

**Scene-1 Login**

Chen Mo's hands were still shaking when he took off the VR headset.

It was an ordinary weekend in 2026. He had just spent three months' salary on the latest "Meta-Beast Master" immersive VR kit, eagerly logging into this game claiming "100% realistic sensory experience."

"Please choose your initial meta-beast..."

A mechanical female voice sounded in his ears. Before Chen Mo, three beastmen of different forms appeared—a muscle-knotted bull, a fluffy brown bear, and a lean, agile gray wolf.

"What the hell..." Chen Mo muttered, reaching out to point at the brown bear, "This one then. Looks like it has thick HP."

"Selection confirmed. Synchronizing neural connection..."

A wave of dizziness hit. When Chen Mo opened his eyes again, he found himself standing on a dimly lit street.

No, not "found." He... was that brown bear.

He could feel the sensation of his thick footpads stepping on concrete, could feel the subtle itch of night wind brushing through his fur, could smell that damp odor in the air, a mixture of rust and decay.

He raised his hand—no, his claw—seeing those five thick digits covered in dark brown fur, the meat pads on his palm thick and rough.

"Holy shit..." Chen Mo murmured, but what came out was a low bear growl.

This was too real.

**Scene-2 Guidance**

"New player?"

A voice came from behind. Chen Mo spun around abruptly, his clumsy movement nearly tripping himself—he hadn't yet adapted to this body's high center of gravity.

Standing behind him was another brown bear beastman, but more burly than him, his fur mixed with silver-white patches, looking quite old. That old bear wore a worn leather armor, a short knife hanging at his waist.

"I... uh..." Chen Mo wanted to say "I'm a player," but suddenly realized that in this world, he probably shouldn't reveal this identity.

"Don't be nervous, kid," the old bear grinned, revealing a mouth of yellowed teeth, "My name's Old Scar, an NPC guide for this area. You 'Arrivals' are all like this when you first come—can't even walk properly."

"Arrivals"? Chen Mo was stunned for a moment, then realized this was what the game called players.

"Come, follow me," Old Scar turned and walked toward the depths of the alley, "First I'll teach you some basic survival rules. This game isn't like those hack-and-slash RPGs. Death is real death—at least in this world it is."

Chen Mo staggered after him. He really wasn't good at controlling this body; every step felt like walking on stilts.

"This game..." Chen Mo probed, "What's the death mechanic like?"

"Dead is dead," Old Scar didn't look back, "Your consciousness returns 'above,' but this body is gone. If you want to come down again, you have to buy game access again, randomly get a new body. And the new body has no memories or skills from before, starting from zero."

"Isn't that... just like really dying?" Chen Mo frowned.

Old Scar stopped walking, turning back to look at him. Something flashed through those cloudy bear eyes that Chen Mo couldn't understand.

"Kid," Old Scar said, "What do you think this is?"

"A game..." Chen Mo answered subconsciously.

Old Scar laughed, the laughter carrying some bitterness.

"To you, it's a game," he said, "To me, this is my world. I was born here, grew up here, and will die here. You Arrivals treat this place as an amusement park, but to us, this is real life."

Chen Mo fell silent. He remembered that phrase from the game's promotional materials: "100% realistic sensory experience." He had thought it was just marketing hype, but now...

He looked at Old Scar's back, realizing for the first time—this "NPC" might not just be code.

**Scene-3 First Blood**

Old Scar brought Chen Mo to a tavern.

Calling it a tavern was actually just a slightly larger broken-down house, with a few crooked wooden tables inside, the air thick with alcohol, sweat, and some indescribable fishy stench.

"Sit," Old Scar patted a chair that looked relatively sturdy, "Have something to drink first, get used to this body."

Chen Mo carefully sat down, the chair emitting a worrisome creak. He looked down at the tabletop—covered with all kinds of scratches and strange symbols, some looking like writing, some like drawings.

"What is this?" He pointed at those symbols.

"Left by previous Arrivals," Old Scar took out two clay bowls from behind the counter, pouring some turbid liquid, "Some are names, some are slogans, and some... are last words."

"Last words?"

"Mm," Old Scar pushed the bowl in front of Chen Mo, "Some Arrivals know they're going to die, so they carve their final words here. Anyway, when this body dies, consciousness can still go back. To them, it's like... carving words on a desk after finishing homework?"

Chen Mo picked up the bowl, sniffing—a fermented grain smell, carrying a faint sour stench.

"Is this drinkable..."

"Drinkable, just a bit strong," Old Scar himself downed a large gulp first, wiping his mouth, "Right, forgot to ask you, when you chose this body, did you look at the stats?"

"What stats?"

"Just..." Old Scar was about to explain when the tavern door was suddenly kicked open.

A wild boar beastman in an olive-green uniform stumbled in, his face carrying a terrified expression. His uniform was torn, a deep claw mark on his chest, blood gushing out.

"Sa... save me..." The wild boar beastman collapsed at Chen Mo's feet, his blood-stained hand grabbing Chen Mo's pant leg, "Bounty hunter... there's a bounty hunter outside..."

Chen Mo froze.

He could feel that wild boar's hand shaking, could feel the sticky mixture of cold sweat and blood in his palm. He could smell that strong bloody scent, could hear the other's rapid panting and heartbeat.

This was too real. So real it made him want to vomit.

"Kid, don't meddle in other people's business," Old Scar lowered his voice, "That's a Beastshield guy, nothing to do with us."

"But he..."

"Listen to me," Old Scar's gaze became serious, "You're just a newly arrived newbie who can't even walk properly. The bounty hunter outside is a professional. You go out and you're delivering yourself to death."

The wild boar beastman raised his head, looking at Chen Mo. Those cloudy eyes were full of despair and pleading.

"Plea... please..." he was breathing faintly, "I don't want to die... I... I still have family..."

Chen Mo opened his mouth, not knowing what to say.

In a game, he should be the hero. He should rush out to defeat the bad guy, save the innocent. But when he looked into that wild boar's eyes, he suddenly realized—this wasn't a game.

This "NPC" would really die.

**Scene-4 Reality**

The door was pushed open again.

This time what came in was a wolf beastman, wearing a black tactical vest, a dagger hanging at his waist. His gaze was sharp, sweeping across the tavern interior, finally settling on the wild boar at Chen Mo's feet.

"Found you, G-892," the wolf beastman grinned, revealing sharp fangs, "Ran quite fast, but still can't escape my palm."

He strode over, completely ignoring Chen Mo and Old Scar's presence.

"Wait," Chen Mo didn't know where his courage came from, standing up, "He... he's injured, can you..."

The wolf beastman stopped, turning to look at Chen Mo. Those gray wolf eyes carried amusement and contempt.

"Newbie?" he asked, "First Arrival?"

"I..."

"Looking at your clumsy appearance, can't even stand steady," the wolf beastman laughed, "Listen, little bear, this isn't your business. This pig is a Beastshield grunt, my prey. You want to be a hero, wait until you're max level."

"But he'll die..."

"He was going to die anyway," the wolf beastman said matter-of-factly, "He's a Beastshield guy, wearing green skin, deserves to die. That's the rule of this world."

He finished speaking, no longer paying attention to Chen Mo, bending down to grab the wild boar beastman by the back of his neck.

"Don't... plea..." The wild boar beastman let out his final plea.

The wolf beastman's movement was fast. Chen Mo didn't even clearly see how he struck, only heard a dull thud, and that wild boar's body went soft.

Then, that warm, fishy liquid splashed onto Chen Mo's face.

It was blood. And something else.

Chen Mo was stunned. He could feel that liquid flowing down his cheek, could feel that strong, nauseating odor drilling into his nostrils. His vision began to blur, his stomach churning.

"First time?" The wolf beastman straightened up, shaking the blood off his hands, "No wonder your reaction is so big. You'll get used to it."

He skillfully pulled off a white long sock from the wild boar's foot, stuffed it into his waist pouch, then turned and left.

The tavern fell into dead silence.

Chen Mo looked down at the corpse at his feet. That wild boar's eyes were still open, pupils dilated, a trace of white foam at the corners of his mouth. His olive-green uniform was soaked with blood, clinging tightly to his fat-over-muscle body. What made Chen Mo unable to look away was the large wet patch at the other's crotch—that wasn't blood, it was urine, the trace of fear-induced incontinence.

He was dead.

Really dead.

In this "game."

**Scene-5 Vomiting**

Chen Mo rushed out of the tavern, kneeling by the roadside, vomiting violently.

But he forgot he was now a bear, a body with a completely different digestive system. What he vomited wasn't food from his stomach, but a stream of sour liquid, mixed with bile and undigested berries.

"Ugh... ugh..."

His body was convulsing, tears flowing uncontrollably. This wasn't sadness; it was a physiological reaction, the brain's self-protection mechanism when facing excessive stimulation.

Old Scar followed out, standing nearby, quietly watching him.

"Everyone's like this the first time," Old Scar said, his tone flat, "My first time seeing death, my reaction was even bigger than yours. Didn't eat for three whole days, vomited as soon as I ate."

"This... this isn't a game..." Chen Mo said with difficulty, his voice hoarse.

"I told you," Old Scar sighed, "To you, it's a game. To us, it's life."

"But that person... that wolfman... he was like... like killing a chicken..."

"To him, it is killing a chicken," Old Scar said, "Bounty hunters do exactly this. Kill Beastshield people, take their socks, exchange for money. Simple, direct, no psychological burden."

"Then... what about those Beastshield people?" Chen Mo raised his head, looking at Old Scar, "Do they deserve to be killed?"

Old Scar was silent for a while.

"You know," he finally said, "What did that wild boar just now, G-892, do?"

Chen Mo shook his head.

"Last month he raped a rabbit youth," Old Scar said, his voice as calm as if stating the weather, "Because that youth owed him fifty lu coins and couldn't pay. He had three accomplices who did it together. Afterward they split the child's belongings, including the scarf his mother knitted for him."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"I'm not saying he deserved to die," Old Scar continued, "I'm just saying, this world is very complex. Beastshield people aren't all victims, and bounty hunters aren't all cold-blooded killers. Everyone has their own reasons, their own choices, their own karma."

"But... but before he died he said he didn't want to die, said he had family..."

"Everyone says that," Old Scar smiled bitterly, "When death is at hand, who doesn't want to live?"

Chen Mo fell silent. He looked at his own bear paws—those claws that had just been splashed with blood and brain matter. In the "real world," he was just an ordinary office worker, facing a computer typing code every day, his biggest thrill on weekends playing games.

But now, he stood in this world full of blood, violence, and death, wearing a body that breathed, bled, and died.

"I... I want to quit," Chen Mo said, "How do I quit?"

Old Scar looked at him, something like... disappointment in his eyes?

"Anytime," he said, "Focus your attention, think 'I want to go back,' and your consciousness will detach from this body, returning 'above.' But I have to remind you—"

He paused.

"The body you purchased, this brown bear body, cost you three months' salary, right? If you quit now, it dies. Not from injury, but because your consciousness leaves, it becomes an empty shell, then rots. If you want to come down again, you have to spend money to buy a new one."

Chen Mo was stunned.

"This body... will die?"

"Will die," Old Scar nodded, "Just like that wild boar, become a corpse, thrown into a trash can, or eaten by wild dogs."

Chen Mo looked down at his own hands—these bear paws. He suddenly realized this wasn't just a "character," this was a life. A life that existed because of him.

"I..."

He didn't know what to say.

**Scene-6 Choice**

Chen Mo ultimately didn't quit.

Not because he was brave, but because he couldn't bear to lose three months' salary. And because he suddenly realized, if he ran away now, this body—this life he was "occupying"—would die meaninglessly.

"Don't think about being a hero," Old Scar walked with him on the night street, "And don't think about changing anything. This world has existed for a long time, longer than you imagine. It has its own rules, its own logic, its own balance."

"Then what should I do?" Chen Mo asked.

"Survive," Old Scar simply said, "Survive in this world. Find your place, find your way of survival. Some people choose to be bounty hunters, some choose to join Beastshield, some choose to be merchants, craftsmen, prostitutes... anyway, find a way to keep yourself alive."

"Prostitutes?" Chen Mo was stunned, "This world has..."

"Yes," Old Scar nodded, "Many. After all, there are only male beastmen here; desire needs an outlet. Many Arrivals choose this path—easy, quick money, and you don't really die."

Chen Mo fell silent. He remembered that wild boar's eyes before death, remembered the sensation of that warm blood splashing on his face. He didn't know if he could adapt to this world, didn't know if he could "get used to it."

But he knew he couldn't run away right now.

At least, not run away like this.

"Old Scar," he said, "Teach me. Teach me how to survive in this world."

Old Scar stopped walking, turning back to look at him. Something like... relief flashed through those cloudy bear eyes?

"You sure?" he asked, "This path isn't easy. You'll see more death, more violence, more... things you can't understand."

"I'm sure," Chen Mo said. He himself wasn't sure why he said this, but the words were already out, unable to be taken back.

"Alright," Old Scar grinned, "Then we'll start from the basics. First step—learn to fight with this body."

He brought Chen Mo to a secluded abandoned construction site.

"Attack me," Old Scar assumed a stance, "With all your strength."

"I... I don't want to hurt you..."

"You can't hurt me," Old Scar laughed, "Come."

Chen Mo took a deep breath, raised his bear paws, and clumsily lunged forward.

**Scene-7 Training**

That night, Chen Mo learned many things.

He learned the advantages and disadvantages of this bear body—great strength, high defense, but slow speed, poor flexibility. He learned how to use weight to suppress opponents, how to use his thick fat layer to absorb damage, how to leverage strength advantages in close-quarters grappling.

He also learned the "rules" of this world.

"See that?" Old Scar pointed at a glowing sign in the distance, "That's a 'safe zone.' In the safe zone, you can't attack others, and others can't attack you. But safe zones are few, and very expensive."

"Expensive?"

"Game coins, or real-world money, both can be exchanged," Old Scar said, "Many Arrivals earn enough money, then hide in safe zones and don't come out, living stable lives."

"What about the others?"

"The others have to struggle outside," Old Scar shrugged, "Just like me."

"You're an NPC, you need money too?"

Old Scar glanced at him, his gaze complex.

"I was born and raised here," he said, "I don't have an 'above' to return to. I have to eat, sleep, get treated when sick. This world is everything to me."

Chen Mo fell silent. He suddenly realized Old Scar's existence was more... real than he had imagined. Not a program, not a character, but a living, breathing, thinking, fearing... life.

"Sorry," Chen Mo said, "I thought you were just an NPC before..."

"It's fine," Old Scar smiled, "You Arrivals all think that. After a long time, you'll understand, everyone in this world has their own story."

He paused, looking into the distance.

"Just like that wild boar just now, G-892. He had a name, had a past, had a dream—though that dream might just be saving enough money to go back home and farm. But he died, because he wore that green skin. That's the cruelty of this world."

"Then... what about bounty hunters?" Chen Mo asked, "That wolfman, does he have his own story?"

"Of course," Old Scar said, "He used to be a Beastshield person too, a G-rank grunt. Later his partner betrayed him, nearly got him killed in a trash can. He escaped and became a bounty hunter, specifically killing Beastshield people."

"So he was... revenge?"

"Started as revenge, later became business," Old Scar said, "Just like I said, everyone has their own reasons, their own choices, their own karma."

Chen Mo looked down at his bear paws—those claws that were slowly learning to fight.

He didn't know what kind of person he would become. Bounty hunter? Beastshield member? Or a coward hiding in a safe zone?

But he knew, from tonight onward, he could no longer treat this as a game.

**Scene-8 Dawn**

When dawn broke, Chen Mo and Old Scar sat on the ruins of the construction site, watching the sky in the distance.

That was his first time seeing sunrise in this world. Orange-red light rose from the horizon, illuminating broken buildings, illuminating dirty streets, illuminating those figures huddled in corners.

"So beautiful," Chen Mo murmured.

"Yes," Old Scar nodded, "No matter what happened the night before, the sun still rises as usual. That's the first thing this world taught me."

"First thing?"

"Mm," Old Scar stood up, patting the dust off his body, "Let's go, take you to eat something. Then I have to take you to meet someone."

"What person?"

"Someone who can teach you more things," Old Scar smiled mysteriously, "A... true Arrival."

"True Arrival? What do you mean?"

"You'll know soon," Old Scar said, "But I have to remind you—that person is a bit... special."

They walked out of the construction site, onto the street.

The morning city began to wake. Chen Mo saw all kinds of beastmen—Beastshield employees in olive-green uniforms, civilians in tattered clothes, and some in strange costumes... Arrivals.

He could tell who was an Arrival now. Those people's eyes were different—more confused, more alert, or rather, more like "players."

"Old Scar," Chen Mo suddenly asked, "Do you hate Arrivals?"

Old Scar was stunned for a moment, then laughed.

"Why hate?" he asked, "You brought new technology, new ideas, new possibilities. Though you also brought chaos and destruction, but... that's the cost of progress, I suppose."

He paused, looking into the distance.

"And, some Arrivals became my friends. True, life-and-death friends."

Chen Mo didn't speak. He remembered that wild boar's eyes before death, remembered the sensation of that warm, fishy liquid splashing on his face.

He didn't know if he could become the kind of friend Old Scar spoke of.

But he wanted to try.

**Scene-9 A New Beginning**

They arrived at an ordinary-looking residence.

Old Scar knocked on the door, three long and two short. The door opened a crack, an eye peeking out from inside.

"Old Scar? This early?"

"Brought a newbie," Old Scar said, "First Arrival, needs a mentor."

The door opened.

Standing behind it was a... rabbit?

Chen Mo was stunned. It was a white rabbit beastman, small and thin, completely different from those muscle-knotted beastmen. But when he saw those eyes, he froze.

Those eyes were red, not a color common for beastmen, but rather... like a human's?

"Newbie?" The rabbit sized up Chen Mo, the corner of his mouth slightly rising, "Looks pretty sturdy. Just the eyes are a bit dull."

"He saw blood for the first time last night," Old Scar said, "Vomited all over the floor."

"Normal," the rabbit dismissed it, "My first time, I pissed myself from fear."

He turned to Chen Mo, extending his claw.

"Welcome aboard, newbie. My name's Whitefang, your mentor."

Chen Mo hesitantly grasped that claw. That claw was small, soft, but he could feel the power contained within.

"My name..." Chen Mo paused, then smiled, "Call me Little Bear. I don't know what my name in this world is yet."

"Little Bear?" Whitefang raised an eyebrow, "Alright then, Little Bear. From today, I'll teach you to survive in this world. Not the techniques of how to kill people, but how to... maintain your humanity."

"Humanity?"

"Mm," Whitefang's expression became serious, "This world will change you. Will make you become cold, become cruel, become... not like yourself. What I want to teach you is how to not lose that 'human' character while becoming stronger."

Chen Mo looked at this white rabbit, suddenly feeling a strange... hope?

Maybe, he really could find his place in this world.

Not as a bystander, not as a player, but as... a true, living being.

"Okay," Chen Mo said, "I'll learn from you."

Whitefang smiled, revealing a pair of cute rabbit teeth.

"Then first lesson," he said, "Learn to eat in this world. Not using your world's money to buy supplies, but really going to find food."

He led Chen Mo toward the kitchen.

"This world has wild fruits, has prey, has... uh, things that don't taste good but fill your stomach. You have to learn to distinguish what can be eaten, what can't be eaten, what gives you diarrhea when eaten, what produces strange hallucinations when eaten..."

Chen Mo followed behind, listening to Whitefang's chatter.

Outside the window, the sun had fully risen, illuminating this world full of blood, violence, but also full of life and hope.

His first game had just begun.

---

**Story END**

> *In this world, death is real, pain is real, tears are real. But hope, is also real.*

## Story Commentary

### Their Final Stories

【Living→Dead - Wild Boar Beastman - G-892】

About this wild boar beastman, we only know his number, not his name. Old Scar said he raped a rabbit youth last month, but who knows if that's true or false? Maybe it was true, maybe Old Scar made up the story to make Chen Mo feel better. We'll never know.

When he died, he grabbed a stranger's pant leg, said he didn't want to die, said he had family. Was this the truth or the instinct to survive? Equally unknown.

That's what this world is like. Every dead beastman has countless untold stories. Some are true, some are false, some are indistinguishable between good and evil. But when blood splashes onto your face, none of that matters.

What matters is—what do you remember?

Chen Mo remembered those cloudy eyes, remembered that warm liquid, remembered the feeling of himself kneeling by the roadside vomiting. This might be the only trace G-892 left behind—a newcomer's first breakdown.

---

### The People Still Alive

【Arrival (VR Player)→Living - Brown Bear Beastman - Chen Mo (Little Bear)】

Chen Mo, 27 years old, a programmer in real life, spent three months' salary on VR equipment. He thought this was just a game, but the first death made him understand this wasn't a game.

He's still shaking now. Every time he closes his eyes, he can see those cloudy eyes. But he didn't choose to quit—not because of bravery, but because he couldn't bear to lose the money, and also because... some indescribable obsession.

He met Old Scar, a native-born "NPC," learned the basic operations of this body. He also met Whitefang, a mysterious mentor, about to start learning "how to maintain humanity."

He doesn't know what the future holds. Maybe he'll become a bounty hunter, maybe he'll become a member of Beastshield, maybe he'll die in some battle, his corpse thrown into a trash can.

But at least, he made a choice. Not to run away, but to face it.

This itself is a beginning.

---

【Native-born Resident→Living - Brown Bear Beastman - Old Scar】

Old Scar, age unknown, has lived in this world for so long he can't remember his own birthday. He's seen too many Arrivals—excited, fearful, indifferent, fanatical. Most of them left, either quitting the game or dying in this world.

He chose to become a newbie guide, not out of kindness, but out of loneliness. He wanted to communicate with these people from "above," wanted to know what that world was like.

But he never asked. He knew, asking was pointless. That world was as unreachable to him as heaven.

He looked at Chen Mo—that little bear whose eyes still maintained some clarity—and had a strange premonition. This person, maybe different from other Arrivals.

Of course, he could also be wrong. Most people, in the end, all became the same.

---

【Arrival (VR Player)→Living - White Rabbit Beastman - Whitefang】

Whitefang, real name unknown, no one knows how long he's been in this world. He calls himself a "mentor," specifically teaching newbies how to survive while maintaining their humanity.

His eyes are red, not a color common for beastmen. What does this mean? Is he a special NPC? Or some kind of hidden-class player?

Old Scar didn't tell Chen Mo the answer. Maybe he himself doesn't know either.

But Whitefang's existence brings a different possibility to this story. In this world full of blood and violence, someone is thinking about something like "humanity."

This itself is a miracle.

---

### Story Postscript

This story has no profound philosophical contemplation, no grand worldview exposition, only a simple question: When you face real death, what will you do?

Chen Mo's choice might not be the bravest, but it's the most real. He's not a hero, just an ordinary person, thrown into a cruel world, trying to find his place.

This story also doesn't give an answer. It's just a beginning.

As for G-892—that dead wild boar—whether he truly raped that rabbit youth or not, no longer matters. What matters is, Chen Mo remembered his death, remembered that sensation of warm, fishy liquid splashing on his face.

This is the first lesson this world taught him: Death is real, pain is real. But the choice of how to face it, is also real.

Good luck, Little Bear.

Good luck, Chen Mo.
