### Story Reflections

When I wrote these commentaries, I felt a heavy suffocation. There are no innocents in the world of "Long Live the Empire," only perpetrators and victims—and often, the perpetrators were once victims too; they just climbed higher and gained the power to hurt others.

B-11407 lived for 23 days. He picked up a maple-leaf-shaped metal piece and put it in his locker. Why? The chip allows 15% self-awareness; what did he do with that 15%? Maybe he just thought that metal piece was pretty, shining in the snow. Maybe that was the only autonomous choice in his brief life: picking up a useless piece of metal and treasuring it.

Then he died, stimulated to ejaculation by a comrade's tail, his military socks pulled off, his brain burned out. Will his metal piece be discovered? Will it be thrown away? Or will it be picked up by some new Kik soldier, continuing this meaningless legacy?

Hu Meng is being promoted, gaining power, but the brand on his face forever reminds him: You are still a slave. He orders the execution of others, but he himself could be executed in the next second. No one is safe in this system, except that high and mighty Emperor—and according to the setting, the Emperor was exiled by a higher civilization. So he is also at some point on the food chain; we just haven't seen what's above him yet.

White Wolf enjoys power but also fears losing it. The snow leopard executes orders but begins to feel "unclean." These are cracks, the self-awareness under chip control quietly growing. The Empire uses fear and pleasure to control Kik soldiers, but those very means of control are giving birth to what they try to destroy: humanity, or rather, the part of beastly nature that cannot be completely tamed.

What haunts me the most are the other black-sock soldiers in that dormitory. B-11408, B-11409... They witnessed the execution of a comrade, fearing, but tomorrow they must continue to patrol, continue to fight, continue to wait for the day when they too might be executed. They share a room, breathe the same air, but cannot have emotional bonds with each other. They are the most familiar strangers, companions sharing the shadow of death.

What kind of world is this? Life is born from the cultivation pod, given a number, implanted with a chip, trained to kill, and then consumed, executed, and recycled. The entire process is efficient, cold, and perfect. But within this perfect control system, a maple-leaf-shaped metal piece is picked up, a moment of trance occurs while washing a tail, and a complex emotion emerges when a slave touches the brand on his face...

These tiny, seemingly meaningless moments are life's final, gentle resistance against the system. They change nothing; B-11407 still died, Hu Meng is still a slave, and White Wolf is still exiled. But these moments exist, like occasional flashes of sunlight in the snow, reminding us: even in this absolutely hopeless world, there is still something striving to live, striving to feel, striving to remember.

Even if only to be forgotten.
