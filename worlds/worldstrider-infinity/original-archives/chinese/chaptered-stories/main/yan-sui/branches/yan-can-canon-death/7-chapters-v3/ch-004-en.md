# Chapter 4 Abyss (Part 2) — Collapse

On the tenth day, Yan Shen's tail began to wag unconsciously.

Not from happiness. It was... hearing a clap and wagging. Hearing footsteps approaching and drooping down tucked between legs. Hearing a whistle and lying down. His body reacted before his consciousness; by the time his brain caught up, the posture was already set.

The first time he discovered himself wagging his tail, Yan Shen froze.

He lay on the ground, cheek pressed against cement, tail swinging left and right behind him, slapping the ground with light patting sounds. That sound was especially crisp and especially... servile in the silent basement. His body in that instant seemed no longer his own, like a skin bag with the soul sucked out, leaving only instinctive conditioned reflexes.

He wanted to stop his tail. But his consciousness sent the command, and the tail kept wagging. Two wags, three, then slowly, reluctantly, drooped down.

Yan Shen stared at the ground, eyes opened wide. His breathing became rapid, chest heaving violently, collar tightening and loosening with each breath. A sound came from his throat—not a whimper, not a growl, the kind of breathy sound a living person makes when frightened by their own existence.

Then he vomited.

There was nothing in his stomach, only acid and bile. He lay on the ground, dry heaving, spasming, transparent liquid flowing from the corner of his mouth, dripping onto the cement floor, mixing with his own saliva. He trembled while vomiting, not from nausea, but from fear.

He was afraid of himself.

On the eleventh day, the fox beastkin brought a larger mirror.

It was a broken mirror, cracks radiating from the center, cutting Yan Shen's reflection into seven or eight pieces. The fox beastkin propped the mirror against the wall, then clapped his hands.

Yan Shen lay down.

His body folded the instant the clap sounded, spine sinking, limbs on the ground, tail drooping between his legs. His movement was fast, very standard, like a well-trained dog. His eyes looked at the ground, not at the mirror. But he knew what was in the mirror.

"Good dog." The fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen had no reaction. Inside he was screaming, tearing, cursing himself with the most vicious words. But his body didn't move, maintaining that posture, like a stone statue carved into a submissive pose.

The fox beastkin walked over, kicking his flank with his boot. Not heavy, but Yan Shen's body instinctively shrank to the side, emitting a low, nearly whimpering breathy sound.

That sound didn't come from him. Or rather, that sound came from him, but not from his will. His throat had produced that sound without his brain's permission—a weakening, pleading,讨好施暴者的 sound.

Yan Shen wanted to die.

He wanted to die so badly it drove him mad. He used his nails to dig at cracks in the cement floor, trying to find a sharp shard to cut his own throat. But his nails had already been worn blunt from these days of crawling, fingertips bloody and模糊, unable to dig off even a complete piece of skin.

The fox beastkin watched him, laughing happily.

"Tomorrow I'll get you a new collar," he said, "leather, with studs. You'll definitely like it."

The door closed, lock clicking.

Yan Shen still lay there. He didn't get up. He didn't know why he should get up. What after getting up? Standing gets beaten. Lying down doesn't. He'd already learned. His body learned this truth before his consciousness.

His gaze moved to the mirror.

In the broken mirror were seven or eight Yan Shens. Each one lying on the ground, gray-and-white fur filthy beyond recognition, collar勒着脖子, eyes hollow like two dry wells. Those Yan Shens looked at him, and he looked at those Yan Shens. They stared at each other, like confirming each other's existence—or each other's nonexistence.

On the thirteenth night, Yan Shen briefly woke up.

That was the quietest moment of the day. The fox beastkin and his accomplices had all left, to drink, to sleep, to enjoy their twisted lives in White Capital. The basement held only Yan Shen alone, and the rustling of rats in the corner.

Yan Shen wasn't lying down. He curled in the corner, iron chain coiled into a circle on the cement floor. His body was feverish, the heat of infected wounds steaming from inside out, putting his consciousness in a half-dream half-wake blur. His left leg had completely lost sensation; the skin at his knee presented an ominous dark purple, burning hot to the touch, but inside was dead with no response.

He looked at his hand.

That hand had once been rough, powerful, able to grip a cane and also a knife. Now it was skin and bones, knuckles protruding abruptly, nails blunt, paw pads abraded, scabbed and split again, blood and mud mixed together, forming black hard lumps in the finger crevices.

He slowly, slowly, curled his fingers, making a fist.

Muscles trembling. Not because there was strength, but because there was no strength. His arm was thin like two firewood sticks; even the action of making a fist was intermittent. But he still clenched it tight. He looked at his fist, at those protruding knuckles, at the sparse gray-white fur on the back of his hand.

Then he remembered Suisui.

When Suisui was little, he would use this hand to hold Suisui's small hand, teaching him to use chopsticks. When Suisui was naughty, scattering rice grains all over the table, he would use this hand to lightly tap Suisui's forehead. When Suisui had nightmares at night, he would use this hand to pat Suisui's back until he fell asleep again.

That was a father's hand.

Now this hand crawled on cement floors, licked sour food basins, automatically propped up its body upon hearing a whistle. Now the owner of this hand would tremble at footsteps, would curl into a ball when kicked, would... not resist when called "good dog."

Yan Shen's fist loosened.

Something shattered in his eyes. Not tears. Tears had long dried up. It was something deeper—that which had supported him for fifty-three years, which let him stand straight as a father, a veteran, a beastkin—shattered.

He was no longer Yan Shen.

Yan Shen wouldn't lie down at a clap. Yan Shen wouldn't wag his tail when beaten. Yan Shen wouldn't watch himself crawl like a dog in the mirror and remain unmoved.

Then what was he?

He was nothing. He was only a lump of chained, breathing, conditioned-reflex meat. A thing without name, without past, without dignity. A... a...

Yan Shen buried his head in his knees.

His body was trembling. Violent, uncontrollable, seizure-like trembling. His teeth bit through his lip, blood seeping out, but he felt no pain. His fingers dug into the flesh of his knees, nails embedding in, but he felt no pain.

He felt nothing anymore.

Except that bone-seeping, overwhelming, more-terrible-than-death shame.

He couldn't go back to see Suisui.

This thought stabbed like a knife into the only place where he still retained a bit of sensation. He couldn't go back. Couldn't let Suisui see him like this. Couldn't let Suisui know his father had been chained, knelt, crawled on the ground licking basins, wagged his tail at a whistle. Couldn't let Suisui know his father—that father who taught him "power and responsibility coexist"—was now only a domesticated dog.

What would Suisui think of him?

Would Suisui hate him? Or pity him? Either one was more terrible than death.

Yan Shen curled into a ball in the corner, the iron chain making fine clattering sounds with his trembling. A sound came from his throat—that was a mixture of human language and beast whimper, a creature that had lost all language ability, emitting the last bit of sound from the depths of despair.

He didn't know how long he curled there. Maybe an hour, maybe the entire night. Time had no meaning in this basement.

But he heard a sound.

That was a distant sound, coming from outside the basement door, from the ground above, from some direction he couldn't imagine. That sound was very dull, like someone walking, then... voices?

Yan Shen didn't raise his head. He'd already learned not to expect. Expectation only brought more beating.

But that sound drew closer. Then, the basement door made a loud noise.

Not the sound of unlocking. It was the sound of ramming. Huge, violent, like something had crashed into it from outside. The iron door vibrated in its frame, wall plaster rustling down.

Then another loud crash.

The lock on the door deformed, flew off, hit the opposite wall with a crisp metallic clatter. The door was shoved open, a gust of cold, snow-scented air pouring in.

Yan Shen shivered from that gust. He slowly raised his head.

A figure stood in the doorway. Backlit, face unclear, only a massive outline visible—broad shoulders, thick chest, drooping ears. That outline in the dim light was like a mountain, a mountain that had suddenly appeared in the abyss.

That person's nose was twitching violently in the air. Canine beastkin's sense of smell. He was smelling. He was smelling the odors in this basement—blood, mold, fear, excrement, and...

"...Senior?"

The voice was very low, very hoarse, carrying an unbelieving tremble.

Yan Shen didn't answer. His body in that instant made a reaction that made him want to die immediately—his tail, that orange-yellow mixed with gray-white tiger tail, upon hearing that voice, unconsciously drooped down, tucked between his legs. His body also instinctively shrank down, like trying to curl into an even smaller ball.

No.

Don't.

Don't look at me.

Don't look at me like this.

The figure in the doorway moved. He stepped through the door, footsteps heavy, each step like stepping on Yan Shen's heart. He walked before Yan Shen, squatting down.

Yan Shen saw his face.

Bernese Mountain Dog. Dark brown and black mixed fur, white chest fur. Drooping ears. Those eyes—those eyes Yan Shen had once watched mature from green youth—now opened wide, pupils contracting into pinpoints in the dim light.

It was Yi An.

Yi An's mouth opened, like wanting to say something, but no sound came out. His gaze moved across Yan Shen's body—from the collar, to the chain, to the body thin as bones, to the abraded paw pads, to Yan Shen's current posture curled in the corner, tail tucked between legs.

His expression twisted in that instant.

Not disgust. Not revulsion. Something more complex, heavier than that—that was the expression a junior makes when seeing the senior he once most respected, ruined to this state.

Yi An reached out his hand.

His hand was large, paw pads rough, palm warm. He reached toward Yan Shen's shoulder, movement very light, like afraid of breaking something.

Yan Shen jerked backward.

His back hit the wall, making a dull sound. His eyes opened wide, pupils unfocused, no joy of recognizing Yi An inside, only a deeper, more thorough fear. His lips trembled,断断续续的气音 coming from his throat.

"...No..." he finally said one word, voice hoarse beyond recognition, "...don't... touch me..."

Yi An's hand stopped in midair.

His ears—those Bernese Mountain Dog characteristic drooping ears—in that instant flattened. Tightly pressed against the sides of his head, like two leaves soaked by rain. His tail, that normally fluffy flag-like tail, was now tucked between his legs.

Canine beastkin's fear response.

But Yi An didn't retreat. His hand only paused a moment, then continued reaching over, lightly, lightly, touching Yan Shen's shoulder.

Yan Shen's body went rigid. Then he started trembling. Violent, uncontrollable trembling. Not from cold, not from pain, but the kind of trembling a thing that has completely shattered makes when touched.

"...Senior." Yi An said. His voice was very low, very deep, squeezed from the deepest part of his chest, "...It's me. Little Bo."

Yan Shen's eyes moved.

His gaze focused, bit by bit, finding focal points on Yi An's face. He looked at Yi An's eyes, at those deep brown, slightly trembling eyes. He opened his mouth, lips moving, but no sound came out.

"...I'm taking you home." Yi An said.

His arm circled Yan Shen's shoulder. Yan Shen's body in that instant went rigid as stone, then went soft. He didn't resist. He no longer had strength to resist. He only let Yi An lift him from the corner, let those rough large hands support his armpits, let his own body be dragged up like an empty flour bag.

Yi An half-supported, half-dragged Yan Shen toward the door. Yan Shen's left leg dragged on the ground, completely useless, boot sole scraping against cement, making rustling sounds. His body was too light—much lighter than Yi An remembered, like a bundle of dry firewood, like an emptied skin.

When they reached the door, Yan Shen suddenly grabbed the doorframe.

His fingers dug into the rusted iron sheet, knuckles white. His body was resisting, not resisting leaving, but resisting being seen. He turned his head, glanced at that basement—that cement floor he'd lain on for fourteen days, that corner where he'd licked the basin, that broken mirror.

Then he turned his head, no longer looking.

Yi An helped him out the door.

Outside was a snowy night. Cold air cut like knives on the face. Yan Shen was naked, only draped in Yi An's taken-off coat, but that coat was too large for his current frame, slippery on his shoulders, about to fall off at any moment.

Yi An carried him on his back.

Yan Shen's body lay on Yi An's back, chin resting on Yi An's shoulder. His breathing was very light, very shallow, carrying the heat of fever, spraying on Yi An's neck. His left leg hung at Yi An's waist side, dragging behind, swaying with Yi An's steps.

Yi An walked step by step forward.

Snow fell on them. On Yi An's dark brown head, on Yan Shen's gray-and-white fur. Footprints extended in the snow, one long one short, the deep one was Yi An's, the shallow one was the trail dragged by Yan Shen's left leg.

"...Senior." Yi An suddenly said.

"..." Yan Shen didn't answer.

"...Hold on." Yi An's voice was trembling, "...Almost there."

Yan Shen's eyes were half-open. He looked at White Capital in the snowy night, at those dilapidated buildings, at the distant lights of Beastshield patrols. His consciousness was drifting, like a leaf without roots.

He was thinking, what is Suisui doing now.

Is Suisui still sleeping? Or already awake, discovering he hadn't come back? Would Suisui worry? Or would he think he'd just gone out to buy something, would be back soon?

Suisui.

Dad can't go back.
