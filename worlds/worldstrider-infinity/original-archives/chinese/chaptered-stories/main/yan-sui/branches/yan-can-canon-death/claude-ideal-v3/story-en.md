# No Return

## One

When Yan Shen was brought back, his whole body was trembling.

Not from cold. The stove fire in the room was blazing, the iron stove wall burning red, heat rolling in the cramped space. But Yan Shen lay on Yi An's bed, wrapped in two thick quilts, teeth still chattering, making that very small, very fine, like gravel scraping glass, clattering sound.

Yi An sat on the chair by the bed, holding a basin of warm water in his hands. The water surface was slightly rippling, not entirely because his hands were trembling—though they were—because his breathing was too heavy, every exhale driving the rise and fall of his chest, driving the tremor of his arms, driving the ripples spreading circle after circle in the basin.

He put down the basin, wrung the towel dry.

The towel was gray, coarse cloth, edges worn to fraying. Yi An spread it on his palm, aired it a while, letting the temperature drop, not too hot. Then, he reached out, to lift the quilt on Yan Shen's body.

His hand had just touched the quilt corner when Yan Shen's body suddenly shrank.

That was a very violent reaction. Like being electrocuted, Yan Shen's shoulders jerked upward, head tilting back, a very short, broken breathy sound coming from his throat. His eyes didn't open—from being carried back until now, he had hardly looked directly at Yi An—but eyeballs were turning violently under the eyelids, eyelashes trembling.

Yi An's hand stopped.

"...Senior." He said. Voice very light, like afraid of disturbing something, "...It's me. Little Bo."

Yan Shen's body froze there, shoulders suspended in midair, maintaining that defensive posture. After a very long, very long time, his muscles relaxed bit by bit, falling back onto the mattress. But he still didn't open his eyes.

Yi An lifted the quilt.

Under the quilt, Yan Shen only wore an oversized coat—that was Yi An's own coat, dark brown, wool blend. The body under the coat...

Yi An's gaze stopped on Yan Shen's chest.

He saw that collar.

Not an ordinary collar. It was the leather kind, very wide, about palm-width, edges镶嵌着一圈已经生锈的铆钉. The collar was deeply embedded into the flesh of Yan Shen's neck,勒出一圈深紫色的淤痕, some places the skin had already been worn away, forming dark red blood scabs, stuck together with the collar's leather, indistinguishable where was skin, where was leather.

Yi An's ears—those Bernese Mountain Dog characteristic ears, normally always温顺地垂在脑袋两侧—at that instant flattened. Tightly pressed against his scalp, like two leaves scalded by boiling water.

He continued looking down.

Yan Shen's chest, gray-and-white fur糊成了一绺一绺的硬块 by sweat and blood污. Ribs protruding, one by one, like a disassembled and reassembled accordion. The pectoral muscles—Yi An remembered there were solid pectoral muscles beneath—now只剩下一层薄薄的皮,贴在骨头上, slightly rising and falling with breath.

Further down, abdomen. There was a large area of bruising there, extending from below the left ribs to above the hip bone, presenting an ominous dark purple, mixed with some deeper dot-like marks in the middle.

Stick injuries. Not fists, but wooden sticks. Precise, purposeful beatings, avoiding vital points, only hitting where pain could be maximized.

Yi An's gaze continued downward.

Yan Shen's hands. Those hands had once been rough and powerful, able to grip a cane, able to knock down troublemaking newcomers, able to wrap dumplings on Suisui's birthday. Now they were skin and bones, knuckles protruding abruptly, paw pads abraded, scabbed and split again, blood and mud mixed together, forming black hard lumps in the finger crevices. Nails blunt, not cut, but worn—worn on rough ground.

Yi An's gaze stopped on Yan Shen's left hand.

That hand's fingers slightly curled, fingertips lightly, unconsciously scratching on the bedsheet, like grasping at something nonexistent. Every scratch carried a rhythm that made Yi An's chest tighten—not pain, but fear. That bone-deep fear that had already become bodily instinct.

Yi An took a deep breath. He smelled it.

Blood. Sweat. Fear. Excrement. And something deeper—something seeping from deep within Yan Shen's skin, like the smell of a rotted soul. Canine beastkin's sense of smell was sharper than feline; Yi An could distinguish many things from these odors: duration of imprisonment (at least ten days), frequency of beatings (at least twice daily), and...

And something that made Yi An's tail involuntarily tighten.

He didn't dare think carefully. He dipped the towel back in water, wrung it dry again, then lightly, lightly, started wiping Yan Shen's face.

From the forehead. The moment the towel touched skin, Yan Shen's eyelids trembled, brows furrowed, but he didn't dodge. Yi An's movement was very light, like afraid of breaking something. He wiped away the dried bloodstains and dirt on Yan Shen's forehead bit by bit, revealing the pale skin beneath.

Then the eye corners. Cheekbones. Chin.

When wiping to the lower jaw, Yan Shen's lips moved.

"...Suisui..." he said. Voice very light, very hoarse, like drifting from very far away, "...Suisui... don't be afraid..."

Yi An's hand paused.

Yan Shen's eyes were still closed. He was talking in his sleep—or rather, talking in delirium in half-consciousness. His head slightly turned to one side, white tiger whiskers spreading on the pillow, illuminated by stove fire into soft silver. His expression in that moment became very soft, soft enough to make Yi An almost unable to hold the towel in his hand.

"...Senior." Yi An said, "...Suisui is at home. He's fine."

He didn't know if Yan Shen heard. But Yan Shen's brows slightly relaxed, lips no longer moving.

Yi An continued wiping.

When wiping to the neck, he stopped. The collar was still there, leather and flesh stuck together, emitting a nauseating fishy-sweet smell. Yi An didn't dare pull hard, only used his finger dipped in warm water, bit by bit moistening the stuck places, trying to separate them.

Yan Shen's body was trembling.

That trembling became more violent when Yi An touched the collar. Not from cold, but some deeper thing—something unrelated to pain, unrelated to temperature, only related to memory. Yan Shen's teeth started chattering again, making that clattering sound. His fingers gripped the bedsheet deathly tight, knuckles white.

"...Senior," Yi An said, "...I'll help you take this off."

Yan Shen didn't answer. But his body in that moment made a reaction that nearly stopped Yi An's heart—his tail, that section poking out from the coat edge, orange-yellow mixed with gray-white tiger tail, upon hearing Yi An's voice, unconsciously drooped down, tucked between his legs.

That was a submissive posture. A conditioned-reflex posture after domestication.

Yi An's gaze stayed on that tail for a long time. His ears pressed tighter. His eyes reddened, but no tears. Tears had already dried when he carried Yan Shen back; now only dry burning pain remained in his eye sockets.

He slowly, slowly, unfastened the collar from Yan Shen's neck.

When leather and flesh separated, it made a very slight sound, like tape being torn open. A breathy sound overflowed from Yan Shen's throat—not a shout, but that kind of final instinctive protest a person already accustomed to pain makes when pain descends again.

The collar was taken off. Yi An placed it on the ground by the bed. The leather was stained with blood and skin flakes, presenting a deep brown, dirty luster under the stove firelight.

Yi An continued wiping. Wiping past shoulders, chest, abdomen. Every bruise, every wound, every place where fur had been worn to expose pink tender flesh, he saw clearly. But he didn't ask. He didn't ask "who did it," didn't ask "what did they do," didn't ask "does it hurt."

He only wiped.

Because he knew, some questions didn't need answers. Answers were written on Yan Shen's body, in those stick marks, in abraded paw pads, in collar marks deeply embedded in flesh, in that tail that unconsciously tightened upon hearing sound.

Yi An changed a basin of water. The water had already turned pale red.

He helped Yan Shen turn over. On Yan Shen's back, the muscles on both sides of the spine presented an abnormal concavity—that was muscle atrophy caused by long-term prone posture. Shoulder blades protruding, like a pair of wings about to break through skin. A long abrasion on the waist side, extending from below ribs to hip bone, already infected, edges red and swollen, middle oozing pale yellow fluid.

Yi An used warm water to clean that wound. Yan Shen's body tensed a bit during cleaning, then went soft. His consciousness was drifting, half-dream half-wake, occasionally overflowing a few meaningless breathy sounds from his throat.

After wiping, Yi An pulled out an old uniform from the camphor wood box under the bed.

Beastshield Golden Age's military-green uniform. Shoulder patches bore the beast claw and shield emblem. That was the uniform Yan Shen wore back then; Yi An had always kept it, folded neatly, hidden in the deepest part of the camphor wood box, rarely taking it out to look even himself.

He put it on Yan Shen.

The uniform on Yan Shen's thin body looked loose. But that looseness had a strange solemnity, like a too-light shell being fitted into too-heavy armor. Yi An helped him button up, straighten the collar, smooth the white tiger whiskers one by one.

"...You wear this," Yi An said, voice light like afraid of startling something, "...most like your proper self."

Yan Shen's eyes opened.

That was the first time after returning that he looked directly at Yi An. His pupils contracted into a thin line under the stove firelight, amber iris devoid of any luster, only that bottomless, dry-well-like emptiness.

He looked at Yi An. For a long time. Like recognizing, like confirming, like trying to overlap the face before him with some shadow in memory.

"...Little Bo." He finally said.

"...It's me, Senior."

Yan Shen's lips moved. His gaze moved from Yi An's face, to the ceiling, to the stove fire, to the whitened night sky outside the window. His Adam's apple rolled, like swallowing something very bitter.

"...Kill me." He said.

## Two

Yi An didn't answer immediately.

He sat on the chair by the bed, body leaning forward, elbows on knees, fingers interlaced against his chin. The stove firelight jumped on his profile, casting his shadow on the wall, stretched long, swaying.

"...No." Yi An said. Voice very hoarse, squeezed from deep in his throat, "...Your injuries will heal. I'll find a military doctor tomorrow, there are people I know in Beastshield, they can..."

"Injuries can heal." Yan Shen said. He interrupted Yi An, tone calm like stating a weather fact, "Some things can't heal."

Yi An's fingers tightened. Interlaced fingers pressed too tight, knuckles whitening, bones making slight creaking sounds.

Yan Shen slowly pulled his right hand from under the quilt. His movement was very slow, every joint making fine, rusted grinding sounds. He raised his hand before his eyes, looking at that bony hand, at abraded paw pads and blunt nails.

"...In my present state," he said, "I can't go back to see Suisui."

His fingers slightly curled, making a fist. Muscles trembling—not because there was strength, but because there was no strength. That fist was clenched very reluctantly, intermittent, like an infant just learning to grasp.

"...Suisui is only twenty-five." Yan Shen continued, voice light like talking to himself, "He still has a long road ahead. He can't have a father who was chained, knelt, crawled on the ground licking basins."

Yi An's lips trembled. He wanted to say something, but his throat seemed blocked by something, no sound coming out.

"...He'll be pointed at." Yan Shen said, "Will be laughed at. People will say 'look, that's Yan Shen's son, his dad was domesticated like a dog.' Suisui already has it hard in Beastshield... I can't add this kind of stain to him..."

"It's not your fault!" Yi An suddenly shouted. His voice was very loud, echoing in the cramped room, startling himself. He panted heavily, eyes opened wide, "...It's those bastards' fault! Those animals! You didn't do anything wrong!"

Yan Shen looked at Yi An. His expression didn't change, but a trace of something like sorrow flashed in his eyes.

"...I know." He said, "I know it's not my fault."

He put his hand back under the quilt, looking at the ceiling again.

"...But Suisui doesn't know. Suisui won't know. He'll only know that his father was chained like a dog for two weeks. He'll know his father lay down at a whistle. He'll know his father wagged his tail."

Yan Shen's voice paused. His Adam's apple rolled, like swallowing something bitter.

"...I can't let him know." He said, "I can't let him see."

Yi An's hand clenched into a fist. His knuckles white, palm dug with deep crescents by his nails. His breathing became very heavy, every inhale carrying a snuffling sound from his nasal cavity.

"...So you want me to kill you?" Yi An's voice dropped, squeezed from between his teeth, "...With my own hands?"

"Mm." Yan Shen said.

"...How do you expect me to live after this?" Yi An asked. His eyes reddened, but no tears. Tears swirled in his eye sockets, but didn't fall. "...You want me to think of this night every time I think of you? To think of me... me..."

He couldn't continue.

Yan Shen turned his head, looking at Yi An. His gaze was very calm, that calm of someone who had finally reached some endpoint after experiencing everything.

"...Little Bo." He said, "When you got promoted to blue uniform, the people below wouldn't accept you. You came to me crying, saying you didn't want to do it anymore. Do you remember what I said?"

Yi An's shoulders trembled.

"...You said," his voice was very light, "...'Little Bo, if you don't do it, that position goes to the troublemaker. If the troublemaker gets promoted, everyone in the team suffers. Don't think for yourself, think for the brothers.'"

"Mm." Yan Shen said, "I also said back then, you're good material, just impatient. I said I'd teach you slowly, polish you into shape."

Yi An's tears finally fell. One drop, two drops, landing on his dark brown hand back, splashing tiny water droplets.

"...You taught me for six years." Yi An said, "Six years. Every time I caused trouble, you cleaned up after me. Every time I wanted to give up, you handed me a cigarette, saying 'think slowly, no rush.' I... I owe you, I can't repay it in this lifetime."

"You don't owe me." Yan Shen said, "I helped you because you were worth helping."

"Then you want me to kill you now?" Yi An raised his head, looking at Yan Shen, tears streaming down his face, "...This is the last lesson you teach me?"

Yan Shen was silent for a while.

"...Yes." He said.

Yi An looked at him, for a long time. The stove firelight jumped on Yan Shen's face, illuminating his thin cheekbones, illuminating his gray-and-white fur, illuminating that bottomless exhaustion in his eyes.

Yi An slowly sat back in the chair. His body seemed to have the bones sucked out, softly sinking into the chair. His hand covered his face, shoulders trembling. No sound, only shoulder shaking, and occasional suppressed breathy sounds leaking from between his fingers.

Yan Shen didn't look at him. He looked at the ceiling again, waiting.

Time passed second by second. Coal blocks burned in the stove, making slight crackling sounds. Snow still fell outside the window; wind drilled in through window cracks, carrying White Capital's characteristic decaying smell. The distant skyline was whitening—not that bright, hopeful white, but that cold, icy, despairing gray-white.

Dawn was near.

Yi An finally raised his head.

His eyes were very red, but tears had dried. His expression changed—from pain to something harder, heavier. Like having made some resolution.

"...The one in the interrogation room." Yi An said. His voice was very hoarse, but every word was very clear, "...The least painful. Everyone in Beastshield knows. The semen gauge. Damage transfers over, when it's full... the person goes. No pain."

Yan Shen's eyes moved. He turned his head, looking at Yi An.

"...Alright." He said.

Yi An stood up, walked to the table, fished out two cigarettes from the drawer. Crumpled, some tobacco leaked out. He handed one to Yan Shen, put one in his own mouth.

"...Light?" Yi An asked.

Yan Shen shook his head. "...Haven't smoked in a long time. Afraid Suisui would smell it."

Yi An held the two cigarettes together, lighting them with the stove fire. He handed one to Yan Shen's lips. Yan Shen held it, took a drag, then slowly exhaled. Smoke curled in the firelight, carrying a bitter, reassuring scent.

Yi An also took a drag. The two of them sat like this by the bedside, sharing that bit of firelight, puffing cigarettes one after another. No talking. Smoke rose and dispersed between them, like some silent farewell.

The cigarette was finished. Yi An pressed the butt out in the stove ash.

"...Lie down, Senior." Yi An said.

Yan Shen lay down. His body flattened under the quilt, hands crossed over his abdomen, posture proper, like waiting for inspection. His eyes looked at Yi An, gaze calm, even carrying a trace of apology.

"...Sorry." Yan Shen said.

"...Don't say that." Yi An's voice was trembling. He sat on the edge of the bed, hand hovering above the quilt, hovering for a long time. His fingers trembled, trembling very badly, like countless needles pricking his nerves.

"...Little Bo." Yan Shen said.

"...Mm."

"...Next life." Yan Shen's eyes looked at the ceiling, voice light like a sigh, "...Next life let me still be your senior."

Yi An's tears welled up again. He bit his lip, forcing it until his lips went pale. His hand finally came down, reaching under the quilt.

Yan Shen's body stiffened slightly. Then relaxed.

Yi An's hand trembled. He pressed his palm against Yan Shen's chest, feeling that heartbeat. Fast. Chaotic. Like making a final struggle. He counted the pulse—one-twenty, one-thirty, one-forty—that heart was accelerating, madly pounding in the chest.

Yi An's hand moved. Movement very light, carrying a nearly reverent caution. His eyes stared at Yan Shen's face, watching his expression, watching his breathing.

Yan Shen's eyes half-closed. His breathing became rapid, chest heaving violently. His body under the quilt slightly tensed, muscles contracting. But he didn't struggle. He only lay there, hands still crossed over his abdomen, posture proper, calm.

Yi An's hand trembled. He stopped, took a deep breath, then continued.

He counted the pulse. One-fifty, one-sixty... then suddenly became chaotic. No rhythm anymore. Yan Shen's body suddenly went rigid, like a bow drawn to its limit, head tilting back, a very short, very light sound coming from his throat—not a shout, but a sigh of relief.

Then, body fell back onto the bed.

Yi An's hand stopped.

He felt Yan Shen's heartbeat on his chest. From fast to chaotic, to... stopped.

Gone.

Nothing at all.

Yi An's hand still stayed there. His fingers slightly curled, palm pressed against a chest gradually cooling. His ears drooped, tail tucked invisible. His body trembled, trembling violently.

He slowly, slowly, pulled the quilt up, covering to Yan Shen's chin.

Then, he bent down, forehead pressed against the edge of the bed. His shoulders trembled, but no sound. No crying out, no wailing, only a kind of极致压缩的、从胸腔深处挤出来的气音.

Outside the window, snow still fell.

Dawn was near.

## Three

When Yi An carried Yan Shen home on his back, the snow had grown lighter.

Yan Shen's body lay across his back, chin resting on his right shoulder. That military-green old uniform in the snowy night presented a deep ink-green. Yi An's hand supported Yan Shen's leg bend—there was too thin there, thin to nothing but bone.

The left leg dragged behind. With every step, that left leg swayed once, boot sole dragging a shallow groove in the snow.

Passing that streetlamp broken for five years, Yi An stopped a moment. Under the lamp, Yan Shen's cane was tilted in the snow. Yi An pulled it out, holding it in his hand, then continued walking. One end of the cane dragged in the snow, together with Yan Shen's left leg, drawing two parallel trails on the snow surface.

The way home wasn't long. But Yi An felt he'd walked very long.

He carried Yan Shen, past the corner of West District's back alley, past that always drafty iron door, past the porch light Suisui always forgot to turn off. The porch light was still on, its dim yellow glow casting a small circle of warm halo in the snowy night.

He didn't have a key. But he knew Yan Shen's habit of hiding a spare key under the doormat. He bent down, lifted the doormat, felt that cold copper key.

His hand trembled. The key missed the keyhole three times before aligning.

The door opened. The house was very quiet. The residual heat of noodle soup still lingered in the air, carrying a faint scent of sesame oil. The bedroom door was closed; from inside came Suisui's breathing—heavy, long.

Yi An gently placed Yan Shen on the living room sofa.

The sofa springs made a protesting creak. Yan Shen's body sank into the cushions, head tilted to one side, gray-and-white fur under the dim yellow light presenting a soft tone. His expression was very calm; the corner of his mouth even carried a trace of very faint arc.

Yi An squatted down, looking at Yan Shen's face. Then he stood up, found a clean old scarf from the cabinet—gray, woolen. He wrapped the scarf round and round around Yan Shen's neck, covering the strangulation marks left by the collar.

"...Going home needs to be proper." Yi An said.

He brought a basin of warm water, soaked the towel, wrung it dry, and started wiping Yan Shen's face. From the forehead down, to the eye corners, to the cheekbones, to the chin. He wiped away the dried bloodstains and dirt on Yan Shen's face bit by bit, then used his finger to smooth the white tiger whiskers one by one.

Next, he helped Yan Shen straighten his uniform. Buttons re-buttoned, collar turned out, shoulder patch straightened.

Finally, he fished out a small paper packet from his inner pocket—styptic powder, found from Yan Shen's pocket. He placed it on the coffee table. Then he took out a piece of paper and a pen from his pocket, under the dim yellow light, and started writing.

"Suisui. I am your father's junior. Your father has passed. Died of illness. He passed very—"

The pen tip stopped there. Yi An looked at the last word, "peacefully."

He thought of Yan Shen's final appearance. Body suddenly going rigid, then falling back onto the bed. That kind of death, could it be called peaceful? The semen gauge indeed didn't hurt, but that death posture—that posture of being taken to death by his own instinctive reaction—could that be called peaceful?

Yi An used the pen to cross out the last word. One stroke, two strokes, three strokes. Black ink smeared the word into a blurry blot.

He didn't write any other word. Just let that sentence stop there.

Yi An folded the note, placed it on the coffee table, under the styptic powder packet. Then he fished out that stack of communication vouchers from his贴身 pocket—Yan Shen's handwritten, crooked but every stroke very serious: "Communication voucher. No matter what, I will definitely talk properly with you."

He placed the communication vouchers under the note.

He walked to the bedroom door, hand gripping the doorknob. The coolness of metal seeped into his palm. He gently pushed open a crack.

The bedroom was very dark. Yan Sui lay on his side on the bed, tail poking out from under the quilt, trembling slightly with each breath. His brows were still slightly furrowed even in sleep, but the corner of his mouth was relaxed, carrying a childlike, unguarded softness.

He slept very deeply.

Yi An gripped the doorknob, not turning it. He stood in the darkness, looking at Yan Sui's sleeping face, for a long time.

Let him sleep a while longer.

Even if just one more hour. In this hour, he still has a father.

Yi An gently closed the door. He walked back to the living room, took one last look at Yan Shen on the sofa. Yan Shen lay there, wearing the old uniform, wrapped in the gray scarf, hands crossed over his abdomen, expression calm. The firelight jumped on the wall, casting his shadow very long.

Yi An picked up the cane from the floor, leaning it against the sofa armrest—by Yan Shen's hand, like before.

Then he turned, walking toward the door.

He didn't look back again.

The door closed gently behind him. The sound of the lock clicking was very light, almost swallowed by the wind and snow.

Yi An stood under the porch light, snowflakes falling on his head. His ears drooped, tail tucked invisible. He looked at that closed door, one last look.

Then he walked into the snow.

On the snow, besides his own footprints, there were two drag marks. One was the cane's, one was Yan Shen's left leg. Those two trails extended parallel in the snowy night, then were covered bit by bit by newly falling snowflakes.

Until nothing could be seen anymore.

## Four

When Yi An walked home, the horizon was turning gray-white.

Not dawn. That coldest, darkest gray-white before dawn. Snow still fell, but had grown lighter, feeble, like the last struggle before something is about to be exhausted.

His apartment was on another street. The hallway light was broken; he groped his way up to the third floor in darkness, key turning half a circle in the lock, door opening.

The room was very cold. The stove fire had long gone out.

Yi An stood in the doorway, not entering immediately. He just stood in the frame, looking at the darkness inside. That bed—that bed Yan Shen had just lain on—stood silently in the corner, the bedsheet still retaining a human-shaped depression, quilt piled messily to one side.

He walked in, closed the door.

Didn't light the lamp. He walked to the stove, picked up the iron tongs, scattering the embers in the stove ash bit by bit. Sparks brightened in the darkness for a moment, then dimmed again. The last bit of warmth dissipated in the air, leaving only the cold iron stove wall and a faint smell of coal ash.

Yi An sat on the edge of the bed.

The bedsheet still retained a bit of warmth. Very faint, about to dissipate soon. He pressed his palm flat on that depression, feeling that last bit of heat seeping up from beneath the fabric, seeping into his palm.

No other sound. Only his own breathing, and the gradually quieting wind outside the window.

His ears slowly—very slowly—perked up from the sides of his head. His tail loosened from between his legs, hanging by the edge of the bed, motionless.

He accepted.

---

When Yan Sui woke up, sunlight was shining in through the curtain crack.

It was a very thin beam, slanting across the bedroom wall, landing on the floor at the foot of the bed, turning the floating dust in the air into golden particles. Yan Sui's eyes squinted from that beam, then he turned over, burying his face in the pillow.

His body was very heavy. Every place that had been beaten was faintly aching. But he slept very well—surprisingly well, like someone had cast some magic, pulling him out from pain, placing him in some warm and safe place.

He opened his eyes, looking at the ceiling.

The room was very quiet. Too quiet. No sound of noodle soup boiling in the pot, no thud-thud of cane tapping cement, no slight rustle of father moving quietly in the kitchen.

Yan Sui sat up.

His movement pulled at the abdominal bruise, making him suck in a breath of pain. But he didn't care. He just sat on the bed, listening to the silence in the room. That silence had weight, pressing on his eardrums, pressing on his chest, making him unable to breathe.

"...Dad?"

He called out. Voice very light, carrying the hoarseness of just waking up.

No answer.

Yan Sui lifted the quilt, walking off the bed barefoot. The floor was cold, but he couldn't feel it. He walked to the bedroom door, pushed it open.

The living room was very bright. Sunlight poured in from the east window, whitening everything. Coffee table, chairs, sofa—

On the sofa lay a person.

Yan Sui's footsteps stopped.

He looked at that person. Gray-and-white fur, military-green old uniform, hands crossed over abdomen, expression calm. Sunlight shone on that person's face, white tiger whiskers gleaming with soft silver light in the rays, like covered with a thin layer of frost.

"...Dad?"

Yan Sui's voice was even lighter than before. Light like afraid of startling something.

He walked over. One step, two steps. He walked to the sofa side, squatting down.

Yan Shen's eyes were closed. Eyelashes cast a small patch of shadow on his pale cheek. The corner of his mouth carried a trace of very faint arc, like having a peaceful dream.

Yan Sui reached out, touching Yan Shen's face.

Cold.

Not the cold of ice, but that kind of... temperature-losing cold. Like hot water placed outside the window in winter, still emitting faint white steam, but no longer hot.

Yan Sui's finger moved to below Yan Shen's nose.

No breathing.

His hand paused. Like frozen in midair by something. His eyes opened very wide, pupils contracting into pinpoints in the morning light. His lips moved, like wanting to say something, but no sound came out.

His gaze moved to the coffee table.

There was a piece of paper there. A folded, uneven-edged paper. Under the paper was a small paper packet; below that—he recognized it—a stack of crumpled paper slips, edges fuzzy, with father's crooked handwriting.

Communication vouchers.

Yan Sui's hand trembled. He picked up that paper, unfolding it.

"Suisui. I am your father's junior. Your father has passed. Died of illness. He passed very—"

The last word was crossed out. One stroke, two strokes, three strokes. Black ink smeared that word into a blurry blot, like a small, black wound.

The paper slipped from Yan Sui's hand, floating down onto the coffee table, landing beside that packet of styptic powder.

Yan Sui didn't look at it. He only turned his head, looking at Yan Shen again. Looking at that calm face, at that old uniform, at those crossed hands.

He gripped Yan Shen's hand.

That hand was smaller than in his memory. Thin. Bones protruding, knuckles stiff. But still retained a bit of warmth—the last bit, like an oil lamp about to burn out.

Yan Sui raised that hand, pressing it against his own forehead.

His forehead pressed against the back of Yan Shen's hand. That hand's warmth seeped through skin, into his forehead, into his skull, into some place he couldn't name.

He didn't cry.

He only knelt there, forehead against the back of his father's hand, like when he was little lying on his father's back. His shoulders didn't shake; no sound came from his throat. Only his tail—that orange-yellow tiger tail—hung on the floor, motionless.

Outside the window, the snow had stopped.

Sunlight shone on them. On Yan Shen's gray-and-white fur, on Yan Sui's light golden fur, on that note with the last word crossed out, on that stack of fuzzy-edged communication vouchers at the edge of the coffee table.

The room was very quiet.

No legends, no dreams, no stars.

Only sunlight, and the warmth on the back of a hand.
