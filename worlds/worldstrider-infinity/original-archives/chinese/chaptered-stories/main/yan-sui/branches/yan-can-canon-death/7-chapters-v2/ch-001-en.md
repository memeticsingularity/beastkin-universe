# Chapter 1 Winter Night, Cane, and Bloodstains

When snow fell, even the filth in White Capital was quiet.

Yan Shen shouldn't have taken this path.

This shortcut through the back alleys of the western district was narrow, dirty, with street lamps broken for three years and no one fixing them. Normally he would take the detour along the main street, even if that road was fifteen minutes longer, even if every extra step his left leg took felt like dancing on knife points. But tonight was different. Tonight Suisui's medicine had run out, and the only pharmacy in the western district still open was deep in the back alleys. So he had to come.

Now he had to walk back.

Yan Shen carried a small bag of styptic powder and bandages just exchanged from the pharmacy, leaning on his cane, hobbling along the snow-covered road. His left leg dragged behind him, the old wound at the knee sending out a familiar, needle-like stinging pain in the cold air. His pace was very slow; every step required him to first probe the cane forward to confirm the ground wasn't icy, before he could shift his weight over.

Thud. Thud.

The cane struck the frozen ground, exceptionally clear in the empty alley. Snow particles hit his gray-white fur, melted, then evaporated by body heat, leaving a layer of fine water droplets.

The end of the alley was home. Just around this corner, and he would see that porch light Suisui always forgot to turn off.

But he stopped at the corner.

Because he heard it.

That was a muffled sound. The sound of fists hitting flesh. Not one sound, but many, intermittent, mixed with indistinct curses and some... pained whimpers.

Yan Shen's pupils contracted slightly.

He didn't rush out immediately. Years of experience made him first hide himself in the shadows of the wall corner, then slightly poke his head out to look toward the source of the sound.

The alley was very dark. The street lamp had long been broken; only the dim yellow light leaking from a distant household window barely illuminated several black shadows on the snow. Those shadows were swaying, gathering, like a pack of hyenas that had smelled blood.

Yan Shen didn't see the figure surrounded in the middle. But he smelled it.

Wind blew out from the alley, carrying a scent. The smell of blood. And—his nostrils flared—the unique, sunshine-scented fur aroma of a tiger beastkin. That was the scent he had raised for twenty-five years. From milk fragrance to youthful sweat, to the strong mature male scent now, he could recognize it with his eyes closed.

Suisui.

Yan Shen's fingers suddenly tightened, knuckles whitening from the force. The pharmacy paper bag crinkled with a harsh squeak in his grip. Without hesitation, he stuffed the bag into the wall corner, leaned on his cane, and walked out.

The cold wind cut like a knife against his face. Yan Shen pulled his worn collar up higher and strode forward.

The alley entrance arrived.

Yan Shen saw that group. Five or six civilians in worn cotton-padded clothes, forming a semicircle, with that orange-yellow figure in the middle. Yan Sui curled on the snow, arms protecting his head, body curled like a shrimp from the pain. His uniform was torn, shoulder badge fallen to one side, orange-yellow fur covered with mud and bloodstains, showing a sickly dark red under the dim light.

A man in a gray cotton-padded coat was raising his foot, preparing to kick at Yan Sui's abdomen.

Yan Shen didn't shout.

He moved.

The cane flew from his hand, thrown like a short spear. The wooden shaft whistled through the air with a dull sound, smashing hard into the back of the knee of the man raising his foot. That man let out an "Ugh—ah!" and lost his balance entirely, falling forward face-first into the snow.

"Who—!" The others suddenly turned around.

Yan Shen was already before them. His left leg dragged behind him, plowing a shallow trail in the snow. He had no cane now, but his fist was still clenched at his side, knuckles thick, palm with thick paw pads. Those amber eyes looked exceptionally cold in the dim light, like two bottomless ancient wells.

"...Step aside." His voice wasn't loud, but every word seemed squeezed from deep in his throat, carrying an unquestionable weight.

"Oh?" A fox beastkin walked out from the crowd, sizing Yan Shen up and down, the corner of his mouth pulling into a malicious smile, "Another Beastshield one? Good luck tonight."

Yan Shen didn't answer. He just looked at Yan Sui curled on the ground. His son's tail was being stepped on by someone, fluffy tiger tail being crushed in the snow, Yan Sui's body trembling because of it, a broken whimper escaping from his throat.

Yan Shen's brow furrowed slightly. Only slightly.

Then he charged forward.

First step, left leg dragging on the ground, body tilting slightly from imbalance. Second step, right fist swung out, with a veteran's precision and ruthlessness, straight into the bridge of the nose of the nearest person. The crisp sound of bone breaking, that person covering his nose and retreating, fresh blood gushing from between his fingers.

"Old bastard!" Another person lunged from the side.

Yan Shen sidestepped—because of his left leg's hindrance, his sidestep wasn't complete, his right shoulder still grazed by the other's palm. But using this momentum, his body suddenly rotated, left elbow striking backward, precisely hitting that person's chin. That person's vision went dark, staggering backward.

But Yan Shen's movement thus revealed an opening.

His left leg couldn't follow his body's center of gravity in time during the rotation, his whole person tilting to one side. A tall civilian seized the opportunity, kicking toward Yan Shen's disabled left leg knee—precisely that deformed joint.

"Ugh—!"

A broken breath escaped Yan Shen's throat. That wasn't a shout, but an instinctive reaction of the body under extreme pain. His left leg bent at an almost impossible angle under that strike, the old wound at the knee feeling like being stabbed by a red-hot knife. He knelt down on one knee, one hand supporting himself on the cold snow, his fingers immediately filling with dirty snow mud.

"Ha!" The fox beastkin let out a sharp sneer, "Former Beastshield's Yan Shen? Just this much skill?"

Yan Shen lowered his head, white tiger whiskers covered with snow particles. His breathing became heavy, every inhalation carrying the taste of blood in his lungs—not his, but Suisui's. He could smell it, that taste permeating the air, so strong it made him want to kill.

His hand on the ground slowly clenched into a fist.

Then, he picked up the cane that had fallen in the snow.

Not to lean on it to stand up. But gripping its middle, like holding a short stick, or a knife.

Yan Shen stood up. Not a normal stand, but using the most energy-saving posture a veteran learned on the battlefield—using upper body strength to prop himself up, left leg merely as a support point, not bearing too much weight. His posture was very strange, like a wounded beast, but there was no fear in this beast's eyes.

"I'll say it once more." His voice was hoarse, but every word was bitten dead, "Step aside."

The fox beastkin's smile stiffened slightly.

Because he saw Yan Shen's gaze. There was no room for negotiation in those amber eyes, no pleading, not even any anger—only a pure, all-out killing intent he had never seen before. That wasn't the gaze a disabled old man should have. That was the gaze a veteran from Beastshield's golden age would show when protecting his most precious thing.

"...Go." The fox beastkin suddenly said.

"Huh?" The person beside him didn't react.

"I said go!" The fox beastkin suddenly turned around, his voice carrying an imperceptible irritation, "This old bastard's gone mad, not worth fighting him."

The others looked at each other. They looked at Yan Shen with his slightly bent knee, leaning on his cane like a knife, at those cold-light eyes in the snowy night, then at Yan Sui already half-unconscious on the ground. Finally, that accumulated anger gradually crumbled before Yan Shen's killing intent. They turned and left one after another, footsteps gradually disappearing in the alley.

Yan Shen didn't chase. He couldn't chase. His left leg was already numb with pain, his whole person swaying.

He threw down the cane, knelt in the snow, beside Yan Sui.

"...Suisui." His voice was so light it was almost swallowed by the wind and snow.

Yan Sui's eyes were half-open, his right eye swollen to only a slit. His lips were broken, blood flowing down his chin, dripping onto the snow, melting out a small patch of dark red mark. He looked at Yan Shen, his gaze unfocused, like he couldn't see clearly.

"...Dad...?" His voice was broken, carrying a grievance only a young person would have, "...They... they said I... was Beastshield's..."

"Don't talk." Yan Shen reached out, his movement very light, but his hand trembling slightly. He touched Yan Sui's forehead, burning hot. Then his hand slid down Yan Sui's shoulder, checking ribs—fortunately, no obvious breaks. But that bruise beneath the fur on his abdomen made his fingers pause.

"...Can you stand up?" he asked.

Yan Sui tried to move, but just as he propped up his upper body, he sucked in a breath of pain, his whole person falling back.

Yan Shen didn't say anything more. He bent down, draped Yan Sui's arm over his shoulder, then used all his strength to lift his son from the snow. Yan Sui was taller than him, sturdier than him; when his full weight pressed over, Yan Shen's left leg suddenly went soft, nearly making both of them fall together.

But he held on.

"...Hold tight." he said.

Yan Sui mumbled a "Mm" in a daze, resting his head on his father's shoulder. His breathing was heavy, carrying the smell of blood, spraying on Yan Shen's neck nest, warm and damp.

Yan Shen held his son's waist with one hand, leaned on his cane with the other, moving step by step toward home.

Snow was still falling. Their footprints extended crookedly on the snow, beside one of the footprint trails, there were also small pits poked out by the cane, and intermittent, dark red bloodstains.

The road home wasn't long. But for Yan Shen, it might have been the longest stretch of road he had ever walked in his life.

---

The room was warm. Yan Shen stopped at the door for a moment, adjusting his breathing, then helped Yan Sui into the bedroom.

That bed wasn't big, but covered with clean bedding. Yan Shen gently placed Yan Sui on the bed, his movements careful like placing a fragile piece of porcelain. Yan Sui's brow furrowed slightly, but didn't wake. He just turned over, burying his face in the pillow, a vague murmur escaping from his throat.

Yan Shen stood by the bedside, looking at his son.

Yan Sui's profile looked exceptionally young under the dim yellow light. His fur was a healthy orange-yellow, but now clumped into locks by blood and mud. The skin around his right eye was already swollen, showing an ugly purplish-red. The wound on his lip was still oozing blood, white tiger teeth stained with blood threads.

Yan Shen turned around, taking out a medical kit from the cabinet. That was something he had kept from his Beastshield days many years ago; the medicines inside had long expired, but there were still some bandages, iodine, and styptic powder.

He brought a basin of warm water, wet the towel, wrung it dry, then began wiping the bloodstains from Yan Sui's face.

Starting from the forehead, downward, to the eye corners, to the cheekbones, to the chin. The movements were very light, but Yan Sui still sucked in air from the pain, his body involuntarily shrinking.

"...Hurts?" Yan Shen stopped.

"...It's fine..." Yan Sui's voice was muffled in the pillow, "...Dad... sorry... caused you trouble again..."

Yan Shen's hand paused.

He didn't say "it's okay," nor did he say "it's not your fault." He just rewet the towel in warm water, wrung it dry, and continued wiping.

"...Eat your noodles." he said.

Yan Sui was stunned for a moment, then the corner of his mouth pulled a very faint smile, tugging the wound at the corner of his mouth, hissing in pain again.

Yan Shen had already turned and walked into the kitchen.

There was still leftover noodle soup in the pot from the evening. He added some water, reheated it, then found an egg from the cabinet, cracking it into the pot. When the noodle soup boiled, steam filled the air, carrying a simple, warm aroma.

When Yan Shen returned to the bedroom with the bowl, Yan Sui was already sitting up, leaning against the headboard, one hand covering his abdomen.

"...Here." Yan Shen handed the bowl over.

That was a very ordinary bowl of noodle soup. The noodles were thick noodles, with a few green vegetables and a poached egg in the soup, floating with a few drops of sesame oil. Nothing special, but on this winter night, that steaming aroma gave a nearly painful comfort.

Yan Sui took the bowl, scooped a spoonful of soup with the spoon, blew on it, and sent it into his mouth.

"...Delicious." he said softly.

Yan Shen "Mm"ed, sitting on the edge of the bed, beginning to check other wounds on Yan Sui's body. He used his fingers to lightly press Yan Sui's ribs, pausing at each spot, observing Yan Sui's reaction.

"...Here?"

"...A bit painful..."

"...Here?"

"...It's fine..."

After confirming no fractures, Yan Shen used iodine to clean the scrapes on Yan Sui's arms and shoulders, then applied styptic powder, wrapping them with bandages. His fingers were thick, but his movements were very steady, like handling something perfectly ordinary.

Yan Sui ate noodles while secretly looking at his father.

Under the lamplight, Yan Shen's profile looked exceptionally aged. Gray-white fur showed an exhausted color under the light, white tiger whiskers extending from both sides of his mouth, long and distinct. His brow was slightly furrowed, his gaze focused on the wounds, but his eye sockets were deeply sunken, the exhaustion inside thick and indissoluble.

"...Dad." Yan Sui suddenly said.

"Mm."

"...Will they... come again?"

Yan Shen's hand paused.

He didn't answer immediately. He just cut the last section of bandage, tied a knot, then packed up the medical kit and stood up.

"...Sleep." he said, voice very light but unquestionable, "Tomorrow... I'll go find someone."

Yan Sui wanted to ask more, but bodily exhaustion and pain prevented him from organizing language. He just nodded, placed the bowl on the nightstand, then slowly lay down.

Yan Shen helped him pull the quilt to his chin, tucking in the corners. His fingers paused when touching that bruise on Yan Sui's cheek. His fingertips were very rough, but the movement was very light, like touching something precious.

"...Dad?" Yan Sui's eyes were already half-closed.

"...Mm."

"...Thank you..."

Yan Shen didn't answer. He just stood there, watching his son's breathing gradually become long and steady.

Outside the window, snow was still falling. Wind drilled in through the window crack, carrying White Capital's unique decaying breath—coal smoke, mud, distant garbage. But the room was warm. The residual heat of the noodle soup still permeated the air, the tiger beastkin on the bed was already deeply asleep, his brow still slightly furrowed even in sleep, but the corner of his mouth had an imperceptible relaxation.

Yan Shen stood by the bedside for a very long time.

He looked at his son's sleeping face, at that tuft of forehead fur—that tuft identical to Yan Shen's own youth—trembling slightly with breath under the light. He remembered many years ago, when Suisui was still small, also curling asleep in his embrace like this. Back then his leg wasn't bad yet, he could still carry his son on his back to see the snow in the back mountains. Suisui would lie on his back, small hands circling his neck, fluffy tiger tail wrapped around his waist, warm and cozy.

How good it was back then.

Yan Shen's hand slowly gripped the cane. That polished-shiny wood transmitted a familiar sensation in his palm.

He knew that group wouldn't let it go. White Capital's hatred was like coal smoke, pervasive, never dissipating. He could scare them off today because his ruthlessness caught them off guard. But next time? Next time they were prepared, what could a disabled old man like him do to protect Suisui?

He couldn't always stay by Suisui's side. Suisui had his own path to walk. And he...

Yan Shen lowered his head, his forehead gently pressing against the cane's grip. That was a praying posture, or a posture of an exhausted old man seeking support.

Tomorrow. Tomorrow he would go, find Yi An. Yi An was a junior from when he was still at Beastshield, a Bernese Mountain Dog, now in blue uniform, living just two streets away. Yan Shen had personally promoted this hot-blooded young man back then, watching him grow from an impulsive kid into a Beastshield soldier who could stand on his own. They hadn't met much in recent years, but Yi An would always visit during holidays, bringing some food, sitting a while, listening to Yan Shen talk about the past.

Yan Shen usually didn't want to disturb this young man. Firstly, he didn't want people to think he was leaning on his seniority, secondly... he wasn't used to asking juniors for help. But tonight was different. That group wouldn't let it go, Suisui was still young, still had a future. And this old man—this old man who could barely walk—could at least do one last thing for his son.

Even if it meant going to beg a junior he had personally promoted.

Yan Shen raised his head, taking one last look at his son's sleeping face.

Then he leaned on his cane, hobbling out of the bedroom, gently closing the door.

The living room was very dark. Yan Shen didn't light the lamp. He just sat in that old chair, in the darkness, listening to the breathing sounds coming from the bedroom.

Thud.

The cane leaned against the table edge, making a light sound.

The winter night was long. But at this moment, at least his son was still alive, still breathing, still beside him.

For Yan Shen, that was enough.

(End of Chapter One)
