# Chapter 3 A Cane on the Snow

Lao Zhao's apartment was just ahead.

Yan Shen raised his head, looking at that old building blackened by coal smoke. In a third-floor window, a lamp was lit; dim yellow light passed through dirty glass, casting a small, blurry spot of light on the snow. Yan Shen recognized that lamp—Lao Zhao always covered the lampshade with newspaper, saying it "gathered light" and saved electricity.

He slightly relaxed.

His left leg was already numb with pain; the joint at his knee was like a rag that had been kneaded and then frozen solid, emitting a harsh grinding sound with every bend. He leaned against the wall, resting slightly, adjusting his breath. White mist sprayed from his mouth and nose, rapidly dissipating in the cold air.

Another fifty meters forward, turn a corner, and it would be the apartment entrance.

Yan Shen straightened up, leaned on his cane, and took a step.

Clack.

The cane tapping the ground echoed in the quiet alley. That sound was very light, but in this empty morning, it was like a stone falling into a deep well, stirring countless invisible ripples.

Yan Shen took three more steps.

Then stopped.

In the shadow of the alley entrance, several people were standing.

They were wearing worn cotton-padded clothes, faces mostly covered by collars and scarves, only revealing a few pairs of eyes. Those eyes gleamed with a turbid, sickly light in the dim light, like starving beasts sizing up prey.

Yan Shen's gaze swept over them one by one.

He recognized one of them—that fox beastkin. Last night in the alley, it was this person who led the group in beating Yan Sui. Yan Shen's cane had left a bruise on his shoulder; now he was still slightly hunching that shoulder.

The fox beastkin also recognized Yan Shen.

The corner of his mouth slowly, slowly pulled upward, revealing an expression that couldn't be called a smile. That was more like a greed of discovering unexpected wealth, mixed with some long-suppressed, finally released satisfaction.

"...Former Beastshield's Yan Shen?" the fox beastkin said, voice carrying a deliberately surprised tone, "Oh my, what a coincidence. We were just wondering where to find you."

Yan Shen didn't answer.

His fingers tightened on the cane grip. Knuckles whitening from the force, the rough paw pads in his palm frictioning against the wood texture. His left leg slightly withdrew half a step backward—that was a veteran's instinctive posture when assessing the situation—not retreating, but looking for a support point.

The fox beastkin walked out from the shadow. Behind him, four others also came up. Five figures formed a human wall in the narrow alley, blocking Yan Shen's path completely.

"Last night let you have your moment of glory." The fox beastkin rubbed his shoulder; the bruise was faintly visible beneath the worn cotton coat, "Old thing, quite some strength."

Yan Shen still didn't speak.

He was just looking behind the fox beastkin—that old apartment building was not far away; the lamp on the third floor was still lit. Lao Zhao might be cooking breakfast inside, might be listening to the radio. Just one more shout, just one more shout and Lao Zhao would hear...

But Yan Shen didn't shout.

He knew Lao Zhao's hearing was bad. He knew the sound insulation effect of this alley. He knew, even if he shouted himself hoarse, Lao Zhao might not necessarily hear. And—his gaze swept over the five people—even if Lao Zhao heard, even if Lao Zhao came down, one retired veteran plus one disabled veteran, could they beat five young, strong civilians?

No.

So Yan Shen didn't shout. He just switched the cane from his right hand to his left, moving his right shoulder. That was a tiny movement, but the fox beastkin noticed.

"Still want to fight?" The fox beastkin laughed, but that laughter had no warmth, "Old thing, last night you had the advantage of a sneak attack. Today—"

He took a step forward.

"—you're not so lucky."

Yan Shen moved.

He didn't wait for the fox beastkin to finish speaking. Years of experience told him that when at an absolute disadvantage in numbers, the only chance was the first move. His cane swung out from his left hand, carrying a fierce whistling wind, straight at the fox beastkin's temple.

The fox beastkin obviously didn't expect Yan Shen to be so decisive. He hastily tilted his head; the cane grazed past his ear, bringing a burning pain. He "hissed," his expression changing from playful to ferocious.

"Get him!"

Five people swarmed forward.

Yan Shen didn't retreat. He knew he couldn't beat so many people, but he couldn't fall—Suisui was still waiting for him at home. His cane parried the first person's fist, then he sidestepped to dodge, but that disabled left leg made his movement a beat slower.

A fist smashed into his abdomen.

Yan Shen grunted, his body bending from the pain, white tiger whiskers trembling. But he didn't fall; he gritted his teeth, used his cane to support his body, then swung his fist to counterattack. His fist landed on one person's face; that person staggered backward, nosebleed flowing.

This was an unequal battle.

Yan Shen was injured, his leg disabled, but he was still a veteran. His cane blocked one person's kick, his shoulder knocked aside another person's pounce, his elbow hit a third person's ribs. But with every movement, that disabled left leg sent heart-piercing pain; the old wound at his knee felt like it was on fire.

"Damn old thing!" someone cursed, picking up a brick from the ground and fiercely smashing it toward Yan Shen's head.

Yan Shen barely tilted his head to avoid it, but the brick still grazed his temple, leaving a deep wound. Fresh blood immediately gushed out, flowing down his cheek, soaking the white tiger whiskers. Warm blood flowed into his eyes, turning his vision into a sheet of blood red.

His vision began to blur, but he still stood.

He didn't shout, didn't threaten. Just silently, with those amber eyes staring at the people before him, like an old beast driven to a dead end.

More people surrounded him. Fists, feet, clubs fell like raindrops. Yan Shen protected his head, using his arms and body to endure the attacks. His fur was torn, skin cut, warm blood seeping out, staining that old overcoat red.

What hurt most was the left leg. Every time that disabled leg was kicked, that old wound sent heart-piercing pain, like countless needles pricking. Yan Shen's forehead broke out in cold sweat, thick brows tightly furrowed together, but he didn't cry out.

A fist fiercely smashed into his abdomen, the force so heavy his body suddenly arched, a broken whimper overflowing from his throat.

"Ugh—!"

That wasn't just from pain.

Yan Shen felt a strange, terrifying heat explode with that blow below his lower abdomen. His cheeks uncontrollably flushed with a sickly crimson; that wasn't a normal reaction to being beaten—his body was betraying him.

"Wait, look..." one of the civilians suddenly stopped, a flash of surprise in his eyes, immediately turning into malicious understanding, "this old thing..."

Yan Shen desperately wanted to curl up, wanted to conceal that willful change in his crotch. But that originally flat area, under continuous beating and pain stimulation, was uncontrollably, slowly and stubbornly rising, forming a clearly visible outline under the worn pants.

"Oh?" that civilian let out a sharp, vicious sneer, "old thing, your body is quite 'sensitive,' huh? Getting beaten can get you 'excited'? You former Beastshield people, do you all have this slutty streak in your bones?"

"Pah! A dog can't stop eating shit!" another civilian spat, a cruel and excited light flashing in his eyes, raising his foot to deliberately kick the area between Yan Shen's buttocks and hip bone, "stop pretending to be proper!"

"Ugh—! No... don't..." Yan Shen's body bounced violently, then fell heavily back onto the filthy ground. "Tsk... truly disgusting." A young civilian turned his face away, voice carrying a barely perceptible tremor, "e-enough, right? He's already like this..."

"What are you going soft for?" the leading fox beastkin barked, but the raised fist hesitated to fall. He looked at the beastkin curled on the ground, covered in blood yet still silently trembling, looked at that face blurred by blood and some indescribable shame, and suddenly felt a wave of inexplicable irritation.

This was no longer revenge.

The fox beastkin lowered his fist. He stared at Yan Shen for a long time, his gaze changing from anger to something deeper—perhaps weariness, perhaps something else.

"...Take him back." he suddenly said.

"What?" The person beside him didn't react.

"I said, take him back." The fox beastkin's voice lowered, carrying a decisiveness, "Just beating him to death like this is too cheap."

He squatted down, reaching out to pinch Yan Shen's chin, forcing him to raise his head. Yan Shen's eyes were half-open, pupils dilated, but there was still something burning inside—not anger, not fear, but something that made the fox beastkin feel uncomfortable... dignity.

"Former Beastshield's Yan Shen." The fox beastkin said softly, voice carrying a nearly intimate cruelty, "Weren't you quite impressive? Didn't you still want to protect your precious son?"

His fingers slid to Yan Shen's Adam's apple, gently pressing once.

"...I'll let you know that in White Capital, former Beastshield people are worse than dogs."

A broken breath escaped Yan Shen's throat. He wanted to speak, wanted to curse back, but the extreme pain in his body and that strange, terrifying warm current prevented him from organizing his words. He just stared at the fox beastkin's eyes, with those amber eyes, stared at him.

"Take him away." The fox beastkin stood up, waving his hand.

Two people came up, one left one right holding Yan Shen's arms, dragging him up from the snow like dragging a bag of garbage. Yan Shen's left leg completely lacked strength, dragging on the ground; the wound at his knee scraped out a dark red trail on the snow.

His consciousness swayed between clarity and blur. He could feel the coldness of the snow, could feel the pain in his body, could feel the sticky wetness in his crotch bringing immense shame. But he could also feel—those people didn't kill him.

Where were they taking him?

He didn't know. He only knew that his cane fell in the snow, emitting a dull sound. That sound echoed once in the quiet alley, then disappeared.

Just like him.

Yan Shen was dragged into the depths of the alley. The fox beastkin walked in front, taking out a key from his pocket, opening a rusted iron door. Behind the door was a downward staircase, pitch black, emitting a moldy smell and some indescribable fishy scent.

"...Go down." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen was pushed, shoved, stumbling down the stairs. His left leg felt like stepping on knife points with every step; the old wound at his knee sent a heart-piercing stabbing pain in the cold air. His hands were twisted behind his back, tied with a rough hemp rope; the knot bit into the skin of his wrists, bringing a dull pain.

At the end of the stairs was a wooden door. The fox beastkin pushed it open; a damp, rotten smell hit their faces.

That was a basement. Not large, but very deep. The walls were bare cement, covered with water stains and mold spots. In the corner were piled some old wooden crates and sacks, covered with rat droppings. An electric wire hung from the ceiling, ending in a dim yellow light bulb, swaying slightly in the air, illuminating everything in the room in flickering light and shadow.

In the center of the basement was a rusty iron pipe, running from floor to ceiling. Tied to the iron pipe was an iron chain; at the end of the chain was a leather collar.

Yan Shen saw that collar.

His pupils suddenly contracted.

The fox beastkin noticed his reaction. He laughed; that was a truly happy laugh, like a child discovering a new toy.

"...Looks like you understand." he said, "Good."

He walked forward, reaching out to undo the buttons of Yan Shen's overcoat. That blood-soaked old overcoat was taken off, thrown on the ground. Then the sweater inside, the shirt. Yan Shen didn't resist—he also had no strength to resist. He just stood there, his naked upper body covered with bruises and wounds, white tiger whiskers trembling under the dim yellow light.

"...Kneel." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen didn't move.

The fox beastkin's smile stiffened slightly. Then he punched Yan Shen's abdomen—exactly that already fragile area.

"Ugh—!" Yan Shen's body suddenly arched, his whole body kneeling down. His knees hit the cold cement floor, emitting a dull sound.

The fox beastkin walked forward, putting that leather collar around Yan Shen's neck. The collar was very tight, biting into the fur, making Yan Shen's breathing difficult. Then, he fastened the iron chain onto the collar, emitting a crisp "click" sound.

"...Welcome to your new home." the fox beastkin said softly.

He turned around, taking the others out of the basement. The wooden door slowly closed before Yan Shen, emitting a heavy muffled sound.

Then, was the sound of the lock tongue clicking.

Click.

Yan Shen knelt on the cold cement floor, naked, bleeding, chained. His forehead pressed against the ground, nose tip almost touching his own blood. His breathing was very shallow; every inhalation made the collar bite tighter, bringing a suffocating dizziness.

Darkness.

Complete darkness.

Only that dim yellow light bulb swayed slightly overhead, casting Yan Shen's shadow on the cement wall, stretching it very, very long, like a tiger that had been skinned.

His consciousness floated. The pain in his body, the stickiness in his crotch, the strangulation marks on his neck—all became blurry in this darkness. The only clear thing was the image in his mind—

Suisui.

Suisui was still at home. Was the door locked? Was the porridge still warm? Dad said he would come back. Dad said...

A tear slid from the corner of Yan Shen's eye. That wasn't a tear of pain, but something deeper—something he couldn't express in words.

He slowly, slowly, within the range the iron chain allowed, curled up his body. Like a wounded old beast, in the darkness, licking his wounds alone.
