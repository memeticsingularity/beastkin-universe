He merely furrowed his brows slightly, a vague murmur escaping his throat, then continued sleeping deeply.

Qi dian sat up from the bed, and by the faint light from outside the window, took one last look at Yan Sui's sleeping face. That tiger beastkin's face looked especially peaceful in sleep, brows no longer furrowed, the corner of his mouth even carrying a faint smile—that was the expression of finally being able to sleep soundly after a whole day of fear, pain, warmth, and pouring out his heart.

"Omnimedia Parsing Engine, activate," Qi dian said softly.

"Two years ago... that night, after he settled me down, he went out again. He said he was going to find an old friend to discuss something... I fell asleep, thinking he would come back. But... he never came back."

The engine began to operate, parsing the spacetime information behind this description. Data streams on the screen flashed rapidly, as if countless timelines were intertwining, colliding. Then, the image gradually clarified—

That was Yan Shen.

Fifty-three-year-old tiger beastkin. His once burly, upright body had been worn by years and hardship into a steady, heavy texture—still solid, with broad shoulders and a thick chest, just lacking the taut, sharp momentum of his youth. When Qi dian opened his eyes, he was standing on a street in White Capital.

The air was cold, carrying a bone-piercing chill. Snowflakes fell from the gray-black sky, but after landing they quickly mixed with the sewage and cinders on the ground into dirty gray mud. Buildings on both sides of the street were tall and dilapidated; dim light leaked from the windows. In the distance came a few dog barks, and faint voices of people.

This was White Capital two years ago, a cold and cruel winter night.

Qi dian looked down the street and saw that figure.

Yan Shen was hobbling along the street, his back slightly hunched and lonely. That dark old overcoat draped over his solid body, looking somewhat loose. His cane tapped on the frozen ground, making "clack, clack" sounds, especially clear in the quiet night.

Qi dian followed him, maintaining a distance.

Yan Shen walked very slowly; every step with his left leg had to be dragged with effort. His breath turned into puffs of white mist in the cold air, every pant carrying a heavy exhaustion. But his gaze was very firm, looking ahead, looking toward the direction he was going.

Qi dian watched his back, a complex emotion welling up in his heart. This was Yan Sui's father, the father who in Yan Sui's words "made the best braised pork" and "would make dumplings with him." And now, he was merely a lonely beastkin, struggling forward on a cold winter night, heading toward unknown danger.

Yan Shen suddenly stopped.

He stood under a dim street lamp, raised his head, and gazed into the distance. Those amber eyes looked especially tired under the light, but still maintained a sharp gleam. He sighed; that sigh turned into a puff of white mist in the cold air, then he continued forward.

Qi dian continued following him.

They crossed several streets, gradually heading toward the western district of White Capital. The environment here grew worse and worse, buildings more and more dilapidated, snow on the streets dirtier and dirtier. The air was permeated with a decaying scent, mixed with the stench of garbage and some indescribable sense of oppression.

There were few pedestrians on the road; occasionally a few civilians in worn cotton-padded clothes hurried past, and when they saw Yan Shen's figure, they would subconsciously avoid him, eyes carrying a mixture of fear and disgust.

Yan Shen seemed accustomed to such gazes; he didn't stop, only continued walking forward.

Finally, he arrived at the entrance of a back alley.

That was a narrow alley, with tall walls on both sides, only a sliver of sky overhead. The alley was very dark; only a faint light came from the distance. Yan Shen stood at the alley entrance, hesitated a moment, then leaning on his cane, hobbled in.

Qi dian followed behind, maintaining his distance.

The alley was very quiet; only the sound of Yan Shen's cane echoed between the walls. He walked very slowly, every step very cautious, as if guarding against something.

Then, several figures appeared ahead.

They were five or six civilians, wearing worn cotton-padded clothes, faces carrying a mixture of exhaustion and anger. They blocked Yan Shen's path, filling the narrow alley completely.

Yan Shen stopped. He raised his head, looking at that group, no fear in his eyes, only a calm exhaustion, and... an imperceptible vigilance.

"Yan Shen?" one of them spoke, voice carrying a teeth-gritting hatred, "former Beastshield's Yan Shen?"

Yan Shen's pupils contracted slightly. He recognized this voice—this was one of that group who had attacked his son that night.

"It's you." Yan Shen's voice sank, no longer calm indifference but carrying a suppressed anger, "That night... the ones who attacked my son... it was you."

"You're not so lucky."

"Wrong person?" another person mocked, "you're Yan Sui's dad, that's enough. Father pays son's debt, son pays father's debt, perfectly justified."

"That night you ruined our good deed," a third person chimed in, eyes flashing with venomous light, "today is perfect, old and new scores settled together."

Yan Shen didn't answer. He just stood there quietly, his slightly hunched body looking especially steady under the dim light. But his hand—that hand gripping the cane—tightened slightly, knuckles whitening from the force.

"Forget it, don't waste words with him," the first person said, "teach him a lesson, let him know former Beastshield people are nothing in White Capital."

He stepped forward, his fist smashing toward Yan Shen's face.

He sidestepped to avoid that punch, his movement carrying the clumsiness characteristic of a lame leg, but still showing traces of past training. Then he swung his cane, fiercely smashing it onto that person's shoulder.

"Ugh!" that person let out a pained cry, staggering backward.

"Get him!" the others swarmed forward.

Yan Shen did not retreat. He knew he couldn't beat so many people, but he couldn't fall—there was someone at home waiting for him. He used his cane to parry the first person's attack, then sidestepped to dodge, but that disabled left leg made his movement a beat slower.

A fist smashed into his abdomen.

Yan Shen grunted, his body bending from the pain, white tiger whiskers trembling. But he didn't fall; he gritted his teeth, used his cane to support his body, then swung his fist to counterattack.

This was an unequal battle.

Yan Shen was injured, his leg disabled, but he was still a warrior. His fist landed on one person's face, his cane blocked another person's kick, he used his shoulder to knock aside a third person. But with every movement, that disabled left leg sent heart-piercing pain, the old wound at the knee like it was on fire.

"Damn old thing!" someone cursed, picking up a rock from the ground and fiercely smashing it toward Yan Shen's head.

Yan Shen barely turned his head to avoid it, but the rock still grazed his temple, leaving a deep wound. Fresh blood immediately gushed out, flowing down his cheek, soaking his whiskers.

His vision began to blur, but he still stood.

He didn't shout, didn't threaten. Just silently, with those amber eyes staring at the people before him, like an old beast driven to a dead end.
