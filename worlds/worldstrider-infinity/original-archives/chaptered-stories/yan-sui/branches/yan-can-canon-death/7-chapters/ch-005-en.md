# Chapter 5 Light Through the Door Crack

When the second week began, Yan Shen no longer quite remembered who he was.

That "not remembering" wasn't amnesia. It was something more subtle—like a person standing before a mirror; the mirror clearly reflected his face, but that face looked like it belonged to a stranger. Yan Shen knew he was Yan Shen, knew he was Yan Sui's father, knew he was a former Beastshield employee. But these "knowings" were like labels pasted in his mind, not things grown from within his body.

His body had learned another set of rules.

On the eighth day, when the fox beastkin walked into the basement, Yan Shen was already awake before the footsteps reached the stairway. His ears—those tiger ears beneath the gray-white fur—turned slightly in the darkness, capturing every vibration in the air. When the sound of the door lock being opened rang out, his heart began to race, blood rushing to his limbs, his stomach tightening into a ball in that instant.

That wasn't fear. At least not entirely.

That was something more complex—a mixture of fear, anticipation, and something he himself was unwilling to admit. Because what the fox beastkin brought wasn't just beating. Sometimes, when he "behaved well," the fox beastkin would give him a slightly cleaner piece of food, or let him drink an extra mouthful of water. Those tiny,施舍-like kindnesses, in this hellish basement, had actually become Yan Shen's only anticipation.

This realization made him feel nauseous.

But his body didn't care.

"...Come here." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen crawled over.

Not walked. Crawled. Because his knees already hurt too much to stand up. His limbs on the ground, iron chain dragging beneath him, emitting harsh friction sounds. His head lowered, vision only able to see the fox beastkin's feet—those boots covered with snow mud and filth.

The fox beastkin threw a piece of something on the ground. That was a hard piece of bread, already moldy, but in Yan Shen's eyes, it was like some precious reward. He lowered his head, using his tongue to roll the bread into his mouth, chewing, swallowing. His stomach let out a satisfied whimper in that instant; that sound was especially clear in the silence, as if mocking his dignity.

"...Good boy." the fox beastkin said.

He reached out, touching Yan Shen's head. Yan Shen's tiger ears trembled slightly beneath that palm, but didn't dodge.

He no longer dodged.

---

Tenth day.

Yan Shen began to dream.

Those weren't normal dreams. Those dreams had no plot, no images, only a vague, warm sensation. He felt himself being wrapped by something, very soft, very warm, carrying a scent of sunshine. His body relaxed in that warmth; all pain disappeared, all fear drifted away.

Then he would wake up.

After waking, he found himself curled on the cement floor, iron chain勒着 neck, body covered with only a thin layer of dirty dust. Cold seeped into his marrow from the ground, making his teeth chatter uncontrollably. He would spend a long time distinguishing—which was the dream, which was reality.

Sometimes, he wished he would never wake up.

On the eleventh day, the fox beastkin brought a new person.

That was a young civilian, looking less than twenty years old, face still carrying a naivety that hadn't experienced too much suffering. He walked into the basement; seeing Yan Shen's appearance, he subconsciously took a step back.

"...This is that former Beastshield one?" The young person's voice trembled somewhat.

"Mm." the fox beastkin said, "Didn't you say you wanted to learn? Today I'll show you how to tame an old dog."

The young person swallowed a mouthful of saliva. His gaze wandered over Yan Shen's body, from the gray-white fur, to the bony ribs, to the collar and iron chain on his neck. In his eyes were fear, disgust, and something Yan Shen was very familiar with—curiosity.

"...Make him lie down." the fox beastkin said to the young person.

The young person hesitated, then learning from the fox beastkin, used a deliberately authoritative voice: "...Lie down."

Yan Shen didn't move.

Not because he was resisting. But because his brain short-circuited in that instant—he heard the command, but that voice wasn't the fox beastkin's, so he didn't know how to react. His body was hesitating, waiting, like a dog trained too well, only recognizing one master's voice.

The fox beastkin laughed.

He walked up, kicking Yan Shen's side waist. That kick wasn't heavy, but it hit exactly an old wound. Yan Shen's body suddenly curled up, a broken whimper overflowing from his throat.

"...I told you to lie down." the fox beastkin said, voice still calm.

Yan Shen lay down.

His chest pressed against the cold cement floor, forehead against the ground, iron chain coiled into a ball beneath him. His tail—that orange-yellow mixed with gray-white tiger tail—hung behind him, tail tip trembling slightly.

The young person watched this scene, his gaze changing from fear to something more complex. He took a step forward, reaching out, carefully touching Yan Shen's tail.

Yan Shen's body stiffened.

Then, his tail curled up in that instant, like a frightened child hugging his own knees.

The young person abruptly withdrew his hand.

"...He, he..." the young person's voice was trembling.

"Fun, right?" the fox beastkin said, his voice carrying an educator's patience, "This is former Beastshield's Yan Shen. Once so impressive, and now?"

He squatted down, using his hand to pinch Yan Shen's chin, forcing him to raise his head. Yan Shen's eyes were half-open, pupils dilated, but there was still something inside—something that made the fox beastkin feel uncomfortable. Even at this time, even when Yan Shen had already become like this, there was still something in those amber eyes.

"...What are you looking at?" the fox beastkin asked.

Yan Shen didn't answer. He couldn't answer. His throat was so hoarse from dryness and overuse that he couldn't make a sound. He just looked at the fox beastkin, looked at those eyes so close, then—

His gaze passed over the fox beastkin's shoulder, looking toward a certain corner of the basement.

There was nothing there. Only a patch of shadow.

But Yan Shen saw something in that shadow. Perhaps hallucination, perhaps not. He saw Suisui. Not the childhood Suisui, but the present Suisui—25-year-old young tiger, orange-yellow fur, robust body, amber eyes identical to Yan Shen's. Suisui stood in the shadow, looking at him, eyes carrying shock, pain, and something Yan Shen couldn't bear... disappointment.

"...Suisui..." Yan Shen's lips moved, but no sound came out.

The fox beastkin didn't notice. He just loosened his hand, stood up, patted the young person's shoulder.

"...Let's go." he said, "That's enough for today."

The door closed before Yan Shen. The light went out.

Darkness descended again.

But this time, Yan Shen didn't immediately curl up. He just lay there, forehead pressed against the cold cement floor, tears silently flowing down.

Because he finally understood.

He wasn't crying for his present self. He was crying for that person still remaining outside, called "Yan Sui's father." That person was already dead. In the first week in the basement, at the moment he said "Wait until Dad comes back to talk," that person was already dead. What lived here now was merely a still-breathing shell, a shell that had learned obedience, learned to please, learned to lick food from the ground.

He couldn't go back.

Even if he walked out this door now, even if he returned to that home, he could no longer face Suisui. Because Suisui would see what he looked like now. Suisui would see the collar marks on his neck, see the wounds on his body, see his knees that couldn't help but bend when hearing "sit down."

Suisui would know.

Suisui would know what his father, that once invincible father in his eyes, had become.

Yan Shen buried his face in his arm. His body was trembling, like a withered leaf in the cold wind. But he didn't make any sound. He just silently wept, tears soaking the gray-white fur, dripping on the cement floor, mixing with dust.

---

Late night of the fourteenth day.

There was only Yan Shen alone in the basement. The light was out; darkness flooded in like tidewater, drowning his senses. He curled up within the range the iron chain allowed, back against the cold cement wall, knees hugged to his chest.

His consciousness floated. In the half-dream, half-awake state, he heard a sound.

That wasn't the fox beastkin's footsteps. Nor was it civilians talking. That was a heavier, slower sound—like some huge thing moving, or like...

The door was knocked open.

Not normally opened. It was knocked open from outside by some huge force. The wooden door shattered in that instant, wood splinters flying everywhere. Light poured down from the stairway, stinging Yan Shen's eyes—he hadn't seen such bright light in a very long time.

Yan Shen curled up even tighter in the darkness. His body reacted in that instant—not fleeing, but curling up even more. His head lowered, forehead against the ground, hands protecting his head, like a young beast seeking shelter in a storm.

That wasn't bravery. That was a conditioned, instinctive posture of submission trained into him.

"...Yan Shen?"

A voice rang out in the darkness. That was a strange, aged voice, carrying something Yan Shen hadn't heard in a very long time—a tone belonging to a "normal person"—that tone carried worry, shock, and suppressed anger.

Yan Shen didn't raise his head. He just curled up even harder, body shivering in cold and fear. His brain could no longer process this sudden situation. He only knew that when someone broke in, he should hide himself, should make himself as small as possible, should wait for punishment to descend.

"...Yan Shen! It's me! Lao Zhao!"

That hand touched his shoulder.

Yan Shen's body suddenly bounced, like being electrocuted. A broken, nearly animal-like whimper overflowed from his throat, then his whole body shrank backward, until the iron chain held him back, unable to retreat further.

"...Don't... don't touch me..." his voice was so hoarse it was almost inaudible, like the last breath squeezed from deep in his throat.

Lao Zhao knelt before him. Borrowing the light coming from the stairway, Yan Shen finally saw his face clearly.

That was an aged, wrinkle-covered face; gray-white fur was already sparse, but the gaze was still sharp. That was Lao Zhao. A veteran from Beastshield's golden age, an old comrade who had fought side by side with Yan Shen.

Lao Zhao looked at Yan Shen. His gaze slid from Yan Shen's face, to his neck—that neck with deeply red strangulation marks from the collar, to his body—that bony, wound-covered body, to his leg—that even more severely deformed left leg.

Lao Zhao's eyes reddened.

"...These beasts." his voice was very low, like squeezed from between teeth.

He reached out, wanting to undo the collar on Yan Shen's neck. But Yan Shen's body trembled violently the instant he approached, eyes tightly closed, breathing rapid, like a child waiting to be beaten.

"...It's me, Lao Zhao." Lao Zhao's voice lightened, carrying a gentleness Yan Shen hadn't heard in a very long time, "...I'm taking you home."

Yan Shen's eyes opened.

His pupils contracted in the dim light, focusing on Lao Zhao's face. He looked at those eyes, those eyes as upright and firm as in memory. His lips moved, wanting to say something, but only a broken breath escaped his throat.

Then, his tears gushed out.

Not tears of gratitude. Not joy of being saved. That was something deeper—a huge shame of being seen in his present state by the person who knew him best. His body began to tremble violently, fingers frantically scratching on the ground, nails filled with dust and blood.

"...Don't look at me..." his voice was hoarse, broken, like a piece of paper crumpled and then unfolded, "...I beg you... don't look at me..."

Lao Zhao didn't avert his gaze.

His hands trembled, but his movements were steady. He undid the collar on Yan Shen's neck, undid the hemp rope on his wrists, undid the iron chain on his ankles. Those things that had bound Yan Shen for fourteen days fell to the ground one by one, emitting dull sounds.

But Yan Shen didn't move.

He just lay there, like an empty shell with its soul extracted. His face buried in his arm, body trembling, tail tightly clamped between his legs.

Lao Zhao took off his own coat, draping it over Yan Shen. That coat was very old, but very clean, carrying a scent belonging to the "outside world"—sunshine, soap, and the taste of normal life. The instant that scent poured into his nasal cavity, Yan Shen's body stiffened.

That was a scent he thought he would never smell again.

"...Can you stand up?" Lao Zhao asked.

Yan Shen didn't answer. He tried to move his body, but his legs had completely lost sensation. His left leg became even more stiff from prolonged kneeling; the old wound at his knee was swollen like a fist, presenting an unhealthy purplish-red.

Lao Zhao sighed. He bent down, draping Yan Shen's arm over his shoulder, then using all his strength, lifted this much taller beastkin from the ground.

Yan Shen's body was soft like a puddle of mud. His head hung on Lao Zhao's shoulder, gray-white fur rubbing against Lao Zhao's collar. His breathing was very light, very shallow, like a wisp of white mist about to dissipate.

"...Hold on tight." Lao Zhao said.

He half-supported, half-dragged Yan Shen out of the basement. The stairs were very steep; Yan Shen's left leg completely lacked strength, every step feeling like stepping on knife points. Lao Zhao used his shoulder to support most of his weight, moving upward step by step.

When outside air poured into his nasal cavity, Yan Shen's body trembled violently.

That was the first time in fourteen days he breathed outside air. The air was very cold, carrying a mixed smell of snow and coal smoke, but that was "outside" air, free air. Yan Shen's lungs spasmed in that instant, then greedily, near-painfully inhaled a large mouthful.

He raised his head.

The sky was gray-black. No moon; only sparks coming from distant factory chimneys. But even such a filthy sky, in Yan Shen's eyes, was like some sacred, inviolable thing.

Because he thought he would never see it again.

Lao Zhao supported him, walking step by step out of that alley. Snow was still falling; snowflakes landed on Yan Shen's fur, melting, then evaporated by body heat. His body shivered in the cold, but his eyes were opened very wide, as if wanting to etch everything into his mind.

The street was empty. Only their footsteps echoed on the snow—Lao Zhao's heavy steps, and the friction sound of Yan Shen's left leg dragging.

"...Lao Zhao." Yan Shen suddenly said.

His voice was very light, very hoarse, like coming from very far away. That was the first time in fourteen days he actively spoke.

"...Mm?" Lao Zhao turned his head.

Yan Shen looked ahead. His gaze was very empty, like two dried-up wells.

"...Don't take me home." he said.

Lao Zhao's footsteps paused.

"...What?"

Yan Shen turned his head, looking at Lao Zhao. There was no emotion in his eyes, only a calmness that made Lao Zhao feel afraid.

"...Don't take me home." he repeated, "...I can't... let Suisui see... me like this."

Lao Zhao didn't speak. He just looked at Yan Shen, looked at those amber eyes gleaming with deathly stillness in the dim light.

Snow fell between them, silently accumulating.
