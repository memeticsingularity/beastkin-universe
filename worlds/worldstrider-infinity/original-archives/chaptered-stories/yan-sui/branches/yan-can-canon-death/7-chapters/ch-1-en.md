# Chapter 1 Winter Night, Cane, and Bloodstains

When snow fell, even the filth in White Capital was quiet.

Yan Shen shouldn't have taken this route.

This shortcut through the western district's back alley was narrow, dirty, and the streetlamps had been broken for three years without repair. Normally he would take the detour along the main street, even though that road added fifteen minutes to his journey, even though every extra step with his left leg felt like dancing on knife points. But tonight was different. Tonight, Suisui's medicine had run out, and the only pharmacy in the western district still open was deep in this back alley. So he had to come.

Now he had to walk back.

Yan Shen carried a small bag of styptic powder and bandages just obtained from the pharmacy, leaning on his cane, hobbling along the snow-covered road. His left leg dragged behind him; the old wound at his knee sent a familiar, needle-like stabbing pain in the cold air. His pace was slow; every step required him to first probe forward with his cane, confirm the ground wasn't icy, then shift his body weight over.

Clack. Clack.

The cane tapped on the frozen ground, especially clear in the empty alley. Snow particles struck his gray-and-white fur, melting, then evaporating from body heat, leaving a fine layer of water droplets.

The end of the alley was home. Just turn this corner, and he would see that porch light Suisui always forgot to turn off.

But he stopped at the corner.

Because he heard it.

It was a muffled sound. The sound of fists striking flesh. Not one sound, but many, intermittent, mixed with indistinct curses and some... pained whimpers.

Yan Shen's pupils contracted slightly.

He didn't immediately rush out. Years of experience made him first conceal himself in the shadow of the wall corner, then slightly poke his head out, looking toward the source of the sound.

The alley was very dark. The streetlamps had long been broken; only the dim yellow light leaking from a distant household window faintly illuminated several dark clusters on the snow. Those dark clusters were swaying, gathering, like a pack of hyenas that had caught the scent of blood.

Yan Shen didn't see the figure surrounded in the middle. But he smelled it.

Wind blew from the alley, carrying a scent. The scent of blood. And—his nostrils flared—the tiger beastkin-specific, sunshine-tinged fur aroma. That was the scent he had raised for twenty-five years. From milky fragrance to youthful sweat, to the intense mature male scent of today, he could recognize it with his eyes closed.

Suisui.

Yan Shen's fingers suddenly tightened, knuckles whitening from the force. The pharmacy bag crinkled with a harsh squeak in his grip. Without hesitation, he stuffed the bag into a wall corner and walked out leaning on his cane.

The cold wind cut like a knife against his face. Yan Shen pulled up his worn collar and strode forward.

The alley entrance arrived.

Yan Shen saw that group. Five or six civilians in worn cotton-padded clothes formed a half-circle; in the middle was that orange-yellow figure. Yan Sui was curled on the ground, arms protecting his head, body curled like a shrimp from the pain. His uniform was torn, his shoulder badge fallen to one side, orange-yellow fur covered with mud and bloodstains, presenting a sickly dark red under the dim light.

A man in a gray cotton coat was raising his foot, preparing to kick Yan Sui's abdomen.

Yan Shen didn't shout.

He moved.

The cane slipped from his hand, thrown like a short spear. The wooden shaft made a dull whistling sound in the air, fiercely smashing into the back of the knee of the man raising his foot. That man "ugh"ed, his whole body losing balance, falling forward face-first into the snow.

"Who—!" The others suddenly turned around.

Yan Shen had already walked before them. His left leg dragged behind him, plowing a shallow trail in the snow. He had no cane now, but his fist was still clenched at his side, knuckles thick, palm with thick paw pads. Those amber eyes looked especially cold in the dim light, like two deep, bottomless ancient wells.

"...Step aside." His voice wasn't loud, but every word seemed squeezed from deep in his throat, carrying an unquestionable weight.

"Oh?" A fox beastkin walked out from the crowd, sizing up Yan Shen, the corner of his mouth pulling into a malicious smile, "Another Beastshield one? Luck's not bad tonight."

Yan Shen didn't answer. He just looked at Yan Sui curled on the ground. His son's tail was being stepped on by someone; the fluffy tiger tail was being crushed in the snow, Yan Sui's body trembling because of it, a broken whimper escaping his throat.

Yan Shen's brow furrowed slightly. Only slightly.

Then he charged forward.

First step, left leg dragging on the ground, body tilting slightly from imbalance. Second step, right fist swinging out, with a veteran's precision and ruthlessness, straight into the nose bridge of the nearest person. A crisp bone-breaking sound; that person covered his nose and retreated, fresh blood gushing from between his fingers.

"Old thing!" Another person pounced from the side.

Yan Shen sidestepped—because of his left leg's burden, his sidestep wasn't complete; his right shoulder was still grazed by the opponent's palm. But borrowing this force, his body suddenly rotated, left elbow striking backward, precisely hitting that person's chin. That person's vision went dark, staggering backward.

But Yan Shen's movement thus revealed an opening.

His left leg couldn't keep up with his body's center of gravity in time during the rotation; his whole body tilted to one side. A tall civilian seized the opportunity, kicking fiercely at Yan Shen's disabled left leg knee—exactly that deformed joint.

"Ugh—!"

A broken breath escaped Yan Shen's throat. That wasn't a shout, but an instinctive reaction of the body under extreme pain. His left leg bent at an almost impossible angle under that blow; the old wound at his knee felt like being stabbed by a red-hot knife. He knelt down on one knee, one hand supporting himself on the cold snow, his fingers immediately filled with dirty snow mud.

"Ha!" That fox beastkin let out a sharp sneer, "Former Beastshield's Yan Shen? Just this much skill?"

Yan Shen lowered his head; white tiger whiskers were dotted with snow particles. His breathing grew heavy; every inhalation carried the bloody taste from his lungs—not his, but Suisui's. He could smell it; that scent permeated the air, so strong it made him want to kill.

The hand supporting him on the ground slowly clenched into a fist.

Then, he picked up the cane that had fallen in the snow.

Not using it to stand up. But gripping its middle, like holding a short stick, or a knife.

Yan Shen stood up. Not a normal standing, but using the most energy-saving posture a veteran learned on the battlefield—using upper body strength to prop himself up, left leg merely as a support point, not bearing too much weight. His posture was very strange, like a wounded beast, but there was no fear in this beast's eyes.

"I'll say it once more." His voice was hoarse, but every word was bitten dead, "Step aside."

The fox beastkin's smile stiffened slightly.

Because he saw Yan Shen's gaze. In those amber eyes there was no room for negotiation, no begging, not even any anger—only something he had never seen before, pure, all-out killing intent. That wasn't the gaze a disabled old man should have. That was the gaze a veteran who had experienced life and death in Beastshield's golden age would show when protecting his most precious thing.

"...Go." The fox beastkin suddenly said.

"Huh?" The person beside him didn't react.

"I said go!" The fox beastkin suddenly turned around, voice carrying an imperceptible irritation, "This old thing's gone mad; it's not worth fighting him desperately."

The others looked at each other. They looked at Yan Shen, half-kneeling, holding his cane like holding a knife, looked at those eyes gleaming with cold light in the snowy night, then looked at the already half-unconscious Yan Sui on the ground. Ultimately, that accumulated anger gradually crumbled before Yan Shen's killing intent. They turned and left one after another, footsteps gradually disappearing in the alley.

Yan Shen didn't chase. He couldn't chase. His left leg was already numb with pain; his whole body swayed unsteadily.

He threw down the cane, knelt in the snow, beside Yan Sui.

"...Suisui." His voice was so light it was almost swallowed by the wind and snow.

Yan Sui's eyes were half-open; his right eye was swollen into a narrow slit. His lips were broken; blood flowed down his chin, dripping onto the snow, melting out a small patch of dark red mark. He looked at Yan Shen, gaze unfocused, as if he couldn't see clearly.

"...Dad...?" His voice was broken, carrying a grievance only young people have, "...They... they said I... was Beastshield..."

"Don't talk." Yan Shen reached out; his movements were very light, but his hand trembled slightly. He touched Yan Sui's forehead—burning hot. Then his hand slid down Yan Sui's shoulder, checking his ribs—fortunately, no obvious fractures. But that bruise beneath the fur on his abdomen made his fingers pause.

"...Can you stand up?" he asked.

Yan Sui tried to move, but just propped up his upper body when pain made him suck in a breath, and his whole body fell back.

Yan Shen didn't say anything more. He bent down, draped Yan Sui's arm over his shoulder, then used all his strength to lift his son from the snow. Yan Sui was taller than him, sturdier than him; when his whole body's weight pressed over, Yan Shen's left leg suddenly went soft, nearly causing both of them to fall together.

But he held steady.

"...Hold on tight." he said.

Yan Sui mumbled a "mm" in a daze, resting his head on his father's shoulder. His breathing was heavy, carrying a bloody smell, spraying on Yan Shen's neck nest, warm and damp.

Yan Shen held his son's waist with one hand, his cane with the other, moving step by step toward the direction of home.

Snow was still falling. The two people's footprints extended crookedly on the snow; beside one trail of footprints were small pits poked out by a cane, and intermittent, dark red bloodstains.

The road home wasn't long. But for Yan Shen, that might have been the longest stretch of road he had ever walked in his life.

---

The room was warm. Yan Shen paused at the door, adjusted his breathing, then helped Yan Sui into the bedroom.

That bed wasn't large, but it was covered with clean bedding. Yan Shen gently placed Yan Sui on the bed, his movements careful as if placing a fragile piece of porcelain. Yan Sui's brow furrowed slightly, but he didn't wake. He just turned over, buried his face in the pillow, a vague murmur escaping his throat.

Yan Shen stood by the bed, looking at his son.

Yan Sui's profile looked especially young under the dim yellow light. His fur was a healthy orange-yellow, but now matted into locks by blood and mud. The skin around his right eye was already swollen, presenting an ugly purplish-red. The wound on his lip was still oozing blood; white tiger teeth were stained with blood threads.

Yan Shen turned around, taking out the first-aid kit from the cabinet. That was something he had left from his Beastshield days many years ago; the medicine inside had long expired, but there were still some bandages, iodine, and styptic powder.

He brought a basin of warm water, wet the towel, wrung it dry, then began to wipe the bloodstains from Yan Sui's face.

Starting from the forehead, downward, to the corner of the eye, to the cheekbone, to the chin. The movements were very light, but Yan Sui still hissed in pain, his body involuntarily shrinking.

"...Hurts?" Yan Shen stopped.

"...It's fine..." Yan Sui's voice was muffled in the pillow, "...Dad... sorry... caused you trouble again..."

Yan Shen's hand paused.

He didn't say "it's okay," nor did he say "it's not your fault." He just re-soaked the towel in warm water, wrung it dry, then continued wiping.

"...Eat your noodles." he said.

Yan Sui was stunned for a moment, then the corner of his mouth pulled into a very faint smile, tugging the wound at the corner of his mouth, hissing again from the pain.

Yan Shen had already turned and walked into the kitchen.

There was still leftover noodle soup in the pot from the evening. He added some water, reheated it, then took out an egg from the cabinet, cracking it into the pot. When the noodle soup boiled, steam spread, carrying a simple, warm aroma.

When Yan Shen returned to the bedroom with the bowl, Yan Sui had already sat up, leaning against the headboard, one hand covering his abdomen.

"...Here." Yan Shen handed the bowl over.

That was a very ordinary bowl of noodle soup. The noodles were thick noodles; in the soup were a few greens and a poached egg, floating with a few drops of sesame oil. Nothing special, but on this winter night, that steaming aroma gave a near-painful comfort.

Yan Sui took the bowl, using a spoon to scoop up a mouthful of soup, blew on it, and sent it into his mouth.

"...Delicious." he said softly.

Yan Shen "mm"ed, sitting on the edge of the bed, beginning to check the other wounds on Yan Sui's body. He used his fingers to gently press Yan Sui's ribs, pressing each spot then pausing, observing Yan Sui's reaction.

"...Here?"

"...A little painful..."

"...Here?"

"...It's okay..."

After confirming no fractures, Yan Shen used iodine to clean the scrapes on Yan Sui's arms and shoulders, then applied styptic powder, wrapping them with bandages. His fingers were thick, but his movements were very steady, as if handling an ordinary matter.

Yan Sui ate noodles while secretly watching his father.

Under the light, Yan Shen's profile looked especially aged. Gray-and-white fur presented an exhausted color under the light; white tiger whiskers extended from both sides of the corners of his mouth, long and distinct. His brows were slightly furrowed, gaze focused on the wounds, but his eye sockets were deeply sunken, the exhaustion inside thick and indissoluble.

"...Dad." Yan Sui suddenly said.

"Mm."

"...Will they... come again?"

Yan Shen's hand paused.

He didn't immediately answer. He just cut the last section of bandage, tied a knot, then put away the first-aid kit, standing up.

"...Sleep." he said, voice very light but unquestionable, "Tomorrow... I'll go find someone."

Yan Sui wanted to ask something more, but the fatigue and pain of his body prevented him from organizing his thoughts. He just nodded, placed the bowl on the nightstand, then slowly lay down.

Yan Shen helped him pull the quilt to his chin, tucking in the corners. His fingers paused when touching that bruise on Yan Sui's cheek. His fingertips were very rough, but the movement was very light, as if touching something precious.

"...Dad?" Yan Sui's eyes were already half-closed.

"...Mm."

"...Thank you..."

Yan Shen didn't answer. He just stood there, watching his son's breathing gradually become long and drawn out.

Outside the window, snow was still falling. Wind drilled in through the window gap, carrying White Capital's unique decaying scent—coal smoke, mud, distant garbage. But the room was warm. The residual heat of the noodle soup still permeated the air; the tiger beastkin on the bed had already sunk into deep sleep, brows even slightly furrowed in sleep, but the corner of his mouth had an imperceptible relaxation.

Yan Shen stood by the bed for a long time.

He looked at his son's sleeping face, looked at that tuft of forehead fur—that tuft identical to Yan Shen's own youth—trembling slightly with breath under the light. He recalled many years ago, when Suisui was still little, also curled sleeping in his embrace like this. Back then his leg wasn't broken yet; he could still carry his son on his back to see the snow in the back mountains. Suisui would lie on his back, small hands encircling his neck, fluffy tiger tail wrapped around his waist, warm and cozy.

How good it was back then.

Yan Shen's hand slowly gripped the cane. That polished wood transmitted a familiar texture in his palm.

He knew that group wouldn't let it go. White Capital's hatred was like coal smoke, pervasive, never dissipating. He could scare them off today because his fierceness caught them off guard. But next time? Next time when they were prepared, could he, a disabled old man, still protect Suisui?

He couldn't always stay by Suisui's side. Suisui had his own path to walk. And he...

Yan Shen lowered his head, his forehead gently pressing against the cane's grip. That was a praying posture, or a posture of an exhausted old man seeking support.

Tomorrow. Tomorrow he would go to the western district, find Lao Zhao. Lao Zhao was his old comrade from his Beastshield days, now living in the western district, though no longer concerned with worldly affairs, but his connections remained. Perhaps... perhaps he could help. Perhaps he could find a middleman to quell that group's anger.

Yan Shen raised his head, taking one last look at his son's sleeping face.

Then he leaned on his cane, hobbling out of the bedroom, gently closing the door.

The living room was very dark. Yan Shen didn't light the lamp. He just sat in that old chair, in the darkness, listening to the breathing sounds coming from the bedroom.

Clack.

The cane leaned against the table, making a light sound.

The winter night was long. But at this moment, at least his son was still alive, still breathing, still by his side.

For Yan Shen, that was enough.

(End of Chapter One)
