# Chapter 7 Bearing

When dawn was near, Lao Zhao moved.

He carefully laid Yan Shen's body flat, letting him lie face-up on the bed, hands crossed over his abdomen. Yan Shen's eyes were half-open; Lao Zhao used his fingers to gently close his eyelids. That movement was very light, like brushing dust off an ancient statue.

Then, Lao Zhao began to tidy up.

He found a piece of Yan Shen's own clothing—that was a dark gray old overcoat, washed white but still neat. Yan Shen always wore it when going out, saying it was "stain-resistant." Lao Zhao helped him put on the overcoat, buttoned the buttons, straightened the collar. His hand paused when touching Yan Shen's neck—the collar strangulation marks had already turned deep purple, like a scar that could never heal.

Lao Zhao's brow furrowed. He took out a scarf from the drawer, carefully wrapping it around Yan Shen's neck, covering that strangulation mark.

"...Be proper." he said softly, "...Going home needs to be proper."

He bent down, picking Yan Shen up from the bed.

Yan Shen was taller than him, sturdier than him; even after fourteen days of torture, the skeleton was still there. Lao Zhao carried him with great effort; his waist wasn't good, his knees weren't good, but he gritted his teeth, walking step by step toward the door. Yan Shen's head rested on his shoulder; gray-white fur rubbed against his collar. That body was still warm—beastkin preservation mechanisms made the boundary between death and life blurry; Lao Zhao almost felt Yan Shen's breath still lingering in his neck nest.

But that was just wind.

Lao Zhao pushed open the door, walking into White Capital's morning.

Snow had stopped. The sky presented a dirty gray-white, like a wall smoked by coal smoke for too long. The street was empty; only a few broken streetlamps swayed slightly in the wind, making creaking sounds. Lao Zhao's footprints left a string of deep marks on the snow, while Yan Shen's left leg dragged on the ground, plowing a shallow arc in the snow.

That picture looked like a father carrying a drunken son home.

If not for Yan Shen's eyes being tightly closed, if not for his chest no longer rising and falling, if not for his fingers already stiffening into a slightly bent posture—that posture looked like holding something invisible—it really would have looked like going home.

Lao Zhao walked step by step. His breathing was very heavy; every pant turned into a puff of white mist in the cold air. His waist protested, knees screamed, but he didn't stop. He just gritted his teeth, lifting Yan Shen up a bit, letting him lean more steadily.

"...Yan Shen." Lao Zhao suddenly said. His voice was very light, like talking to himself, "...Do you still remember? Many years ago... once on a mission... you carried me for three li... my leg was blown injured... you walked while cursing... saying I was too heavy..."

His voice trembled somewhat.

"...Now it's my turn to carry you." he said, "...Fair."

No one answered him.

Only wind and snow whistled by his ears, like some ancient, wordless elegy.

---

Yan Shen's home arrived.

That was a dilapidated apartment building; wall paint peeling, windows covered with thick frost. Lao Zhao stood at the door, adjusting his breath, then freed a hand, taking out the key from Yan Shen's pocket.

Key inserted into the lock, turned, emitting a dull click sound.

The door opened.

The room was very quiet. Quieter than Lao Zhao imagined. That quietness wasn't the quietness of being empty, but a... frozen quietness. Like time had stopped flowing here, like some huge, invisible thing had pressed everything in place.

Lao Zhao carried Yan Shen into the bedroom, gently placing him on the bed. He pulled over the quilt, covering it to Yan Shen's chin, then used his palm to smooth the wrinkles on the quilt. His movements were very careful, like tidying a precious gift.

Then, he heard a sound.

That was a very slight, somewhat dazed breathing sound. Coming from the adjacent room.

Lao Zhao turned around, walking toward the adjacent room's door. He pushed open the door—

Yan Sui lay on the bed, still sleeping.

His breathing was very steady, very deep. That orange-yellow tiger tail extended from under the quilt, resting on the bedside, tail tip trembling slightly with breath. His brow slightly furrowed, like having some not-so-good dream, but the corner of his mouth carried an imperceptible relaxation.

Lao Zhao stood in the doorway, looking at this young man.

Yan Sui and Yan Shen looked very much alike. Same wide forehead, same thick brows, same, even in sleep, slightly upturned corner of the mouth. Lao Zhao recalled many years ago, in Beastshield's golden age, he had once held childhood Suisui. At that time Suisui was only three years old, fluffy, like a small orange, lying in his embrace without crying or fussing, just looking at him with those amber eyes identical to Yan Shen's, then suddenly smiled, revealing two small tiger teeth.

How good it was back then.

Lao Zhao's eye rims reddened again. He walked up, gently tucking in the quilt corners for Yan Sui. His movements were very light, afraid of waking this still-dreaming young man.

Then he saw the things on the nightstand.

That was a stack of handwritten paper, edges yellowed, worn fuzzy by time. Lao Zhao used two fingers to gently pull one out, glanced at it—

"With this coupon, Dad will definitely talk properly with Suisui no matter what."

Lao Zhao's hand trembled.

He put the communication coupon back in place, gently pushing the drawer closed. Then, he bent down, lightly, lightly kissing the top of Yan Sui's head.

That was an elder's kiss to a junior, carrying an unspeakable compassion and apology.

"...Child." Lao Zhao's voice was so light it was almost inaudible, "...Sorry."

He turned and walked out of the bedroom, gently closing the door.

---

Lao Zhao began to tidy Yan Shen's final appearance.

He brought a basin of warm water from the kitchen, wet the towel, wrung it dry, then wiped Yan Shen's body bit by bit. Starting from the forehead, downward, to neck, to shoulders, to chest. His movements were very steady, very slow, like performing some ancient ritual only old veterans understood.

Yan Shen's body was still warm. That warmth made Lao Zhao's fingers tremble slightly with every touch. He always felt that perhaps the next second, Yan Shen would open his eyes, look at him with that somewhat helpless gaze, and say "Lao Zhao, what are you crying for, I'm not dead yet."

But Yan Shen didn't open his eyes.

He just quietly lay there, the corner of his mouth carrying a faint smile, like having a very warm dream.

Lao Zhao combed his fur for him. Gray-white fur gleamed with a soft silver under the light, like moonlight sprinkled on snow. He arranged Yan Shen's tiger whiskers one by one, letting them hang down in one direction. Then he helped Yan Shen change into a more proper set of clothes—not that old overcoat, but the old uniform from Beastshield's golden age that Yan Shen had treasured for many years.

That uniform was already very old, color faded to dark green; the Beastshield badge on the shoulder was also worn beyond recognition. But Lao Zhao still insisted on putting it on him. Because he knew that Yan Shen's proudest time was when wearing this uniform.

"...You always wanted to go back, right?" Lao Zhao said while buttoning, voice somewhat hoarse, "...Back to that time... back to when Beastshield was still Beastshield..."

No one answered him.

After fastening the last button, Lao Zhao stepped back, looking at Yan Shen on the bed.

That old uniform on Yan Shen's bony body looked somewhat loose. But the lapel was neat, the shoulder badge proper, the collar button fastened to the topmost one. Gray-white fur was combed neatly; white tiger whiskers gleamed with soft luster under the light. Yan Shen closed his eyes, expression peaceful, even serene.

He looked like he was asleep.

Like he might wake up at any moment, then lean on his cane, hobbling into the kitchen, cooking a bowl of hot noodle soup for Suisui.

Lao Zhao's tears flowed down.

He didn't wipe them. He just stood by the bedside, looking at Yan Shen, looking for a long time. Then he turned around, taking out a piece of paper and a pen from his pocket, sitting by the table, beginning to write.

That was a brief letter. Written to Yan Sui. But he didn't know how to begin, how to tell a young man that his father was already dead. He wrote and crossed out, crossed out and wrote, ultimately leaving only one line:

"Your father was a true Beastshield soldier. He loved you."

Lao Zhao folded the letter, placing it at Yan Shen's bedside, pressing it down with that stack of communication coupons.

Then he stood up, taking one last look at Yan Shen.

"...Go well, brother." he said.

He turned and walked out of the bedroom, closing the door.

---

Lao Zhao didn't leave immediately.

He sat in the chair in the living room, in the darkness, sitting for a whole morning. He needed time. Needed courage. Needed to think through how to face what came next.

At noon, he stood up.

He walked out of Yan Shen's home, locked the door, put the key in his pocket. Then, he leaned on his own cane—that old cane polished bright just like Yan Shen's cane—and walked toward White Capital's eastern district.

He was going to see a person.

That person was called Lao Zhou, his old superior when he was still at Beastshield. Now Lao Zhou was already a middle-level cadre of Beastshield; though his power wasn't great, he could at least still speak. Lao Zhao needed to arrange a way out for Yan Sui through Lao Zhou.

The streets of the eastern district were a bit wider than the western district, but only a bit better. Apartment buildings with peeling wall paint, rusty iron fences, and those mottled slogans on the walls. Lao Zhao walked step by step, cane tapping on the frozen ground, making clack, clack sounds.

The Beastshield office building was just ahead. That was a gray building; on the outer wall was the Beastshield badge—a beast claw surrounded by a shield. That badge was golden in Yan Shen's era; now it had turned dark red, like dried blood.

Lao Zhao stood at the door, taking a deep breath.

Then he walked in.

---

Lao Zhou was more aged than Lao Zhao imagined.

His office was permeated with a mixed smell of smoke and tea leaves. Lao Zhou sat behind the desk; seeing Lao Zhao walk in, a flash of surprise appeared in his eyes, immediately turning into some complex emotion.

"...Lao Zhao?" Lao Zhou's voice was somewhat hoarse, "...Why did you come?"

Lao Zhao didn't sit down. He just stood before the desk, both hands on his cane, like an old tree swaying in the wind but never falling.

"...Yan Shen is dead." he said.

Lao Zhou's hand stopped in midair. He held a pen in his hand; the pen tip hung above the paper, ink soaking out a small patch of black mark.

"...What?"

"...Yan Shen. Dead." Lao Zhao repeated, voice very calm, but every word like squeezed from between teeth, "...Last night. I sent him."

Lao Zhou's eyes widened. He put down the pen, stood up, walked around the desk, coming before Lao Zhao. His gaze wandered over Lao Zhao's face, like looking for traces of some joke. But he didn't find any.

"...How did he die?" Lao Zhou asked.

Lao Zhao looked at him. In his gaze was very calm, but deep inside was something that made Lao Zhou feel afraid.

"...Tortured by people for fourteen days." Lao Zhao said, "...When brought back... already couldn't make it."

Lao Zhou's expression changed.

"...Who did it?" His voice lowered, carrying a suppressed anger.

"...Western district civilians." Lao Zhao said, "...That group who beat Yan Sui some time ago. Same batch."

Lao Zhou's hand clenched into a fist. His knuckles whitened from the force, veins bulging on his arms. He walked two steps in the office, then stopped, back toward Lao Zhao.

"...What about Yan Sui?" he asked.

"...Still at home." Lao Zhao said, "...Doesn't know. Still sleeping."

Lao Zhou turned around, looking at Lao Zhao. In his eyes were many things—anger, sorrow, and some... exhaustion.

"...What do you want me to do?" he asked.

Lao Zhao took out a piece of paper from his pocket. That was what he wrote last night, listing Yan Shen's home address, Yan Sui's basic situation, and everything he could arrange for Yan Sui.

"...Take care of him." Lao Zhao said, "...Yan Shen only has this one son. He's still young... still has a future... can't let him stay alone in White Capital."

Lao Zhou took the paper, glanced at it. His hand trembled slightly.

"...I can arrange him into Beastshield." Lao Zhou said, "...Starting from the bottom... but at least can have enough to eat... have a place to stay."

Lao Zhao shook his head.

"...Don't tell him the truth." he said, "...At least... don't tell him now. Tell him... Yan Shen died of illness. Tell him... his father passed very peacefully."

Lao Zhou looked at him, looked for a very long time. Then, he nodded.

"...I promise you." he said.

Lao Zhao slightly bowed. That was a veteran's respect for another veteran, a father's entrustment to another father.

"...Thank you." he said.

Then he turned around, walking out of the office.

---

When Lao Zhao walked out of the Beastshield office building, the sky was already dark.

White Capital's winter night came very quickly, like someone suddenly pulling closed a black curtain. Streetlamps on the street lit up, but most were broken; only a few swayed in the wind and snow, illuminating the ground in flickering light and shadow.

Lao Zhao walked on the snow, cane tapping the ground echoing in the silence. He didn't go home immediately. He just walked, step by step, like looking for something, like escaping something.

He passed by Yan Shen's home.

That porch light was still on—Suisui always forgot to turn off the light. Dim yellow light leaked from the window, casting a small patch of warm light on the snow. Lao Zhao stood across the street, looking at that window, looking for a very long time.

He imagined the scene inside. Yan Sui might have already woken up, might have discovered his father wasn't home, might be anxious, might be worried. He imagined Yan Sui pushing open the bedroom door, seeing that body wearing the old uniform on the bed. He imagined Yan Sui's expression—from confusion to shock, from shock to collapse.

He imagined that young man kneeling by the bedside, holding his father's already stiff but still warm hand, crying out "Dad."

Lao Zhao's tears flowed down.

He didn't wipe them. He just stood there, in the wind and snow, looking at that lamp.

Then he turned around, walking step by step into White Capital's night.

He didn't look back.

Because he knew, some farewells, once you look back, you can never walk away again.

---

Many years later, Yan Sui in some corner of Beastshield, accidentally heard a legend.

Legend has it that long, long ago, White Capital once had a true Beastshield soldier. He was upright, brave, and to protect his son, spared no cost. He died on a winter night, in an old comrade's embrace, the corner of his mouth carrying a smile.

Legend has it his soul became a star in the sky above White Capital, shining on winter nights, guarding every beastkin still struggling in the darkness.

Yan Sui heard this legend, only smiling.

He didn't tell others that his father was also called Yan Shen. He also didn't tell others that many years ago on that winter night, he once had a dream. In the dream, his father carried him on his back to see the snow in the back mountains; his small hands encircled his father's neck, fluffy tail wrapped around his father's waist, warm and cozy.

In the dream, his father said to him: "Suisui, Dad loves you."

That dream was very long, very long. So long that when Yan Sui woke up, his pillow was full of tears.

(End of Chapter Seven)
