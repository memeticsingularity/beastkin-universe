# Chapter 6 Old Comrade

Lao Zhao's room was very small.

A bed, a table, two chairs, a stove with a faint fire. On the wall hung a faded old Beastshield flag; in the corner were piled a few yellowed training manuals. The air carried a stale but clean scent—soap, tea leaves, and some aged beastkin-specific, old-book-page-like smell.

Yan Shen was placed on the bed. That was a single bed; the bedding was washed white but very clean. Lao Zhao covered him with two thick quilts, but Yan Shen was still trembling. That trembling wasn't from cold, but an uncontrollable shiver seeping from deep in his bones, like his body was only now, after being rescued, finally having time to be afraid.

Lao Zhao's hand stopped in midair.

"...It's me." His voice lightened, carrying a gentleness Yan Shen hadn't heard in a very long time, "...Lao Zhao. I won't hurt you."

Yan Shen's eyes slowly focused on Lao Zhao's face. He looked at those eyes, those eyes as upright and tired as in memory, and his body gradually relaxed. But he still curled up, tail tightly clamped between his legs, like a young beast ready to flee at any moment.

Lao Zhao sighed. He began wiping again, movements even lighter, even slower than before. He started from Yan Shen's forehead, wiping away bloodstains and dirt, then downward, to neck, to shoulders, to chest.

When he wiped to the left leg knee, Yan Shen's body suddenly stiffened.

"...It's okay." Lao Zhao said, "...It's okay."

His fingers gently touched that deformed joint. It was swollen like a fist, skin presenting an unhealthy purplish-red, burning hot to the touch. Lao Zhao's brow tightly furrowed together. He knew this injury—many years ago, when Yan Shen was still at Beastshield. Back then they went together to confront Beastshield; Yan Shen was beaten and disabled. Lao Zhao was also there at the time, but he protected his head, not Yan Shen's leg.

He had always owed Yan Shen a leg.

Now, he owed even more.

Lao Zhao put the towel back in the water basin. The water had already turned red, like a basin of diluted blood. He carried the basin, walked to the door, poured out the water, then changed a basin of clean warm water.

He returned to the bedside, finding Yan Shen looking at him.

That was a very strange gaze. Not gratitude, not trust, but something deeper—like looking at a person who came from very far away, a person from a world Yan Shen could no longer return to.

"...Lao Zhao." Yan Shen spoke.

His voice was very light, very hoarse, like squeezed from deep in his throat. That was the first time in fourteen days he actively called out a person's name.

"...Mm." Lao Zhao sat by the bedside, "...Speak."

Yan Shen's eyes looked at the ceiling. There was a crack on it, extending from the wall corner to the lamp base, like a dried-up river.

"...Help me with something." he said.

"...What thing?"

Yan Shen turned his head, looking at Lao Zhao. His gaze was very calm, calm enough to make Lao Zhao feel afraid.

"...Kill me."

The room was very quiet. The fire in the stove made slight crackling sounds, like something quietly shattering.

Lao Zhao's hand tightened on the basin edge. Knuckles whitening from the force, water in the basin slightly rippling, reflecting his twisted reflection.

"...What did you say?" His voice trembled somewhat.

"...Kill me." Yan Shen repeated, voice still calm, like saying something perfectly ordinary, "...With your own hands. Like... like on the battlefield... giving a severely wounded brother a quick end."

Lao Zhao suddenly stood up.

The basin was shaken by him, water splashing out, falling on the ground, making slight dripping sounds. Lao Zhao walked two steps in the room, then stopped, back toward Yan Shen. His shoulders trembled slightly; gray-white fur looked especially aged under the light.

"...You're crazy." His voice was very low, like squeezed from between teeth.

"...I'm not crazy." Yan Shen said, "...I'm very clear."

"...Clear my ass!" Lao Zhao suddenly turned around, eyes red, "...I finally found you... I rescued you from those beasts... and now you want me to kill you?!"

His voice was trembling, not from anger, but from something deeper—fear. He was afraid Yan Shen truly wanted to die. He was afraid what he rescued wasn't a person, but a shell that had already decided to abandon life.

Yan Shen looked at Lao Zhao. There was no ripple in his gaze, only an understanding that made Lao Zhao feel heartbroken.

"...Lao Zhao." he said, "...Look at me."

Lao Zhao didn't move.

"...Look at me." Yan Shen repeated, voice still calm, but with something more—not pleading, but a heavier, nearly commanding firmness.

Lao Zhao slowly turned around.

He looked at Yan Shen. Looked at that bony body, looked at the collar strangulation marks on his neck, looked at those already scabbed or still oozing wounds, looked at that even more severely deformed left leg. He looked at Yan Shen's eyes—those amber eyes, once carrying a steady light that made everyone feel reassured. Now that light was still there, but it had been covered by something—a thick layer of dead, ashen gray.

"...In my present state..." Yan Shen said, "...I can't go back to see Suisui."

His fingers unconsciously rubbed on the quilt, movements very light, like stroking something precious.

"...He will see." Yan Shen continued, "...He will see my body... see my wounds... see my knees... that can't help but bend when hearing 'sit down.'"

His voice began to break somewhat, but he forced himself to continue.

"...He will know what his father became." Yan Shen's eyes looked at Lao Zhao, "...He will know... his father was treated like a dog... will know his father licked food from the ground... will know his father... couldn't even... couldn't even..."

He couldn't continue.

A broken breath escaped his throat, then he buried his face in the quilt. His body was trembling, like a withered leaf in the cold wind. But his voice was muffled in the quilt, becoming a fuzzy, suppressed whimper.

Lao Zhao stood by the bedside. His hands hung at his sides, fingers trembling slightly.

"...He won't." His voice was very light, like speaking to himself, "...Suisui won't think that... he's your son..."

"...Precisely because he's my son..." Yan Shen's voice came from the quilt, muffled, carrying a wet heaviness, "...I can't let him know."

Lao Zhao didn't speak.

He slowly walked back to the bedside, sat down. He reached out, wanting to pat Yan Shen's shoulder, but stopped when his hand reached halfway. He recalled Yan Shen's reaction when touched just now; his fingers stiffly hung in the air for a moment, then withdrew.

"...I can take him away." Lao Zhao said, "...I can take you away from White Capital. Go somewhere else. Start over."

Yan Shen raised his head from the quilt. His eyes were very red, but already dry. He looked at Lao Zhao, the corner of his mouth pulling a very faint smile—that wasn't a happy smile, but a smile containing too many things.

"...Lao Zhao." he said, "...Do you think... I can 'start over'?"

Lao Zhao opened his mouth, but couldn't speak.

"...My body remembers." Yan Shen's voice was very light, like telling someone else's story, "...I close my eyes... and I can hear the iron chain's sound... can smell the cement floor's taste... can feel... someone touching my head... telling me 'good boy.'"

His fingers tightened on the quilt, knuckles whitening from the force.

"...These things... will follow me my whole life." he said, "...No matter where I go... no matter what I do... they will follow me."

Lao Zhao lowered his head. His eye sockets were very red, tears swirling inside, but he didn't let them flow down.

"...And..." Yan Shen continued, "...I'm no longer Suisui's father."

"...Don't talk nonsense." Lao Zhao's voice trembled somewhat.

"...I'm not talking nonsense." Yan Shen's gaze was very calm, "...A father... is supposed to protect his son. Is supposed to make his son proud. Is supposed to... stand straight... before his son."

He lowered his head, looking at his own hands. Those hands were once thick and powerful, palms with thick paw pads, able to carry Suisui on his back to see the snow in the back mountains. "...I can't stand straight anymore, Lao Zhao." Yan Shen said, "...Whether before others... or before Suisui... I can't stand straight anymore."

The room quieted down again.

The fire in the stove gradually grew smaller; the room's temperature slowly dropped. Yan Shen and Lao Zhao both didn't move. They just sat there, one lying on the bed, one sitting by the bedside, like two statues forgotten by time.

"...Lao Zhao." Yan Shen spoke again.

"...Mm."

"...Please."

Those two words were very light. But they echoed in the silence, like two stones falling into a deep well, stirring countless invisible ripples.

Lao Zhao's hand covered his face. His shoulders trembled violently, throat emitting a suppressed, broken sound—that wasn't crying, but something deeper, like something shattering inside his body.

"...Yan Shen..." His voice came from between his fingers, muffled, carrying a wet despair, "...You're my brother... how could I... how could I with my own hands..."

"...Precisely because we're brothers..." Yan Shen said, "...that's why you have to help me with this."

Lao Zhao's hand slowly slid down from his face. His eyes were very red, face fur wet from tears, sticking together. He looked at Yan Shen, looked at those calm, terrifying eyes.

"...You're certain?" His voice was very light, like confirming a dream.

Yan Shen nodded.

"...Certain." he said, "...I've already thought it through."

Lao Zhao closed his eyes. His lips trembled slightly, like praying, like cursing. His body swayed slightly on the chair, like a tree about to fall in a storm.

Then he opened his eyes.

Something in those eyes had changed. Not numbness, not indifference, but something deeper—a heavy calmness after accepting fate.

"...Alright." he said.

That one word, like a mountain falling to the ground.

Lao Zhao didn't act immediately.

He sat by the bedside, silent for a long time. The fire in the stove made slight crackling sounds, especially clear in the silence. Yan Shen lay in the quilt, eyes half-open, looking at the crack on the ceiling. His breathing was very light, very shallow, like a wisp of white mist about to dissipate.

"...I know a way." Lao Zhao finally spoke. His voice was very light, but every word was bitten dead, "...Won't be too painful."

Yan Shen turned his head, looking at him.

"...The kind from the torture room?" Yan Shen asked. His voice was also very light, carrying a calmness of understanding.

Lao Zhao's hand trembled.

Lao Zhao had seen it in the torture room. Yan Shen had also seen it.

"...Mm." Lao Zhao said. His eye sockets were very red, but his gaze was very firm, "...I've seen it in the torture room. That... least likely to be painful."

Yan Shen looked at him, looked for a long time. Then, he slightly nodded.

"...Alright." he said, "...You do it."

Lao Zhao began to prepare.

He changed a basin of clean warm water, wiping Yan Shen's body one last time. His movements were very slow, very careful, like performing some ancient ritual. From forehead to toes, every inch of skin was carefully wiped, as if wanting to wash away fourteen days of dirt and humiliation together.

Then, he didn't dress Yan Shen. He just pulled the quilt up, covering it to Yan Shen's chest, letting Yan Shen's body gradually relax in the warm quilt.

Yan Shen closed his eyes.

His breathing was very steady, but Lao Zhao could see his chest slightly rising and falling—not from nervousness, but from an... acceptance. Yan Shen accepted. His fingers unconsciously rubbed on the quilt, movements very light, like stroking something precious.

Lao Zhao's hand slowly, slowly moved downward.

His fingers touched Yan Shen's skin. That skin was very hot, much higher than normal body temperature, like having a low fever. Lao Zhao's fingers trembled, but he forced himself to steady them. He knew what to do. He had seen it too many times in Beastshield's torture room—knew which position was most sensitive, knew what rhythm was most effective, knew how to make a person reach the peak in the shortest time.

But he had never thought that one day he would use these hands, to do this thing.

Not for torture. Not for humiliation.

To send his most intimate comrade on his way.

Yan Shen's body slightly stiffened under Lao Zhao's touch. That was an instinctive reaction—fourteen days of conditioning made his body full of vigilance toward any touch. But only for a moment. Then he relaxed. Because he knew, this hand wasn't the fox beastkin's hand, wasn't those civilians' hands. This was Lao Zhao's hand. Was the hand of an old comrade who had fought side by side with him.

"...Lao Zhao." Yan Shen suddenly said. His voice was very light, carrying a fuzzy, nearly dreamlike quality.

"...Mm?"

"...Your hand... is very warm."

Lao Zhao's tears silently slid down, dripping on the quilt, soaking out a small patch of dark mark. He didn't speak, only gripped Yan Shen's hand even tighter, while quickening the rhythm of the other hand.

Yan Shen's breathing began to grow heavy.

His body gradually heated under Lao Zhao's hand; beneath the gray-white fur, skin flushed with a sickly crimson. His chest heaved more and more violently; every inhalation carried a moist, trembling sound. His fingers tightly gripped the quilt, knuckles whitening from the force.

The semen gauge was rising.

Yan Shen could feel it. That familiar warm current began to spread from below his lower abdomen, like a lit fire, slowly, slowly diffusing toward his limbs and bones. That wasn't pain. That was a pure, physical pleasure he hadn't felt in a very long time. In that hellish basement, his body had been conditioned into a machine that only reacted to pain and humiliation. But now, Lao Zhao's hand reminded him—his body could also feel happiness.

"...Lao Zhao..." Yan Shen's voice became even more broken, carrying a suppressed trembling.

"...I'm here." Lao Zhao said. His voice was also trembling, but his hand didn't stop.

Yan Shen's head tilted backward, resting against the pillow, Adam's apple rolling violently below the collar strangulation mark. His fingers on the quilt stopped.

Stopped in a slightly bent posture, like holding something invisible—perhaps Suisui's hand, perhaps communication coupons, perhaps the tail of that little tiger lying on his father's back many years ago.

Then, everything stopped.

Breathing stopped. Heartbeat stopped. Body temperature began to slowly, irreversibly drop.

Lao Zhao didn't immediately withdraw his hand.

His hand was still on Yan Shen's body. According to human common sense, a corpse should quickly turn cold. But Yan Shen wasn't human. Beastkin bodies had a natural preservation mechanism; even after the heart stopped beating, body temperature would remain warm for a very long time. Lao Zhao felt the residual temperature of that body beneath his palm—that temperature was still there, like Yan Shen was merely asleep, might open his eyes at any moment, look at him with those amber eyes, and say "Lao Zhao, I'm hungry."

But Yan Shen didn't open his eyes.

That warm but no longer moving body was more cruel than ice-cold. Lao Zhao's tears silently flowed, dripping on Yan Shen's chest, absorbed by the gray-white fur, disappearing without a trace.

He bent down, kissing Yan Shen's forehead.

That was a very light, very light kiss, like a snowflake landing on skin, like a mother kissing her sleeping child.

"...Go well." Lao Zhao said, "...Brother."

The fire in the stove completely went out. The room's temperature rapidly dropped. But Lao Zhao didn't move. He just sat by the bedside, holding Yan Shen's gradually cold hand, in the darkness, sitting for a whole night.

Outside the window, snow was still falling. White Capital's winter night was long and cold, but at this moment, in this small room, something deeper than cold was permeating—that was an ancient, unspeakable sorrow, like some elegy coming from very far away, echoing in the wind and snow, then disappearing into the gray-black sky.

Yan Shen was gone.

Not as a domesticated dog. Not as a destroyed father.

But as a veteran, in an old comrade's embrace, in final warmth, peacefully left this world that could no longer let him stand.
