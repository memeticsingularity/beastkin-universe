# Chapter 4 The Seventh Day

Darkness has weight.

Yan Shen first realized this after he woke up. That dim yellow light bulb had gone out at some point, leaving only pure, thick darkness in the basement. That darkness wasn't "not being able to see," but a physical oppression, like a quilt soaked in water, heavily pressing on his eyes, ears, and skin.

He tried to move.

The iron chain made a crisp clashing sound, amplified countless times in the silence. That sound came from his neck—the collar was very tight, fur pressed apart to both sides, rough leather surface frictioning against the skin below his Adam's apple; every swallow brought that suffocating restraint.

Yan Shen tried to move his hand again.

His wrists were twisted behind his back; the hemp rope had already bitten into his skin, bringing a burning sting. He tried to break free, but the knot was a dead knot; the more he struggled, the tighter it bit. His fingers groped in the darkness, touching the cold cement ground, then the root of the iron pipe, then the links of the iron chain—link after link, cold, hard, without end.

He stopped.

Not because he gave up. But because a sound came from overhead.

That was the creaking of the wooden door. Then footsteps, walking down the stairs step by step. Those footsteps were very light, but especially clear in the darkness, like some predator approaching prey.

Yan Shen's ears—those tiger ears hidden beneath gray-white fur—instinctively flattened backward. His body also unconsciously curled up, like an old beast driven to the corner, trying to reduce the area of attack with the smallest volume.

That was fear. Pure, biological fear.

The light turned on.

The dim yellow bulb was pulled on by someone, stinging Yan Shen's eyes. He subconsciously turned his head; white tiger whiskers trembled under the light. After adapting to the light, he saw the person standing before him.

Fox beastkin. And two other civilians.

The fox beastkin held an enamel basin in his hand; something was in it, emitting faint steam. He walked before Yan Shen, squatted down, placed the basin on the ground, then patted the basin edge with his hand.

"...Eat." he said.

Yan Shen didn't move.

He just stared at that basin. In the basin was some paste-like thing, gray in color, unidentifiable. But that smell—a pungent odor mixed with sourness and some cheap seasoning—made Yan Shen's stomach spasm.

"I told you to eat." The fox beastkin's voice was still calm, but with something more.

Yan Shen still didn't move.

The fox beastkin sighed. That sigh sounded very sincere, as if feeling regret for Yan Shen's stubbornness. Then he stood up, raised his foot, and kicked Yan Shen's ribs.

"Ugh—!"

That kick wasn't heavy, but it hit exactly last night's bruise. Yan Shen's body suddenly curled up, like a cooked shrimp. A broken breath escaped his throat; white tiger whiskers trembled violently from pain.

"Not eating?" the fox beastkin asked, "Then starve."

He waved his hand; the other two people came up, one left one right holding Yan Shen's arms, dragging him to the center of the room like dragging a piece of furniture. Yan Shen's knees scraped across the cement floor, leaving two dark red blood trails. His left leg completely lacked strength, dragging on the ground; the old wound at his knee sent heart-piercing pain with every friction.

"...Kneel." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen didn't kneel. His body was stiff, like a frozen log.

The fox beastkin smiled. He walked forward, reaching out to press Yan Shen's back neck, forcing him to lower his head. Yan Shen wanted to resist, but his body was already weak to the extreme—last night's blood loss, cold, hunger, and the continuous pain of that disabled left leg made his will like a string pulled to its limit, ready to break at any moment.

"...I'll say it once more." The fox beastkin's voice was very close, right by his ear, carrying a damp, hot breath, "...Kneel."

Yan Shen's knees bent down.

Not because he wanted to kneel. But because the fox beastkin, while speaking, used his knee to fiercely strike the back of Yan Shen's left leg knee pit from behind—exactly the most fragile place of that deformed joint. Yan Shen's body lost balance, his whole body falling forward, forehead heavily hitting the cement floor.

"Good boy." the fox beastkin said.

He picked up the basin from the ground, pouring the contents onto the floor. That lump of grayish-white thing spread out on the cold cement floor, emitting an even stronger sour smell. Then, the fox beastkin kicked that lump of stuff to Yan Shen's face with his foot.

"...Eat." he said, "Like a dog."

Yan Shen's face was pressed against the cement floor. He could smell that sour smell, could smell the dust, could smell his own blood. His forehead was broken from that collision just now; fresh blood flowed down his brow bone, dripping onto the ground, mixing with that lump of stuff.

He closed his eyes.

"...Not eating?" The fox beastkin's voice carried an understanding calmness, "Doesn't matter. We have plenty of time."

He turned around, taking the other two people out of the basement. The door closed before Yan Shen; the lock tongue clicked. Then, the light also went out.

Darkness descended again.

Yan Shen knelt in the darkness for a long time. His knees had already lost sensation; the wound on his forehead throbbed with pain in the cold air. His stomach spasmed from hunger; that pain wasn't sharp, but a dull, deep-seated colic, like a hand twisting his internal organs.

He slowly, slowly, buried his face into his arm.

He didn't remember how long he stayed in the darkness. Time lost meaning in this basement. No windows, no light changes; only that occasionally lit bulb and occasionally heard footsteps marked the passage of time.

First day, he refused to eat.

Second day, he still refused.

Every time the fox beastkin came, he would repeat the same procedure: command him to kneel, pour food on the ground, then leave. Yan Shen didn't eat every time. His body gradually emaciated from hunger; fur lost its luster, gray-white hair in the darkness like a piece of faded old cloth.

Third day, he began to hallucinate.

He thought he heard Suisui's voice. That voice came from very far away, like separated by a thick layer of water: "Dad... Dad..." He wanted to respond, but his throat was too dry to make a sound. He wanted to stand up, but the iron chain pulled him back to reality.

Fourth day, his body began to betray him.

That wasn't because of weak will. That was pure physiological reaction. When the fox beastkin once again poured food on the ground, Yan Shen's stomach let out a huge, impossible-to-ignore rumble. That sound echoed in the quiet basement, like a declaration of shame.

The fox beastkin laughed.

"...Looks like you finally understand." he said, "In this place, there are no 'people,' only 'alive' and 'dead.' If you want to live, you have to learn to be a good dog."

Yan Shen stared at the food on the ground. That lump of grayish-white paste had already cooled, a thin film forming on the surface. His stomach was twitching, screaming, demanding he swallow that thing.

His fingers dug out blood marks on the cement floor.

Then, he lowered his head.

Not lowering all at once. But slowly, bit by bit, like performing some ancient, humiliating ritual. His nose tip touched that lump of food; the sour smell shot straight into his nasal cavity. His stomach spasmed at that instant, but he forced himself to open his mouth.

First mouthful.

That thing tasted even worse than he imagined. Sour, rancid, bitter, and an indescribable metallic taste. Yan Shen's throat instinctively wanted to spit it out, but he forced himself to swallow it. That lump of paste slid down his dry esophagus, bringing a near-painful burning sensation.

Second mouthful.

Third mouthful.

The fox beastkin stood beside, watching him. There was no mockery in his eyes, no satisfaction, only a calm, nearly research-like focus, as if observing an experimental subject.

"...Very good." When Yan Shen finally licked the ground clean, the fox beastkin said. His voice carried a praise; that praise was more cruel than any mockery.

"...From today on," he said, "you eat every meal like this. Understand?"

Yan Shen didn't answer. He just lay on the ground, forehead pressed against the cold cement floor, white tiger whiskers still stained with food residue. His body was trembling, not from cold, but from a huge, unspeakable shame.

He cried.

Not crying out loud. But silently, tears sliding from tightly closed corners of his eyes, dripping on the cement floor, mixing with food residue. He didn't make any sound; only his shoulders trembled slightly, like an old beast dying alone in the darkness.

The fox beastkin turned and left.

---

Fifth day, the conditioning began.

That wasn't beating. Beating was only the means, not the goal. The goal was to make Yan Shen understand—in this basement, before these people, he wasn't "Yan Shen," wasn't "former Beastshield employee," wasn't "Yan Sui's father." He was merely an object that could be ordered, punished, and domesticated.

"...Come here." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen knelt in a corner of the room; the iron chain's length allowed him to move within approximately two meters. He heard the command, but his body didn't move. His brain made a judgment in that instant: cannot move. Cannot obey. Once he obeyed the first time, there would be a second, a third... until he completely became what they wanted.

The fox beastkin sighed.

He walked up, kicking Yan Shen's abdomen. That kick wasn't heavy, but it hit exactly the stomach's position. The food Yan Shen ate yesterday surged up at that instant; sour, foul vomit sprayed from his mouth, splashing on the cement floor.

"...Come here." The fox beastkin repeated.

Yan Shen didn't move.

Another kick.

Another kick.

By the fifth kick, Yan Shen couldn't vomit anything anymore. His stomach was spasming, twitching, like a balloon being squeezed. His forehead pressed against his own vomit; white tiger whiskers were stained with sour, foul liquid.

"...Come here." The fox beastkin's voice was still calm.

Yan Shen's knees moved forward an inch.

That inch of movement was more painful to him than any beating. Because he knew, that was the sound of his dignity as "Yan Shen" shattering.

"...Good boy." the fox beastkin said.

He squatted down, reaching out to touch Yan Shen's head. That wasn't an action expressing goodwill—that was an owner's action toward a pet, carrying a condescending,施舍-like pity.

Yan Shen's tiger ears flattened backward at that instant. Not from anger, but from something deeper—something he couldn't name, but more terrible than anger.

---

Seventh day.

Yan Shen no longer remembered how long he had stayed in this basement. Time had lost meaning. His world was left with only a few things: the coldness of the iron chain, the strangulation marks of the collar, the roughness of the cement floor, the sour smell of food, and the fox beastkin's footsteps.

His body was emaciating. Ribs were clearly visible beneath the fur, abdominal muscles atrophied from hunger, leaving only a loose skin. His left leg became even more stiff from prolonged kneeling; the old wound at his knee was swollen like a fist, presenting an unhealthy purplish-red.

But his body was also changing.

That change wasn't visible to the naked eye, but deeper. When he heard footsteps on the stairs, his heart would start racing before his brain could react. When the fox beastkin's voice rang out, his body would tremble before his consciousness could judge. When words like "lie down," "come here," "kneel" echoed in the air, his limbs would react before he told himself "don't obey."

That was a conditioned reflex. A survival instinct the body learned on its own under repeated punishment and pain stimulation.

Late night of the seventh day, there was only Yan Shen alone in the basement.

The light was out. Darkness flooded in like tidewater, drowning his senses. He curled up within the range the iron chain allowed, back against the cold cement wall, knees hugged to his chest.

His consciousness floated. In the half-dream, half-awake state, he saw Suisui.

Not the present Suisui. The childhood Suisui. That five-year-old little tiger, lying on his back, fluffy tail wrapped around his waist, small hands encircling his neck, saying something in his ear. He couldn't hear clearly, but he knew it must be sweet, warm words.

"...Dad..." Suisui's voice came from very far away.

Yan Shen opened his eyes in the darkness.

That wasn't a dream. That was his own voice. He had unconsciously called out that name.

His tears flowed down again. But this time not from shame. But from something deeper—something he realized he was losing.

He recalled before going out, Suisui said "I have something I want to tell you."

He recalled himself saying "Wait until Dad comes back to talk."

He recalled that stack of communication coupons in the drawer. "With this coupon, Dad will definitely talk properly with Suisui no matter what."

He couldn't go back.

Not because he didn't want to go back. But because—his body knew, even if he walked out this door now, even if he returned to that home, he could no longer be Suisui's father. A beastkin who would automatically kneel upon hearing a command, a beastkin who would lick food from the ground, a beastkin who couldn't even control his own bladder—

How could he still be a father?

Yan Shen buried his face in his knees. His body was trembling, like a withered leaf in the cold wind. The iron chain made slight clashing sounds with his trembling; that sound was especially harsh in the silence, as if mocking his weakness.

"...Suisui..." his voice was so light it was almost inaudible, like a wisp of white mist about to dissipate, "...Dad can't go back..."

There was no response in the darkness.

Only the slight clashing sound of the iron chain, and his own suppressed, broken breathing, echoing in the basement, like a wordless elegy.
