# Chapter 6 Homeward Journey

When Yi An hoisted Yan Shen onto his back, the snow was falling even harder.

Not that gentle, drifting snow. It was dense, heavy, like the sky was dumping crushed glass. Snowflakes hit the face, not melting, caked into a layer of ice shell by body heat, clinging tightly to the fur.

Yi An walked step by step forward.

Yan Shen's body lay across his back, chin resting on his right shoulder. That military-green old uniform in the snowy night presented a deep, nearly black dark green. Yi An's hand supported Yan Shen's leg bend—or rather, supported where the leg bend should have been. It was too thin there, thin to nothing but bone; even through the uniform fabric one could feel the protruding joints.

The left leg dragged behind.

With every step, that left leg swayed once, boot sole dragging a shallow groove in the snow. Sometimes the boot sole would catch on an ice chip, and Yi An would have to stop, bend down to pull that foot free, then continue. That foot was already ice-cold; even through the boot one could feel that stiff, not-belonging-to-a-living-person chill.

Yi An didn't cry.

His tears had already dried when Yan Shen died. Now his eyes were dry, swollen, like two dry wells with the water burned away. He only carried Yan Shen, step by step, walking in the snow.

Passing that streetlamp broken for five years, Yi An stopped a moment.

Under the lamp, Yan Shen's cane was still tilted in the snow. Lonely, like a grave without a tombstone. Yi An walked over, pulled the cane out, holding it in his hand. The pagoda wood grip was moistened by snow, surface smooth like a warm stone.

He switched the cane to his other hand, then continued walking.

One end of the cane dragged in the snow, together with Yan Shen's left leg, drawing two parallel trails on the snow surface. One deep, one shallow. One of wood, one of flesh.

The way home wasn't long. But Yi An felt he'd walked very long.

He carried Yan Shen, past the corner of West District's back alley, past that always drafty iron door, past the porch light Suisui always forgot to turn off. The porch light was still on, its dim yellow glow casting a small circle of warm halo in the snowy night. Yi An stood in the halo, looking at that familiar door, stopped for a long time.

He didn't have a key.

But he knew Yan Shen's habit of hiding a spare key under the doormat—many years ago Yan Shen had told him, saying in case Suisui forgot his key, he could still get in. Yi An bent down, lifted the doormat, and indeed felt a cold copper key.

His hand trembled. The key missed the keyhole three times before aligning.

The door opened.

The house was very quiet. The residual heat of noodle soup still lingered in the air, carrying a faint scent of sesame oil. The bedroom door was closed; from inside came Suisui's breathing—heavy, long, carrying the slightly blocked snuffle unique to the injured.

Yi An gently placed Yan Shen on the living room sofa.

The sofa springs made a protesting creak. Yan Shen's body sank into the cushions, head tilted to one side, gray-and-white fur under the dim yellow light presenting a soft, old-photo-like tone. His expression was very calm; the corner of his mouth even carried a trace of very faint, like relief, arc.

Yi An squatted down, looking at Yan Shen's face.

He looked for a long time. Like memorizing every detail, like taking a final photograph with his eyes. Then he stood up, found a clean old scarf from the cabinet—gray, woolen, edges somewhat pilling, but washed clean. That was the scarf Yan Shen used to wear in winter.

Yi An wrapped the scarf round and round around Yan Shen's neck.

The scarf covered the strangulation marks left by the collar. Those deep purple, flesh-turned-outside, brand-like strangulation marks were hidden under the soft gray wool. Yi An wrapped very carefully, neither tight nor loose, just enough to cover the wounds without making Yan Shen look like he'd been deliberately bandaged.

"...Going home needs to be proper." Yi An said. Voice very light, like afraid of startling something.

Then he brought a basin of warm water, soaked the towel, wrung it dry, and started wiping Yan Shen's face.

From the forehead down, to the eye corners, to the cheekbones, to the chin. Movements very light, like wiping a precious porcelain. He wiped away the dried bloodstains and dirt on Yan Shen's face bit by bit, revealing the pale skin beneath. Then he used his finger dipped in a little water, smoothing the white tiger whiskers one by one. The broken whiskers he couldn't fix; he could only smooth those broken ends, making them look less jarring.

Next, he helped Yan Shen straighten his uniform. Buttons re-buttoned, collar turned out, shoulder patch straightened. The military-green uniform under the light looked solemn and steady, the beast claw and shield emblem quietly lying on the shoulder, like some silent guardianship.

Finally, Yi An fished out a small paper packet from his inner pocket—that he'd found in Yan Shen's pocket on the way here. Styptic powder. The crumpled paper packet still had Yan Shen's palm sweat stains, softened by body heat.

Yi An placed the styptic powder on the coffee table.

Then he took out a piece of paper and a pen from his pocket. The paper was torn from his notebook, edges uneven, with rough fibers. The pen was a ballpoint pen uniformly issued by Beastshield, the cap cracked.

He sat on the sofa, under the dim yellow light, and started writing.

His hand trembled, so the writing was very slow, stroke by stroke, like a child just learning to write.

"Suisui. I am your father's junior. Your father has passed. Died of illness. He passed very—"

The pen tip stopped there.

Yi An looked at the last word. "Peacefully."

He thought of Yan Shen's final appearance. Body suddenly going rigid, then falling back onto the bed. That kind of death, could it be called peaceful? The semen gauge indeed didn't hurt, but that death posture—that posture of being taken to death by his own instinctive reaction—could that be called peaceful?

Yi An used the pen to cross out the last word.

One stroke, two strokes, three strokes. Black ink smeared the word into a blurry blot, like a small, black wound.

He didn't write any other word. Just let that sentence stop there, with a crossed-out tail, like an unfinished farewell.

Yi An folded the note, placed it on the coffee table, under the styptic powder packet. Then he fished out something from his贴身 pocket—a stack of crumpled paper slips, edges rubbed fuzzy, with Yan Shen's handwriting, crooked but every stroke very serious: "Communication voucher. No matter what, I will definitely talk properly with you."

Yi An placed the communication vouchers under the note.

Underneath. Not on top. Letting Suisui see the note first, then the communication vouchers. Or first seeing the bad news, then seeing the promise his father left. Yi An didn't know which was more cruel. He only felt that the communication vouchers should be with Yan Shen, even in this way.

He stood up, looking at the bedroom door.

Suisui's breathing was still there. Very deep, very steady, like knowing nothing.

Yi An walked to the bedroom door, hand gripping the doorknob. The coolness of metal seeped into his palm. He gently, bit by bit, pushed the door open a crack.

The bedroom was very dark. Only the reflection of snow outside the window, barely illuminating the orange-yellow figure on the bed. Yan Sui lay on his side on the bed, tail poking out from under the quilt, trembling slightly with each breath. His brows were still slightly furrowed even in sleep, but the corner of his mouth was relaxed, carrying a childlike, unguarded softness.

He slept very deeply.

Yi An stood in the doorway, looking at Yan Sui.

He looked at this twenty-five-year-old young man, at his healthy fur, at his solid muscles, at his currently unknowing appearance. He knew that in a few hours, this young man would wake up, would walk out of the bedroom, would see his father on the living room sofa. He would learn from a note, a packet of styptic powder, and a stack of communication vouchers, that the person closest to him in this world was already gone.

Yi An gripped the doorknob, not turning it.

He stood in the darkness, looking at Yan Sui's sleeping face, for a long time. His hand tightened on the doorknob, then loosened, then tightened. His lips trembled, like wanting to say something, but ultimately said nothing.

Let him sleep a while longer.

Even if just one more hour. In this hour, he still has a father.

Yi An gently closed the door.

He walked back to the living room, took one last look at Yan Shen on the sofa. Yan Shen lay there, wearing the old uniform, wrapped in the gray scarf, hands crossed over his abdomen, expression calm. The firelight jumped on the wall, casting his shadow very long, like a guarding ghost.

Yi An picked up the cane from the floor, leaning it against the sofa armrest—by Yan Shen's hand, like before.

Then he turned, walking toward the door.

He didn't look back again.

The door closed gently behind him. The sound of the lock clicking was very light, almost swallowed by the wind and snow.

Yi An stood under the porch light, snowflakes falling on his head, on his shoulders, on his dark brown fur. His ears drooped, tail tucked invisible. He looked at that closed door, one last look.

Then he walked into the snow, into White Capital's deepest, blackest night.

On the snow, besides his own footprints, there were two drag marks. One was the cane's, one was Yan Shen's left leg—left when he carried Yan Shen here. Those two trails extended parallel in the snowy night, then were covered bit by bit by newly falling snowflakes.

Until nothing could be seen anymore.
