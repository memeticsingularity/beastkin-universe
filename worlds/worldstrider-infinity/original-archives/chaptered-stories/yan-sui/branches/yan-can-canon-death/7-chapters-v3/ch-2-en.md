# Chapter 2 Beating and Abduction

The streetlamp at the corner had been broken for five years, the lampshade filled with coal ash and bird carcasses.

When Yan Shen walked beneath it, his shadow was cast on the ground by nonexistent light, twisting into a lame-legged monster. He didn't stop. Cane probing forward, tapping ground, weight shifting right, left leg dragging over. One step. Another.

Thud. Thud.

The set of paw prints following him also stopped. Then resumed, rhythm offset by half a beat, like a fox prowling through snow.

Yan Shen knew someone was following. He'd known for a while. A veteran's intuition didn't need to turn around to confirm—the hostility in the air was too thick, thick enough to choke. But he didn't speed up. Speed was useless; his left leg couldn't outrun any able-bodied beastkin. He only gripped his cane tighter, knuckles pressing against the pagoda wood grain, like holding a knife still in its sheath.

The alley split into two up ahead. Left led to the main street, with streetlamps, patrols, Beastshield's eyes. Right led to the factory district, abandoned warehouses, rusted railway tracks, and White Capital's deepest, blackest night.

Yan Shen turned left.

He'd only taken two steps when the first dark mass darted out from the right alley. Then a second, a third. They blocked the alley mouth like stones growing out from the wall.

Yan Shen stopped.

He slowly turned around, cane horizontal before him. Five men stood before him; the leader wore a dusty cotton-padded jacket, ears sticking out from under the brim of his hat—fox beastkin. The one who'd led the daytime beating of Suisui in the snow. The malicious smile still hung at his lips, only now thicker, cloyingly thick.

"Former Beastshield's Yan Shen?" The fox beastkin tilted his head, voice sharp and thin, like sandpaper scraping tin, "Old thing, legs aren't convenient, but ears are sharp enough."

Yan Shen didn't answer. His gaze swept across the five faces. Three fox beastkin, two civilians, all young males, eyes burning with something Yan Shen knew well—not hatred, but venting. The pathological venting bred by White Capital's environment. They chewed hatred for Beastshield, swallowed it, then spat it out on someone weaker than themselves.

"Move aside." Yan Shen said. Voice not loud, but every word clear.

The fox beastkin laughed. He walked forward two steps, boot soles crunching on snow. He pulled something from his chest, waving it before Yan Shen's eyes.

It was a shoulder patch. Beastshield green uniform's shoulder patch. Bloodstained.

"Something your son dropped." The fox beastkin said, "I picked it up. Thought... a father should come collect it, right?"

Yan Shen's pupils contracted. Only once. His expression didn't change, nor did his hand holding the cane tremble. But if there were light, you could see the small patch of gray-white fur at the base of his white tiger whiskers, trembling slightly.

"...Put it down." Yan Shen said.

"Put it down?" The fox beastkin threw the patch into the snow, then stomped on it, grinding, "Beastshield scum, worthy of it?"

Yan Shen moved.

He didn't throw his cane like in daytime. A cane was for walking, not fighting—at least not tonight. He tucked the cane under his armpit, right leg pushing off the ground, his whole body pouncing like an aged tiger. Right fist straight toward the fox beastkin's face, carrying a veteran's precision, carrying a father's disregard for everything.

The fox beastkin hadn't expected a lame old man could still have such explosive power. He jerked back hastily; the fist scraped past his nose, the wind pressure making him squint. But the man behind him moved. A sturdy civilian slammed into Yan Shen's right ribs from the side, shoulder striking hard.

Yan Shen's body was knocked tilting to one side. He instinctively tried to brace with his left leg, but the left leg couldn't keep up. The old wound at his knee made an ominous dull sound in that instant, like bone dislocating in the joint. His left leg buckled, body crashing heavily against the brick wall beside him.

Brick fragments embedded into his fur, drawing a string of fine blood beads.

"Old thing!" The fox beastkin rubbed his nose, cursing viciously, "Let you show off in daytime, really think you're still Beastshield's soldier?"

Yan Shen slid down the wall, kneeling on one knee. He used his cane to brace against the ground, trying to stand, but his left leg wouldn't obey. That impact had ruptured the old wound in his knee; he could feel warm fluid spreading in the joint cavity—not blood, but accumulated fluid leaking out, or something else. Pain. Bone-piercing pain. But he had no time for pain.

The second fist had already arrived.

Yan Shen dodged sideways; the fist smashed into the wall, brick chips flying. He took the chance to strike the man's shinbone hard with the cane grip, precise pressure point attack making the other scream, hugging his leg and hopping away. But a third person pounced from behind, two arms wrapping around Yan Shen's shoulders, tearing him from the wall.

Yan Shen's back hit the snow. Cold. Bone-piercing cold. Snow poured into his collar, melting, then evaporating by body heat, taking away more warmth. He struggled to roll over, but that man was riding his back, knee pressing into his lower back, pinning him firmly to the ground.

"Hold him down!" The fox beastkin shouted.

Two more pounced. One pinned Yan Shen's right arm; another went to snatch his cane. Yan Shen's fingers gripped the cane deathly tight, knuckles white, tiger's mouth splitting, blood flowing down the shaft. But the other was young, strong, prying his fingers open bit by bit.

The cane was snatched away, thrown aside, rolling twice in the snow before stopping.

Yan Shen's right fist smashed into the face of the man pinning his arm. The sound of breaking teeth. That man screamed and retreated, covering his mouth, blood gushing from between his fingers. But at the same time, the fox beastkin walked over, lifted his foot, aimed at Yan Shen's left knee—that knee deformed for over a decade—and stomped down with all his weight.

That kick used his entire body weight.

Yan Shen didn't shout.

A sound came from his throat... not a shout, not a whimper, something deeper, more primal. Like bones screaming, like nerves burning. His body arched violently, like a bow drawn to its limit, then crashed back into the snow. His eyes opened wide, amber pupils dilating in the dim light, focus scattering, seeing nothing.

Pain. Too much pain. So much pain he couldn't breathe, couldn't think, couldn't perceive his own existence.

"...Again." The fox beastkin said, voice carrying a morbid excitement, "Old thing's pretty tough."

The second kick landed on his abdomen. Yan Shen's body folded; stomach contents surged to his throat, forcibly swallowed back down. The third punch hit his jaw; his head snapped back, back of skull hitting the frozen ground, golden stars exploding before his eyes. Fourth kick, fifth punch, sixth strike—they circled like beasts around a bonfire, taking turns, pouring all the humiliation and anger accumulated in daytime onto this old beastkin who couldn't fight back.

Yan Shen curled up. Arms protecting his head, knees tucked toward his abdomen. That was the last thing he could do. He felt his ribs vibrating, possibly cracked, possibly not. He felt his left face swelling, eye blinded by blood, vision reduced to a blur of dark red. He felt his left leg no longer belonged to him, like a broken wooden stick, dragging in the snow, swaying unconsciously with each kick.

But he didn't beg for mercy.

Not a single word.

He just... was thinking.

Suisui. Suisui was still at home. Suisui was still sleeping. Suisui didn't know he'd gone out. Suisui didn't know he was here. Suisui would wake up tomorrow morning, thinking it was just an ordinary outing. Suisui would wait. Suisui would wait until noon, until evening, until the next day. Suisui would think he'd run into some trouble, would go out to search. Suisui would worry. Suisui would...

"...Stop."

A voice interrupted the fox beastkin's raised hand.

Yan Shen already couldn't see clearly. His right eye was completely swollen shut; his left could only make out blurry outlines. But he recognized that voice—another fox beastkin, younger, standing at the edge of the group, voice carrying a trace of... uncertainty.

"...Beat him more and he'll die." That young voice said.

"Dead is dead." The lead fox beastkin panted, "Beastshield scum, one less is one less."

"No." That young voice came closer, "Dead... no fun."

Snow fell on Yan Shen's face, melting, mixing with blood, flowing into his ears. He heard brief whispers between the fox beastkin, like discussing something. He couldn't hear clearly. His consciousness was drifting, like a fallen leaf, tossed by wind, up and down.

Then, a hand grabbed his hair, lifting his head from the snow.

The fox beastkin's face drew close. Yan Shen could only see a blurry, sneering outline.

"Old thing," the fox beastkin said, breath spraying on his face, carrying the stink of smoke and garlic, "You were pretty impressive in daytime. Hitting people with a cane? Scaring us?"

Yan Shen's lips moved. Blood flowed from the corner of his mouth, dripping onto the snow.

"...You..." his voice was like sandpaper grinding a rusty iron pipe, "...come at me... don't touch... Suisui..."

The fox beastkin laughed. He released Yan Shen's hair, letting his head fall back into the snow. Then he kicked Yan Shen's completely numb left leg with his boot, like kicking a rotten log.

"Take him away." He said.

"Where?" Someone asked.

"The usual place." The fox beastkin said, "West district warehouse. The underground level."

"This old thing... why not just finish him?"

The fox beastkin squatted down, poking Yan Shen's swollen cheek with his finger. Yan Shen didn't react. He no longer had strength to react.

"Finish him?" The fox beastkin's voice became very light, carrying something viscous that made Yan Shen's nape go cold, "What a waste. Former Beastshield... Golden Age veteran... letting him live like a dog, isn't that more interesting?"

Yan Shen's body trembled. Not from cold. Because of something in those words—something he couldn't immediately understand, but instinctively feared.

Then he was dragged up.

Two people hoisted his arms, dragging him forward like a bag of garbage. His left leg dragged on the snow, everything below the knee completely unresponsive, boot sole plowing a deep groove on the snow surface. His head hung against his chest, gray-and-white fur caked with blood and mud, white tiger whiskers glued together by blood, clinging to his jaw in strands.

They dragged him, walking the path he'd just walked. Past the streetlamp broken for five years. Past that fork. Then turned right—toward the factory district, toward abandoned warehouses, toward White Capital's deepest, blackest night.

The cane was left in the snow. Lonely, tilted, stuck in a pile of dirty snow, like a grave without a tombstone.

In the last moment before losing consciousness, only one sentence remained in Yan Shen's mind.

Suisui was still at home waiting for him.

(End of Chapter 2)
