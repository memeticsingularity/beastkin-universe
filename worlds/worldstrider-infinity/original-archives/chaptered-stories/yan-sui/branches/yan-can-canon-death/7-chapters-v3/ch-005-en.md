# Chapter 5 Plea for Death

Yi An's place was very small.

A bed, a table, a chair, a coal-burning stove. On the wall hung a neatly folded blue uniform, the shoulder patch gleaming with cold, hard blue under the stove fire's light. Besides that, almost no other furniture. The apartments Beastshield assigned to blue uniforms were all like this—clean, practical, impersonal.

Yan Shen lay on the bed.

The bedsheet was white, washed stiff but clean. His body was wrapped in a thick quilt, only his head and one hand exposed. That hand rested at the edge of the quilt, fingers slightly curled, knuckles protruding, skin pale enough to see the blue veins beneath.

Yi An sat on the chair by the bed, body leaning forward, elbows on knees, fingers interlaced against his chin. He hadn't lit the lamp, only relying on the stove fire for light. The firelight cast his shadow on the wall, stretched long, swaying.

How long had they sat like this?

Maybe an hour, maybe two. No one spoke. Occasionally the coal blocks in the stove made a slight cracking sound, sparks splashing on the iron stove wall with fine noises.

Yan Shen's eyes were open. He looked at the ceiling, gaze unfocused, like having no focal point, or like looking at some very distant place. His breathing was very light, very shallow; every inhale brought pain to his ribs—there were internal injuries from stick beatings there; breathing deep hurt.

Yi An was watching him. Watching that circle of deep purple strangulation marks on his neck, watching the broken skin and congealed blood scabs at the edge of the marks. Watching the bluish-purple bruise on his cheekbone. Watching the dried dirt stuck to his white tiger whiskers. Watching his posture lying on the clean bed—that wasn't a relaxed posture; it was curled, defensive, a posture ready to receive blows at any moment.

Yi An's ears drooped, pressed against the sides of his head. Tail tucked very tight.

"...Senior." He finally spoke. Voice hoarse, like he hadn't drunk water for a long time.

Yan Shen's eyes moved. He didn't turn his head, only his eyeballs shifted slightly toward Yi An's direction.

"...Shall I get you some water?" Yi An asked.

Yan Shen didn't answer.

Yi An stood up, walked to the table, picked up the kettle, poured a cup of water. The water was warm, taken from the stove. He walked back to the bedside with the cup, bringing it to Yan Shen's lips.

Yan Shen's lips moved, but didn't open.

"...Drink a little." Yi An's voice was very light, carrying a nearly pleading tone, "You have a fever."

Yan Shen slowly turned his head, looking at Yi An. His gaze stayed on Yi An's face for a long time, like recognizing, like confirming whether this person was real. Then, his lips opened.

Yi An tilted the cup; water flowed into Yan Shen's mouth. Yan Shen swallowed once, his Adam's apple rolling above the collar's strangulation mark. Then he turned his head away, no longer drinking.

Yi An put the cup back on the table, sitting back in the chair.

More silence.

The fire in the stove was slowly dimming. Yi An picked up the iron tongs, clamped a piece of coal, added it to the stove. The firelight brightened again, illuminating his dark brown profile, illuminating his tightly furrowed brows.

"...Little Bo." Yan Shen suddenly said.

Yi An's body went rigid. His hand still held the tongs, suspended in midair.

"...Senior?" He turned his head, looking at Yan Shen.

Yan Shen's eyes looked at him. Those amber eyes in the firelight presented a transparent, glass-like quality. No anger, no sadness, no fear. Only a kind of... emptiness. That emptiness of a person who had already hollowed himself out completely.

"...Kill me." Yan Shen said.

Voice not loud. Even very light. But every word was like a stone, smashing onto Yi An's eardrums, onto his heart, onto the softest place in his chest.

Yi An's hand trembled. The tongs fell to the ground, making a crisp metallic clatter.

"...What did you say?" Yi An's voice was also trembling.

"Kill me." Yan Shen repeated. Tone unchanged, as casual as saying "get me a cup of water."

Yi An stood up, sat down, stood up again. His body swayed above the chair, like a tree blown by wind, at a loss. His ears pressed tighter, tail tucked almost invisible.

"...No." Yi An said. He spoke very fast, like preventing himself from thinking, "...No, Senior. You can hold on. Your injuries... injuries will heal. I'll find a doctor tomorrow, there are military doctors I know in Beastshield, they..."

"Injuries can heal." Yan Shen said. He interrupted Yi An, voice still calm, "Some things can't heal."

Yi An fell silent.

Yan Shen slowly pulled his right hand from under the quilt. His movement was very slow, like every joint hurting. He raised his hand before his eyes, looking at that bony hand, at the abraded paw pads and blunt nails.

"...In my present state," he said, "I can't go back to see Suisui."

His fingers slightly curled, like wanting to grasp something, but there was nothing in his hand.

"...Suisui is only twenty-five." Yan Shen continued, voice light like talking to himself, "He's still young. He still has a long road ahead. He can't... he can't have a father who was chained, knelt, crawled on the ground licking basins."

Yi An's lips trembled. He wanted to say something, but his throat seemed blocked by something, no sound coming out.

"...He'll be pointed at." Yan Shen said, "Will be laughed at. People will say 'look, that's Yan Shen's son, his dad was domesticated like a dog.' Suisui already has it hard in Beastshield... I can't add this kind of stain to him..."

"It's not your fault!" Yi An suddenly shouted. His voice was very loud, echoing in the small room, startling himself. He panted heavily, eyes opened wide, "...It's those bastards' fault! Those animals! You didn't do anything wrong!"

Yan Shen looked at Yi An. His expression didn't change, but a trace of something like sorrow flashed in his eyes.

"...I know." He said, "I know it's not my fault."

He put his hand back under the quilt, looking at the ceiling again.

"...But Suisui doesn't know. Suisui won't know. He'll only know that his father was chained like a dog for two weeks. He'll know his father lay down at a whistle. He'll know his father wagged his tail."

Yan Shen's voice paused. His Adam's apple rolled, like swallowing something bitter.

"...I can't let him know." He said, "I can't let him see."

Yi An's hand clenched into a fist. His knuckles white, palm dug with deep crescents by his nails. His breathing became very heavy, every inhale carrying a snuffling sound from his nasal cavity.

"...So you want me to kill you?" Yi An's voice dropped, squeezed from between his teeth, "...With my own hands?"

"Mm." Yan Shen said.

"...How do you expect me to live after this?" Yi An asked. His eyes reddened, but no tears. Tears swirled in his eye sockets, but didn't fall. "...You want me to think of this night every time I think of you? To think of me... me..."

He couldn't continue.

Yan Shen turned his head, looking at Yi An. His gaze was very calm, that calm of someone who had finally reached some endpoint after experiencing everything.

"...Little Bo." He said, "When you got promoted to blue uniform, the people below wouldn't accept you. You came to me crying, saying you didn't want to do it anymore. Do you remember what I said?"

Yi An's shoulders trembled.

"...You said," his voice was very light, "...'Little Bo, if you don't do it, that position goes to the troublemaker. If the troublemaker gets promoted, everyone in the team suffers. Don't think for yourself, think for the brothers.'"

"Mm." Yan Shen said, "I also said back then, you're good material, just impatient. I said I'd teach you slowly, polish you into shape."

Yi An's tears finally fell. One drop, two drops, landing on his dark brown hand back, splashing tiny water droplets.

"...You taught me for six years." Yi An said, "Six years. Every time I caused trouble, you cleaned up after me. Every time I wanted to give up, you handed me a cigarette, saying 'think slowly, no rush.' I... I owe you, I can't repay it in this lifetime."

"You don't owe me." Yan Shen said, "I helped you because you were worth helping."

"Then you want me to kill you now?" Yi An raised his head, looking at Yan Shen, tears streaming down his face, "...This is the last lesson you teach me?"

Yan Shen was silent for a while.

"...Yes." He said.

Yi An looked at him, for a long time. The stove firelight jumped on Yan Shen's face, illuminating his thin cheekbones, illuminating his gray-and-white fur, illuminating that bottomless exhaustion in his eyes.

Yi An slowly sat back in the chair. His body seemed to have the bones sucked out, softly sinking into the chair. His hand covered his face, shoulders trembling. No sound, only shoulder shaking, and occasional suppressed breathy sounds leaking from between his fingers.

Yan Shen didn't look at him. He looked at the ceiling again, waiting.

Time passed second by second. Coal blocks burned in the stove, making slight crackling sounds. Snow still fell outside the window; wind drilled in through window cracks, carrying White Capital's characteristic decaying smell.

Yi An finally raised his head.

His eyes were very red, but tears had dried. His expression changed—from pain to something harder, heavier. Like having made some resolution.

"...The one in the interrogation room." Yi An said. His voice was very hoarse, but every word was very clear, "...The least painful. Everyone in Beastshield knows. The semen gauge. Damage transfers over, when it's full... the person goes. No pain."

Yan Shen's eyes moved. He turned his head, looking at Yi An.

"...Alright." He said.

Yi An stood up, walked to the bedside. His hand was trembling. He tried to control it, but couldn't. He pulled out an old uniform from the box under the bed—Beastshield Golden Age's military-green uniform, shoulder patches bearing the beast claw and shield emblem. That was the uniform Yan Shen wore back then; Yi An had always kept it, folded neatly, in a camphor wood box.

"...I'll help you change." Yi An said.

Yan Shen didn't object. He only slowly sat up, letting Yi An help him take off that oversized coat. His movements were very slow, very stiff, like a rusted machine. When Yi An's hand touched his skin, his body stiffened slightly, but didn't dodge.

Yi An used warm water to wipe Yan Shen's body. Movements very light, like wiping a precious porcelain. He avoided the most severe wounds, only lightly wiping away dirt and blood scabs. Yan Shen closed his eyes, letting him manipulate. His face had no expression, but fingers curled slightly under the quilt.

Then, Yi An helped him put on that old uniform.

The military-green uniform on Yan Shen's thin body looked loose. But that looseness had a strange solemnity, like a too-light shell being fitted into too-heavy armor. Yi An helped him button up, straighten the collar, then smoothed the white tiger whiskers one by one.

"...You look most proper wearing this," Yi An said, voice very light.

Yan Shen opened his eyes, lowering his head to look at himself. His fingers traced over the shoulder patch patterns, movements very slow, carrying a nostalgic warmth.

"...Haven't worn it in a long time." He said.

Yi An fished out two cigarettes from the drawer. Crumpled, some tobacco leaked out. He handed one to Yan Shen, put one in his own mouth.

"...Light?" Yi An asked.

Yan Shen shook his head. "...Haven't smoked in a long time. Afraid Suisui would smell it."

Yi An held the two cigarettes together, lighting them with the stove fire. He handed one to Yan Shen's lips. Yan Shen held it, took a drag, then slowly exhaled. Smoke curled in the firelight, carrying a bitter, reassuring scent.

Yi An also took a drag. The two of them sat like this by the bedside, sharing that bit of firelight, puffing cigarettes one after another. No talking. Smoke rose and dispersed between them, like some silent farewell.

The cigarette was finished. Yi An pressed the butt out in the stove ash.

"...Lie down, Senior." Yi An said.

Yan Shen lay down. His body flattened under the quilt, hands crossed over his abdomen, posture proper, like waiting for inspection. His eyes looked at Yi An, gaze calm, even carrying a trace of apology.

"...Sorry." Yan Shen said.

"...Don't say that." Yi An's voice was trembling. He sat on the edge of the bed, hand hovering above the quilt, hovering for a long time. His fingers trembled, trembling very badly, like countless needles pricking his nerves.

"...Little Bo." Yan Shen said.

"...Mm."

"...Next life." Yan Shen's eyes looked at the ceiling, voice light like a sigh, "...Next life let me still be your senior."

Yi An's tears welled up again. He bit his lip, forcing it until his lips went pale. His hand finally came down, reaching under the quilt.

Yan Shen's body stiffened slightly. Then relaxed.

Yi An's hand trembled. He pressed his palm against Yan Shen's chest, feeling that heartbeat. Fast. Chaotic. Like making a final struggle. He counted the pulse—one-twenty, one-thirty, one-forty—that heart was accelerating, madly pounding in the chest, like a trapped beast.

Yi An's hand moved. Movement very light, carrying a nearly reverent caution. His eyes stared at Yan Shen's face, watching his expression, watching his breathing.

Yan Shen's eyes half-closed. His breathing became rapid, chest heaving violently. His body under the quilt slightly tensed, muscles contracting. But he didn't struggle. He only lay there, hands still crossed over his abdomen, posture proper, calm.

Yi An's hand trembled. He stopped, took a deep breath, then continued.

He counted the pulse. One-fifty, one-sixty... then suddenly became chaotic. No rhythm anymore. Yan Shen's body suddenly went rigid, like a bow drawn to its limit, head tilting back, a very short, very light sound coming from his throat—not a shout, but a sigh of relief.

Then, body fell back onto the bed.

Yi An's hand stopped.

He felt Yan Shen's heartbeat on his chest. From fast to chaotic, to... stopped.

Gone.

Nothing at all.

Yi An's hand still stayed there. His fingers slightly curled, palm pressed against a chest gradually cooling. His ears drooped, tail tucked invisible. His body trembled, trembling violently, like a machine that couldn't stop on a cold night.

He slowly, slowly, pulled the quilt up, covering to Yan Shen's chin.

Then, he bent down, forehead pressed against the edge of the bed. His shoulders trembled, but no sound. No crying out, no wailing, only a kind of极致压缩的、从胸腔深处挤出来的气音.

Outside the window, snow still fell.

The fire in the stove slowly dimmed.

Yan Shen lay there, wearing the Golden Age old uniform, hands crossed over his abdomen, expression calm, even carrying a trace of relief. His white tiger whiskers gleamed with soft silver light in the stove fire's last glow, like covered with a thin layer of frost.

He was freed.
