# Chapter 1 Going Out

When blood seeped through the bandages, Yan Shen knew those punches had landed hard.

He sat on the edge of the bed, his left leg splayed to one side, the old wound at his knee throbbing with each beat of his heart in the winter night. In his right hand he held a roll of gauze; his left cradled Yan Sui's wrist. That wrist was thick, muscular, the strength of a twenty-five-year-old beastkin—and yet now it lay limp in his palm like a wooden stick with the marrow sucked out.

"...Don't move."

Yan Sui's brows furrowed, and a muffled whimper rolled from his throat. He didn't open his eyes, only instinctively tried to pull his arm back. Yan Shen tightened his grip slightly, not letting him break free.

"...Dad..."

"Mm."

"...It hurts..."

"I know." Yan Shen wound the gauze around, one loop, two loops, his hands as steady as when he'd bandaged comrades back in Beastshield, "Bear it."

Yan Sui fell silent. His breathing was heavy, clogged with blood in his nasal cavity, making a snuffling sound. His orange-yellow fur was caked with mud and snow, hardened into crusty scabs after being warmed by body heat. That dark-green uniform was torn at the left shoulder, exposing the bruised flesh beneath—Beastshield's green uniform. Yan Shen looked at that color, his eyes narrowing slightly.

Suisui had been in Beastshield for nearly two years. He'd signed up behind Yan Shen's back; by the time Yan Shen found out, his son had already brought home a brand-new uniform and stood at the doorway, head lowered, waiting to be scolded like a child who'd broken a bowl.

Yan Shen had said nothing at the time. He simply walked over, rubbed the fabric of the uniform between his fingers, then patted Yan Sui's shoulder.

"...Come back for dinner tonight."

That was the heaviest thing he could give.

Now that green uniform had a tear in it, the shoulder patch crooked, stiffened by blood. Yan Shen tied off the gauze, cut it, then picked up the bag of styptic powder from the bedside—Suisui had bought it for him last month, saying he was getting old, his legs weren't convenient, and they needed to keep medicine at home. And now it was being used on Suisui himself.

Ironic. But Yan Shen didn't smile. He simply sprinkled the powder on the gash at Yan Sui's temple, watching the wound twitch slightly under the sting.

Yan Sui's body tensed, teeth grinding.

"...Almost done." Yan Shen said. His voice wasn't loud, but Yan Sui's body loosened a little, strangely.

That was a habit formed since childhood. When Suisui was little, he was afraid of pain—he'd cry at shots, wail at falls. Later Yan Shen discovered that as long as he said "almost done," Suisui would believe it. Even knowing it would still hurt a while, he'd believe.

Now he was twenty-five, and still believed.

Yan Shen pressed the last piece of adhesive tape in place, then tucked Yan Sui's arm under the quilt. The quilt was an old cotton one, washed white but clean. He tucked the corner under Yan Sui's chin, his movements gentle.

Yan Sui's eyes opened. His right eye was swollen, only a slit remaining; his left could still open, the amber pupil looking unfocused under the dim yellow light.

"...Dad," his voice was hoarse, "...did they come back?"

"No." Yan Shen said, "They're gone."

"...They won't go." Yan Sui said. He spoke very lightly, but very clearly, "They... hate Beastshield. Hate all of Beastshield."

Yan Shen didn't answer. He stood up, his left leg dragging on the floor, making a slight scraping sound. That was bone and deformed joint grinding—he'd been used to that sound for over a decade. But Suisui had heard it for so many years, and every time still shifted his gaze over, looked once, then looked away.

Just like now.

Yan Shen pretended not to notice. He walked to the doorway, hung the blood-stained towel on the basin; the water had already turned pale red. He lifted the basin, walked out of the bedroom, and poured the water down the kitchen drain. The sound of flowing water was especially loud in the silent night.

He stood in the kitchen, not returning immediately.

Outside the window was White Capital's winter night. The snow had stopped, but the wind hadn't, drilling in through the window cracks, carrying a mixed smell of coal smoke and rust. In the distance dogs barked, quickly hushed by someone. Further away came the footsteps of Beastshield patrols—neat, heavy, a chilling rhythm.

Yan Shen knew Suisui was right. That group wouldn't leave. White Capital's hatred was alive, had teeth; it bit, and after biting it remembered the taste, followed the scent of blood to come back.

Tonight he'd happened to be there. Happened to hear the muffled sounds from the alley. Happened to recognize Suisui's green uniform reflecting in the snow. Happened to use his cane to shatter the first man's kneecap, then use Beastshield veteran's most ruthless methods to take down the second, the third, until the rest scattered cursing.

But happenstance doesn't always happen.

And this old man with a crippled left leg couldn't be there by happenstance every time.

Yan Shen put the basin back in place, then reached atop the cabinet for an iron box. The box was rusted, the lid stuck tight. He pried it open with his fingernails; inside was a stack of crumpled bills—half a year's savings for medicine, originally planned to buy Suisui a new pair of cotton boots next week.

He counted; not much. But enough.

He stuffed the money into his inner pocket, then picked up the old overcoat draped over the chair back. The coat was thick, but the cuffs were worn through, exposing the cotton batting inside. He put on the coat, then took the cane leaning by the door. The cane was pagoda wood, used for over a decade, the grip blackened and polished shiny by the sweat of his palm.

Thud.

The cane tapped the cement floor, a dull sound.

He walked back to the bedroom doorway, not entering, just standing in the frame, looking at Yan Sui on the bed. Yan Sui had fallen asleep again, brows still furrowed, but his breathing had steadied somewhat. His tail poked out from under the quilt, orange-yellow, the tip with a tuft of darker fur, trembling slightly with each breath.

Yan Shen stood in the doorway for a long time.

He remembered many years ago, when Suisui was just that tiny, also curled asleep on the bed like this, tail wrapped around his wrist like a warm scarf. Back then his leg hadn't gone bad yet; he could still carry Suisui on his back to see the snow on the back mountain. Suisui lay on his back, small arms circling his neck, fluffy tiger tail wrapped around his waist, warm and toasty.

How good it was back then.

But now wasn't the time to think of these things.

Yan Shen turned around, walking step by step toward the door. His left leg dragged on the floor, each step carrying that slight scraping sound. He opened the door; cold wind gusted in, making his gray-and-white fur quiver. He pulled the coat collar up higher, stepped over the threshold.

Behind him, the door closed gently.

---

The alley was narrow, the snow trampled into dirty gray ice chips. Yan Shen leaned on his cane, walking step by step. His shadow was stretched long by the streetlamp, then swallowed section by section by the darkness.

White Capital's night was alive. You could feel those eyes hidden behind windows, those gazes peeping through door cracks. When Yan Shen walked past, those gazes would stick to him, carrying a temperature mixed of fear and disgust.

Former Beastshield. Golden Age veteran. Now a cripple.

He knew what those gazes were saying. He'd heard them for over a decade, had long learned not to care.

But tonight, those gazes seemed a bit more than usual.

Yan Shen stopped at the corner. He didn't turn around, only tapped his cane on the ground, making a crisp sound. Then he fished a cigarette from his inner pocket—the last one, crumpled, half the tobacco leaked out. He didn't light it, only held it between his fingers, bringing it to his nose to smell.

The scent of tobacco relaxed him slightly.

Then he continued walking.

Yi An lived in an old apartment building two streets away. Bernese Mountain Dog, forty-six, blue uniform. Back then Yan Shen had personally picked this hot-blooded young man from the ranks, polished him bit by bit, then with several old comrades helped him up the ladder. Yi An would visit during festivals, bringing food, sitting a while, listening to him talk about the past. Yan Shen called him "Little Bo"; that kid called him "senior."

Not brothers. Senior and junior.

Yan Shen didn't want to disturb him. A retired veteran going to find an active blue uniform in the middle of the night didn't sound good. But tonight he had no other choice. That group wouldn't rest easy; Suisui was still young, Suisui still had a future. And this old man who could barely walk—he could still do one last thing for his son.

Even if it meant begging a junior he'd helped up the ladder himself.

The wind grew stronger. Yan Shen stuffed the cigarette back in his pocket, burying his face in the coat collar. His left leg hurt terribly in the cold wind, like someone hammering his kneecap blow by blow. But he didn't stop. He only probed forward with his cane, confirming the ground wasn't icy, then shifted his weight over.

Thud. Thud.

The sound of cane tapping ground echoed in the empty alley.

He didn't notice—or rather, he noticed but didn't care—about thirty meters behind him, a dark mass slid out from the shadow of the wall corner. That mass made no footsteps, only hugged the wall base, following him from afar.

On the snow, besides Yan Shen's limping footprints, there was now another set of shallow claw marks, nearly obscured by wind and snow.

(End of Chapter 1)
