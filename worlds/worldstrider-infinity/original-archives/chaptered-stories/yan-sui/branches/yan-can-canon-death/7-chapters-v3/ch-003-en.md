# Chapter 3 Abyss (Part 1) — Domestication

Yan Shen woke to a pungent moldy smell.

It was like rotting wood, rusted iron, and something older and indescribable mixed together, clogging his nasal cavity, making him want to vomit. His head was heavy, like being hammered from the inside; every heartbeat made his temples throb with pain. He tried to raise his hand to touch his forehead, but his hand wouldn't lift.

Not because he lacked strength. Because something was locked around his neck.

Yan Shen's eyes snapped open.

Before him was darkness. Not the darkness of night, but man-made darkness—a very small, grime-covered window overhead let in a bit of gray-white light, but far from enough to illuminate this space. His pupils dilated in the darkness, adapting bit by bit, and then he saw.

Cement floor. Gray, rough, with streaks of black marks, like traces left by blood dried long ago. Piles of tattered burlap sacks and rusted iron pipes in the corner. The air was thick with damp mold, and the fishy stench of rat droppings.

His left leg hurt terribly; the old wound at his knee was like a lump of red-hot charcoal, throbbing and burning his nerves. But he couldn't care about that leg anymore. His full attention was on his neck.

It was an iron chain. Thick, about thumb-width, its surface covered with reddish-brown rust, but the lock was new, gleaming with cold, hard metallic luster. One end of the chain was locked to a leather collar around his neck; the other end was fixed to an iron ring in the corner. The collar was very tight, tight enough that every swallow made him feel the leather pressing above his Adam's apple.

Yan Shen tried to dig at the edge of the collar with his hand. But his hand had just risen when it stopped.

He realized he wasn't wearing clothes.

Not a single piece.

His body lay naked on the cold cement floor, gray-and-white fur in direct contact with the rough cement surface, every hair feeling the granular texture and bone-piercing chill of the ground. His overcoat, sweater, pants, underwear, boots—all gone. Not even socks left.

Yan Shen's body went rigid.

It was a stiffness deeper and more primal than cold. His mind went blank for an instant, then a scalding wave surged up from his chest—not shame, but something fiercer than shame. Anger. The naked anger of being deprived of the last shred of covering.

He sat up violently. The chain clattered with a harsh racket, echoing in the empty basement. The collar tightened, his trachea compressed, breathing became difficult. But he didn't stop. He grabbed the chain with both hands, pulling hard, muscles tensing, knuckles white, even the pain in his left leg ignored.

The iron ring was fixed in the corner cement, unmoving.

Yan Shen panted heavily, stopping. His chest heaved violently, the collar tightening and loosening with each breath, rubbing the skin of his neck, already chafing a red ring. He looked around, trying to find anything usable—a shard of glass, an iron nail, anything.

Nothing.

This basement was terrifyingly empty. Besides him, besides that chain, besides the rubbish in the corner, there was nothing. Not even his cane had been brought in.

The door sounded.

It was a heavy iron door, at the other end of the basement, with a small observation window. Now, that door made a dull metallic grinding sound, then was pushed open a crack.

Light leaked in from the crack, making Yan Shen squint.

The fox beastkin's voice came from the doorway, carrying a condescending, playful lightness: "Oh, awake? Old thing, sleep well?"

Yan Shen didn't answer. He only stared at that door, at those eyes glowing in the backlight. His fingers slowly curled into fists, nails embedding into his palms.

The door opened fully.

The fox beastkin walked in, followed by two people. They were all wearing thick cotton-padded clothes, carrying things in their hands. The fox beastkin's gaze swept across Yan Shen's naked body, the corner of his mouth pulling into a meaningful smile.

"Former Beastshield's Yan Shen," he said slowly, like savoring this name, "Golden Age veteran. Great hero who protected White Capital."

He walked before Yan Shen, squatting down. Yan Shen could smell the smoke and some cheap perfume on him. The fox beastkin reached out, poking Yan Shen's chest fur—poking at the heart position, not heavy force, but carrying a nauseating frivolity.

"And now," the fox beastkin's finger slid down Yan Shen's chest, across the abdominal fur, stopping near the navel, "you're a dog."

Yan Shen's fist swung out.

The fox beastkin seemed to have anticipated it. He dodged backward; Yan Shen's fist scraped past his nose. But the chain's length wasn't enough; Yan Shen's body was yanked back by the collar, the chain clattering, the collar biting into flesh, leaving a deeper red mark.

The fox beastkin laughed. He stood up, retreated two steps, then nodded to the people behind him.

"Teach him."

Those two came forward. One held a wooden stick; the other carried an iron basin. Before Yan Shen could react, the stick smashed onto his back.

That wasn't ordinary beating. It was precise, purposeful striking—avoiding vital points, only hitting where muscles were thickest, causing maximum pain without killing. Yan Shen's body pitched forward, palms bracing on the cement floor; the rough surface immediately abraded his paw pads, blood seeping out, mixing with dust on the ground.

The second strike hit his left shoulder. The third hit the back of his thigh.

Yan Shen didn't shout. He only gritted his teeth, a low, beast-like growl coming from his throat. His eyes stared deathly at the fox beastkin, those amber eyes burning with something that made the fox beastkin frown slightly—not fear, not begging for mercy, but that veteran's unyielding, even-if-bones-are-shattered-won't-bow-down ferocity.

"...Pretty tough." The fox beastkin said, tone losing some playfulness, gaining some impatience, "Continue."

The stick fell. Once, twice, thrice. Yan Shen's body curled up on the cement floor, arms protecting his head and abdomen, like when he was surrounded and beaten in the alley. But there was no snow here. Here there was only cold cement and even colder air. His fur was soaked with sweat, clinging to his skin, then dried by body heat, then soaked again.

Don't know how long it lasted. Maybe only minutes, maybe as long as a century.

When the stick finally stopped, Yan Shen couldn't move anymore. His body was trembling, not from cold, but from instinctive muscle spasms under extreme pain. His back, shoulders, and thighs were covered with stick marks, bluish-purple bruises startlingly vivid under gray-white fur.

The fox beastkin squatted down again. This time he was closer, close enough for Yan Shen to see his own reflection in his pupils—that reflection showed an old beastkin lying on the ground, naked, collar locked around his neck.

"Know why you're being beaten?" The fox beastkin asked.

Yan Shen didn't answer. His mouth was full of the taste of blood, teeth clenched too tight, gums bleeding.

"Because you stood up." The fox beastkin said, voice light like coaxing a child, "Dogs can't stand up. Dogs can only crawl."

He stood up, kicking that iron basin with his foot. A clattering sound came from inside.

"This is your food." He said, "Dog, crawl over and eat."

Yan Shen didn't move.

The fox beastkin sighed, like somewhat regretful. Then he waved his hand.

The stick fell again.

Day one, Yan Shen didn't crawl.

He was beaten unconscious, then splashed awake with a bucket of cold water, beaten again, unconscious again, splashed awake again. His consciousness swayed between pain and darkness, like a broken ship in a storm. Each time he woke, he could see that iron basin—placed three meters away, containing half a basin of sour-smelling gruel.

He didn't crawl over. He just lay on the ground, eyes closed, waiting for the next beating.

But they didn't beat him anymore. The fox beastkin seemed to have lost interest in pure beating. He only had two people guard the door, entering every few hours to turn Yan Shen over, check his breathing, then leave.

They were waiting.

Waiting for hunger and thirst to accomplish what violence couldn't.

Day two, Yan Shen started feeling hungry.

That wasn't ordinary hunger. It was his stomach contracting, burning, digesting its own inner lining. His mouth was dry like stuffed with sand, tongue swollen to fill his entire oral cavity. He lay on the ground, listening to his intestines making gurgling sounds, that noise especially loud and especially shameful in the silent basement.

The fox beastkin came in once, pushing the iron basin closer to him.

"Crawl over." He said, "Crawl over like a dog, and you'll have food."

Yan Shen opened his eyes, looking at him. Then he closed his eyes.

The fox beastkin smiled, pushing the iron basin a bit further away.

Day three, Yan Shen's body began to betray him.

In half-dream half-wake, he found himself moving toward the direction of the iron basin. Not crawling—he still retained the last bit of dignity, unwilling to go on all fours like a dog. He was using his elbows to prop against the ground, inching forward bit by bit. His left leg was completely useless, only able to drag behind, the wound at his knee rubbing against the rough ground, leaving a dark red trail.

He inched to the side of the iron basin.

The gruel inside had already gone sour, emitting a putrid sour smell. But Yan Shen couldn't care anymore. He used his hand—not lowering his head directly to eat, but using his hand—grabbed a handful of gruel, stuffed it into his mouth.

The taste made him want to vomit. But he swallowed it down. Then another handful. Another.

The fox beastkin stood at the doorway watching, not entering. But his laughter came in, like a small knife, slicing Yan Shen's eardrums piece by piece.

"Using hands?" The fox beastkin said, "Dogs don't use hands. Dogs lick with their tongues."

Yan Shen's hand stopped.

He lowered his head, looking at his fingers covered with sour gruel. His fingers were trembling. Not from cold, not from hunger, but from something deeper—something he dared not name, something crumbling from inside his body.

Day four, he started crawling.

Not all fours from the beginning. First knees on ground, then palms. His body was trembling, every muscle resisting this posture. But his stomach was growling, his throat was burning, his consciousness was blurring. He crawled to the iron basin, lowered his head, using his tongue to lick the gruel in the basin.

That was a very small movement. But for Yan Shen, it was like a mountain collapsing before him.

When he took the first lick, tears nearly welled up. Not from sadness, but from a massive, unbearable shame. He was Yan Shen. Former Beastshield's Yan Shen. Golden Age veteran. He had once protected comrades on the battlefield, once stood at the very front during protests, once carried his son through White Capital's coldest winter night.

Now he was using his tongue to lick a basin of sour gruel.

The fox beastkin clapped his hands walking in, like seeing some splendid performance.

"Good dog." He said.

Yan Shen's body stiffened. Then continued licking.

Day five, they started teaching him "rules."

The rules were simple. Hearing a whistle, lie down. Hearing a clap, wag tail. Can't do it, get beaten. Can do it, get a slightly fresher piece of food.

Yan Shen didn't want to do it. He wanted to die. He wanted to bash his head against the wall, or strangle himself in the collar. But every time he tried to hurt himself, they would rush in, hold him down, then beat him harder. They didn't want his life. They wanted his obedience.

The first time he lay down upon hearing the whistle, the fox beastkin laughed very loudly.

"Look!" The fox beastkin said to the people behind him, "Former Beastshield's soldier, so smart! Learns in one lesson!"

Day six, his speed of lying down upon hearing the whistle became faster.

Not his intention. It was his body—that body tormented by hunger, pain, and fear for six days—starting to react before consciousness. The moment the whistle sounded, his limbs automatically folded, spine sinking, tail—his tail, that orange-yellow mixed with gray-white tiger tail—unconsciously drooping down, tucked between his legs.

He was trembling. Not from cold. Because he was hating himself.

After every obedience, he hated himself a little more. Hated how fast his knees bent. Hated how low his tail drooped. Hated how his stomach sold everything for a small piece of moldy bread.

But he couldn't stop.

Day seven night, the fox beastkin walked into the basement one last time.

He didn't bring a stick. He brought a mirror.

It was a very small, border-rusted mirror, probably scavenged from some ruin. The fox beastkin placed the mirror before Yan Shen, then retreated two steps, arms crossed, standing.

"Look at yourself." He said.

Yan Shen didn't move. He lay on the ground, eyes staring at the floor.

"I said," the fox beastkin's voice cooled, "look at yourself."

Yan Shen slowly raised his head.

He looked at the mirror.

In the mirror was a beastkin. Gray-and-white fur, filthy beyond recognition, matted, covered with dirt and dried bloodstains. The collar around his neck had chafed out a circle of deep purple bruises; some places the fur had already been worn away, exposing the red, swollen, broken skin beneath. His eyes—those eyes that had once been sharp as blades on the battlefield—now were turbid, unfocused, eye sockets deeply sunken, whites of eyes covered with bloodshot threads. His cheeks had thinned down, cheekbones protruding abruptly, white tiger whiskers dirty and stuck to his jaw, several broken.

His body was curled up, on all fours, back arched, tail drooping between his legs. That was the posture of a submissive. The posture of a domesticated.

That wasn't Yan Shen.

Or rather, that once was Yan Shen.

Yan Shen looked at that thing in the mirror, watching that thing look back at him with the same gaze. His lips trembled, like wanting to say something, but no sound came out. A sound came from his throat... not a whimper, not a growl, something deeper, more hollow. Like the last breath a person exhales when falling off a cliff.

The fox beastkin took the mirror away.

"Sleep," he said, "tomorrow continues."

The door closed behind him, the sound of the lock clicking echoing in the basement.

Yan Shen still maintained that posture. Lying on the ground, on all fours, iron chain pressing on his back. He didn't move. He only stared at that closed door, at the thin strip of light seeping in from below.

Then, his consciousness began to drift.

He saw Suisui. Suisui was still very small, lying on his back, small arms circling his neck, tail wrapped around his waist. Back then his leg hadn't gone bad yet, could still carry Suisui running. Suisui laughed in his ear, hot breath spraying on his earlobe, ticklish, warm.

"Daddy," Suisui said, "am I heavy?"

"Not heavy." He said, "Suisui isn't heavy at all."

Then the scene changed. He saw another scene—himself wearing Beastshield's Golden Age uniform, standing before the ranks, giving lectures to new recruits. His voice wasn't loud, but every word carried weight. He said, Beastshield is the beastkin's shield. He said, power and responsibility coexist. He said, having power means bearing more responsibility.

Those young people looked at him, light in their eyes. That was the light of trust.

Then the scene changed again. He saw himself. Not the thing in the mirror. Another himself—that self lying in the snow, using a cane to prop up his body, standing before Suisui. That self who wouldn't fall even if his left leg was broken. That self who still had killing intent in his eyes.

That self looked at him, with a disappointment he couldn't bear.

Yan Shen's eyes closed.

A drop of liquid slid from the corner of his eye, along the fur of his cheek, dripping onto the cement floor. That wasn't tears. Or rather, that wasn't just tears. That was a father, a veteran, a beastkin who had once lived proudly, after dignity was completely shattered, shedding the last drop of something.

He lay in the darkness, lying like a dog, waiting for tomorrow's whistle.
