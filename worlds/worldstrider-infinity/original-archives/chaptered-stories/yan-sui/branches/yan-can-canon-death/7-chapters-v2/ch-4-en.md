# Chapter 4 The Seventh Day

Darkness has weight.

Yan Shen first realized this after he woke up. That dim yellow light bulb had gone out at some point, leaving only pure, thick darkness in the basement. That darkness wasn't "not seeing," but a physical oppression, like a quilt soaked in water, heavily pressing on his eyes, ears, and skin.

He tried to move.

The chain made a crisp clashing sound, amplified countless times in the silence. That sound came from his neck—the collar was very tight, fur pressed to both sides, rough leather surface rubbing against the skin below his Adam's apple, every swallow bringing that suffocating restraint.

Yan Shen tried to move his hand again.

His wrists were twisted behind his back, the hemp rope already embedded into his skin, bringing a fiery stinging pain. He tried to break free, but the rope was tied in a dead knot, the more he struggled the tighter it bit. His fingers groped in the darkness, touching the cold cement floor, then the root of the iron pipe, then the links of the chain—link after link, cold, hard, without end.

He stopped.

Not because he gave up. But because a sound came from overhead.

That was the creaking of the wooden door. Then footsteps, coming down the stairs step by step. Those footsteps were very light, but exceptionally clear in the darkness, like some predator approaching prey.

Yan Shen's ears—those tiger ears hidden beneath the gray-white fur—instinctively flattened backward. His body also unconsciously curled up, like a beast driven to the corner, trying to use the smallest volume to reduce the area of attack.

That was fear. Pure, biological fear.

The light came on.

The dim yellow light bulb was pulled on by someone, stinging Yan Shen's eyes. He subconsciously turned his head, white tiger whiskers trembling under the light. When he adapted to the light, he saw the person standing before him.

Fox beastkin. And two other civilians.

The fox beastkin held an enamel basin, containing something inside, emitting faint steam. He walked before Yan Shen, squatted down, placed the basin on the ground, then patted the basin rim with his hand.

"...Eat." he said.

Yan Shen didn't move.

He just stared at that basin. Inside was some paste-like thing, gray in color, couldn't tell what it was. But that smell—a pungent smell mixed with sourness and some cheap seasoning—made Yan Shen's stomach spasm.

"I told you to eat." The fox beastkin's voice was still calm, but with something more.

Yan Shen still didn't move.

The fox beastkin sighed. That sigh sounded very sincere, like feeling regret for Yan Shen's stubbornness. Then he stood up, lifted his foot, and kicked Yan Shen's ribs.

"Ugh—!"

That kick wasn't heavy, but precisely hit last night's bruise. Yan Shen's body suddenly curled up, like a cooked shrimp. A broken breath escaped his throat, white tiger whiskers trembling violently from the pain.

"Not eating?" The fox beastkin asked, "Then starve."

He waved his hand, two other people came up, one left one right holding Yan Shen's arms, dragging him to the center of the room like dragging a piece of furniture. Yan Shen's knees scraped across the cement floor, leaving two dark red blood trails. His left leg completely couldn't use strength, dragging on the ground, the old wound at the knee sending out heart-piercing pain with every friction.

"...Kneel." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen didn't kneel. His body was stiff, like a frozen log.

The fox beastkin smiled. He walked forward, reaching out to press Yan Shen's back neck, forcing him to lower his head. Yan Shen wanted to resist, but his body was already weak to the extreme—last night's blood loss, cold, hunger, and the continuous pain of that disabled left leg, made his will like a string pulled to its limit, ready to break at any moment.

"...I'll say it once more." The fox beastkin's voice was very close, right beside his ear, carrying a damp heat, "...Kneel."

Yan Shen's knees bent down.

Not because he wanted to kneel. But because the fox beastkin, while speaking, suddenly kneed the back of Yan Shen's left leg's popliteal fossa from behind—precisely the most vulnerable spot of that deformed joint. Yan Shen's body lost balance, his whole person falling forward, his forehead heavily hitting the cement floor.

"Good boy." The fox beastkin said.

He picked up the basin on the ground, pouring the paste-like contents onto the floor. That lump of gray-white thing spread on the cold cement floor, emitting an even stronger sour smell. Then, the fox beastkin kicked that lump to Yan Shen's face with his foot.

"...Eat." he said, "Eat like a dog."

Yan Shen's face pressed against the cement floor. He could smell that sour smell, could smell the dust, could smell his own blood. His forehead was broken in that collision just now, fresh blood flowing down his brow bone, dripping onto the ground, mixing with that lump of paste.

He closed his eyes.

"...Not eating?" The fox beastkin's voice carried a knowing calmness, "Doesn't matter. We have plenty of time."

He turned around, taking the other two out of the basement. The door closed before Yan Shen, lock tongue clicked. Then, the light also went out.

Darkness fell again.

Yan Shen knelt in the darkness for a very long time. His knees had already lost sensation, the wound on his forehead throbbing painfully in the cold air. His stomach spasmed from hunger, that pain not sharp, but a dull, deep-seated colic, like a hand twisting his internal organs.

He slowly, slowly, buried his face into his arm.

He didn't remember how long he stayed in the darkness. Time lost meaning in this basement. No windows, no light changes, only that occasionally lit light bulb, and occasionally sounding footsteps, to mark the passage of time.

First day, he refused to eat.

Second day, he still refused.

Every time the fox beastkin came, he would repeat the same procedure: command him to kneel, pour food on the ground, then leave. Yan Shen didn't eat every time. His body gradually withered from hunger, fur lost its luster, gray-white hair like a faded old cloth in the darkness.

Third day, he began to hallucinate.

He thought he heard Suisui's voice. That voice came from very far away, like separated by a thick layer of water: "Dad... Dad..." He wanted to respond, but his throat was too dry to make a sound. He wanted to stand up, but the chain pulled him back to reality.

Fourth day, his body began to betray him.

That wasn't because of weak will. That was pure physiological reaction. When the fox beastkin once again poured food on the ground, Yan Shen's stomach let out a huge, impossible-to-ignore growl. That sound echoed in the silent basement, like a shameful declaration.

The fox beastkin laughed.

"...Looks like you finally understand." he said, "In this place, there are no 'people,' only 'alive' and 'dead.' If you want to live, you have to learn to be a good dog."

Yan Shen stared at the food on the ground. That lump of gray-white paste had already cooled, surface forming a thin film. His stomach was twitching, screaming, demanding he swallow that thing.

His fingers dug out blood marks on the cement floor.

Then, he lowered his head.

Not lowering all at once. But slowly, bit by bit, like performing some ancient, humiliating ritual. His nose tip touched that lump of food, the sour smell rushing straight into his nasal cavity. His stomach spasmed in that instant, but he forced himself to open his mouth.

First mouthful.

That thing tasted even worse than he imagined. Sour, rotten, bitter, and an indescribable metallic taste. Yan Shen's throat instinctively wanted to spit it out, but he forced himself to swallow it. That lump of paste slid through his dry esophagus, bringing a nearly painful burning sensation.

Second mouthful.

Third mouthful.

The fox beastkin stood nearby, watching him. There was no mockery in his eyes, no satisfaction, only a calm, nearly research-like focus, like observing an experimental subject.

"...Very good." When Yan Shen finally licked the ground clean, the fox beastkin said. His voice carried a praise, that praise more cruel than any mockery.

"...From today on," he said, "you eat every meal like this. Understand?"

Yan Shen didn't answer. He just lay on the ground, forehead pressed against the cold cement floor, white tiger whiskers still stained with food residue. His body was trembling, not from cold, but from a huge, unspeakable shame.

He cried.

Not crying out loud. But silently, tears sliding from tightly closed eye corners, dripping on the cement floor, mixing with food residue. He didn't make any sound, only his shoulders trembling slightly, like an old beast dying alone in the darkness.

The fox beastkin turned and left.

---

Fifth day, the conditioning began.

That wasn't beating. Beating was only the means, not the purpose. The purpose was to make Yan Shen understand—in this basement, before these people, he wasn't "Yan Shen," wasn't "former Beastshield employee," wasn't "Yan Sui's father." He was just an object that could be commanded, punished, domesticated.

"...Come here." The fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen knelt in a corner of the room, the chain's length allowing him to move within about two meters. He heard the command, but his body didn't move. His brain made a judgment in that instant: can't move. Can't obey. Once he obeyed the first time, there would be a second, third... until he completely became what they wanted.

The fox beastkin sighed.

He walked up, kicking Yan Shen's abdomen. That kick wasn't heavy, but precisely hit the stomach position. The food Yan Shen ate yesterday surged up in that instant, sour vomit spraying from his mouth, splashing on the cement floor.

"...Come here." The fox beastkin repeated.

Yan Shen didn't move.

Another kick.

Another kick.

By the fifth kick, Yan Shen couldn't vomit anything anymore. His stomach was spasming, twitching, like a balloon being squeezed. His forehead pressed against his own vomit, white tiger whiskers covered with sour liquid.

"...Come here." The fox beastkin's voice was still calm.

Yan Shen's knees moved forward an inch.

That inch of movement hurt him more than any beating. Because he knew, that was the sound of his dignity as "Yan Shen" shattering.

"...Good boy." The fox beastkin said.

He squatted down, reaching out to touch Yan Shen's head. That wasn't an action expressing goodwill—that was an owner's action toward a pet, carrying a condescending,施舍-like pity.

Yan Shen's tiger ears flattened backward in that instant. Not from anger, but from something deeper—something he couldn't name, but more terrible than anger.

---

Seventh day.

Yan Shen already couldn't remember how long he had stayed in this basement. Time lost meaning. His world only had a few things left: the cold of the chain, the strangulation marks of the collar, the roughness of the cement floor, the sour smell of food, and the fox beastkin's footsteps.

His body was withering. Ribs clearly visible beneath the fur, abdominal muscles atrophied from hunger, leaving only a loose skin. His left leg became even stiffer from long-term kneeling, the old wound at the knee swollen like a fist, showing an unhealthy purplish-red color.

But his body was also changing.

That change wasn't visible to the naked eye, but deeper. When he heard footsteps on the stairs, his heart would start racing before his brain reacted. When the fox beastkin's voice sounded, his body would start trembling before his consciousness made a judgment. When words like "lie down," "come here," "kneel" echoed in the air, his limbs would react first before he told himself "don't obey."

That was a conditioned reflex. A survival instinct the body learned on its own under repeated punishment and painful stimulation.

Late night of the seventh day, only Yan Shen was in the basement.

The light went out. Darkness flooded in like tidewater, drowning his senses. He curled up within the range the chain allowed, back against the cold cement wall, holding his knees to his chest.

His consciousness floated. Between half-dream and half-wake, he saw Suisui.

Not the current Suisui. The childhood Suisui. That five-year-old little tiger, lying on his back, fluffy tail wrapped around his waist, small hands circling his neck, saying something in his ear. He couldn't hear clearly, but he knew it must be sweet, warm words.

"...Dad..." Suisui's voice came from very far away.

Yan Shen opened his eyes in the darkness.

That wasn't a dream. That was his own voice. He had unconsciously called out that name.

His tears flowed again. But this time not from shame. But from something deeper—something he realized he was losing.

He remembered before going out, Suisui said "I have something I want to tell you."

He remembered himself saying "Wait until Dad comes back."

He remembered that stack of communication coupons in the drawer. "With this coupon, Dad will definitely talk properly with Suisui no matter what."

He couldn't go back.

Not because he didn't want to go back. But because—his body knew, even if he walked out that door now, even if he returned to that home, he could no longer be Suisui's father. A beastkin who would automatically kneel when hearing commands, a beastkin who would lick food from the ground, a beastkin who couldn't even control his own bladder—

How could he still be a father?

Yan Shen buried his face in his knees. His body was trembling, like a withered leaf in the cold wind. The chain made slight clashing sounds with his trembling, that sound exceptionally harsh in the silence, like mocking his weakness.

"...Suisui..." His voice was so light it was almost inaudible, like a wisp of white mist about to dissipate, "...Dad can't go back..."

There was no response in the darkness.

Only the slight clashing sound of the chain, and his own suppressed, broken breathing, echoing in the basement, like a requiem without lyrics.
