# Chapter 5 Light Through the Door Crack

When the second week began, Yan Shen could barely remember who he was anymore.

That "not remembering" wasn't amnesia. But something more subtle—like a person standing before a mirror, the mirror clearly reflecting his own face, but that face looked like it belonged to a stranger. Yan Shen knew he was Yan Shen, knew he was Yan Sui's father, knew he was a former Beastshield employee. But these "knowings" were like labels stuck in his mind, not things growing from his body.

His body had already learned another set of rules.

Eighth day, when the fox beastkin walked into the basement, Yan Shen had already woken before the footsteps reached the stair entrance. His ears—those tiger ears beneath the gray-white fur—slightly turned in the darkness, capturing every vibration in the air. When the sound of the door lock being opened rang out, his heart started racing, blood rushing to his limbs, his stomach tightening into a ball in that instant.

That wasn't fear. At least not entirely.

That was something more complex—a mixture of fear, anticipation, and something he himself wasn't willing to admit. Because the fox beastkin didn't just bring beating. Sometimes, when he "performed well," the fox beastkin would give him a slightly cleaner piece of food, or let him drink an extra mouthful of water. Those tiny,施舍-like kindnesses, in this hellish basement, had actually become Yan Shen's only anticipation.

This realization made him feel sick.

But his body didn't care.

"...Come here." The fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen crawled over.

Not walked. Crawled. Because his knees already hurt too much to stand up. His four limbs on the ground, chain dragging beneath him, making harsh friction sounds. His head lowered, gaze only able to see the fox beastkin's feet—that pair of boots covered with snow mud and dirt.

The fox beastkin threw a piece of something on the ground. That was a hard piece of bread, already moldy, but in Yan Shen's eyes, it was like some precious reward. He lowered his head, using his tongue to roll the bread into his mouth, chewing, swallowing. His stomach let out a satisfied whimper in that instant, that sound exceptionally clear in the silence, like mocking his dignity.

"...Good boy." The fox beastkin said.

He reached out, touching Yan Shen's head. Yan Shen's tiger ears slightly trembled beneath that palm, but didn't dodge.

He already didn't dodge.

---

Tenth day.

Yan Shen began to dream.

Those weren't normal dreams. Those dreams had no plot, no images, only a vague, warm sensation. He felt himself being wrapped by something, very soft, very warm, carrying a sunshine-like scent. His body relaxed in that warmth, all pain disappeared, all fear receded.

Then he would wake up.

After waking, he found himself curled on the cement floor, chain勒着脖子, with only a thin layer of dirty dust on his body. Cold seeped into his marrow from the ground, making his teeth chatter uncontrollably. He would spend a very long time distinguishing—which was the dream, which was reality.

Sometimes, he wished he would never wake up.

Eleventh day, the fox beastkin brought a new person.

That was a young civilian, looking less than twenty years old, face still carrying a kind of innocence that hadn't experienced too much suffering. He walked into the basement, seeing Yan Shen's appearance, subconsciously took a step back.

"...This is that former Beastshield one?" The young person's voice was somewhat trembling.

"Mm." The fox beastkin said, "Didn't you say you wanted to learn? Today I'll show you how to condition an old dog."

The young person swallowed a mouthful of saliva. His gaze wandered over Yan Shen's body, from the gray-white fur, to the bony ribs, to the collar and chain on his neck. There was fear in his eyes, disgust, and something Yan Shen was very familiar with—curiosity.

"...Make him lie down." The fox beastkin said to the young person.

The young person hesitated, then learning from the fox beastkin, used a deliberately authoritative voice to say: "...Lie down."

Yan Shen didn't move.

Not because he was resisting. But because his brain short-circuited in that instant—he heard the command, but that voice wasn't the fox beastkin's, so he didn't know how to react. His body was hesitating, waiting, like a dog trained too well, only recognizing one master's voice.

The fox beastkin laughed.

He walked up, kicking Yan Shen's side waist. That kick wasn't heavy, but precisely hit an old wound. Yan Shen's body suddenly curled up, a broken whimper escaping from his throat.

"...I told you to lie down." The fox beastkin said, voice still calm.

Yan Shen lay down.

His chest pressed against the cold cement floor, forehead against the ground, chain coiled beneath him. His tail—that orange-yellow mixed with gray-white tiger tail—hung behind him, tail tip slightly trembling.

The young person watched this scene, his gaze changing from fear to something more complex. He took a step forward, reached out, carefully touching Yan Shen's tail.

Yan Shen's body stiffened.

Then, his tail curled up in that instant, like a frightened child hugging his own knees.

The young person abruptly withdrew his hand.

"...He, he..." The young person's voice was trembling.

"Fun, right?" The fox beastkin said, his voice carrying an educator's patience, "This is former Beastshield's Yan Shen. Once so impressive, and now?"

He squatted down, pinching Yan Shen's chin with his hand, forcing him to raise his head. Yan Shen's eyes were half-open, pupils unfocused, but there was still something inside—something that made the fox beastkin feel uncomfortable, even at this time, even when Yan Shen had already become this appearance, there was still something in those amber eyes.

"...What are you looking at?" The fox beastkin asked.

Yan Shen didn't answer. He couldn't answer. His throat was too hoarse from dryness and overuse to make a sound. He just looked at the fox beastkin, at those eyes so close, then—

His gaze passed over the fox beastkin's shoulder, looking at a certain corner of the basement.

There was nothing there. Only a patch of shadow.

But Yan Shen saw something in that shadow. Perhaps hallucination, perhaps not. He saw Suisui. Not the childhood Suisui, but the current Suisui—a 25-year-old young tiger, orange-yellow fur, robust body, amber eyes identical to Yan Shen's. Suisui stood in the shadow, looking at him, eyes carrying shock, pain, and something Yan Shen couldn't bear... disappointment.

"...Suisui..." Yan Shen's lips moved, but no sound came out.

The fox beastkin didn't notice. He just released his hand, stood up, patted the young person's shoulder.

"...Let's go." he said, "That's enough for today."

The door closed before Yan Shen. The light went out.

Darkness fell again.

But this time, Yan Shen didn't immediately curl up. He just lay there, forehead pressed against the cold cement floor, tears silently flowing down.

Because he finally understood.

He wasn't crying for his current self. He was crying for that person still left outside, called "Yan Sui's father." That person was already dead. In the first week in the basement, at the moment he said "Wait until Dad comes back," that person was already dead. What lived here now was just a breathing shell, a shell that had learned to obey, learned to please, learned to lick food from the ground.

He couldn't go back.

Even if he walked out that door now, even if he returned to that home, he could no longer face Suisui. Because Suisui would see what he looked like now. Suisui would see the collar marks on his neck, see the wounds on his body, see his knees that couldn't help but bend when hearing "sit down."

Suisui would know.

Suisui would know his father, that once invincible father in his eyes, had become something like this.

Yan Shen buried his face in his arm. His body was trembling, like a withered leaf in the cold wind. But he didn't make any sound. He just silently cried, tears soaking through the gray-white fur, dripping on the cement floor, mixing with dust.

---

Late night of the fourteenth day.

Only Yan Shen was in the basement. The light was out, darkness flooding in like tidewater, drowning his senses. He curled up within the range the chain allowed, back against the cold cement wall, holding his knees to his chest.

His consciousness floated. Between half-dream and half-wake, he heard a sound.

That wasn't the fox beastkin's footsteps. Nor civilians' talking. That was a heavier, slower sound—like some huge thing moving, or like...

The door was smashed open.

Not normally opened. But smashed open from outside by some huge force. The wooden door shattered in that instant, wood splinters flying everywhere. Light poured down from the stair entrance, stinging Yan Shen's eyes—he hadn't seen such bright light in a very long time.

Yan Shen curled up even tighter in the darkness. His body reacted in that instant—not fleeing, but curling up even more. His head lowered, forehead against the ground, hands protecting above his head, like a young beast seeking shelter in a storm.

That wasn't bravery. That was the conditioned, instinctive posture of submission.

"...Captain Yan?"

A voice rang out in the darkness. That was an unfamiliar, young voice, carrying a tone Yan Shen hadn't heard in a very long time—a tone belonging to a "normal person"—that tone had worry, shock, and suppressed anger.

Yan Shen didn't raise his head. He just curled up even harder, body shivering in cold and fear. His brain could no longer process this sudden situation. He only knew that when someone broke in, he should hide himself, should make himself as small as possible, should wait for punishment to arrive.

"...Captain Yan! It's me! Yi An!"

That hand touched his shoulder.

Yan Shen's body suddenly bounced, like being electrocuted. A broken, nearly animal-like whimper escaped his throat, then his whole person shrank backward, until the chain held him back, unable to retreat further.

"...Don't... don't touch me..." His voice was so hoarse it was almost inaudible, like the last breath squeezed from deep in his throat.

Yi An knelt before him. Borrowing the light coming from the stair entrance, Yan Shen finally saw his face clearly.

That was a young, well-defined face, deep brown and black mixed fur showing a healthy luster under the light. That was Yi An. The junior Yan Shen had personally promoted, that Bernese Mountain Dog who had come to him complaining with a face full of不服 when promoted. Now he wore a blue uniform, the badge on his shoulder gleaming with cold light in the dim light.

Yi An looked at Yan Shen. His gaze slid from Yan Shen's face, to his neck—that neck with deep red strangulation marks from the collar, to his body—that bony, wound-covered body, to his leg—that left leg even more severely deformed.

Yi An's eyes reddened.

"...These bastards." His voice was very low, squeezed from between teeth, carrying a young person's uncontrollable rage.

He reached out, wanting to undo the collar on Yan Shen's neck. But Yan Shen's body suddenly trembled violently the instant he approached, eyes tightly closed, breathing rapid, like a child waiting to be beaten.

"...It's me, Yi An." Yi An's voice lightened, carrying a gentle tone Yan Shen hadn't heard in a very long time, "...I'm here to take you home."

Yan Shen's eyes opened.

His pupils contracted in the dim light, focusing on Yi An's face. He looked at those eyes, those eyes as upright and bright as in memory. His lips moved, wanting to say something, but only a broken breath escaped his throat.

Then, his tears gushed out.

Not tears of gratitude. Not the joy of being rescued. That was something deeper—a huge shame of being seen in his current state by someone he knew best. His body began to tremble violently, fingers scratching madly on the ground, nails filling with dust and blood.

"...Don't look at me..." His voice was hoarse, broken, like a piece of paper crumpled and then unfolded, "...Please... don't look at me..."

Yi An didn't avert his gaze.

His hands trembled, but his movements were steady. He undid the collar on Yan Shen's neck, undid the hemp rope on his wrists, undid the chain on his ankles. Those things that had bound Yan Shen for fourteen days, fell to the ground one by one, making dull sounds.

But Yan Shen didn't move.

He just lay there, like an empty shell with its soul extracted. His face buried in his arm, body trembling, tail tightly clamped between his legs.

Yi An took off his blue uniform coat, draping it over Yan Shen. That coat was very large, carrying a scent belonging to the "outside world"—sunshine, soap, and the smell of normal life. Yan Shen's body stiffened the instant that scent rushed into his nasal cavity.

That was a scent he thought he would never smell again.

"...Can you stand up?" Yi An asked.

Yan Shen didn't answer. He tried to move his body, but both legs had completely lost sensation. His left leg had become even stiffer from long-term kneeling, the old wound at the knee swollen like a fist, showing an unhealthy purplish-red color.

Yi An sighed. He bent down, draping Yan Shen's arm over his shoulder, then using all his strength, lifting this beastkin much taller than himself from the ground.

Yan Shen's body was soft as mud. His head drooped on Yi An's shoulder, gray-white fur rubbing against Yi An's collar. His breathing was very light, very shallow, like a wisp of white mist about to dissipate.

"...Hold tight to me." Yi An said.

He half-supported, half-dragged Yan Shen out of the basement. The stairs were very steep, Yan Shen's left leg completely couldn't use strength, every step felt like stepping on knife points. Yi An supported most of his weight with his shoulder, moving upward step by step. The young body burst out with astonishing strength, that was the Bernese Mountain Dog's innate physique advantage—broad, sturdy, like a moving mountain.

When outside air rushed into his nasal cavity, Yan Shen's body suddenly trembled.

That was the first time in fourteen days he breathed outside air. The air was very cold, carrying a mixed smell of snow and coal smoke, but that was "outside" air, free air. Yan Shen's lungs spasmed in that instant, then greedily, nearly painfully inhaled a large mouthful.

He raised his head.

The sky was gray-black. No moon, only sparks coming from distant factory chimneys. But even such a dirty sky, in Yan Shen's eyes, was like some sacred, inviolable thing.

Because he thought he would never see it again.

Yi An supported him, walking step by step out of that alley. Snow was still falling, snowflakes landing on Yan Shen's fur, melting, then evaporated by body heat. His body shivered in the cold, but his eyes were opened very wide, like he wanted to etch everything into his mind.

The street was empty. Only their footsteps echoed on the snow—Yi An's heavy footsteps, and the friction sound of Yan Shen's left leg dragging.

"...Yi An." Yan Shen suddenly said.

His voice was very light, very hoarse, like coming from very far away. That was the first time in fourteen days he actively opened his mouth to speak.

"...Mm?" Yi An turned his head.

Yan Shen looked ahead. His gaze was very empty, like two dried-up wells.

"...Don't take me home." he said.

Yi An's footsteps paused.

"...What?"

Yan Shen turned his head, looking at Yi An. There was no emotion in his eyes, only a calmness that made Yi An feel afraid.

"...Don't take me home." he repeated, "...I can't... let Suisui see... what I look like now."

Yi An didn't speak. He just looked at Yan Shen, at those amber eyes showing dead silence in the dim light.

Snow fell between them, silently accumulating.
