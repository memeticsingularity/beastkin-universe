# Chapter 7 Bearing

When dawn was near, Yi An moved.

He carefully laid Yan Shen's body flat, letting him lie face-up on the bed, hands crossed over his abdomen. Yan Shen's eyes were half-open; Yi An used his fingers to gently close his eyelids. That movement was very light, like brushing dust off an ancient statue.

Then, Yi An began to tidy up.

He found a piece of Yan Shen's own clothing—that was a dark gray old overcoat, washed white but still neat. Yan Shen always wore it when going out, saying it was "stain-resistant." Yi An helped him put on the overcoat, buttoned the buttons, straightened the collar. His hand paused when touching Yan Shen's neck—the collar strangulation marks had already turned deep purple, like a scar that could never heal.

Yi An's brow furrowed. He took out a scarf from the drawer, carefully wrapping it around Yan Shen's neck, covering that strangulation mark.

"...Be proper." he said softly, "...Going home needs to be proper."

He bent down, picking Yan Shen up from the bed.

Yan Shen was taller than him, sturdier than him; even after fourteen days of torture, the skeleton was still there. But Yi An was a Bernese Mountain Dog, young and strong, shoulders broad like a door. He held him very steadily; though struggling, not like an aging body swaying precariously. He gritted his teeth, walking step by step toward the door. Yan Shen's head rested on his shoulder; gray-white fur rubbed against his collar. That body was still warm—beastkin preservation mechanisms made the boundary between death and life blurry; Yi An almost felt Yan Shen's breath still lingering in his neck nest.

But that was just wind.

Yi An pushed open the door, walking into White Capital's morning.

Snow had stopped. The sky presented a dirty gray-white, like a wall smoked by coal smoke for too long. The street was empty; only a few broken streetlamps swayed slightly in the wind, making creaking sounds. Yi An's footprints left a string of deep marks on the snow, while Yan Shen's left leg dragged on the ground, plowing a shallow arc in the snow.

That picture looked like a junior carrying a drunk senior home.

If not for Yan Shen's eyes being tightly closed, if not for his chest no longer rising and falling, if not for his fingers already stiffening into a slightly bent posture—that posture looked like holding something invisible—it really would have looked like going home.

Yi An walked step by step. His breathing was very heavy; every pant turned into a puff of white mist in the cold air. His shoulders protested, back screamed, but he didn't stop. He just gritted his teeth, lifting Yan Shen up a bit, letting him lean more steadily.

"...Captain Yan." Yi An suddenly said. His voice was very light, like talking to himself, "...Do you still remember? When I just got promoted to blue uniform... the people below didn't accept me... it was you... who talked to them one by one... telling them... I, Yi An... was someone Captain Yan approved of..."

His voice trembled somewhat.

"...Now it's my turn to carry you." he said, "...Fair."

No one answered him.

Only wind and snow whistled by his ears, like some ancient, wordless elegy.

---

Yan Shen's home arrived.

That was a dilapidated apartment building; wall paint peeling, windows covered with thick frost. Yi An stood at the door, adjusting his breath, then freed a hand, taking out the key from Yan Shen's pocket.

Key inserted into the lock, turned, emitting a dull click sound.

The door opened.

The room was very quiet. Quieter than Yi An imagined. That quietness wasn't the quietness of being empty, but a... frozen quietness. Like time had stopped flowing here, like some huge, invisible thing had pressed everything in place.

Yi An carried Yan Shen into the bedroom, gently placing him on the bed. He pulled over the quilt, covering it to Yan Shen's chin, then used his palm to smooth the wrinkles on the quilt. His movements were very careful, like tidying a precious gift.

Then, he heard a sound.

That was a very slight, somewhat dazed breathing sound. Coming from the adjacent room.

Yi An turned around, walking toward the adjacent room's door. He pushed open the door—

Yan Sui lay on the bed, still sleeping.

His breathing was very steady, very deep. That orange-yellow tiger tail extended from under the quilt, resting on the bedside, tail tip trembling slightly with breath. His brow slightly furrowed, like having some not-so-good dream, but the corner of his mouth carried an imperceptible relaxation.

Yi An stood in the doorway, looking at this young man.

Yan Sui and Yan Shen looked very much alike. Same wide forehead, same thick brows, same, even in sleep, slightly upturned corner of the mouth. Yi An recalled many years ago, in Beastshield's golden age, he had once held childhood Suisui. At that time Suisui was only three years old, fluffy, like a small orange, lying in his embrace without crying or fussing, just looking at him with those amber eyes identical to Yan Shen's, then suddenly smiled, revealing two small tiger teeth.

How good it was back then.

Yi An's eye rims reddened again. He walked up, gently tucking in the quilt corners for Yan Sui. His movements were very light, afraid of waking this still-dreaming young man.

Then he saw the things on the nightstand.

That was a stack of handwritten paper, edges yellowed, worn fuzzy by time. Yi An used two fingers to gently pull one out, glanced at it—

"With this coupon, Dad will definitely talk properly with Suisui no matter what."

Yi An's hand trembled.

He put the communication coupon back in place, gently pushing the drawer closed. Then, he bent down, lightly, lightly kissing the top of Yan Sui's head.

That was an elder's kiss to a junior, carrying an unspeakable compassion and apology.

"...Child." Yi An's voice was so light it was almost inaudible, "...Sorry."

He turned and walked out of the bedroom, gently closing the door.

---

Yi An began to tidy Yan Shen's final appearance.

He brought a basin of warm water from the kitchen, wet the towel, wrung it dry, then wiped Yan Shen's body bit by bit. Starting from the forehead, downward, to neck, to shoulders, to chest. His movements were very steady, very slow, like performing some ancient ritual only old veterans understood.

Yan Shen's body was still warm. That warmth made Yi An's fingers tremble slightly with every touch. He always felt that perhaps the next second, Yan Shen would open his eyes, look at him with that somewhat helpless gaze, and say "Little Bo, what are you crying for, I'm not dead yet."

But Yan Shen didn't open his eyes.

He just quietly lay there, the corner of his mouth carrying a faint smile, like having a very warm dream.

Yi An combed his fur for him. Gray-white fur gleamed with a soft silver under the light, like moonlight sprinkled on snow. He arranged Yan Shen's tiger whiskers one by one, letting them hang down in one direction. Then he helped Yan Shen change into a more proper set of clothes—not that old overcoat, but the old uniform from Beastshield's golden age that Yan Shen had treasured for many years.

That uniform was already very old, color faded to dark green; the Beastshield badge on the shoulder was also worn beyond recognition. But Yi An still insisted on putting it on him. Because he knew that Yan Shen's proudest time was when wearing this uniform.

"...You always wanted to go back, right?" Yi An said while buttoning, voice somewhat hoarse, "...Back to that time... back to when Beastshield was still Beastshield..."

No one answered him.

After fastening the last button, Yi An stepped back, looking at Yan Shen on the bed.

That old uniform on Yan Shen's bony body looked somewhat loose. But the lapel was neat, the shoulder badge proper, the collar button fastened to the topmost one. Gray-white fur was combed neatly; white tiger whiskers gleamed with soft luster under the light. Yan Shen closed his eyes, expression peaceful, even serene.

He looked like he was asleep.

Like he might wake up at any moment, then lean on his cane, hobbling into the kitchen, cooking a bowl of hot noodle soup for Suisui.

Yi An's tears flowed down.

He didn't wipe them. He just stood by the bedside, looking at Yan Shen, looking for a long time. Then he turned around, taking out a piece of paper and a pen from his pocket, sitting by the table, beginning to write.

That was a brief letter. Written to Yan Sui. But he didn't know how to begin, how to tell a young man that his father was already dead. He wrote and crossed out, crossed out and wrote, ultimately leaving only one line:

"Your father was a true Beastshield soldier. He loved you."

Yi An folded the letter, placing it at Yan Shen's bedside, pressing it down with that stack of communication coupons.

Then he stood up, taking one last look at Yan Shen.

"...Go well, Captain Yan." he said.

He turned and walked out of the bedroom, closing the door.

---

Yi An didn't leave immediately.

He sat in the chair in the living room, in the darkness, sitting for a whole morning. He needed time. Needed courage. Needed to think through how to face what came next.

At noon, he stood up.

He walked out of Yan Shen's home, locked the door, put the key in his pocket. Then, he put on his blue uniform coat, walking toward White Capital's eastern district.

He was going to see a person.

That person was his direct superior, someone in Beastshield who he could still speak to. As a blue uniform, Yi An had his own channels and connections. Yan Shen had personally promoted him to this position, and now, he would use this position to fight for a way out for Yan Shen's son.

The streets of the eastern district were a bit wider than the western district, but only a bit better. Apartment buildings with peeling wall paint, rusty iron fences, and those mottled slogans on the walls. Yi An walked step by step, blue uniform shoulder badge gleaming with cold light under the gray-white sky.

The Beastshield office building was just ahead. That was a gray building; on the outer wall was the Beastshield badge—a beast claw surrounded by a shield. That badge was golden in Yan Shen's era; now it had turned dark red, like dried blood.

Yi An stood at the door, taking a deep breath.

Then he walked in.

---

The superior was more aged than Yi An imagined.

His office was permeated with a mixed smell of smoke and tea leaves. The superior sat behind the desk; seeing Yi An walk in, a flash of surprise appeared in his eyes, immediately turning into some complex emotion.

"...Yi An?" The superior's voice was somewhat hoarse, "...Why did you come?"

Yi An didn't sit down. He just stood before the desk, both hands hanging at his sides, like a tree swaying in the wind but never falling.

"...Yan Shen is dead." he said.

The superior's hand stopped in midair. He held a pen in his hand; the pen tip hung above the paper, ink soaking out a small patch of black mark.

"...What?"

"...Yan Shen. Dead." Yi An repeated, voice very calm, but every word like squeezed from between teeth, "...Last night. I sent him."

The superior's eyes widened. He put down the pen, stood up, walked around the desk, coming before Yi An. His gaze wandered over Yi An's face, like looking for traces of some joke. But he didn't find any.

"...How did he die?" The superior asked.

Yi An looked at him. In his gaze was very calm, but deep inside was something that made the superior feel afraid.

"...Tortured by people for fourteen days." Yi An said, "...When brought back... already couldn't make it."

The superior's expression changed.

"...Who did it?" His voice lowered, carrying a suppressed anger.

"...Western district civilians." Yi An said, "...That group who beat Yan Sui some time ago. Same batch."

The superior's hand clenched into a fist. His knuckles whitened from the force, veins bulging on his arms. He walked two steps in the office, then stopped, back toward Yi An.

"...What about Yan Sui?" he asked.

"...Still at home." Yi An said, "...Doesn't know. Still sleeping."

The superior turned around, looking at Yi An. In his eyes were many things—anger, sorrow, and some... exhaustion.

"...What do you want me to do?" he asked.

Yi An took out a piece of paper from his pocket. That was what he wrote last night, listing Yan Shen's home address, Yan Sui's basic situation, and everything he could arrange for Yan Sui.

"...Take care of him." Yi An said, "...Yan Shen only has this one son. He's still young... still has a future... can't let him stay alone in White Capital."

The superior took the paper, glanced at it. His hand trembled slightly.

"...I can arrange him into Beastshield." The superior said, "...Starting from the bottom... but at least can have enough to eat... have a place to stay."

Yi An shook his head.

"...Don't tell him the truth." he said, "...At least... don't tell him now. Tell him... Yan Shen died of illness. Tell him... his father passed very peacefully."

The superior looked at him, looked for a very long time. Then, he nodded.

"...I promise you." he said.

Yi An slightly bowed. That was a junior's respect for a senior, a comrade's entrustment to another comrade.

"...Thank you." he said.

Then he turned around, walking out of the office.

---

When Yi An walked out of the Beastshield office building, the sky was already dark.

White Capital's winter night came very quickly, like someone suddenly pulling closed a black curtain. Streetlamps on the street lit up, but most were broken; only a few swayed in the wind and snow, illuminating the ground in flickering light and shadow.

Yi An walked on the snow, blue uniform boots making dull sounds on the frozen ground. He didn't go home immediately. He just walked, step by step, like looking for something, like escaping something.

He passed by Yan Shen's home.

That porch light was still on—Suisui always forgot to turn off the light. Dim yellow light leaked from the window, casting a small patch of warm light on the snow. Yi An stood across the street, looking at that window, looking for a very long time.

He imagined the scene inside. Yan Sui might have already woken up, might have discovered his father wasn't home, might be anxious, might be worried. He imagined Yan Sui pushing open the bedroom door, seeing that body wearing the old uniform on the bed. He imagined Yan Sui's expression—from confusion to shock, from shock to collapse.

He imagined that young man kneeling by the bedside, holding his father's already stiff but still warm hand, crying out "Dad."

Yi An's tears flowed down.

He didn't wipe them. He just stood there, in the wind and snow, looking at that lamp.

Then he turned around, walking step by step into White Capital's night.

He didn't look back.

Because he knew, some farewells, once you look back, you can never walk away again.

---

Many years later, Yan Sui in some corner of Beastshield, accidentally heard a legend.

Legend has it that long, long ago, White Capital once had a true Beastshield soldier. He was upright, brave, and to protect his son, spared no cost. He died on a winter night, in a junior's embrace, the corner of his mouth carrying a smile.

Legend has it his soul became a star in the sky above White Capital, shining on winter nights, guarding every beastkin still struggling in the darkness.

Yan Sui heard this legend, only smiling.

He didn't tell others that his father was also called Yan Shen. He also didn't tell others that many years ago on that winter night, he once had a dream. In the dream, his father carried him on his back to see the snow in the back mountains; his small hands encircled his father's neck, fluffy tail wrapped around his father's waist, warm and cozy.

In the dream, his father said to him: "Suisui, Dad loves you."

That dream was very long, very long. So long that when Yan Sui woke up, his pillow was full of tears.

(End of Chapter Seven)
