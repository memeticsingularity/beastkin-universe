# Chapter 3 A Cane on the Snow

Yi An's apartment was just ahead.

Yan Shen raised his head, looking at that old building blackened by coal smoke. A light was on in the third-floor window, dim yellow light passing through dirty glass, casting a small blurry patch of light on the snow. Yan Shen recognized that light—Yi An always woke very early, that light often on in the early morning, like an eye watching in the gray darkness.

He slightly relaxed a breath.

His left leg was already numb with pain, the joint at the knee like a rag that had been kneaded and then frozen hard, every bend making a harsh friction sound. He leaned against the wall, resting slightly, adjusting his breathing. White mist sprayed from his mouth and nose, rapidly dissipating in the cold air.

Another fifty meters forward, turn a corner, and it was the apartment entrance.

Yan Shen straightened up, leaning on his cane, taking a step.

Thud.

The cane struck the ground, echoing in the silent alley. That sound was very light, but in this empty early morning, it was like a stone falling into a deep well, stirring countless invisible ripples.

Yan Shen took three more steps.

Then stopped.

In the shadows at the alley entrance, several people stood.

They wore worn cotton-padded clothes, faces mostly covered by collars and scarves, only revealing a few pairs of eyes. Those eyes showed a turbid, sickly light in the dim light, like starving beasts sizing up prey.

Yan Shen's gaze swept over them one by one.

He recognized one of them—that fox beastkin. Last night in the alley, it was this person who led the group in beating Yan Sui. Yan Shen's cane had left a bruise on his shoulder; now he was still slightly hunching that shoulder.

The fox beastkin also recognized Yan Shen.

The corner of his mouth slowly, slowly pulled upward, revealing an expression that couldn't be called a smile. That was more like a greed of discovering unexpected wealth, mixed with some suppressed, finally-released satisfaction.

"...Former Beastshield's Yan Shen?" the fox beastkin said, his voice carrying a deliberately surprised tone, "Oh my, what a coincidence. We were just wondering where to find you."

Yan Shen didn't answer.

His fingers tightened on the cane grip. Knuckles whitening from the force, the rough paw pads in his palm rubbing against the wood grain. His left leg slightly withdrew half a step backward—that was a veteran's instinctive posture when assessing the situation—not retreating, but looking for a support point.

The fox beastkin walked out from the shadows. Behind him, four other people also came up. Five figures formed a human wall in the narrow alley, blocking Yan Shen's path completely.

"Last night you got to show off." The fox beastkin rubbed his shoulder, where the bruise faintly showed beneath the worn cotton-padded coat, "Old bastard, quite some strength."

Yan Shen still didn't speak.

He was just looking behind the fox beastkin—that old apartment wasn't far, the third-floor light was still on. Yi An might be inside tidying his uniform, might be eating breakfast. Just one more shout, just if Yi An heard...

But Yan Shen didn't shout.

He knew Yi An lived on the third floor, separated by thick walls and coal-smoke-blackened windows, might not necessarily hear the movement below. He knew Yi An was in blue uniform, but one person coming down to face five civilians might not necessarily have the advantage either. And—his gaze swept over the five people—even if Yi An heard, even if Yi An came down, could one blue uniform plus one disabled veteran beat five young and strong civilians?

No.

So Yan Shen didn't shout. He just switched the cane from his right hand to his left, moving his right shoulder. That was a tiny movement, but the fox beastkin noticed.

"Still want to fight?" The fox beastkin laughed, but there was no warmth in that laughter, "Old bastard, last night you had the advantage of a sneak attack. Today—"

He took a step forward.

"—you won't be so lucky."

Yan Shen moved.

He didn't wait for the fox beastkin to finish speaking. Years of experience told him that when at an absolute numerical disadvantage, the only chance was the first move. His cane swung out from his left hand, carrying a sharp wind sound, straight at the fox beastkin's temple.

The fox beastkin clearly didn't expect Yan Shen to be so decisive. He hastily tilted his head, the cane flying past his ear, bringing a fiery pain. He "hissed," his expression changing from playful to ferocious.

"Get him!"

Five people swarmed up.

Yan Shen didn't retreat. He knew he couldn't beat so many people, but he couldn't fall—Suisui was still waiting for him at home. His cane parried the first person's fist, then sidestepped to dodge, but that disabled left leg made his movement a beat slower.

A fist smashed into his abdomen.

Yan Shen grunted, his body bending from the pain, white tiger whiskers trembling. But he didn't fall, he gritted his teeth, using the cane to support his body, then swung his fist to counterattack. His fist landed on a person's face, that person staggering backward, nosebleed flowing.

This was an unequal battle.

Yan Shen had injuries, a disabled leg, but he was still a veteran. His cane blocked one person's kick, his shoulder knocked aside another person's lunge, he used his elbow to hit a third person's ribs. But every movement, that disabled left leg sent out heart-piercing pain, the old wound at the knee feeling like being burned by fire.

"Damn old bastard!" someone cursed, picking up a brick from the ground, smashing it hard toward Yan Shen's head.

Yan Shen barely tilted his head to avoid it, but the brick still grazed his temple, leaving a deep wound. Fresh blood immediately gushed out, flowing down his cheek, soaking his white tiger whiskers. Warm blood flowed into his eyes, turning his vision into a sea of red.

His vision began to blur, but he still stood.

He didn't shout, didn't threaten. Just silently, with those amber eyes staring at the people before him, like a beast driven to a dead end.

More people surrounded him. Fists, feet, clubs, falling like raindrops. Yan Shen protected his head, using his arms and body to endure the attacks. His fur was torn, skin cut open, warm blood seeping out, dyeing that old overcoat red.

The most painful was the left leg. Every time that crippled leg was kicked, that old wound sent out heart-piercing pain, like countless needles stabbing. Yan Shen's forehead broke out in cold sweat, thick brows tightly furrowed together, but he didn't cry out.

A fist smashed heavily into his abdomen, the force so heavy his body suddenly arched, a broken whimper escaping from his throat.

"Ugh—ah—!"

That wasn't just because of pain.

Yan Shen felt a strange, terrifying heat flow explode below his lower abdomen along with that strike. His cheeks uncontrollably flushed with a sickly crimson, that wasn't a normal reaction to being beaten—his body was betraying him.

"Wait, look..." one of the civilians suddenly stopped his movement, a flash of surprise in his eyes, immediately turning into malicious understanding, "This old bastard..."

Yan Shen desperately wanted to curl up, wanted to hide that willful change in his crotch. But that originally flat part, under continuous beating and pain stimulation, was uncontrollably, slowly and stubbornly standing up, pushing up a clearly visible outline beneath the worn pants.

"Oh?" that civilian let out a sharp and vicious sneer, "Old bastard, your body's quite 'sensitive'? Getting beaten gets you 'excited'? You former Beastshield ones, is this slutty cheapness in your bones?"

"Ptui! A dog can't stop eating shit!" another civilian spat, a cruel and excited light flashing in his eyes, lifting his foot more deliberately to kick at the position between Yan Shen's buttocks and hip bone, "Pretending to be proper!"

"Ugh—ah—! No... don't..." Yan Shen's body violently bounced, then heavily fell back onto the filthy ground. The erection in his crotch became even harder, hotter, the front fabric rapidly soaked and penetrated by a small stream of warm liquid. A strong, personal-scented fishy smell inevitably mixed into the blood and dust smell, permeating the narrow alley.

"Tsk... truly disgusting." A young civilian turned his face away, his voice carrying an imperceptible tremor, "E-enough, right? He's already like this..."

"What are you being soft-hearted for?" The leading fox beastkin shouted sharply, but the raised fist hesitated to fall for a long time. He looked at the beastkin curled on the ground, covered in blood yet still silently trembling, at that face blurred by bloodstains and some unspeakable shame, suddenly feeling a wave of inexplicable irritation.

This was no longer revenge.

The fox beastkin lowered his fist. He stared at Yan Shen for a long time, his gaze changing from anger to something deeper—perhaps weariness, perhaps something else.

"...Take him back." he suddenly said.

"What?" The person beside him didn't react.

"I said, take him back." The fox beastkin's voice lowered, carrying a decisiveness, "Just beating him to death like this is too easy."

He squatted down, reaching out to pinch Yan Shen's chin, forcing him to raise his head. Yan Shen's eyes were half-open, pupils unfocused, but there was still something burning inside—not anger, not fear, but something that made the fox beastkin feel uncomfortable... dignity.

"Former Beastshield's Yan Shen." The fox beastkin said softly, his voice carrying a nearly intimate cruelty, "Weren't you quite impressive? Didn't you still want to protect that precious son of yours?"

His finger slid to Yan Shen's Adam's apple, lightly pressing once.

"...I want you to know that in White Capital, former Beastshield people aren't even worth a dog."

A broken breath escaped Yan Shen's throat. He wanted to speak, wanted to curse back, but the extreme pain in his body and that strange, terrifying heat flow prevented him from organizing language. He just stared at the fox beastkin's eyes, with those amber eyes, staring at him.

"Take him away." The fox beastkin stood up, waving his hand.

Two people came up, one left one right holding Yan Shen's arms, dragging him up from the snow like dragging a bag of garbage. Yan Shen's left leg completely couldn't use strength, dragging on the ground, the wound at the knee wiping out a dark red trail on the snow.

His consciousness swayed between clear and fuzzy. He could feel the cold of the snow, could feel the pain of his body, could feel the sticky wetness in his crotch bringing huge shame. But he could also feel—those people didn't kill him.

Where were they taking him?

He didn't know. He only knew that his cane fell in the snow, making a dull sound. That sound echoed once in the silent alley, then disappeared.

Like him.

Yan Shen was dragged into the depths of the alley. The fox beastkin walked in front, pulling out a key from his pocket, opening a rusted iron door. Behind the door was a downward staircase, pitch black, emitting a moldy smell and some indescribable fishy smell.

"...Go down." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen was pushed, shoved, stumbling down the stairs. Every step his left leg felt like stepping on knife points, the old wound at the knee sending out a heart-piercing stinging pain in the cold air. His hands were twisted behind his back, tied with a rough hemp rope, the knot biting into the skin of his wrists, bringing a dull pain.

At the end of the stairs was a wooden door. The fox beastkin pushed open the door, a damp, rotten smell rushing out.

That was a basement. Not big, but very deep. The walls were bare cement, covered with water stains and mold spots. In the corner were piled some broken wooden crates and hemp sacks, covered with rat droppings. A wire hung from the ceiling, ending in a dim yellow light bulb, swaying slightly in the air, illuminating everything in the room in flickering light and shadow.

In the center of the basement, there was a rusty iron pipe, running from floor to ceiling. Tied to the iron pipe was an iron chain, and at the end of the chain was a leather collar.

Yan Shen saw that collar.

His pupils suddenly contracted.

The fox beastkin noticed his reaction. He laughed, that was a truly happy laugh, like a child discovering a new toy.

"...Looks like you understand." he said, "Good."

He walked forward, reaching out to unbutton Yan Shen's overcoat. That blood-soaked old overcoat was taken off, thrown on the ground. Then the sweater inside, the shirt. Yan Shen didn't resist—he also had no strength to resist. He just stood there, his naked upper body covered with bruises and wounds, white tiger whiskers trembling under the dim yellow light.

"...Kneel." the fox beastkin said.

Yan Shen didn't move.

The fox beastkin's smile stiffened slightly. Then he punched Yan Shen's abdomen—precisely that already vulnerable part.

"Ugh—ah—!" Yan Shen's body suddenly arched, his whole person kneeling to the ground. His knees hit the cold cement floor, making a dull sound.

The fox beastkin walked forward, putting that leather collar around Yan Shen's neck. The collar was very tight, biting into the fur, making Yan Shen's breathing difficult. Then, he fastened the chain to the collar, making a crisp "click" sound.

"...Welcome to your new home." The fox beastkin said softly.

He turned around, taking the others out of the basement. The wooden door slowly closed before Yan Shen, making a heavy dull sound.

Then, was the sound of the lock tongue clicking.

Click.

Yan Shen knelt on the cold cement floor, naked, bleeding, chained. His forehead pressed against the ground, nose tip almost touching his own blood. His breathing was very shallow, every inhalation making the collar tighten more, bringing a suffocating dizziness.

Darkness.

Complete darkness.

Only that dim yellow light bulb swaying overhead, casting Yan Shen's shadow on the cement wall, stretching it very, very long, like a skinned tiger.

His consciousness floated. The pain of his body, the stickiness in his crotch, the strangulation marks on his neck, all became fuzzy in this darkness. The only clear thing was the image in his mind—

Suisui.

Suisui was still at home. Was the door locked? Was the porridge still hot? Dad said he would come back. Dad said...

A tear slid from the corner of Yan Shen's eye. That wasn't a tear of pain, but something deeper—something he couldn't express in words.

He slowly, slowly, within the range the chain allowed, curled up his body. Like a wounded old beast, in the darkness, licking his wounds alone.
